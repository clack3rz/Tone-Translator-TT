SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & MANDATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 CONTEXT & ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE
Tone Translator (TT) is undergoing a fundamental architectural evolution: transitioning
from an AmpliTube 5 (AT5) preset-generation utility into a platform-independent,
acoustically grounded, professional AI Sound Engineer architecture.

The target macro-architecture establishes a strict, unidirectional causal flow:

    INPUT / EVIDENCE
           ↓
    AI SOUND ENGINEER (Reasoning from foundational acoustic first principles)
           ↓
    ENGINEERING DECISION (Declarative, platform-agnostic diagnostic and intervention plans)
           ↓
    SEMANTIC TONE DESIGN (High-level abstract audio topology and parameterization)
           ↓
    DETERMINISTIC VALIDATION (Acoustic rule verification, conflict detection, bounds check)
           ↓
    PLATFORM TRANSLATOR (Target-specific semantic-to-hardware mapping engine)
           ↓
    PLATFORM-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION / EXPORT (IK Multimedia AT5, Quad Cortex, Helix, Kemper, etc.)

In this future state, target platforms such as AmpliTube 5 remain supported deployment
targets, but they NEVER dictate, constrain, or define the Sound Engineer's internal
reasoning, diagnostic ontology, or epistemic knowledge base.

Current TT predates this separation of concerns. As a consequence, the existing repository
is an archaeological accumulation of:
  - Genuine, high-value sound-engineering principles and acoustic insights;
  - Hardcoded heuristic rules-of-thumb and genre conventions;
  - Curated artist and album tonal recipes;
  - Post-generation deterministic surgeries and forced parameter overrides;
  - Complex prompt engineering instructions;
  - Platform-independent acoustic concepts interleaved with platform-specific quirks;
  - Deep AmpliTube 5 hardware catalogue and XML parameter serialization schemas;
  - Advanced semantic abstractions (4D microphone placement, VIR coordinate systems);
  - Rigorous structural validation and deduplication logic;
  - Advanced Phase 1A/1B Sound Engineer architectural contracts and shadow execution scaffolding;
  - Dormant or obsolete features and bypassed UI inputs;
  - Multiple overlapping or potentially contradictory representations of the same engineering concepts.

Before the formal Phase 1C.4e Current TT Knowledge Migration Specification can be drafted,
this comprehensive, read-only archaeological survey establishes a complete, forensic record
of all engineering knowledge, heuristics, deterministic rules, and scaffolding present in
the repository.

THE MANDATE OF THIS SURVEY IS STRICTLY DESCRIPTIVE:
IT RECORDS WHAT EXISTS. IT DOES NOT YET DECIDE HOW THE NEW SYSTEM WILL IMPLEMENT IT.

  1. Prompts as Heuristic Knowledge Repositories:
     The central prompt (`MASTER_PROMPT` in `src/services/geminiService.ts`, ~49KB) embeds a
     sophisticated, highly structured acoustic philosophy ("Killer Rig Systematic Tone"). It defines
     core principles such as the "Gain Pivot" (pre-gain shaping vs. post-gain refining),
     "Gain Staging" (incremental distribution, pedal tightening), "Frequency Dominance" (midrange

     masking). It also enforces a strict 10-step systematic tone design reasoning order.
  2. Extensive Post-Generation Deterministic Surgery:
     Because the LLM frequently failed to satisfy strict engineering constraints (such as
     tight thrash rhythm parameters or exact microphone coordinate bindings), the codebase
     developed extensive deterministic override layers (`adjustThrashPedalSelection`,
     `filterDuplicateEqsWithRemoved`, `ensureSignalChainSemanticPlacements`). In critical
     benchmarks, hardcoded TypeScript logic overrides the LLM's output.
  3. Static Recipe Bypasses:

     Metallica Kill 'Em All), static deterministic recipes in `geminiService.ts` and
     `toneProfileBuilder.ts` completely bypass LLM reasoning when validation flags or keyword
     triggers match, returning hand-crafted preset structures with 100% confidence.
  4. Advanced Platform-Independent Semantic Abstractions:
     Tone Translator developed an exceptionally mature 4-dimensional semantic microphone placement
     model (Position: Cap/Cap Edge/Cone/Cone Edge; Orientation: N/E/S/W; Distance: Close/Medium/Far;
     Angle: On Axis/45° Off Axis) and a 17-dimensional Tone Profile taxonomy. These abstractions
     are genuinely platform-independent and represent high-value candidate assets for migration.
  5. Deep Platform-Specific Implementation Knowledge:
     The repository houses exhaustive knowledge of AmpliTube 5 internals: 386 catalogued gear items,

     Cartesian grid coordinate tables, XML parameter serialization rules, and undocumented hardware

  6. Disconnected Sensory Pipeline:
     While the UI presents dropzones for both "Target Audio" and "Your Recording", the "Your Recording"
     input (`recordingAudioFile` / `currentAudio`) is accepted, base64 encoded, passed into
     `translateTone()`, and then completely ignored. No DSP extraction occurs, and all nine audio
     metrics on `ToneProfile` are explicitly forced to `undefined`.
  7. Mature Phase 1A/1B Architectural Scaffolding:
     The repository contains a fully designed, strongly typed Sound Engineer architecture
     under `src/sound-engineer/` (EvidenceRecord, EngineeringDecisionRecord, SemanticToneDesign,

2.2 THE FIVE CRITICAL ARCHAEOLOGICAL DISTINCTIONS
For every discovered item in the repository, the survey applies five orthogonal diagnostic axes:

  AXIS A: ENGINEERING PROPOSITION
  - What declarative or imperative claim does this item make regarding sound, acoustics,
    signal behaviour, circuit dynamics, psychoacoustics, perceptual diagnosis, or engineering intervention?
  - Example: "Cutting low frequencies prior to non-linear distortion reduces intermodulation mud."

  AXIS B: PLATFORM / IMPLEMENTATION SPECIFICITY
  - Is the proposition a universal, platform-agnostic principle of sound engineering?
  - Or is it strictly bound to AmpliTube 5's internal models, parameter scales (0-10 vs dB vs Hz),
    GUID lookups, slot limits, or XML export requirements?

  AXIS C: DETERMINISTIC DECISION VS. HEURISTIC GUIDANCE
  - Is the mechanism deterministic (hardcoded TypeScript override, clamping function, mathematical
    coordinate transformation, strict deduplication filter)?
  - Or is it heuristic guidance (LLM prompt instruction, soft guideline, probabilistic recommendation)?

  AXIS D: OPERATIONAL STATUS
  - ACTIVE: Reachable and actively executed in the main user generation path (`translateTone`).
  - CONDITIONAL: Executed only when specific flags (`useValidationRecipes`) or keyword patterns match.
  - SHADOW: Executed in parallel observation/instrumentation mode without affecting user output.
  - TEST-ONLY: Exercised only within unit test suites (`src/tests/*`).
  - DORMANT / DEAD: Present in source files but unreachable by any active runtime call graph.

  AXIS E: CANDIDATE VALUE FOR PHASE 1C.4e MIGRATION
  - HIGH: Contains foundational sound engineering knowledge, acoustic rules, or mature semantic
    abstractions that should be deconstructed, attributed, and validated for the canonical knowledge base.
  - MEDIUM: Useful heuristic, recipe, or diagnostic pattern requiring significant disentanglement
    from platform-specific assumptions.
  - PLATFORM-TRANSLATION ONLY: Retains value strictly as target-specific mapping logic for the AT5
    Platform Translator, not as general sound engineering reasoning.
  - SCAFFOLDING / DISPENSABLE: Architectural glue, obsolete UI wrappers, or transient shims with
    no independent knowledge value.

3.1 THE MASTER PROMPT (`src/services/geminiService.ts`)
The `MASTER_PROMPT` represents the primary engineering instruction set provided to the
Gemini 3.8 Flash model. It spans over 4,500 words and embodies a comprehensive acoustic
philosophy termed "Killer Rig Systematic Tone".

3.1.1 Embedded Acoustic Philosophy: "Killer Rig Systematic Tone"
The prompt articulates five foundational engineering tenets:
  1. The Gain Pivot:
     - Core Proposition: Gain defines the harmonic structure of the signal. The signal chain
       is bifurcated around the primary non-linear saturation block (the amplifier preamp/power amp).
     - Pre-Gain (Input/Stomp): Dedicated strictly to shaping dynamic behaviour and frequency content
       entering saturation (Wah, Compressor, Mid-boosters, bass-tightening EQ).
     - Post-Gain (Loop/Rack): Dedicated strictly to refining post-saturation tonal balance (EQ for
       mix positioning, modulation for stereo width/dimension, delay/reverb for spatial acoustics).
  2. Gain Staging & Pre-Distortion Tightening:
     - Core Proposition: Gain should be distributed incrementally across multiple gentle stages
       rather than driven to maximum in a single stage.
     - Specific Heuristic: High-gain amplifier heads produce excessive intermodulation distortion
       and "flub" if driven with unattenuated low frequencies. Inserting an overdrive pedal
       (specifically OverScream / TS9) with Drive set to minimum (0-1) and Level set high (9-10)
       attenuates sub-bass rumble while pushing upper-mid frequencies into the amp's first gain stage.
  3. Frequency Dominance & Midrange Priority:
     - Core Proposition: The electric guitar is fundamentally a midrange instrument occupying
       the 300 Hz - 4 kHz band. Extreme mid-scooping removes the guitar from its functional mix window.
     - Specific Heuristic: Even in genres requiring a scooped curve (Thrash/Death Metal), the scoop
       must be moderate (2-4 on an equalizer scale) and must be compensated for with post-gain
       presence and upper-mid bite to retain articulation.
  4. Causal Troubleshooting Logic:
     - Muddy Tone -> Causal Intervention: Reduce Bass BEFORE distortion (pre-gain high-pass filtering).
     - Harsh / Fizzy Tone -> Causal Intervention: Reduce Treble/Presence AFTER distortion (post-gain smoothing).
     - Lost in Mix -> Causal Intervention: Boost midrange frequencies between 700 Hz and 1.5 kHz.
     - Messy / Cluttered Delays -> Causal Intervention: Synchronize delay time to musical tempo
       and position the delay strictly post-distortion in the effects loop or rack.

3.1.2 Systematic 10-Step Tone Design Order
`MASTER_PROMPT` enforces a mandatory sequential reasoning order for signal chain synthesis:
  Step 1:  Identify tone goal (genre, role, era, production environment).
  Step 2:  Select amplifier family (American Clean, British Crunch, High-Gain California, Solid-State).
  Step 3:  Select specific amplifier model from the injected catalogue.
  Step 4:  Select cabinet construction type (Open Back, Closed Back, Semi-Open).
  Step 5:  Select speaker family (Vintage 30, Greenback, G12T-75, Jensen, Electro-Voice).
  Step 6:  Assign microphone roles (Primary Attack, Body/Punch, Warmth/Fizz-Control, Detail, Room).
  Step 7:  Select specific microphone models matching those roles (Dynamic 57, Ribbon 121, etc.).
  Step 8:  Select 4D semantic placement strategy (Position, Orientation, Distance, Angle).
  Step 9:  Select acoustic room ambience and level.
  Step 10: Document causal explanations in `amplifier_debug` and `microphone_debug` blocks.

3.1.3 Cabinet ↔ Speaker Coupling Logic in Prompt
The prompt injects explicit acoustic rules governing cabinet enclosure and transducer pairing:
  - Open-Back + Jensen (American 12C/12K): Spacious, three-dimensional, sparkling American clean
    with extended high-frequency air and unconstrained low-end dispersion.
  - Closed-Back + Celestion Vintage 30 (Brit V1/V2): Focused, punchy, directional projection
    with aggressive 2-4 kHz upper-mid spike; industry standard for cutting through dense drums.
  - Closed-Back + Electro-Voice (EV Darkness): High-headroom, uncolored, ultra-tight low-end
    capable of handling massive low-frequency transients without cone breakup.
  - Closed-Back + Celestion Greenback (Brit Green): Warm, organic, mid-rich vintage crunch with
    early mechanical cone saturation and soft high-frequency rolloff.

3.1.4 Microphone Role & Placement Guidance in Prompt
The prompt codifies specific acoustic interactions between microphone transducers and speaker cones:
  - Shure SM57 ("Dynamic 57"): Primary attack/presence mic.
    * Standard placement: "Cap Edge, Close, On Axis" (captures immediate transient attack while
      avoiding the harsh phase cancellation and extreme treble spike of the dust-cap center).
    * High-gain fizz mitigation: "Cap Edge, Close, 45° Off Axis" (preserves transient bite while
      utilizing off-axis frequency rejection to smooth fizz).
  - Royer R-121 ("Ribbon 121"): Warmth and fizz-control mic.
    * Standard placement: "Cone, Close, 45° Off Axis" (ribbon figure-8 pattern and smooth top-end
      tame high-frequency sizzle while capturing warm lower-mid resonance).
    * Vintage crunch placement: "Cone, Close, On Axis" (natural warm bloom without treble damping).
  - Neumann U87 ("Condenser 87"): Clean detail, high-fidelity polish, or room microphone.
    * Placed at "Cap Edge, Close, On Axis" for pristine detail, or "Cone, Medium, On Axis" for room air.
  - Sennheiser MD421 ("Dynamic 421"): Body and punch mic.
    * Placed at "Cone, Close, On Axis" to capture woody low-mids and heavy palm-mute punch.

3.2 THE TONE PROFILE BUILDER PROMPT (`src/services/toneProfileBuilder.ts`)
The `SYSTEM_PROMPT` in `toneProfileBuilder.ts` establishes a dedicated pre-processing stage.
Its purpose is to extract a multidimensional taxonomy prior to hardware selection.

Key Knowledge Encoded:
  - Strict abstraction: The prompt explicitly forbids selecting specific gear brand names or AT5
    models at this stage; it forces the LLM to output abstract character-driven descriptors.
  - 17-Dimensional Classification Schema:
    * `style`: Multi-tag genre categorization (classic rock, thrash metal, grunge, etc.).
    * `role`: rhythm, lead, clean, acoustic_style, bass, mixed.
    * `gain_level`: clean, edge, low, medium-low, medium, medium-high, high.
    * `distortion_type`: clean, edge_of_breakup, crunch, overdrive, saturated, fuzz.
    * `low_end`: loose, controlled, tight, boomy.
    * `midrange`: scooped, neutral, forward, aggressive.
    * `highs`: dark, smooth, bright, harsh.
    * `attack`: soft, balanced, sharp.
    * `dynamics`: dynamic, slightly_compressed, compressed.
    * `effects_profile`: none, minimal, moderate, heavy.
    * `room`: dry, small, medium, large.
    * `width`: mono, narrow, double_tracked, wide.
    * `production_style`: raw, polished, live, studio.
  - Initial Family Guidance with Negative Constraints:
    Forces the generation of explicit `amp_family_avoid` and `effects_avoid` lists. E.g., for
    classic rock rhythm, the system explicitly commands avoidance of modern high-gain heads,
    preamp distortion, and delay/chorus wash.

SECTION 5 — DETERMINISTIC OVERRIDES, SURGICAL MUTATIONS & HARDCODED RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A dominant discovery of this archaeological survey is that Tone Translator relies heavily
on post-generation TypeScript mutations to enforce sound engineering rules that the LLM

5.1 THRASH PEDAL SELECTION & SURGERY (`adjustThrashPedalSelection`)
Located in `src/services/geminiService.ts` (lines 350-520):

5.1.1 The OverScream vs. PROdrive Dilemma:
  - Engineering Proposition: For tight rhythm thrash metal (especially into Marshall-style amps),
    a Tubescreamer-style symmetrical clipping overdrive (OverScream) with high output level and low
    drive functions as an active high-pass filter and mid-boost. Conversely, a hard-clipping distortion
    pedal (such as the ProCo RAT / PROdrive) introduces excessive low-end fuzz and chaotic clipping
    diodes that turn palm-muted rhythm chugs into indistinct mud.
  - Hardcoded Logic:
    * The system inspects prompt text for thrash keywords (`metallica`, `kill em all`, `thrash`,
      `palm mute`, `pick attack`, `nwobhm`) and inspects whether a Marshall-style head was selected.
    * If thrash context is detected, ANY distortion pedal (PROdrive, The RAT, fuzz, etc.) is
      DETERMINISTICALLY REPLACED with "OverScream".
    * If the user explicitly requested vintage raw clipping (`fuzzy`, `raw`, `buzzy`, `dirty`),
      OverScream settings are forced to: Drive: 4.5, Tone: 5.5, Level: 6.5.
    * If modern tight thrash is requested, settings are forced to: Drive: 1.0, Tone: 6.8, Level: 9.0.
    * If no overdrive was generated by Gemini, code surgically INSERTS an OverScream pedal
      immediately before the amplifier in the signal chain.
    * The engineering notes are programmatically rewritten to justify the OverScream insertion.

5.2 NOISE GATE RECALIBRATION & SCALING
Located in `src/services/geminiService.ts` (lines 360-440):

5.2.1 Parameter Scaling & Unit Harmonization:
  - Engineering Proposition: A high-gain rhythm tone requires fast gating to truncate string hum
    and silence between syncopated chugs, but excessive threshold cuts note decay unnaturally.
  - Hardcoded Logic:
    * Gemini frequently outputs raw numbers (0-10) or fractional seconds (0.12s) for release time.
    * TypeScript logic intercepts every Noise Gate in the chain.
    * Release scaling: If value <= 1.5, it scales by 1000 (seconds to ms). If value <= 10, it converts
      using linear formula `20 + value * 148` ms. Clamped between 20 ms and 1500 ms.
    * For thrash contexts, Release is hard-calibrated to exactly "120 ms", Threshold to "-45 dB",
      and Depth to "-60 dB", unless the user explicitly requested long sustain.

5.3 SIGNAL CHAIN NORMALIZATION & EQ DEDUPLICATION (`at5SignalChainNormalizer.ts`)

SECTION 6 — DOMAIN TAXONOMY, SEMANTIC ABSTRACTIONS & ACOUSTIC MODELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Current TT contains several highly sophisticated, platform-independent semantic abstractions
that represent genuine engineering models rather than AmpliTube-specific code.

6.1 THE 4-DIMENSIONAL SEMANTIC MICROPHONE PLACEMENT MODEL
Located in `src/services/at5MicPlacementService.ts` and `src/types.ts`:

6.1.1 Semantic Axes:
  Tone Translator models microphone placement using four independent spatial axes:
  1. Position (Radial distance from speaker cone center):
     - `Cap`: Direct center of dust cap. Maximum high-frequency capture, immediate transient bite,
       highest susceptibility to harshness and phase anomalies.
     - `Cap Edge`: The junction between dust cap and cone body. Optimal balance of attack definition
       and full body. Industry standard default starting position.
     - `Cone`: The sloping paper/composite cone body. Warmer, attenuated highs, emphasized lower-mids.
     - `Cone Edge`: Outer perimeter near the cabinet surround. Darkest response, maximum treble rolloff.
  2. Orientation (Cardinal angular position on speaker face):
     - `N` (12:00 clock), `E` (03:00 clock), `S` (06:00 clock), `W` (09:00 clock).
     - Acoustic principle: While a circular cone is theoretically symmetrical, real-world speaker
       baffles exhibit boundary reflections and proximity interactions with cabinet floors/walls.
     - In AT5 VIR: Moving to 'W' or 'E' activates specific impulse response capture positions.
  3. Distance (Proximity to speaker grill cloth):
     - `Close`: Direct grill contact (< 2 inches). Maximum acoustic proximity effect (bass boost),
       dry, zero room reflection, maximum transient punch.
     - `Medium`: 6 to 18 inches off grill. Natural acoustic acoustic blend, reduced proximity bass,
       subtle room air.
     - `Far`: > 3 feet. Ambient spatial room capture.
  4. Angle (Incidence to speaker cone surface):
     - `On Axis` (0°): Microphone diaphragm is perpendicular to speaker face. Preserves direct highs.
     - `45° Off Axis`: Microphone diaphragm is tilted 45 degrees. Utilizes polar rejection to roll off
       fizz (> 6 kHz) while preserving mid-frequency attack.

6.2 THE VIR (VOLUMETRIC IMPULSE RESPONSE) COORDINATE ENGINE
Located in `src/services/at5MicPlacementService.ts`:

6.2.1 Mathematical Transformation from Semantics to Cartesian Coordinates:
  Tone Translator maps the high-level 4D semantic model into IK Multimedia's proprietary
  VIR 3D coordinate space [X, Y, Z] + rotation angles [RotX, RotY]:
  - `VIR_CALIBRATION_COORDINATES` table defines exact physical coordinates for every valid semantic permutation:
    * Cap, Close, On Axis: X = 0.0, Y = 0.0, Z = 0.0
    * Cap Edge, W, Close, On Axis: X = -0.32, Y = 0.0, Z = 0.0
    * Cap Edge, W, Close, 45° Off Axis: X = -0.32, Y = 0.0, Z = 0.0, RotY = 45.0
    * Cone, W, Close, On Axis: X = -0.58, Y = 0.0, Z = 0.0
    * Cone, W, Close, 45° Off Axis: X = -0.58, Y = 0.0, Z = 0.0, RotY = 45.0
    * Cone Edge, W, Close, On Axis: X = -0.85, Y = 0.0, Z = 0.0
  - Supports calibration overrides (`VIRReferenceOverrides`) permitting fine-tuning of coordinates
    per reference cabinet without altering semantic reasoning.

6.3 MULTI-MICROPHONE ROLE-BASED BLENDING ENGINE
Located in `src/services/at5MicPlacementReasoning.ts`:

6.3.1 Acoustic Role Taxonomy:
  The system defines six formal functional microphone roles:
  1. `primary attack/presence mic`: SM57, i5. Captures pick attack and upper-mid clarity.
  2. `warmth/fizz-control mic`: R121, VR1, R160. Smooths harsh distortion sizzle, adds body.
  3. `body/punch mic`: MD421. Thickens fundamental low-mids and palm-mute punch.
  4. `detail/polish mic`: U87, C414. High-fidelity acoustic air and string separation.
  5. `room/space mic`: Ambient condensers/ribbons positioned at medium/far distance.
  6. `bass low-end/fullness mic`: RE20, D112, MD421 on bass enclosures.

6.3.2 Multi-Mic Relational Constraints:
  - Complementary pairing rule: Mic 0 and Mic 1 must not duplicate identical roles and positions.
  - If Mic 0 is assigned attack (Cap Edge, Close, On Axis), Mic 1 must be assigned warmth/body
    (Cone, Close, Off Axis or On Axis).

SECTION 14 — CONTRADICTIONS, DUPLICATIONS, PLATFORM COUPLING & EPISTEMIC ANOMALIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The archaeological survey revealed several significant architectural anomalies, contradictions,
and coupling issues that must be addressed during Phase 1C.4e:

14.1 PROMPT REASONING VS. CODE OVERRIDES (COGNITIVE DISSONANCE)
  - Anomaly: The system instructs Gemini in `MASTER_PROMPT` to reason autonomously about pedal selection,
    noise gate parameters, and microphone coordinates.
  - Conflict: In the active execution path, `adjustThrashPedalSelection` and `ensureSignalChainSemanticPlacements`
    subsequently inspect the LLM output and ruthlessly overwrite it with hardcoded values whenever thrash,
    metal, or specific amps are detected.
  - Epistemic Impact: The application claims to be an "AI Sound Engineer", but in high-stakes genres it
    functions largely as an AI keyword extractor feeding a deterministic TypeScript state machine.

14.2 SENSORY DECOUPLING (THE GHOST AUDIO PIPELINE)
  - Anomaly: The UI presents the user with an audio recording dropzone.
  - Conflict: The audio is accepted, converted to base64, and passed into `translateTone()`, where it
    is completely dropped and never used. In `toneProfileBuilder.ts`, all audio feature extraction
    fields are forced to `undefined`.
  - Epistemic Impact: Zero sensory grounding currently exists; all decisions are driven by text prompts.

14.3 PLATFORM CONTAMINATION OF SOUND ENGINEERING ADVICE
  - Anomaly: The `amplifier_debug` and `microphone_debug` explanations emitted by the system frequently
    justify engineering choices using AmpliTube-specific constraints (e.g., "Darrell 100 requires Gear
    Discovery for proper GUID serialization", or "Parametric EQ is limited to 2 bands in AT5").
  - Epistemic Impact: The AI Sound Engineer's reasoning is contaminated with platform export mechanics,
    violating the core architectural requirement that the sound engineer must remain platform-independent.

14.4 MULTIPLE OVERLAPPING SCHEMAS FOR CAB SETTINGS
  - Anomaly: In `at5SignalChainNormalizer.ts` and `presetExporter.ts`, code contains complex logic to detect
    whether cab settings use legacy 1-indexed keys (`Mic_1`, `Mic_2`, `Mic 1 Placement`) or canonical
    0-indexed keys (`Mic_0`, `Mic_1`, `Mic_0_Placement`).
  - Epistemic Impact: Unnecessary scaffolding exists solely to reconcile historical schema mutations.

14.5 DIVERGENCE BETWEEN PHASE 1B CONTRACTS AND RUNTIME EXECUTION
  - Anomaly: The rich domain contracts in `src/sound-engineer/layer-contracts/` (EvidenceRecord,
    EngineeringDecisionRecord, SemanticToneDesign) are fully typed and tested, but they do NOT execute
    in the live user preset generation path. They execute only in synthetic test fixtures and shadow runs.
