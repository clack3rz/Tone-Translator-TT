  1. PRE-ACTION VALIDATION & PREDICTION CONSUMPTION: Ingesting sealed Stage 11 outputs, elaborating observable consequences, performing pre-action simulation/testing, and verifying preservation constraints prior to execution handoff.

  7. RETROSPECTIVE ENGINEERING REVIEW: Conducting independent, non-punitive audits of diagnostic validity, intervention suitability, predictive accuracy, and generalizable learnings (Stage 14).

SECTION 5 — THE 21 FROZEN PRINCIPLES OF SOUND ENGINEERING REASONING
================================================================================
Phase 1C.5e operates in strict subordination to the 21 Principles of Sound Engineering Reasoning, established in Phase 1C.5a v0.3c.
In strict compliance with VC-01, each principle is presented in a two-part decoupled structure:
  1. FROZEN PRINCIPLE: The exact verbatim principle title and text from Phase 1C.5a v0.3c.
  2. PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION: A separately labelled operational explanation of how Phase 1C.5e applies the principle to post-decision outcome evaluation, iteration, and review.
Under no circumstances is an operational interpretation described as frozen or verbatim, nor are Phase 1C.5e rules merged into the frozen principle itself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  1. Evidence Precedes Diagnosis.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  In the post-decision lifecycle, actual outcome evaluation begins with captured, calibrated acoustic observations and critical listening data. Evaluation of whether an intervention succeeded or failed must never precede the acquisition of actual post-intervention evidence.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  2. Observation is not Interpretation.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  Raw acoustic telemetry (FFT spectra, LUFS readings, crest factor measurements) and unprocessed listening notes must be recorded independently of interpretative judgments. An observed 3 dB drop at 250 Hz is an empirical observation; asserting that "low-mid mud has been eliminated" is an interpretative claim.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  3. Missing Evidence is not Negative Evidence.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  The absence of post-intervention measurement or listening feedback along a particular dimension (e.g. unmeasured transient response or uncollected vocal air telemetry) is never treated as proof that no degradation occurred. Missing evidence is explicitly logged as UNCERTAIN_UNMEASURED.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 4
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  4. A Symptom Does Not Identify Its Cause.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  A change in an audible symptom following an intervention does not automatically identify the physical mechanism that produced the shift. Apparent reduction of harshness may stem from desired resonance attenuation or accidental high-frequency rolloff elsewhere in the chain.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 5
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  5. Diagnosis Should Be Causal Where Evidence Permits.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  Causal attribution of an observed outcome to an executed intervention requires isolating the active physical mechanism. Post-intervention evaluation audits external acoustic and processing confounders before attributing observed changes to the intervention.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 6
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  6. Competing Hypotheses Survive Until Evidence Justifies Narrowing Them.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  When an unexpected outcome or partial failure occurs, all plausible explanations (e.g. sub-optimal parameter choice, incorrect intervention locus, unexpected acoustic masking, or external tracking variance) must be preserved until discriminating tests eliminate them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 7
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  7. Context Informs Reasoning but Does Not Prove Causation.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  Arrangement density, musical genre, and instrumentation provide essential priors for establishing evaluation tolerances, but contextual expectations never substitute for empirical post-intervention outcome evidence.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 8
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  8. Engineering Intent Constrains the Solution.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  Outcome success is measured strictly against the explicitly declared engineering requirements and preservation constraints established in Stage 08. An intervention that alters the signal pleasantly but violates declared intent is not evaluated as a successful outcome.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 9
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  9. Generate Alternatives When the Problem Admits Alternatives.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  When an intervention fails or incurs unacceptable trade-offs, the system does not endlessly adjust parameters at the same locus. It evaluates credible alternative intervention candidates formulated during Phase 1C.5d Stage 09.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRINCIPLE 10
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  10. Intervention Selection Follows Diagnosis.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:
  Any corrective adjustment or iterative refinement must remain causally bound to an earned diagnosis. The system is forbidden from applying arbitrary downstream signal processing without an identified diagnostic basis.

FROZEN PRINCIPLE:
  11. Intervention Should Occur at the Causally Appropriate Point.

PHASE 1C.5e OPERATIONAL INTERPRETATION:

6.1 PREDICTION CONSUMPTION CONTRACT
Phase 1C.5e consumes two sealed upstream records delivered at the Stage 11 boundary:

6.2 PRE-ACTION VALIDATION & SIMULATION LANE
Phase 1C.5d explicitly delegates pre-action consequence elaboration, simulation, and predictive refinement to Phase 1C.5e.
Phase 1C.5e establishes a dedicated Pre-Action Validation Lane prior to Stage 12 dispatch:
  1. Consequence Elaboration: Deconstructing high-level predictions into measurable acoustic delta expectations across frequency, dynamics, phase, and time domains.
  2. Model-Based Simulation: Where feasible and appropriate, running predictive acoustic or DSP simulations (e.g. transfer function simulation, phase cancellation modeling, gain-staging projection) to test whether the intervention will behave as expected.
  3. Pre-Action Verification of Preservation & Trade-Offs: Verifying that simulated consequences do not breach the non-exceedance bounds defined in `ConstraintAndTradeOffEvaluationRecord`.
  4. Targeted Pre-Execution Tests: Where predictive uncertainty remains high, designing non-destructive, discriminating micro-tests (e.g. temporary bypass sweeps or brief offline test renders).
  5. Refinement of Expected Consequence Model: Updating the testable expectation model with higher-resolution bounds derived from simulation without modifying the sealed upstream records.

6.3 THE SIMULATION FIREWALL
A fundamental architectural law governs the Pre-Action Validation Lane:
  "SIMULATION IS NOT ACTUAL OUTCOME EVIDENCE."
Deterministic algorithmic models and simulation routines validate physical constraints and bounds; they do not replace sound engineering judgment (Principle 17). Simulated outcomes are testable expectations; they NEVER satisfy the requirement for empirical post-intervention evidence.

================================================================================
SECTION 7 — THE OUTCOME EVIDENCE FIREWALL
================================================================================
7.1 CORE FIREWALL AXIOM (PRINCIPLE 18)
  "A PREDICTED OUTCOME IS A TESTABLE EXPECTATION. IT IS NOT EVIDENCE THAT THE INTERVENTION WORKED."
The Outcome Evidence Firewall strictly decouples expectations from observations:
  - Upstream Stage 11 predicted outcomes, pre-action simulation models, and engineer expectations exist exclusively on the EXPECTATION side of the firewall.
  - Post-intervention audio waveforms, measured telemetry, critical listening observations, and user feedback exist exclusively on the EVIDENCE side of the firewall.

7.2 FIREWALL GOVERNANCE RULES
  - Rule FW-01 (Strict Decoupling): Outcome evaluation engines must never consume predicted delta values as evidence inputs.
  - Rule FW-02 (Anti-Assumption Rule): The system must never assume an action succeeded merely because an external execution engine reported success. Code execution only proves that instructions ran; it provides no proof of acoustic efficacy.
  - Rule FW-03 (Anti-Conversion Rule): The system must never convert a predicted parameter into an observed metric (e.g. converting a predicted "-3 dB cut" into an observed claim of "-3 dB mud reduction").
  - Rule FW-04 (Missing Evidence Preserved): If post-intervention audio, telemetry, or user listening observations are missing or incomplete, the missing dimension must be explicitly recorded as `UNCERTAIN_UNMEASURED` (Principle 3, 21). Missing evidence is never populated with simulated or predicted values.

SECTION 8 — ACTUAL OUTCOME EVIDENCE ARCHITECTURE (STAGE 13)
================================================================================
8.1 MULTI-MODAL EVIDENCE INTAKE
Actual outcome evidence enters Stage 13 through four distinct, non-fungible modalities:
  1. Acoustic Waveform & Spectral Telemetry:
     - Post-intervention audio captures, FFT spectral analysis, spectrograms, loudness metrics (LUFS momentary, short-term, integrated), crest factor, and true-peak levels.
  2. Measured Equipment & DSP State Telemetry:
     - Realized parameter states from external engines (verified filter center frequencies, Q factors, gain reduction meters, THD metrics).
  3. Professional Critical Listening Observations:
     - Auditory evaluations by trained sound engineers focusing on timbre, depth, masking dynamics, envelope impact, and musicality.
  4. User / Client Perceptual Feedback:
     - Subjective reports, qualitative observations, and artistic preference declarations from the user.

8.2 MODALITY-DEPENDENT & QUESTION-RELATIVE EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY
Evidence sufficiency cannot be judged by an abstract universal metric or a generic score. In sound engineering, sufficiency is strictly:
  - MODALITY-DEPENDENT: Certain questions require specific modalities. For example, verifying that total harmonic distortion stayed below threshold requires calibrated spectral measurement; assessing whether emotional vocal intimacy is preserved requires critical listening.
  - QUESTION-RELATIVE: Evidence is evaluated relative to the specific hypothesis, requirement, or non-exceedance bound under test:
    * Evaluating attenuation of a high-Q acoustic resonance requires high-resolution spectral analysis;
    * Evaluating transient punch on drums requires time-domain envelope analysis across the attack phase;
    * If evidence is sufficient to evaluate spectral changes but missing for transient preservation, the system records:
      `EVALUATION_FINDING: PARTIALLY_EVIDENCED (Improvement: SUFFICIENT; Preservation: INSUFFICIENT_UNCERTAIN)`.
      In accordance with Principle 3 and Principle 21, uncertainty survives precisely where evidence is absent.

8.3 CASE-SPECIFIC GAIN-MATCHING & CALIBRATION DISCIPLINE
To avoid the Fletcher-Munson equal-loudness trap (where louder signals are systematically misperceived as better sounding), pre- and post-intervention signals must be gain-matched before comparative critical listening or subjective evaluation.
Phase 1C.5e rejects arbitrary universal prescriptions:
  - Universal ±0.1 LUFS mandates are removed. While close loudness alignment is necessary, the exact alignment window, integration period, and metric (integrated LUFS vs momentary peak vs RMS) must be case-specific and appropriate to the musical material.
  - Level-matched bypass switching must be used during critical listening to isolate spectral and dynamic shifts from gain changes.
  - Measurement calibration must account for dynamic variations across takes or performance sections.

================================================================================
SECTION 9 — OUTCOME COMPARISON & MULTI-DIMENSIONAL DELTA EVALUATION
================================================================================
9.1 THE 5-WAY COMPARISON MATRIX
Outcome evaluation compares the actual post-intervention evidence against five distinct benchmarks:
  1. Baseline State (Pre-Intervention Evidence): The acoustic state prior to the action (establishing the raw delta).
  2. Engineering Requirement (Phase 1C.5d Stage 08): The explicit physical and acoustic requirement to be satisfied (Principle 8).
  3. Predicted Outcome (Phase 1C.5d Stage 11): The specific changes, bounds, and trade-offs expected prior to execution (Principle 12).
  4. Preservation Bounds: Non-exceedance limits established in `ConstraintAndTradeOffEvaluationRecord` to protect musical integrity.
  5. Reference Target (if applicable): Target reference audio, evaluated without copying non-contextual artifacts.

9.2 MULTI-DIMENSIONAL DELTA ANALYSIS
Acoustic changes must be evaluated across independent acoustic dimensions:
  - Spectral Delta (Delta_S): Frequency-dependent energy redistribution across critical bands.
  - Dynamic Delta (Delta_D): Crest factor shifts, envelope sharpness, macro-dynamic range.
  - Phase & Time Delta (Delta_T): Group delay variations, comb filtering notches, transient phase smearing.
  - Harmonic Delta (Delta_H): Total harmonic distortion, intermodulation distortion, saturation profile.
  - Spatial Delta (Delta_W): Stereo correlation, width coefficient, mid/side energy balance, perceived depth.

9.3 EVALUATING PREDICTED VS OBSERVED DELTAS
For each dimension, the system computes:
  - Accuracy of Improvement: Did the observed delta match the predicted direction and magnitude?
  - Falsification Check: Did any observed delta breach explicit falsification criteria established in Stage 11?
  - Non-Exceedance Check: Did any secondary parameter breach preservation bounds?
All evaluations use requirement-derived, case-specific tolerances rather than dogmatic universal numbers.

================================================================================
SECTION 10 — PRESERVATION REQUIREMENTS EVALUATION
================================================================================
10.1 THE PRESERVATION IMPERATIVE
An intervention that cures an acoustic defect while destroying essential musical qualities is an engineering failure.
Outcome evaluation must explicitly audit all preservation requirements defined in `EngineeringRequirementRecord`.

10.2 AUDITED PRESERVATION QUALITIES
Stage 13 performs dedicated preservation audits across:
  1. Articulation & Intelligibility: Vocal consonant definition, guitar pick attack, bass note separation.
  2. Transient Punch & Definition: Drum impact; ensuring compressors or clippers have not blunted leading edges.
  3. Low-End Weight & Authority: Sub-bass fundamental integrity (40–90 Hz); ensuring filtering has not weakened foundation.
  4. Body, Warmth & Sustain: Core midrange resonance (200–800 Hz); ensuring notch filters have not hollowed out the instrument.
  5. High-Frequency Air & Smoothness: Top-end extension (>10 kHz); ensuring de-essing has not produced a dull, suffocated presentation.
  6. Dynamic Expression: Micro-dynamics and natural breathing; ensuring over-compression has not created a static wall of sound.
  7. Spatial Imaging & Phase Integrity: Stereo field width and mono compatibility; ensuring processing has not introduced destructive phase cancellation.
  8. Noise Floor & Artifact Profile: S/N ratio, absence of digital quantization noise, pumping, or aliasing.

10.3 PRESERVATION DISPOSITION
If any primary preservation requirement is breached beyond agreed project tolerances:
  - The outcome cannot be judged a clean success;
  - It is registered as an adverse finding (`finding_preservation_breached` / `finding_expected_tradeoff_excessive`);
  - It triggers immediate consideration of checkpoint-aware reversion (Section 16).


================================================================================
SECTION 11 — TRADE-OFF, COLLATERAL DAMAGE & UNEXPECTED EFFECT EVALUATION
================================================================================
11.1 TRADE-OFF VERIFICATION (PRINCIPLE 13)
In Phase 1C.5d Stage 10, candidate interventions underwent trade-off deliberation recorded in `ConstraintAndTradeOffEvaluationRecord`.
Post-intervention evaluation must determine:
  1. Did predicted trade-offs actually materialize?
  2. Was the magnitude of the trade-off consistent with prediction?
  3. Did the trade-off remain acceptable within project context?
In accordance with Principle 13, trade-offs are evaluated qualitatively or quantitatively as appropriate to the domain, without dogmatic demands for numerical quantification where qualitative evaluation is technically sound.

11.2 UNEXPECTED COLLATERAL EFFECTS
Interventions frequently generate secondary consequences that were not foreseen:
  - Phase distortion from aggressive minimum-phase equalization;
  - Dynamic pumping or breathing in multiband processing;
  - Intermodulation distortion arising when saturating complex polyphonic material;
  - Unmasking of previously buried harsh resonances when cleaning low-mid mud.
When unexpected effects occur, they are recorded in an `UnexpectedOutcomeRecord` detailing acoustic character, severity, and musical impact.

11.3 REMOVAL OF UNIVERSAL NUMERIC / METHOD PRESCRIPTIONS
Phase 1C.5e eliminates all arbitrary universal numeric cutoffs across sound engineering decisions:
  - There are NO universal rules such as "mandatory ±0.1 LUFS matching everywhere", "mandatory 1/24-octave FFT", "mandatory 20 ms transient analysis window", or "fixed 1% THD limit".
  - In professional sound engineering:
    * An acoustic fingerpicking guitar may tolerate zero added THD (>0.1% unacceptable), whereas an aggressive industrial bass synth intentionally benefits from heavy saturation.
    * A high-Q notch on a stationary whistle requires narrow FFT binning, whereas broad vocal warmth adjustments are evaluated across octave-fraction bands.
  - All measurement methods, integration windows, and tolerance thresholds must be CASE-SPECIFIC, QUESTION-RELATIVE, and REQUIREMENT-DERIVED.

================================================================================
SECTION 12 — CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION ARCHITECTURE & CONFOUNDER GOVERNANCE
================================================================================
12.1 THE CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION QUESTION (PRINCIPLES 5 & 18)
Did the executed intervention actually produce the observed change, or did the change result from confounding variables?
Causal attribution is never assumed. It requires rigorous confounder isolation.

12.2 COMMON SOUND ENGINEERING CONFOUNDERS
Stage 13 explicitly audits six primary confounder categories:
  1. Gain / Loudness Discrepancies: Perceptual shifts caused purely by an uncompensated output level boost from a plug-in.
  2. Performance & Take Variability: Differences in player dynamics, pick attack strength, or vocalist distance between takes.
  3. Monitoring Chain & Room Acoustics: Non-linear monitor response, room mode interference, or monitoring volume shifts.
  4. Multi-Track Masking & Arrangement Shifts: Apparent clarity caused by fader adjustments, mutes, or arrangement density changes elsewhere in the mix.
  5. Measurement Window & FFT Leakage: Mismatched time windows or non-representative analysis frames.
  6. Concurrent Processing Changes: Downstream bus compressors or limiters reacting differently to altered peak structures.

12.3 CAUSAL EXCLUSION & FALSIFICATION DISAMBIGUATION
Phase 1C.5e establishes ten strict constitutional rules governing causal attribution:
  1. A failed remedy does NOT automatically falsify the diagnosis. An intervention may fail due to sub-optimal parameterization, wrong tool locus, or masking, while the diagnosed causal mechanism remains real.
  2. A successful remedy does NOT automatically confirm the diagnosis. A symptom may improve through coincidental broadband masking or level shifts even if the diagnosed cause was inaccurate.
  3. Excluding one cause does NOT prove another cause. Disproving pre-amp clipping does not automatically prove speaker breakup; affirmative evidence is required for every asserted cause.
  4. Directional improvement does NOT prove unique causal mechanism.
  5. Lack of symptom change is only diagnostic evidence if the test validly manipulated or isolated the hypothesized cause.
  6. Falsification requires an adequately sensitive, controlled, discriminating test (Principle 21).
  7. Confounders, execution failure, inadequate intervention magnitude, measurement limitations, and multi-causal mechanisms must remain plausible explanations where applicable.
  8. Phase 1C.5e records the outcome's epistemic impact; it does NOT unilaterally rewrite diagnostic causal models.
  9. Authoritative diagnosis revision remains strictly owned by Phase 1C.5b / 1C.5c.
  10. New intervention selection following diagnostic revision remains strictly owned by Phase 1C.5d.

Under VC-05, dogmatic claims like "If reducing a cause fails to alter the defect, the diagnosis must be falsified" are purged.
Instead:
  "If a valid discriminating test adequately isolates or removes the hypothesized causal variable and the predicted causal consequence fails under controlled conditions, the hypothesis may be weakened, excluded, or falsified at the specificity actually earned by the evidence."

================================================================================
SECTION 13 — TWO-LAYER OUTCOME EVALUATION MODEL: FINDINGS & ACTIONS
================================================================================
13.1 DECOUPLING FINDINGS FROM ACTIONS
Real-world sound engineering outcomes are nuanced and multi-dimensional. Forcing a complex outcome into a single closed 9-state label is artificially restrictive and erases secondary findings.
Phase 1C.5e replaces the single closed taxonomy with a two-layer model:

13.2 LAYER A — INDEPENDENT OUTCOME FINDINGS
Multiple findings can and do coexist simultaneously. They are not mutually exclusive:
  - `finding_intended_requirement_satisfied`: Target acoustic requirement achieved.
  - `finding_partial_improvement`: Directional improvement observed, but fell short of target.
  - `finding_requirement_unmet`: Target acoustic requirement not achieved.
  - `finding_preservation_intact`: All audited musical qualities remained within non-exceedance bounds.
  - `finding_preservation_breached`: One or more protected qualities degraded beyond tolerance.
  - `finding_expected_tradeoff_acceptable`: Anticipated trade-offs materialized and remain acceptable.
  - `finding_expected_tradeoff_excessive`: Anticipated trade-offs exceeded acceptable severity.
  - `finding_unexpected_adverse_effect`: Unpredicted collateral damage or artifact observed.
  - `finding_unexpected_beneficial_effect`: Unpredicted positive musical quality emerged.
  - `finding_new_symptom_observed`: Previously unobserved defect or masking issue unmasked.
  - `finding_causal_attribution_supported`: Observed change definitively linked to intervention.
  - `finding_causal_attribution_uncertain`: Confounders prevent definitive causal linkage.
  - `finding_confounded`: External variables heavily distorted the observation.
  - `finding_evidence_insufficient`: Incomplete observations across one or more dimensions.
  - `finding_diagnostic_consequence_unresolved`: Observed delta neither proves nor disproves diagnosis.

13.3 LAYER B — ENGINEERING ACTION / NEXT STEP
Based on the synthesis of Layer A findings, the system determines the justified engineering action:
  - `action_accept_and_close`: Requirement met, preservation intact; finalize and advance to Stage 14.
  - `action_retain_with_monitoring`: Minor acceptable trade-off; retain and monitor in full mix.
  - `action_bounded_refinement`: Diagnostic mechanism supported, preservation intact; authorize parameter tuning within same locus.
  - `action_alternate_intervention`: Diagnosis supported, but current locus ineffective or collateral damage excessive; revert to checkpoint and select alternative candidate from Phase 1C.5d.
  - `action_partial_reversion`: Undo a specific component of a multi-parameter change while retaining beneficial elements.
  - `action_full_reversion_to_checkpoint`: Undo current intervention completely, restoring last accepted checkpoint.
  - `action_full_reversion_to_original_baseline`: Restore original pre-intervention baseline.
  - `action_return_to_evidence_acquisition`: Request calibrated audio/telemetry from Phase 1C.5b (Stage 02/03).
  - `action_return_to_diagnosis`: Issue directive to Phase 1C.5c (Stage 06/07) to re-evaluate causal hypotheses.
  - `action_return_to_requirement_formulation`: Issue directive to Phase 1C.5d (Stage 08) to reformulate requirements.
  - `action_open_new_diagnostic_branch`: Acknowledge new unmasked symptom and branch diagnostic graph.
  - `action_escalate_to_human_engineer`: Disagreement between technical metrics and critical listening; escalate.
  - `action_abstain_under_uncertainty`: Evidence ambiguous; declare uncertainty and halt automated intervention.

13.4 PROPOSED SUMMARY LABEL
Where a single summary label is required for reporting, it is explicitly classified as:
`PHASE_1C5E_PROPOSED_SUMMARY` (e.g. `PROPOSED_SUMMARY_ACCEPTABLE_TRADEOFF`, `PROPOSED_SUMMARY_REVERSION_REQUIRED`).
This summary label NEVER erases or replaces the underlying Layer A findings.


================================================================================
SECTION 14 — ITERATION ARCHITECTURE & PATHWAY GOVERNANCE
================================================================================
14.1 ITERATION PATHWAYS
Phase 1C.5e defines eight structured iteration pathways mapped from Layer B actions:
  - PATHWAY A (Accept & Complete): Advances to Stage 14 Engineering Review.
  - PATHWAY B (Bounded Parameter Refinement): Tunes parameters within the same intervention locus.
  - PATHWAY C (Alternative Intervention Selection): Reverts current action to checkpoint and selects alternative candidate from Phase 1C.5d.
  - PATHWAY D (Checkpoint-Aware Reversion): Restores last accepted checkpoint or original baseline.
  - PATHWAY E (Return Upstream to Re-Diagnosis): Issues directive to Phase 1C.5c (Stage 06/07).
  - PATHWAY F (Return Upstream to Requirement Reformulation): Issues directive to Phase 1C.5d (Stage 08).
  - PATHWAY G (Return Upstream to Evidence Re-Acquisition): Issues directive to Phase 1C.5b (Stage 02/03).
  - PATHWAY H (Human Engineering Escalation): Escalates to senior engineer / artist for authoritative resolution.

14.2 EVIDENCE-JUSTIFIED ITERATION AUTHORIZATION
Bounded refinement may be justified when:
  1. The current intervention remains technically and causally plausible;
  2. The observed outcome provides sufficient reason to believe refinement may improve requirement satisfaction;
  3. Preservation constraints remain acceptable;
  4. No material evidence requires reopening diagnosis;
  5. Another pass has reasonable expected information gain;
  6. Stopping criteria are defined for the case.

Directional improvement may SUPPORT refinement.
It does NOT prove the causal diagnosis.
Monotonic convergence is NOT a universal requirement.

14.3 ANTI-THRASHING / REVIEW TRIGGERS
The following trigger reassessment rather than automatic reversion:
  - Diminishing benefit;
  - Increasing trade-offs;
  - Reversal of parameter direction;
  - Oscillating parameter values;
  - Repeated inconclusive changes.

Parameter reversal may legitimately occur during:
  - Bracketing;
  - Bounded search;
  - Nonlinear-response exploration;
  - Perceptual optimization.

Iteration should stop, return upstream, or escalate when justified by evidence, constraints, budget, or persistent non-convergence.

================================================================================
SECTION 15 — REFINEMENT VS ALTERNATIVE VS RE-DIAGNOSIS BOUNDARY MATRIX
================================================================================
A core source of architectural failure in sound engineering systems is confusing:
  - When to slightly adjust a parameter (Refinement);
  - When to choose a different tool/locus (Alternative Intervention); and
  - When to return upstream to re-diagnose (Re-Diagnosis).

+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Acoustic & Causal State    | REFINEMENT (Pathway B)      | ALTERNATIVE (Pathway C)     | RE-DIAGNOSIS (Pathway E)    |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Diagnostic Mechanism       | Supported & Active          | Supported & Active          | Weakened or Falsified       |
| Status                     | (acoustic response aligns)  | (cause is real, tool wrong) | (discriminating test failed)|
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Observed Acoustic Delta    | In intended direction, but  | Negligible change OR        | Contradicts causal theory   |
|                            | insufficient magnitude      | excessive side effect       | under controlled test       |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Intervention Locus         | Correct locus               | Sub-optimal locus; need     | Irrelevant locus; need      |
|                            | (e.g. pre-gain dynamic cut) | alternative from Phase 1C.5d| new diagnostic graph (1C.5c)|
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Action Boundary            | Parameter adjustment within | Revert to checkpoint;       | Revert to checkpoint;       |
|                            | current tool parameters     | activate candidate from 1C5d| return to Stage 06/07       |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+

CRITICAL MANDATE:
Under no circumstances may a system disguise a failed diagnostic hypothesis as "parameter refinement." If an adequately sensitive, controlled test isolates the hypothesized cause and fails to alter the symptom, the system must not endlessly tweak parameters; it must record the diagnostic failure and route upstream (Principles 18 & 21).

SECTION 16 — CHECKPOINT-AWARE REVERSION ARCHITECTURE & NON-STACKING DISCIPLINE
================================================================================
16.1 REVERSION AS A FIRST-CLASS ENGINEERING OUTCOME
In amateur sound engineering, an ineffective action is frequently "fixed" by adding another filter, compressor, or exciter on top of the failed chain. This creates catastrophic additive creeping, phase smearing, headroom loss, and irrecoverable signal degradation.
In professional sound engineering:
  "REVERSION TO A KNOWN STABLE STATE IS A PRIMARY, RESPECTABLE, AND SAFEGUARDING ENGINEERING ACTION."

16.2 THREE DISTINCT ARCHITECTURAL STATES
Rather than a naive universal "restore original baseline" rule, Phase 1C.5e defines three distinct operational states:
  1. STATE 1: ORIGINAL BASELINE (The raw, un-intervened state prior to all actions in the session).
  2. STATE 2: LAST ACCEPTED CHECKPOINT (The most recent stable state where prior engineering interventions were validated, preserved, and accepted).
  3. STATE 3: CURRENT INTERVENTION STATE (The state incorporating the active, newly tested intervention).

16.3 CHECKPOINT RESTORATION LOGIC
When an active intervention fails, introduces unacceptable trade-offs, or proves regressive:
  - Adverse outcome findings or anti-thrashing indicators prompt deliberate reassessment of the intervention trajectory before authorizing checkpoint rollback (Section 14.3). Parameter exploration techniques (such as bracketing or nonlinear search) do not constitute premature grounds for automatic rollback.
  - Where reversion is authorized, the default restoration target is the LAST ACCEPTED CHECKPOINT (State 2), NOT necessarily the Original Baseline (State 1).
  - This preserves previously validated and accepted engineering gains (e.g. an earlier successful low-end cleanup on bass) rather than unnecessarily wiping out all session progress.
  - If the earlier accepted gains are themselves called into question by new evidence, or if the entire processing chain must be abandoned, full reversion to the Original Baseline (State 1) is executed.

16.4 REVERSION LIFECYCLE PHASES
Reversion is an audited process distinguishing five phases:
  1. Reversion Recommendation: Evaluation engine identifies adverse finding and recommends rollback target.
  2. Reversion Authorization: Engineer or governing policy approves the rollback.
  3. Reversion Execution: Operational handoff to external engine to undo parameter changes.
  4. Observed Restoration Verification: Re-capturing audio/telemetry to empirically verify that the signal returned to the checkpoint state.
  5. Restoration Uncertainty Assessment: Documenting whether physical restoration is exact, approximate, or subject to analog hysteresis/drift.
  Phase 1C.5e strictly does NOT claim physical or perceptual bit-identical restoration where analog modeling, non-linear DSP, or physical acoustic spaces are involved.

16.5 FORBIDDING UNJUSTIFIED CORRECTIVE STACKING
Phase 1C.5e distinguishes between:
  - Justified Compound Interventions: Coordinated multi-locus strategies (e.g. pre-gain dynamic EQ paired with post-gain presence) that were causally deliberated and justified in Phase 1C.5d Stage 10. These are fully permitted.
  - Unjustified Corrective Stacking: Applying ad-hoc corrective patches to fix side effects created by an active intervention without reverting the failed action. This is STRICTLY PROHIBITED.

SECTION 17 — RETURN-UPSTREAM DIRECTIVES & PARENT-CHILD RUN TRACEABILITY
================================================================================
17.1 RETURN-UPSTREAM DIRECTIVES
When an outcome demonstrates that upstream reasoning was deficient, Phase 1C.5e issues a formal `ReturnUpstreamDirectiveRecord`.
Each directive contains:
  - `directive_id`: Unique identifier;
  - `target_upstream_stage`: Exact upstream destination (Stage 02/03 Evidence, Stage 06/07 Diagnosis, or Stage 08 Requirements);
  - `triggering_findings`: Layer A findings justifying the return;
  - `epistemic_impact`: Evidence-grounded reason the upstream reasoning requires reassessment;
  - `quarantined_elements`: Hypotheses or intervention candidates barred from re-use without new affirmative evidence.

The `epistemic_impact` field expresses one of the following review labels:
  - `SUPPORTED`: Upstream hypothesis or assumption received affirmative empirical reinforcement.
  - `WEAKENED`: Evidence reduces confidence in the upstream reasoning without outright disproof.
  - `CONTRADICTED`: Observed acoustic delta conflicts directly with upstream predictions or requirements.
  - `EXCLUDED`: A specific candidate or causal path is ruled out by discriminating observation.
  - `FALSIFIED`: Upstream hypothesis or premise is formally disproven by controlled discriminating test.
  - `INCONCLUSIVE`: Observations do not permit a definitive epistemic determination.
  - `TEST_INVALID`: Measurement, test protocol, or execution was confounded or flawed, preventing valid inference.
  - `EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`: Required observation modality or data density was missing or inadequate.
  - `CONTEXT_CHANGED`: External project context, monitoring, take, or arrangement altered the operating premises.

These labels are Phase 1C.5e review labels unless already inherited elsewhere.

GOVERNING RETURN RULE:
A return upstream does not require disproof. It may be justified because evidence weakens an assumption, contradicts it, exposes insufficient support, reveals an invalid test, introduces a material context change, preserves unresolved uncertainty, or establishes a valid exclusion/falsification at the specificity actually earned by the evidence.
Uncertainty is never converted into premature falsification, and affirmative disproof is not required for every upstream return.

17.2 PARENT-CHILD RUN TRACEABILITY
To prevent circular reasoning while preserving historical audit trails:
  - The failed run is sealed with its complete evaluation history intact;
  - A linked child run is instantiated, referencing the parent `run_id`;
  - Parent evidence is preserved with full provenance, but it is not automatically re-qualified as current child-run evidence;
  - This ensures complete, immutable traceability across iterations without rewriting history.

At child-run intake, inherited evidence must be reassessed for:
  - Relevance to the child-run question;
  - Current rig/state comparability;
  - Temporal comparability;
  - Performance comparability;
  - Monitoring-chain comparability;
  - Source/input comparability;
  - Processing-chain comparability;
  - Changed load-bearing assumptions;
  - Continued discriminating value.

To document the status of inherited parent evidence at child-run intake, Phase 1C.5e defines the following optional proposed review labels:
  - `RELEVANT_AND_COMPARABLE`: Evidence directly pertains to the child question and conditions remain comparable.
  - `RELEVANT_BUT_LIMITED`: Evidence is pertinent but subject to known boundary limitations or partial comparability.
  - `HISTORICAL_CONTEXT_ONLY`: Evidence serves as historical provenance and context, not active decision basis.
  - `REQUIRES_REVALIDATION`: Changed conditions or assumptions mandate empirical verification before reuse.
  - `NOT_COMPARABLE`: Physical, temporal, or signal-chain changes invalidate direct comparison.
  - `NOT_RELEVANT_TO_CHILD_QUESTION`: Evidence relates to aspects outside the scope of the child run's focus.

These labels are Phase 1C.5e proposed review labels, not frozen lifecycle vocabulary.
Parent evidence provenance and historical traceability are strictly preserved; the architecture neither silently discards parent evidence nor silently treats historical evidence as current evidence.


================================================================================
SECTION 18 — RETROSPECTIVE ENGINEERING REVIEW ARCHITECTURE (STAGE 14)
================================================================================
18.1 PURPOSE OF RETROSPECTIVE REVIEW (PRINCIPLES 19 & 20)
Stage 14 provides an independent, post-hoc engineering review of the complete decision-and-outcome lifecycle.
Its purpose is NOT to rationalize past decisions, but to evaluate reasoning quality, identify systemic predictive or diagnostic discrepancies, and capture generalizable sound engineering experience (Principles 19 & 20).

18.2 THE SIX REVIEW DIMENSIONS
Stage 14 audits the lifecycle across six rigorous dimensions:
  1. Diagnostic Validity Audit: Did post-intervention evidence support, weaken, or falsify the causal mechanism identified in Stage 06/07?
  2. Intervention Suitability Audit: Was the chosen intervention locus the minimum necessary and most parsimonious action capable of achieving the goal (Principle 14)?
  3. Predictive Accuracy Audit: How accurately did Stage 11 predictions match empirical deltas across improvements, trade-offs, and bounds (Principle 12)?
  4. Preservation Integrity Audit: Were critical musical qualities safeguarded, or were unpredicted collateral damages incurred?
  5. Trade-Off Proportionality Audit: Was the musical cost of the trade-off proportionate to the benefit gained (Principle 13)?
  6. Generalizable Engineering Learnings: What transferable acoustic knowledge or gear interaction insights were revealed?

18.3 INDEPENDENCE & ANTI-DEFENSIVE AUDITING
Stage 14 enforces strict independence:
  - Non-Punitive Posture: Identifying that an intervention failed or a diagnosis was wrong is treated as an essential error-correction event, not a failure.
  - Zero Defensive Rationalization (Principle 18): The system is forbidden from asserting that "the track would have sounded worse without the intervention" or shifting criteria post-hoc.
  - Candidate Lesson Quarantine: Any architectural lesson proposed for system-wide knowledge base updates is placed in quarantine until reviewed by sound engineering governance.

  2. `PreservationEvaluationRecord`: Audits individual musical qualities against non-exceedance bounds.
  3. `UnexpectedOutcomeRecord`: Logs unexpected acoustic phenomena and severity.
  4. `CausalAttributionRecord`: Logs confounder audits and causal attribution reasoning.
  5. `IterationDecisionRecord`: Documents Layer B action selection and convergence tracking.
  6. `ReturnUpstreamDirectiveRecord`: Formally packages directives to upstream stages.

================================================================================
SECTION 20 — UNCERTAINTY SURVIVAL ACROSS EVALUATION & REVIEW
================================================================================
20.1 THE SURVIVAL OF UNCERTAINTY INVARIANT (PRINCIPLES 3 & 21)
  "UNCERTAINTY MUST SURVIVE THE DECISION."
In sound engineering evaluation, uncertainty is an inescapable physical reality. Where evidence is incomplete, ambiguous, or confounded, uncertainty MUST survive.
The system is strictly forbidden from:
  - Forcing an ambiguous result into an unearned binary verdict;
  - Substituting missing post-intervention measurements with predicted numbers;
  - Assuming an intervention succeeded because no negative feedback was registered; or
  - Fabricating causal confidence when multiple confounders remain unisolated.

20.2 UNCERTAINTY CATEGORIES
Uncertainty is categorized and preserved:
  - `UNCERTAINTY_MEASUREMENT`: Due to acoustic noise floor, FFT window leakage, or microphone position variance.
  - `UNCERTAINTY_CONFOUNDING`: Due to concurrent mix processing, take dynamics, or arrangement masking shifts.
  - `UNCERTAINTY_PERCEPTUAL`: Due to subtle acoustic changes falling within psychoacoustic just-noticeable-difference bounds.
  - `UNCERTAINTY_CAUSAL_DIRECTION`: When an observed improvement cannot be definitively linked to the intervention versus external dynamics.
Every uncertain dimension is documented with explicit bounds and carried forward into `EngineeringReviewRecord`.


================================================================================
SECTION 21 — HUMAN / USER EVALUATION & MULTI-TRACK COEXISTENCE
================================================================================
21.1 THE FOUR EVALUATIVE TRACKS
Sound engineering exists at the intersection of acoustic physics and human artistic expression.
Stage 13 establishes four distinct evaluative tracks that must coexist without artificial subordination:
  1. TRACK 1: Objective Acoustic & DSP Measurements (FFT spectra, LUFS, crest factor, THD).
  2. TRACK 2: Professional Critical Listening (trained auditory evaluation of timbre, balance, depth, mask dynamics).
  3. TRACK 3: Reference Track Comparison (contextual similarity to target commercial production).
  4. TRACK 4: User / Artist Subjective Experience (emotional response, perceived vibe, artistic intent).

21.2 HANDLING DIVERGENCE BETWEEN TRACKS
Divergence between tracks is natural and informative:
  - Divergence Type A (Measurement Perfect, Listening Rejects): An EQ curve perfectly flattens a resonant peak, but critical listening reveals the instrument now sounds lifeless and sterile. In accordance with Principle 17, algorithmic rules do not replace engineering judgment; the intervention must be refined or reverted.
  - Divergence Type B (Measurement Shows Trade-Off, Artist Enthusiastic): Adding tube saturation increases THD and compresses dynamics, but the artist loves the "vintage grit." The system documents the trade-off and registers user-authorized trade-off acceptance.
    * Constraint Boundary: User preference DOES NOT bypass previously declared non-negotiable physical constraints (e.g. digital overs exceeding full scale or fatal phase cancellation collapsing a mix in mono). Non-negotiable technical limits take precedence over casual preference.
  - Divergence Type C (User Complains, Technical Metrics Unchanged): The user reports that a vocal sounds "distant" despite identical mid frequencies. This indicates a spatial or masking artifact that raw spectral metrics failed to capture.

21.3 PROHIBITION OF GASLIGHTING & FORCED CONFORMITY
The system is strictly forbidden from:
  - Dismissing user perceptual feedback by asserting that "the numbers prove the track is objectively better" (Principle 18);
  - Forcing the user to agree with algorithmic evaluations; or
  - Smoothing over genuine disagreements between critical listening and objective telemetry.
Divergences are preserved as explicit tension records in `OutcomeComparisonRecord`.

================================================================================
SECTION 22 — FAILURE MODES & PROHIBITED ANTI-PATTERNS
================================================================================
Phase 1C.5e explicitly outlaws seven fatal architectural anti-patterns:

1. ANTI-PATTERN AP-01 (Confirmation Bias & Self-Fulfilling Success):
   - Prohibited: Assuming that because an intervention was chosen and executed, it must have succeeded.
   - Defense: Strict enforcement of the Outcome Evidence Firewall (Principle 18).

2. ANTI-PATTERN AP-02 (Unjustified Corrective Stacking):
   - Prohibited: Applying additional corrective plug-ins or EQ filters on top of a failed intervention to compensate for its side effects without reverting the failed action.
   - Defense: Enforced checkpoint-aware reversion (Section 16).

3. ANTI-PATTERN AP-03 (Goalpost Shifting & Defensive Rationalization):
   - Prohibited: Redefining criteria of success after an action is executed.
   - Defense: Requirements and non-exceedance bounds are locked prior to execution in `EngineeringRequirementRecord` (Principles 8, 12 & 18).

4. ANTI-PATTERN AP-04 (Infinite Iteration & Blind Parameter Hunting):
   - Prohibited: Endlessly tweaking filter frequencies or compressor thresholds without evidence justification or defined stopping criteria.
   - Defense: Evidence-justified iteration bounds, anti-thrashing reassessment triggers, and checkpoint-aware reversion (Principle 14).

5. ANTI-PATTERN AP-05 (Sacrificing Musicality for Telemetry):
   - Prohibited: Chasing flat frequency lines or arbitrary metric targets at the expense of musical tone and punch.
   - Defense: Mandatory preservation audits across musical qualities; critical listening coexists with telemetry (Principle 17).

6. ANTI-PATTERN AP-06 (Causal Overreach & Premature Diagnostic Falsification):
   - Prohibited: Either declaring a diagnosis fully proven when external confounders were present, or throwing out a correct diagnosis simply because a single sub-optimal filter value was tried (Principles 5 & 21).
   - Defense: Confounder isolation; discriminating tests required before causal falsification (Section 12.3).

7. ANTI-PATTERN AP-07 (Blaming the User for System Failure):
   - Prohibited: Rationalizing bad acoustic outcomes by blaming the user's listening environment or musical tastes.
   - Defense: Honest logging of outcome divergence; non-defensive logging in `EngineeringReviewRecord` (Principles 18 & 20).

27.1 21-PRINCIPLE CONSTITUTIONAL COMPLIANCE MAPPING
Every section of Phase 1C.5e is strictly aligned with the verbatim Phase 1C.5a 21 Principles:
  - Principle 1 (Evidence Precedes Diagnosis): Evaluation begins with empirical post-intervention observations.
  - Principle 2 (Observation is not Interpretation): Raw FFT/listening data separated from causal claims.
  - Principle 3 (Missing Evidence is not Negative Evidence): Missing observations preserved as UNCERTAIN_UNMEASURED.
  - Principle 4 (A Symptom Does Not Identify Its Cause): Superficial symptom shifts do not prove root mechanism.
  - Principle 5 (Diagnosis Should Be Causal Where Evidence Permits): Causal attribution requires isolating mechanism.
  - Principle 6 (Competing Hypotheses Survive Until Evidence Justifies Narrowing Them): Competing explanations for unexpected effects preserved.
  - Principle 7 (Context Informs Reasoning but Does Not Prove Causation): Genre priors inform tolerances but do not prove success.
  - Principle 8 (Engineering Intent Constrains the Solution): Outcome evaluated directly against declared requirements.
  - Principle 9 (Generate Alternatives When the Problem Admits Alternatives): Multiple intervention candidates explored when remedies fail.
  - Principle 10 (Intervention Selection Follows Diagnosis): Corrective actions strictly bound to diagnosis.
  - Principle 11 (Intervention Should Occur at the Causally Appropriate Point): Interventions target the causally appropriate locus based on diagnosis and constraints.
  - Principle 12 (Predict Consequences Before Acting): Pre-action predictions consumed and verified before handoff.
  - Principle 13 (Every Intervention Has Potential Trade-Offs): Collateral consequences audited qualitatively or quantitatively as appropriate.
  - Principle 14 (Parsimony: Do Not Intervene Without Justified Engineering Purpose): Minimum necessary intervention audited in Stage 14.
  - Principle 15 (Platform Translation Operates Downstream of Engineering Reasoning): Reasoning is platform-neutral prior to external DSP execution.
  - Principle 16 (Reject Unacceptable Platform Compromises): Platform latency or distortion compromises rejected.
  - Principle 17 (Deterministic Code Validates Constraints; It Does Not Replace Sound Engineering Judgement): Metric algorithms validate bounds; listening coexists.
  - Principle 18 (Evaluate Outcomes Honestly Without Circular Justification): Strict Outcome Evidence Firewall enforced; no circular rationalization.
  - Principle 19 (Capture Engineering Experience for Governed Review): Systematic retrospective learnings logged in Stage 14.
  - Principle 20 (The Deliberation Record Must Support Independent Retrospective Audit): Non-punitive, anti-defensive audit trail in Stage 14.
  - Principle 21 (Where Evidence is Missing, Seek Discriminating Evidence Rather Than Guessing): Discriminating tests required for falsification.
