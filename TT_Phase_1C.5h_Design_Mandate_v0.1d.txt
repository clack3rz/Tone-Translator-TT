2. PURPOSE OF THIS MANDATE

2.1 The Constitutional Design Fence
This mandate defines the authoritative design fence for Phase 1C.5h before drafting the Phase 1C.5h architectural specification.
Its central governing rule is:

`THE DESIGN MANDATE DEFINES THE FENCE. IT DOES NOT BUILD THE VALIDATION LAB.`

It exists to prevent Phase 1C.5h from:
  - redefining the frozen upstream reasoning architecture (Phases 1C.5a through 1C.5f);
  - inventing new reasoning lifecycle stages or altering the 27 frozen lifecycle statuses;
  - assuming ownership of substantive diagnostic, causal, intervention, or trade-off reasoning;
  - assuming ownership of legacy estate discovery, classification, remapping, transformation, or adoption execution (Phase 1C.5g);
  - presuming that architectural elegance, schema compliance, or unit-test passing guarantees acoustic excellence;
  - reducing acoustic quality and musicality to simplistic, scalar, synthetic pass/fail metrics;
  - validating audio systems without qualified human sound engineer listening and guitar playability evaluation;
  - permitting "big-bang", unverified, un-canaried, or un-monitored production deployments;
  - conflating Phase 1C.5g migration completeness (Gates 1–7) with Phase 1C.5h production readiness (Gate 8);
  - authorizing production cutover without verified, deterministic rollback assurance;
  - silencing, hiding, or diluting empirical uncertainty and edge-case degradations;
  - destabilizing ongoing commercial Tone Translator tone generation and IK Multimedia AmpliTube 5 (AT5) export capabilities.

Phase 1C.5h operates as the terminal empirical validation, verification, and readiness governance authority between the completed reasoning and migration architecture and live commercial production.
Its role is to empirically evaluate acoustic fidelity, verify reasoning integrity under real-world audio stress, detect regressions, validate user acceptance across multiple professional personas, and govern Gate 8 Cutover Authorization.

8. GOVERNING VALIDATION & READINESS INVARIANTS

The Phase 1C.5h architecture must enforce ten inviolable constitutional invariants:

INVARIANT 1: EMPIRICAL PRIMACY INVARIANT
Structural compliance, schema validity, and passing unit tests are necessary prerequisites, but NEVER constitute sufficient proof of validation. An asset or reasoning engine is validated if and only if it demonstrates acoustic excellence and sound reasoning under empirical audio testing.

INVARIANT 2: MULTI-DIMENSIONAL ACOUSTIC EVALUATION INVARIANT (ANTI-SCALAR COLLAPSE)
Acoustic quality, musical fidelity, and tone validity shall NEVER be collapsed into a single scalar score, average metric, or simplistic threshold. Evaluation must occur across orthogonal dimensions (spectral, dynamic, harmonic, phase, mix context, and playability feel), where failure in any critical dimension constitutes validation rejection.

INVARIANT 3: PRINCIPLE COMPLIANCE INVARIANT
Empirical success achieved via unprincipled shortcuts, hardcoded cheats, or violations of the frozen 21 Principles of Sound Engineering Reasoning is invalid. Every validated run must exhibit strict adherence to the 21 Principles across its full decision trace.

INVARIANT 4: GOLDEN FIXTURE IMMUTABILITY & INDEPENDENCE INVARIANT
The golden acoustic fixture library used for validation must be independently governed, versioned, and immutable. Test fixtures must never be used as training data, prompt engineering tuning sets, or heuristic fitment sets for the candidate engine.

INVARIANT 5: MATERIAL REGRESSION PROTECTION INVARIANT
The new architecture shall not introduce unresolved material regressions into commercial guitar tone generation capability, AT5 preset compatibility, or operational stability within the authorized production scope relative to the established baseline. Intentional architectural corrections and verified neutral divergences do not constitute regressions. Any unresolved material acoustic, functional, or operational regression within the production-critical path of the authorized scope unconditionally blocks Gate 8 authorization.

INVARIANT 6: GATE 8 EXCLUSIVITY & INDEPENDENCE INVARIANT
Gate 8 Production Cutover Authorization can be granted solely by Phase 1C.5h through an independently verifiable `AdoptionDecisionRecord`. Gate 8 is exclusive, explicit, non-bypassable, scoped, traceable, and subject to governed revalidation or withdrawal where material change or new evidence invalidates the authorization basis. Neither Phase 1C.5g, operational engineering, nor automated test scripts may self-authorize production cutover.

INVARIANT 7: FIT-FOR-PURPOSE EVIDENCE INVARIANT
`VALIDATION EVIDENCE MUST BE FIT FOR THE QUESTION BEING EVALUATED.`
In accordance with the Phase 1C.5h mandate statement: "WHAT CAN BE MEASURED SHOULD BE MEASURED. WHAT MUST BE HEARD SHOULD BE HEARD." Objective evidence is required where the validation question is objectively measurable; perceptual/human evidence is required where musical quality, feel, preference, usability, or other perceptual questions are materially at issue. Some validation activities may be purely deterministic or operational (and do not require a human listening panel, such as deterministic XML/schema/export validation or parameter boundary verification); some may be primarily perceptual; and some require mixed evidence. Deterministic metrics shall never substitute for perceptual evaluation where musical quality or feel is materially at issue, nor may subjective opinion bypass objective acoustic and platform compliance. Gate 8 readiness as a whole must reconcile all materially relevant evidence classes.

INVARIANT 8: ADVERSARIAL & EDGE-CASE SUFFICIENCY INVARIANT
Validation cannot be granted based solely on "happy-path" scenarios. Every candidate component, rule, and workflow must be rigorously evaluated against applicable pathological, degraded, noisy, clipped, ambiguous, and contradictory edge-case inputs within its operational domain to verify robust handling, graceful degradation, and explicit uncertainty preservation.

INVARIANT 9: REVERSIBILITY & ROLLBACK VERIFICATION INVARIANT
No component or path may receive Gate 8 Cutover Authorization without prior empirical verification that its operational rollback mechanism (PATH-ROLLBACK) executes reliably, cleanly, and within governed operational recovery objectives under simulated failure conditions.

INVARIANT 10: COMPLETE EVIDENTIAL LINEAGE INVARIANT
Every validation decision, score, audit result, and sign-off must possess an unbroken, tamper-evident Phase 1C.5f provenance trail linking the input audio fixtures, user prompts, reasoning traces, parameter outputs, rendered audio, and evaluator identities.


9. MULTI-DIMENSIONAL ACOUSTIC EVALUATION MODEL

9.1 The Failure of Scalar Audio Metrics
In professional audio engineering, scalar metrics fail to represent musical reality:
  - An output may have minimal spectral distance from a target yet suffer from unmusical phase smearing or dynamic harshness;
  - A tone may match an EQ curve yet completely lack pick-attack responsiveness and power-amp sag;
  - An aggressive high-gain rhythm tone may measure high in distortion yet possess ideal note separation and punch in a mix.
Phase 1C.5h mandates an orthogonal, multidimensional acoustic evaluation model.

9.2 Orthogonal Validation Dimensions
`VALIDATION DIMENSIONS ARE ORTHOGONAL EVIDENCE DOMAINS, NOT A UNIVERSAL FIXED SCORECARD.`
The detailed Phase 1C.5h architecture must define test-profile-appropriate applicability across these six dimension families:

  1. Dimension Family 1: Spectral Balance & Tonal Tilt
     Evaluates frequency distribution across the audible spectrum (sub-bass clarity, low-end punch, low-mid warmth, midrange presence, upper-mid articulation, and high-end air without harsh resonance).

  2. Dimension Family 2: Dynamic Responsiveness & Transient Preservation
     Evaluates signal dynamics, transient reproduction of pick attack, dynamic crest factor, touch sensitivity, and power-tube sag and bloom across playing velocity.

  3. Dimension Family 3: Harmonic Structure & Saturation Quality
     Evaluates non-linear harmonic generation, balance of musical even and odd harmonics, intermodulation distortion management, aliasing artifact rejection, and noise floor.

  4. Dimension Family 4: Phase, Spatial & Stereo Coherence
     Evaluates multi-microphone phase alignment, mono compatibility, stereo imaging, comb-filtering avoidance, and cabinet acoustic reflections.

  5. Dimension Family 5: Contextual & Mix Integration
     Evaluates how the guitar tone sits within an arrangement (bass/kick separation, vocal pocket preservation, masking avoidance, and headroom impact).

  6. Dimension Family 6: Musical Playability & Guitarist Perception
     Evaluates tactile feel, immediacy, note separation under complex chords, sustain bloom, and authenticity of tonal response.

9.3 Prohibition of Universal Numeric Freezing
`NO UNIVERSAL NUMERIC VALIDATION THRESHOLD MAY BE CONSTITUTIONALLY FROZEN WITHOUT EMPIRICAL JUSTIFICATION FOR ITS APPLICABLE DOMAIN.`
The constitutional mandate strictly prohibits freezing arbitrary universal decibel, frequency, or percentage thresholds across all genres and test types. Detailed Phase 1C.5h architecture must define domain-appropriate, empirically justified, test-specific, versioned, auditable, and change-controlled thresholds. Specific numeric figures in early discussions are strictly non-normative illustrations.


10. GOLDEN ACOUSTIC FIXTURE LIBRARY & BENCHMARK STEM MANAGEMENT

10.1 Curated Fixture Corpus Architecture
Phase 1C.5h mandates the establishment of an authoritative, versioned Golden Acoustic Fixture Library (`GAFL`).
The library provides governed audio recordings, dry guitar DI signals, and multitrack stems specifically curated to stress and validate AI sound engineering decisions.

10.2 Fixture Governance Principles
Every fixture in the library must possess:
  - Stable, unique identity and version metadata;
  - Documented provenance and recording chain history;
  - Explicit declared purpose and applicable validation domain;
  - Declared known limitations and acoustic boundary conditions;
  - Documented calibration assumptions (e.g., input gain alignment, level references);
  - Strict segregation from model training, prompt tuning, and heuristic optimization sets.

`NO FIXTURE OR FIXTURE FAMILY BECOMES UNIVERSAL GROUND TRUTH OUTSIDE ITS DECLARED VALIDATION SCOPE.`

10.3 Fixture Corpus Scope (Non-Normative Illustrations)
Detailed architecture will define the exact fixture corpus. As illustrative examples for future corpus design, fixtures may span:
  - Clean, edge-of-breakup, crunch, modern high-gain, singing lead, fuzz/saturation, acoustic DI, and bass tracking archetypes;
  - Diverse pickup classes (single-coil, humbucker, active, P-90);
  - Diverse playing dynamics (fingerpicking, aggressive picking, palm muting, sustained bends);
  - Multitrack stems across diverse musical styles;
  - Adversarial fixtures (clipped signals, electromagnetic hum, impedance mismatches, boomy resonances).
The constitutional mandate does NOT freeze a fixed number of archetypes, specific hardware brand names, or mandatory recording formats.

10.4 Verifiable Fixture Integrity
All fixtures must be maintained under tamper-evident, version-controlled governance consistent with Phase 1C.5f. Integrity verification must ensure fixtures are immutable and independently auditable without mandating specific cryptographic hashing algorithms.


11. USER ACCEPTANCE TESTING (UAT) PROTOCOLS & EVALUATION PERSPECTIVES

11.1 Multi-Perspective Evaluation Methodology
Acceptance cannot be determined by a single engineer or automated script. Phase 1C.5h mandates evaluation across distinct professional perspectives:
  - The Master Recording & Mix Engineer: Focusing on spectral balance, phase coherence, headroom, masking, and professional engineering standards.
  - The Professional Performing & Session Guitarist: Focusing on touch sensitivity, pick attack dynamics, volume knob clean-up, power-amp sag, and musical feel.
  - The Creative Producer & Songwriter: Focusing on emotional resonance, genre appropriateness, prompt expression, and sonic character.
  - The Commercial Tone Platform Operator: Focusing on deterministic execution, stability, resource predictability, preset integrity, and operational safety.

11.2 Flexible Listening-Test Governance
`THE LISTENING METHODOLOGY MUST FIT THE QUESTION BEING EVALUATED.`
The constitutional mandate strictly rejects mandating double-blind MUSHRA or ABX tests for all evaluations.
Phase 1C.5h must support governed, fit-for-purpose use of:
  - Sighted expert review;
  - Blind A/B testing;
  - Double-blind A/B testing;
  - ABX discrimination testing;
  - MUSHRA multi-stimulus perceptual rating;
  - Paired comparison;
  - Reference matching;
  - Preference testing;
  - Defect detection listening;
  - Musical-context evaluation;
  - Real-time playability and feel testing.

Every formal listening test protocol must explicitly document the evaluator role, testing methodology, listening environment context, calibration standards, and interpretation criteria.

11.3 UAT Acceptance & Scoring Governance
The detailed Phase 1C.5h architecture must define test-specific scoring rubrics, passing criteria, and confidence thresholds.
The constitutional mandate prohibits hard-coding a universal 1.0–5.0 scale, fixed passing averages (e.g., 4.2), or fixed panel sizes as universal constitutional law. Thresholds must be scoped, justified, and change-controlled within detailed test specifications.


12. REGRESSION DETECTION & DIFFERENTIAL AUDITING ARCHITECTURE

  - Axis 2: Candidate Architecture vs. Human Master Engineer Benchmark;

12.2 Legacy Baseline Policy
`LEGACY OUTPUT ≠ EXPECTED TRUTH`
The legacy production path (PATH-PROD) serves as an indispensable historical baseline, regression comparator, and operational fallback. It does NOT automatically define the acoustically correct result.

Differences between candidate and legacy output must be categorized into governed classes:
  - Intentional Architectural Correction: The candidate output corrects a known legacy flaw or unprincipled artifact;
  - Neutral Divergence: The candidate output differs in valid engineering implementation without acoustic degradation;
  - Genuine Regression: The candidate output degrades tone quality, dynamic response, usability, or stability;
  - Unresolved Divergence: The divergence lacks sufficient explanation or justification.

12.3 Regression Governance
Detailed architecture must define domain-appropriate regression criteria, spectral comparison methods, and null-testing procedures. Any unmitigated genuine regression in a production-critical capability blocks Gate 8 authorization.

18. UPSTREAM FEEDBACK LOOPS, DEFECT REMAND & CANDIDATE LESSON GOVERNANCE

18.1 Defect Classification and Remand Routing
When a component or reasoning execution fails empirical validation, defects are routed according to their verified root cause:
  - Route A: Remand to Phase 1C.5g (Migration Governance)
    Trigger: Failure caused by flawed legacy rule remapping, transformation defects, or configuration extraction errors.
  - Route B: Remand to Phase 1C.5b–e (Reasoning Rules & Causal Models)
    Trigger: Failure caused by fundamentally unsound causal diagnosis, flawed hypotheses, or improper trade-off criteria.
  - Route C: Remand to Platform Translation & Exporter Governance (Stage 12 Execution)
    Trigger: Failure caused by incorrect parameter scaling, knob mapping, or preset serialization defects downstream of Stage 11 decisions.

18.2 Governed Candidate Lesson Formulation
`VALIDATION EVIDENCE MAY PRODUCE A CANDIDATE LESSON ONLY WHEN THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS A POTENTIALLY GENERALIZABLE ENGINEERING INSIGHT REQUIRING GOVERNED REVIEW.`
Not every validation failure or defect is a Candidate Lesson. Operational defects, test harness bugs, environment outages, corrupted fixtures, and deployment errors remain validation defects.
Only genuine engineering insights into sound engineering reasoning or acoustic trade-offs may be formulated as Candidate Lessons, which must remain strictly quarantined under Stage 14 rules until authoritative Knowledge Governance review and promotion approval. Phase 1C.5h possesses zero unilateral promotion authority.


19. FAILURE ATTRIBUTION & VALIDATION DEGRADATION CONCEPTS

19.1 Failure Attribution Separation
To satisfy Principle 4 ("A Symptom Does Not Identify Its Cause"), a failed validation test must never be arbitrarily blamed on reasoning, migration, or target platforms without evidence. Phase 1C.5h defines nine failure attribution categories (marked PHASE_1C5H_PROPOSED):
  1. VALIDATION_ENVIRONMENT: Failure in test harness, runner, or host infrastructure;
  2. TEST_FIXTURE: Defect, corruption, or improper calibration in audio fixture;
  3. MIGRATION_MAPPING: Flaw in Phase 1C.5g remapping or transformation logic;
  4. RUNTIME_IMPLEMENTATION: Bug or unhandled exception in software implementation;
  5. REASONING: Unsound logic, Principle violation, or bad causal diagnosis in Stages 01–11;
  6. PLATFORM_TRANSLATION: Defect in Stage 12 parameter mapping or serialization;
  7. TARGET_PLATFORM: Bug, crash, or limitation in external target software (e.g., AT5 engine);
  8. OPERATIONAL_INFRASTRUCTURE: Network, compute, or resource starvation failure;
  9. UNKNOWN: Failure cause unverified; requires investigation.

19.2 Twenty Required Validation Failure & Degradation Conditions
The Phase 1C.5h architecture must address at least the following twenty failure and degradation conditions (marked PHASE_1C5H_PROPOSED):
  - VAL-FAIL-01: Invalid Test Fixture (Fixture corrupted, format error, or uncalibrated);
  - VAL-FAIL-02: Fixture Provenance Missing (Fixture lacks verifiable history or origin metadata);
  - VAL-FAIL-03: Calibration Mismatch (Reference audio level, sample rate, or gain staging misaligned);
  - VAL-FAIL-04: Non-Reproducible Result (Evaluation yields divergent results across identical deterministic runs);
  - VAL-FAIL-05: Objective Metrics Pass but Listening Fails (Audio passes spectral metrics but exhibits unmusical harshness or dead dynamics);
  - VAL-FAIL-06: Listening Passes but Deterministic/Export Validation Fails (Tone sounds good but preset violates parameter constraints);
  - VAL-FAIL-07: PATH-PROD and PATH-SHADOW Not Comparable (Inputs, prompts, or test contexts misaligned between paths);
  - VAL-FAIL-08: Unexplained Material Divergence (Output differs significantly from baseline without documented causal justification);
  - VAL-FAIL-09: Export Succeeds but Target-Platform Playback Fails (Preset serializes cleanly but crashes or mutes host plugin);
  - VAL-FAIL-10: Acoustic Result Passes but Runtime Is Unstable (Audio is excellent but execution exhibits memory leaks or thread contention);
  - VAL-FAIL-11: Rollback Validation Fails (Simulated rollback fails to revert traffic cleanly within operational SLA);
  - VAL-FAIL-12: Undocumented Test-Environment Dependency (Result depends on hidden local cache or undocumented host configuration);
  - VAL-FAIL-13: Expert Reviewers Materially Disagree (Qualified evaluators produce irreconcilable split verdicts);
  - VAL-FAIL-14: UAT Result Inconclusive (Insufficient statistical power or ambiguous evaluator feedback);
  - VAL-FAIL-15: Critical Domain Remains Untested (Validation battery omits essential genre, pickup, or dynamic playing domains);
  - VAL-FAIL-16: Material Implementation Change Occurs After PASS (Code, prompt, or model changes deployed post-validation without re-test);
  - VAL-FAIL-17: Test Evidence Corrupted or Unverifiable (Audit trail, trace, or audio recording lost or tampered with);
  - VAL-FAIL-18: Target-Platform Update Invalidates Prior Evidence (Host plugin update alters DSP modeling or preset format);
  - VAL-FAIL-19: Gate 8 Requested With Unresolved Critical Uncertainty (Attempt to authorize cutover while high-risk uncertainty remains);
  - VAL-FAIL-20: Requested Production Authorization Scope Exceeds Tested Scope (Cutover requested for broad traffic when only narrow capability was validated).

These failure conditions are validation governance categories; they MUST NOT be added to the 27 frozen reasoning lifecycle statuses.

21. ARCHITECTURAL STRESS SCENARIOS

The Phase 1C.5h architecture must be explicitly evaluated against twenty comprehensive architectural stress cases.
`ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIOS MUST NOT FABRICATE EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE.`
The scenarios below represent mandatory architectural walkthrough requirements, defining how the governance and validation architecture responds to critical challenges without fabricating fictional empirical test results:

SCENARIO 1: Architecture Passes Structural Checks but Sounds Clearly Worse
The reasoning engine strictly obeys Stages 01–11 schemas, but rendered audio exhibits unmusical thinness.
Governance Walkthrough: Architectural conformance is recognized as necessary but insufficient (Invariant 1). Acoustic evaluation flags failure. Gate 8 blocked. Defect routed to Stage 08 Acoustic Target and Stage 10 Trade-Off reasoning.

SCENARIO 2: New System Sounds Better than Legacy but Differs Significantly
Candidate output sounds noticeably fuller and cleaner than legacy, but spectral differential analysis reveals major divergence.
Governance Walkthrough: System analyzes PATH-COMPARE data. Legacy is recognized as baseline, not truth (Correction 18). Trace demonstrates divergence is an intentional correction of legacy distortion. Evaluators verify improvement. Divergence accepted as INTENTIONAL IMPROVEMENT.

SCENARIO 3: Deterministic Export Failure Despite Good Acoustic Reasoning
Stages 01–11 produce an acoustically sound decision, but Stage 12 generates an AT5 parameter outside acceptable bounds.
Governance Walkthrough: Target-platform verification intercepts invalid parameter prior to deployment. Failure attributed to PLATFORM_TRANSLATION. Gate 8 blocked. Reasoning traces preserved while translation adapter is corrected.

SCENARIO 4: Objectively Similar Spectra but Perceptually Different Tone
Two tones exhibit near-identical frequency response curves, but human evaluators report one sounds "dead and stiff" while the other is "dynamic and punchy".
Governance Walkthrough: Multi-dimensional evaluation engages Dimension Family 2 (Dynamic Responsiveness). Analysis identifies transient squashing in the first tone. Demonstrates anti-scalar collapse (Invariant 2) and necessity of dual-perspective evaluation (Invariant 7).

SCENARIO 5: Reference Audio Differs Due to Player/Performance Rather than System Failure
A validation test against reference guitar audio shows divergence caused by pick velocity differences in the recorded performance.
Governance Walkthrough: Failure attribution isolates root cause to TEST_FIXTURE rather than REASONING. Fixture metadata updated with playing dynamic constraints. System protected from incorrect blame.

SCENARIO 6: Isolated High-Gain Case Passes but Clean Tones Fail
A candidate amplifier model performs exceptionally well on high-gain metal leads but produces harsh distortion on clean chord arpeggios.
Governance Walkthrough: Gate 8 full-scope authorization denied. If appropriate, Phase 1C.5h issues CONDITIONAL authorization strictly bounded to High-Gain Lead scope, while Clean capability is remanded for remediation.

SCENARIO 7: Expert Reviewers Materially Disagree
In a subjective evaluation, the Mix Engineer persona rates a tone highly for mix clarity, but the Performing Guitarist persona rates it poorly for lack of touch sensitivity and sag.
Governance Walkthrough: Governed dispute protocol triggered. Conflict analyzed across orthogonal dimensions. System recognizes guitarist feel as a distinct requirement. Gate 8 held pending trade-off re-evaluation in Stage 10.

SCENARIO 8: Automated Metrics Disagree with Listening Panel
An automated metric flags high harmonic distortion, but the expert listening panel judges the tone to be exceptionally authentic vintage germanium fuzz.
Governance Walkthrough: Dual-perspective reconciliation applies Principle 8 ("Engineering Intent Constrains the Solution"). Because user intent specifically demanded vintage fuzz saturation, the distortion is recognized as intentional musical character rather than defect.

SCENARIO 9: Edge-Case Input Crashes New Path but Legacy Survives
A pathological user prompt containing extreme contradictory gear requests causes an unhandled exception in the new reasoning path, whereas legacy TT falls back to a default preset.
Governance Walkthrough: Comparative monitor detects failure. Failure attributed to RUNTIME_IMPLEMENTATION. Invariant 8 enforced: graceful degradation required. Candidate blocked until robust fallback reasoning is implemented.

SCENARIO 10: Legacy Fails While New Architecture Succeeds
Legacy TT crashes on an unmapped custom pickup configuration, while the new architecture reasons over acoustic fundamentals and generates an articulate tone.
Governance Walkthrough: Comparative logging records legacy failure and new architecture success. Provides auditable evidence of superior robustness, supporting adoption case.

SCENARIO 11: Rollback Test Fails Under Simulation
During pre-cutover testing, a simulated failure trigger is injected, but PATH-ROLLBACK hangs due to a stale configuration lock.
Governance Walkthrough: Invariant 9 enforced. Gate 8 authorization unconditionally refused regardless of perfect acoustic scores. Cutover blocked until rollback mechanism is proven deterministic.

SCENARIO 12: Test Fixture Calibration Later Discovered to Be Incorrect
Following validation, discovery reveals an input DI fixture was recorded 6 dB hotter than specified.
Governance Walkthrough: Version-bound validation triggers revalidation. Prior PASS status suspended. Fixture re-calibrated. All affected test suites automatically re-run.

SCENARIO 13: Target-Platform Update Changes Audio Output
An update to AmpliTube 5 modifies power-amp modeling algorithms, subtly altering high-frequency response across existing presets.
Governance Walkthrough: Platform version change triggers mandatory revalidation. Automated test suites re-render fixtures against new host version. Discrepancies audited to ensure continued compliance.

SCENARIO 14: Validated Gear Family Passes but Adjacent Untested Family Requested for Cutover
A migration package for American Clean amps passes all UAT, but operational teams request cutover for the entire amplifier library.
Governance Walkthrough: Scope limitation enforced (VAL-FAIL-20). Gate 8 authorization strictly restricted to the validated American Clean scope. Broader cutover prohibited.

SCENARIO 15: Migrated Feature Passes Alone but Fails When Combined With Another Adopted Feature
A noise gate module and a high-gain amp module pass validation independently, but when chained together, the gate chokes sustain unnaturally.
Governance Walkthrough: Multi-component integration testing required. Interaction detected in Dimension Family 6. Gate 8 authorization for the combined chain held until composite behavior is re-tuned.

SCENARIO 16: Candidate Lesson Changes Output After Prior Validation
An approved upstream candidate lesson updates trade-off weights in Stage 10, altering parameter decisions for previously validated tones.
Governance Walkthrough: Upstream reasoning modification triggers mandatory revalidation. Regression suites verify that the new lesson improves target capability without degrading existing baseline tones.

SCENARIO 17: Minor Code Refactor Claims No Acoustic Impact
A backend refactor is submitted with assertions that changes are purely internal and carry zero acoustic impact.
Governance Walkthrough: Policy enforces that acoustic neutrality cannot be assumed. Automated regression suite executed against golden fixtures. Verified before deployment.

SCENARIO 18: User Workflow Succeeds Technically but Is Confusing or Unacceptable
The system generates valid presets, but the UAT Creative Producer persona reports that tone description feedback and control labels are confusing and counter-intuitive.
Governance Walkthrough: Persona feedback recorded under UAT protocol. UAT result marked CONDITIONAL. Usability enhancements scheduled before commercial rollout.

SCENARIO 19: Canary Cohort Reports Issue Not Reproduced in Lab
During a restricted canary slice, live users report intermittent audio clipping on a specific low-output vintage guitar model not represented in initial tests.
Governance Walkthrough: Canary telemetry captures incident. Phase 1C.5h issues adoption hold. New audio fixture created representing the specific guitar. Edge case reproduced in lab and remediated.

SCENARIO 20: Gate 8 Package Contains Conflicting Evidence and Unresolved Uncertainty
A migration handoff package exhibits strong objective acoustic scores but unresolved diagnostic uncertainty in complex speaker cabinet impedance modeling.
Governance Walkthrough: Invariant: UNCERTAINTY MUST SURVIVE THE DECISION enforced. Cutover cannot proceed by suppressing uncertainty. Gate 8 granted only with explicit conditional scope or held until discriminating evidence resolves the causal mechanism.

24. EXPLICIT PROHIBITIONS

Phase 1C.5h explicitly and strictly PROHIBITS:

  1. BIG-BANG PRODUCTION CUTOVER: Mandating or attempting an un-canaried, un-scoped, all-at-once replacement of production tone generation.
  2. MIGRATION AS VALIDATION: Treating Phase 1C.5g migration completeness (Gates 1–7) as a substitute for empirical acoustic testing.
  3. SCALAR COLLAPSE: Collapsing acoustic quality into a single composite metric, average score, or synthetic benchmark.
  4. SUBJECTIVE-FREE VALIDATION: Authorizing production readiness without qualified expert human listening and playability evaluation where perceptual quality is at issue.
  5. OBJECTIVE-FREE VALIDATION: Approving tones based purely on subjective impressions without objective acoustic and platform verification.
  6. FIXTURE CONTAMINATION: Using golden validation fixtures for model training, prompt tuning, or rule heuristic optimization.
  7. REGRESSION BLINDNESS: Ignoring, concealing, or downplaying detected acoustic degradations relative to the legacy commercial baseline.
  8. UNVERIFIED ROLLBACK CUTOVER: Authorizing cutover without an empirically tested, functional, deterministic rollback mechanism.
  9. REASONING BYPASS: Validating tones that sound acceptable but were produced by hallucinated, unprincipled, or trace-free reasoning.
  10. SELF-AUTHORIZATION: Permitting Phase 1C.5g, developers, or automated scripts to grant Gate 8 Cutover Authorization.
  11. UNCERTAINTY SUPPRESSION: Forcing ambiguous, conflicting, or edge-case validation results into artificially definitive passing scores.
  12. UPSTREAM RULE MUTATION: Modifying the frozen 21 Principles or 14 reasoning lifecycle stages to accommodate validation convenience.
  13. UNAPPROVED LESSON INGESTION: Directly promoting empirical failure findings into authoritative engineering knowledge without Stage 14 quarantine review.
  14. PLATFORM EXPORT ASSUMPTION: Assuming a preset is valid without test-loading and rendering it in the actual target environment.
  15. ADVERSARIAL OMISSION: Omitting clipped, noisy, or pathological fixtures from the validation test battery.
