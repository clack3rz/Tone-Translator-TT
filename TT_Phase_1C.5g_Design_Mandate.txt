1.2 Scope and Role
This document is NOT an implementation specification.

Its purpose is to establish the scope, ownership boundaries, invariants, prohibited overreach, migration principles, disposition model, coexistence requirements, rollback requirements, provenance requirements, and downstream interfaces for migrating current Tone Translator behaviour and knowledge into the frozen AI Sound Engineer architecture.

No subsequent Phase 1C.5g architectural specification, migration plan, mapping catalog, shadow execution controller, or adoption roadmap may weaken, contradict, or exceed the boundaries established herein.


2. PURPOSE OF THIS MANDATE

This mandate defines the authoritative design boundaries for Phase 1C.5g before drafting the Phase 1C.5g architectural specification.

It exists to prevent Phase 1C.5g from:
  - redefining the frozen upstream reasoning architecture (Phases 1C.5a through 1C.5f);
  - inventing new lifecycle stages or lifecycle statuses;
  - assuming ownership of substantive reasoning, diagnostic, or trade-off decisions;
  - assuming ownership of empirical UAT execution or acoustic validation (Phase 1C.5h);
  - presuming that legacy Tone Translator behaviour is inherently valid or architecturally correct;
  - executing high-risk, uncoordinated "big-bang" replacements of production systems;
  - silently migrating, mutating, or discarding legacy rules without auditable provenance;
  - contaminating production tone generation with unvalidated shadow-mode execution;
  - treating distinct assets (prompts, heuristics, configuration, platform mappings) as interchangeable;
  - destabilizing current production Tone Translator and IK Multimedia AmpliTube 5 (AT5) export capability.

Phase 1C.5g operates as the governance and migration bridge between the legacy Tone Translator implementation and the frozen AI Sound Engineer target architecture.
Its role is to discover, classify, remap, transform, quarantine, retire, shadow-run, and safely transition legacy assets into the frozen target system without destabilizing operations.

8. GOVERNING MIGRATION INVARIANTS

The Phase 1C.5g architecture must strictly enforce ten governing invariants:

8.1 Invariant 1 — Frozen Architecture Wins
Where legacy Tone Translator behaviour conflicts with the frozen Phase 1C.5a–f architecture, the frozen architecture is authoritative without exception.
Migration must never distort or dilute the frozen architecture to maintain backwards compatibility with flawed legacy behaviour.

8.2 Invariant 2 — Existing Production Capability Must Be Protected
Current production Tone Translator tone generation, signal-chain construction, and AT5 configuration/export capabilities must remain fully functional and operationally available throughout the migration process.
Migration work must never require destructive in-place replacement of the operational production path before thorough validation.

8.3 Invariant 3 — No Big-Bang Migration
Migration must proceed via incremental, staged, and reversible transitions.
No architectural plan, release design, or deployment strategy may mandate an all-or-nothing cutover of the complete reasoning system.

8.4 Invariant 4 — Legacy Provenance Must Survive Migration
Every legacy asset—whether retained, remapped, transformed, quarantined, or retired—must retain full provenance documenting:
  - its exact legacy source location and identifier;
  - its historical behaviour and operational context;
  - the migration disposition assigned to it;
  - the technical rationale behind the disposition;
  - its target destination in the frozen architecture (if applicable);
  - whether its semantic meaning was altered during migration;
  - the governance approval metadata where approval is required.

8.5 Invariant 5 — Migration Must Not Manufacture Validity
Historical existence or operational longevity does not constitute architectural correctness.
The fact that a legacy prompt or heuristic historically produced acceptable tones is empirical evidence of prior utility, not proof of architectural validity or causal truth.

8.6 Invariant 6 — Uncertainty Must Survive Migration
If the correctness, scope, evidential basis, or architectural fit of a legacy asset is uncertain, that uncertainty must remain explicitly recorded and visible.
Uncertain legacy assets must NEVER be forced into RETAIN or REMAP dispositions solely to satisfy migration milestones or give an illusion of completeness.

8.7 Invariant 7 — Migration Must Be Reversible Until Validated
Every cutover, remapping, and adoption step executed prior to formal Phase 1C.5h empirical sign-off must possess an operational, deterministic rollback path.

8.8 Invariant 8 — Historical Behaviour Must Remain Reconstructable
When legacy rules are transformed, deprecated, or retired, their original code, prompts, configurations, and behavioural traces must remain archived and accessible for regression testing, historical audit, and post-mortem analysis.

8.9 Invariant 9 — Shadow Results Are Not Production Authority
Outputs, parameter maps, and signal chains generated by the new AI Sound Engineer architecture during shadow-mode execution must never silently override, replace, or pollute live production outputs prior to governed validation and cutover approval.

8.10 Invariant 10 — Migration Completion Is Not UAT Success
Technical completion of asset remapping or code migration does not constitute empirical success.
Only Phase 1C.5h holds the authority to certify acoustic fidelity, behavioural equivalence, regression resistance, and operational readiness.


9. LEGACY ESTATE DISCOVERY & INVENTORY MODEL

9.1 Discovery Imperative
Migration cannot govern what has not first been identified.
Phase 1C.5g must define a rigorous discovery and inventory model that audits, identifies, and registers every constituent asset of the existing Tone Translator estate.

9.2 Scope of Discoverable Assets
The discovery process must comprehensively catalog:
  - Prompts: system prompts, task prompts, parameter generation prompts, evaluation prompts, and dynamic prompt templates;
  - Reasoning Rules: heuristics, expert rules-of-thumb, acoustic assumptions, genre-specific rules;
  - Hardcoded Heuristics: embedded numerical constants, threshold limits, magic numbers, clamp ranges;
  - Deterministic Decision Logic: conditional branching, switch statements, lookup rules dictating gear or settings;
  - Signal-Chain Generation Logic: topology ordering, block placement, routing rules, serial/parallel splits;
  - Gear-Selection Logic: amplifier, cabinet, speaker, microphone, pedal, and rack unit selection algorithms;
  - Parameter-Selection Logic: gain staging, EQ curves, bias settings, microphone placement coordinates (X/Y/Z/angle);
  - Transformation Rules: unit conversions, perceptual-to-technical mappings, curve normalizations;
  - Fallback Behaviour: default values, error catch-blocks, missing-gear fallbacks, silent default assignments;
  - UI-Triggered Logic: settings derived directly from UI state or user toggles that bypass engineering reasoning;
  - Service-Layer Rules: logic living in frontend services (e.g., at5AmplifierKnowledge, at5CabinetKnowledge, at5Catalog, at5MicPlacementReasoning, etc.);
  - Data & Configuration Assets: JSON catalogs, preset archives, reference tone databases, seed profiles;
  - Manifests & Mapping Tables: gear manifests, parameter manifests, verified parameter overrides, alias tables;
  - Knowledge Documents: markdown guides, architectural notes, historical specs, curriculum blueprints;
  - Runtime Assumptions: implicit expectations regarding sample rate, buffer size, stereo/mono busing, input gain;
  - Implicit Code Behaviour: side effects, order-of-operation dependencies, caching assumptions;
  - Historical Tuning Logic: special-case tweaks implemented for specific artists, genres, or albums;
  - Exception Handling & Bypasses: error recovery paths, edge-case bypasses, compatibility shims.

9.3 Technology-Neutral Inventory
The discovery process requires a structured inventory model but does not mandate a specific storage technology (database, JSON registry, or spreadsheet). The architectural mandate requires completeness of discovery, uniqueness of legacy identification, and attribution.


10. MIGRATION OBJECT CLASSES

To avoid conflating disparate engineering concepts, Phase 1C.5g must strictly separate legacy assets into six distinct Migration Object Classes:

10.1 Class 1: Migrated Knowledge (MIG-OBJ-KNOW)
Domain knowledge regarding acoustics, psychoacoustics, amplifier design, microphone physics, cabinet resonance, and audio engineering principles.
Must map into upstream reasoning structures (Phases 1C.5a–e).

10.2 Class 2: Migrated Configuration (MIG-OBJ-CONF)
Static datasets, gear metadata, hardware specifications, parameter ranges, component lists, and physical dimensions.
Must map into reference catalogs and configuration schemas.

10.3 Class 3: Migrated Deterministic Logic (MIG-OBJ-DET)
Deterministic code algorithms, mathematical transforms, coordinate calculations, unit conversions, and constraint validation logic.
Must map into deterministic engine utilities and validation functions.

10.4 Class 4: Migrated Generative / Prompted Reasoning (MIG-OBJ-GEN)
Natural language instructions, system instructions, few-shot examples, and chain-of-thought prompts previously fed to language models.
Must be decomposed and mapped to discrete reasoning stages (Stage 01 to Stage 14) or replaced with deterministic logic.

10.5 Class 5: Migrated Runtime Behaviour (MIG-OBJ-RUN)
Session management, state persistence, error handling, retry policies, user override handling, and cancellation logic.
Must map into execution controllers and lifecycle management.

10.6 Class 6: Migrated Platform-Specific Logic (MIG-OBJ-PLAT)
Logic strictly tied to a target delivery format (e.g., IK Multimedia AmpliTube 5 XML/IKMPAK formats, parameter IDs, proprietary encoding, DAW track formats).
Must be strictly quarantined downstream of Stage 11 (within Platform Translation Plan / Stage 12 execution). Under no circumstances may platform-specific quirks pollute upstream engineering reasoning.


11. MIGRATION DISPOSITION MODEL

Every discovered legacy asset must be formally assigned one of seven mutually exclusive migration dispositions:

11.1 RETAIN
Definition: The legacy asset is technically sound, conforms cleanly to the frozen Phase 1C.5a–f architecture, has verified provenance, and can be adopted without substantive structural or semantic modification.

11.2 REMAP
Definition: The underlying engineering substance of the legacy asset is valid, but it must be relocated or attached to a different frozen architectural concept, record type, stage boundary, taxonomy, or governance schema.

11.3 TRANSFORM
Definition: The engineering intent or domain insight of the legacy asset is valuable, but its structure, expression, or representation must be fundamentally altered to conform to frozen architectural contracts (e.g., decomposing a monolithic prompt into distinct Stage 04 Hypotheses and Stage 09 Interventions).

11.4 QUARANTINE
Definition: The legacy asset's correctness, evidential foundation, provenance, or compatibility cannot currently be determined or verified. The asset is safely isolated, preserved in history, but strictly barred from execution in the authoritative path.

11.5 DEPRECATE
Definition: The legacy asset remains temporarily available for backward compatibility during phased cutover, but is designated for imminent removal and is barred from future development.

11.6 RETIRE
Definition: The legacy asset is permanently deactivated and excised from active use because it conflicts with frozen architecture, duplicates modern functionality, or represents invalid engineering practices.

11.7 DEFER
Definition: A migration determination cannot yet be responsibly reached due to missing upstream information, unbuilt downstream dependencies, or pending empirical evidence. The asset remains in its legacy state pending resolution.

11.8 The Zero-Entitlement Mandate
Governing Rule:
"NO LEGACY ASSET IS AUTOMATICALLY ENTITLED TO RETENTION."
The default disposition for any legacy asset prior to formal evaluation is DEFER or QUARANTINE, never RETAIN.


12. KNOWLEDGE MIGRATION FIREWALL

12.1 The Firewall Statement
"LEGACY KNOWLEDGE MUST NOT BE PROMOTED INTO FROZEN REASONING STRUCTURES SOLELY BECAUSE IT EXISTS IN CURRENT TT."

12.2 Promotion Preconditions
Before any legacy knowledge asset can be promoted across the firewall into the frozen AI Sound Engineer reasoning architecture, it must possess:
  1. Verified Source: An unambiguous origin, author, version, or extraction provenance;
  2. Assigned Disposition: An approved RETAIN, REMAP, or TRANSFORM disposition;
  3. Destination Mapping: A precise target field, record type, and stage assignment within the 1C.5a–f schema;
  4. Compatibility Assessment: Proven structural and semantic compatibility with frozen contracts;
  5. Conflict Assessment: Formal verification that the asset does not conflict with the 21 Principles or frozen rules;
  6. Uncertainty Declaration: Explicit documentation of any remaining doubts, assumptions, or limitations;
  7. Governance Sign-off: Formal sign-off by knowledge governance;
  8. Validation Eligibility: Defined test criteria ready for evaluation in Phase 1C.5h.

12.3 Elimination of Silent Carry-Forward
Any legacy heuristic, prompt instruction, or parameter default that fails any of these criteria must be stopped at the firewall and placed into QUARANTINE or RETIRE.


13. COMPATIBILITY & CONFLICT ASSESSMENT

13.1 Mandatory Conflict Detection Checks
Phase 1C.5g must execute explicit conflict detection against all legacy assets for the following failure modes:
  - Principle Violation: The legacy rule directly contradicts one of the 21 Principles of Sound Engineering Reasoning (e.g., claiming certainty where evidence is ambiguous, violating Principle 5);
  - Evidential Bypass: The legacy rule bypasses Stage 01–03 evidence interpretation or Stage 05–07 diagnosis to directly assert an intervention (violating Principle 1 & Principle 3);
  - Platform Leakage: The legacy rule embeds target-platform assumptions (e.g., AT5-specific parameter limits or GUI knob steps) inside upstream perceptual reasoning;
  - Uncertainty Erasure: The legacy logic artificially suppresses or rounds away evidential, diagnostic, or acoustic uncertainty;
  - Causal Hallucination: The legacy behaviour produces an EQ or gain change without an established causal diagnosis explaining why the acoustic problem exists;
  - Stage Monolith: The legacy rule collapses multiple distinct stages (e.g., diagnosis, requirement, candidate intervention, and decision) into a single opaque heuristic or prompt;
  - Fact Fabrication: The legacy fallback fabricates acoustic evidence or gear parameters when input data is missing, rather than acknowledging incomplete information;
  - Hardcoded Reasoning Override: An arbitrary hardcoded constant overrides reasoned engineering decisions;
  - Provenance Void: Knowledge whose factual origin, technical source, or author cannot be identified;
  - Inter-Rule Contradiction: Two or more legacy rules or heuristics prescribe contradictory interventions for the same acoustic condition;
  - Hidden Implicit Dependency: Legacy behaviour relies on an undocumented side effect, global variable, or timing quirk.

13.2 Governing Conflict Response
When a conflict is discovered:
  1. PRESERVE THE LEGACY ARTIFACT IN HISTORICAL RECORDS.
  2. DO NOT SILENTLY REWRITE HISTORY OR OBFUSCATE THE CONFLICT.
  3. Formally register the conflict in the migration conflict log.
  4. Assign QUARANTINE, RETIRE, or TRANSFORM as dictated by governance rules.

15. PRODUCTION PROTECTION REQUIREMENTS

15.1 Continuous Operational Availability
Current Tone Translator production capabilities must remain online, functional, and unaffected throughout the Phase 1C.5g lifecycle.
Tone generation, signal-chain calculation, and AT5 export must continue serving users without disruption.

15.2 Non-Destructive Parallelism
Migration must not delete, overwrite, or mutate production code paths in place.
New architecture components must be introduced in parallel, maintaining total logical and physical isolation from the active production runtime.

15.3 Strict Path Disambiguation
Every execution path, service call, data query, and log entry must unambiguously indicate whether it is operating under the LEGACY_PRODUCTION path or the NEW_ARCHITECTURE path.

15.4 Preservation of AT5 Export Pipeline
The existing AT5 export pipeline (including IKMPAK packaging and preset generation) must remain fully operational.
Platform mapping improvements must be verified in shadow mode before any cutover can touch production export pipelines.

15.5 Protection Is Not an Architectural Exemption
Production protection must never be used as a justification to preserve architecturally defective legacy patterns inside the new system.
Legacy accommodations belong strictly in legacy boundaries or temporary translation adapters, never in core target architecture.


16. COEXISTENCE & SHADOW ARCHITECTURE

Phase 1C.5g must define a robust, technology-neutral coexistence model comprising five distinct operational paths:

16.1 Path 1: Production Path (PATH-PROD)
The active legacy Tone Translator pipeline that serves production requests, creates presets, and generates AT5 export files.

16.2 Path 2: Shadow Path (PATH-SHADOW)
The parallel execution path where incoming requests or benchmark fixtures are dispatched asynchronously to the new AI Sound Engineer architecture without exposing outputs to end users.

16.3 Path 3: Comparison Path (PATH-COMPARE)
The automated differential evaluation engine that compares PATH-PROD outputs against PATH-SHADOW outputs across signal topology, gear selection, parameter values, and reasoning traces.

16.4 Path 4: Controlled Adoption Path (PATH-ADOPT)
The gated routing mechanism that gradually channels designated production requests (by feature, genre, or user tier) to the new architecture once Phase 1C.5h has validated that specific capability.

16.5 Path 5: Rollback Path (PATH-ROLLBACK)
The fail-safe reversion mechanism that instantly routes traffic back to PATH-PROD in the event of an anomaly, error, or acoustic degradation in PATH-ADOPT.


17. SHADOW-MODE REQUIREMENTS

17.1 Non-Authoritative Execution
Shadow mode must execute complete, multi-stage reasoning runs of the new AI Sound Engineer architecture against realistic inputs without granting the resulting output production authority.

17.2 Independent Legacy Output Integrity
Legacy production outputs must be generated and delivered completely independently of the success, failure, latency, or characteristics of the shadow execution.

17.3 Structured Differential Logging
The shadow environment must record structured side-by-side diffs between legacy outputs and new outputs, detailing:
  - Divergences in amplifier, cabinet, microphone, or pedal selection;
  - Divergences in parameter settings (gain, EQ, master volume, mic placement);
  - Differences in signal chain routing and topology;
  - Structural differences in underlying reasoning, hypotheses, and justifications.

17.4 Zero Production State Contamination
Shadow execution must be strictly isolated.
It must never write to production user sessions, mutate production databases, or corrupt active preset storage.

17.5 Fault Isolation
Any unhandled exception, timeout, memory overflow, or reasoning failure occurring within the shadow path must be cleanly trapped and logged without affecting the production response.

17.6 Fixture Generation for Downstream Validation
Shadow comparison runs that highlight material divergences must be automatically packageable into diagnostic fixtures for Phase 1C.5h empirical analysis.

17.7 Shadow Mode Is Evidence, Not Validation
Shadow execution demonstrates execution feasibility and exposes behavioural variance.
It does NOT prove acoustic correctness, which can only be certified by Phase 1C.5h.


18. CONTROLLED ADOPTION & ADOPTION GATES

18.1 Gated Progression
No component, rule, or subsystem may transition from legacy to new architecture without successfully passing through formal adoption gates.

18.2 Phase 1C.5g Adoption Readiness Gates
Phase 1C.5g defines the technical and structural readiness gates that qualify a component for delivery to Phase 1C.5h:
  - Gate 1: Structural Mapping Complete — The legacy asset is formally inventoried and mapped to valid 1C.5a–f record schemas;
  - Gate 2: Architectural Compatibility Verified — Zero conflicts with the 21 Principles or frozen reasoning rules;
  - Gate 3: Provenance & Lineage Sealed — Origin, transformation rationale, and migration metadata are complete and immutable;
  - Gate 4: Shadow Execution Exercised — The component has executed successfully in shadow mode across representative test cases;
  - Gate 5: Differential Analysis Documented — Output divergences from legacy behaviour are fully catalogued and explained;
  - Gate 6: Rollback Path Operational — Deterministic reversion to the legacy implementation is verified;
  - Gate 7: Handoff Package Assembled — The component, test fixtures, and expected behaviours are delivered to Phase 1C.5h.

18.3 Prohibition of Unilateral Cutover
Phase 1C.5g CANNOT declare an adoption gate passed for production release.
Only Phase 1C.5h, following empirical UAT and acoustic sign-off, possesses the authority to grant Cutover Authorization (Gate 8).


19. ROLLBACK & REVERSION GOVERNANCE

19.1 Granularity of Rollback
The architecture must support fine-grained rollback capabilities, allowing reversion at multiple levels:
  - System-level: Reverting the entire application to the legacy production pipeline;
  - Subsystem-level: Reverting a specific lifecycle stage or engine component (e.g., reverting microphone placement logic while keeping amplifier selection on the new path);
  - Domain-level: Reverting specific genres, gear models, or acoustic categories;
  - Transaction-level: Automatically falling back to legacy generation if a specific run encounters an unhandled exception.

19.2 State & History Preservation
A rollback must restore legacy operational execution without deleting or obscuring the failed run.
The complete trace, failure context, and diagnostic record of the abandoned run must be preserved in accordance with Phase 1C.5f.

19.3 Governed Post-Mortem
Every rollback event must generate an auditable incident record detailing:
  - the triggering condition or failure mode;
  - the exact scope and timestamp of reversion;
  - the affected inputs and user contexts;
  - the migration disposition of the component involved.
Rollback is an operational safety mechanism, not an eraser of history.


20. PROVENANCE, LINEAGE & AUDIT REQUIREMENTS (PHASE 1C.5f INTEGRATION)

20.1 Direct Consumption of Phase 1C.5f Governance
Phase 1C.5g must directly adopt and implement the governance, traceability, and provenance standards established by Phase 1C.5f:
  - Every migrated record must carry immutable trace identity and record lineage;
  - Cross-stage causal dependencies must be preserved;
  - Uncertainty must be captured in explicit, un-coerced fields;
  - Parent/child run relationships must govern iterative migrations;
  - Tamper-evident integrity records must secure migration catalogs.

20.2 Prohibition of Competing Traceability Models
Phase 1C.5g shall NOT invent a custom logging, tracing, or audit format that competes with or diverges from Phase 1C.5f.
Migration governance records are first-class participants in the Phase 1C.5f traceability universe.


21. CANDIDATE-LESSON PROMOTION BOUNDARY

21.1 Strict Division of Authority
Phase 1C.5g does NOT possess the authority to approve the promotion of candidate lessons into authoritative engineering knowledge.

21.2 The Upstream Governance Role
As established in Phase 1C.5e and Phase 1C.5f:
  - Stage 14 generates quarantined Candidate Lessons;
  - Authoritative Knowledge Governance independently reviews and approves or rejects candidate lessons;
  - Phase 1C.5g only executes the technical migration, ingestion, and deployment mechanics of APPROVED lessons.

21.3 Ingestion Firewall for Candidate Lessons
Any candidate lesson that lacks an explicit, cryptographically verifiable governance approval record must be rejected by Phase 1C.5g migration tooling and remanded to QUARANTINE.


22. DOWNSTREAM BOUNDARY & INTERFACE TO PHASE 1C.5h

22.1 Division of Responsibilities
  - Phase 1C.5g owns: Migration inventory, asset classification, structural remapping, knowledge transformation, shadow harness design, and delivery of migration packages.
  - Phase 1C.5h owns: Empirical UAT design, acoustic evaluation fixtures, blind listening tests, algorithmic validation, regression testing, pass/fail certification, and final production sign-off.

22.2 Handoff Deliverables from Phase 1C.5g to Phase 1C.5h
Phase 1C.5g must supply Phase 1C.5h with complete handoff packages containing:
  1. Complete Legacy Asset Inventory;
  2. Migration Disposition Records for all assets;
  3. Formal Mapping & Transformation Schemas;
  4. Catalog of Expected Behavioural Changes (identifying where new reasoning intentionally departs from flawed legacy heuristics);
  5. Active Migration Conflict Log;
  6. Catalog of Preserved Uncertainties;
  7. Shadow Comparison Datasets and Divergence Metrics;
  8. Verified Rollback and Reversion Procedures;
  9. Migration-Ready Candidate Adoption Packages.

22.3 Prohibition of Self-Certification
Phase 1C.5g must never declare itself "validated," "production-ready," or "acoustically certified."


23. MIGRATION FAILURE & DEGRADATION CONCEPTS

The Phase 1C.5g architecture must anticipate and define governance handling for at least fifteen migration failure and degradation conditions:

23.1 MIG-FAIL-01: Unidentified Legacy Dependency
An asset slated for migration relies on an uncatalogued, hidden legacy function or global state variable.
Action: Execution halted; asset remanded to QUARANTINE; discovery inventory updated.

23.2 MIG-FAIL-02: Unresolved Provenance
A legacy heuristic has unknown origin and cannot be attributed to a verified acoustic or engineering source.
Action: Asset barred from RETAIN; placed in QUARANTINE or DEFER.

23.3 MIG-FAIL-03: Ambiguous Migration Destination
A legacy asset addresses multiple overlapping concepts without a clear single mapping target in 1C.5a–f.
Action: Mandatory decomposition; asset assigned to TRANSFORM.

23.4 MIG-FAIL-04: Frozen Architecture Conflict
A legacy rule directly contradicts one of the 21 Principles or frozen stage boundaries.
Action: Immediate rejection; asset assigned to RETIRE or QUARANTINE.

23.5 MIG-FAIL-05: Conflicting Legacy Rules
Two historical rules provide contradictory guidance for the same acoustic condition.
Action: Both rules isolated; assigned to QUARANTINE pending external governance resolution.

23.6 MIG-FAIL-06: Semantic Information Loss
A proposed transformation strips away subtle domain nuance or critical acoustic constraints.
Action: Transformation rejected; asset remanded to TRANSFORM for redesign or DEFER.

23.7 MIG-FAIL-07: Scope Expansion / Semantic Drift
A remapped rule inadvertently broadens its applicability beyond its valid historical domain (e.g., applying high-gain metal heuristics to acoustic folk).
Action: Scope constrained; asset remanded to TRANSFORM.

23.8 MIG-FAIL-08: Shadow Comparison Infrastructure Failure
The differential logging engine fails to capture or compare shadow outputs against production.
Action: Shadow runs marked UNVERIFIED; promotion to adoption gates blocked.

23.9 MIG-FAIL-09: Rollback Mechanism Failure
The reversion path fails automated validation testing or takes unacceptable time to restore legacy state.
Action: Immediate block on all cutover activities until rollback integrity is restored.

23.10 MIG-FAIL-10: Target Dependency Unimplemented
The target stage or record schema in 1C.5a–f required by an asset has not yet been built in the runtime engine.
Action: Asset assigned to DEFER.

23.11 MIG-FAIL-11: Downstream Validation Unavailable
Phase 1C.5h testing harness or test fixtures are unavailable for the specific asset category.
Action: Cutover blocked; asset held in adoption readiness staging.

23.12 MIG-FAIL-12: Production-Critical but Architecturally Incompatible
A legacy heuristic is vital for ongoing commercial tone generation but is hopelessly flawed from an architectural standpoint.
Action: Asset retained strictly within the legacy production path; barred from target architecture; assigned to DEPRECATE with high-priority Phase 1C.5d replacement reasoning required.

23.13 MIG-FAIL-13: Material Unexplained Divergence
Shadow execution produces radically divergent tone parameters without an intelligible causal justification in the trace.
Action: Flagged for Phase 1C.5h diagnostic triage; adoption blocked.

23.14 MIG-FAIL-14: Missing Evidential Foundation
A transformed rule requires structured evidence inputs that the current frontend or user intake cannot supply.
Action: Asset assigned to DEFER or constrained to conditional execution.

23.15 MIG-FAIL-15: Unapproved Candidate Lesson Ingestion Attempt
An attempt is made to ingest an unapproved Stage 14 candidate lesson into the active migration catalog.
Action: Hard firewall rejection; lesson quarantined; security/governance alert logged.

Note: These failure concepts are migration governance categories. They must NOT be added to the frozen 27 reasoning lifecycle statuses.


24. STRESS SCENARIOS

The Phase 1C.5g architecture must be explicitly evaluated against twenty stress scenarios:

SCENARIO 1: Clean 1:1 Mapping
A legacy tube amplifier impedance curve calculation cleanly maps directly to Stage 10 Constraint Evaluation without modification.
Governing Flow: Inventory -> Verification -> RETAIN -> Structural Registration -> Ready for 1C.5h.

SCENARIO 2: Monolithic Rule Decomposition
A legacy rule calculates preamp gain, power tube sag, and microphone distance in a single block.
Governing Flow: Inventory -> TRANSFORM -> Decomposed into Stage 08 Requirement, Stage 09 Intervention, and Stage 12 Translation -> Verified -> Ready for 1C.5h.

SCENARIO 3: Direct Principle Conflict
A legacy rule asserts: "Always cut 400 Hz by 6 dB for rock guitar" regardless of recorded evidence (violating Principle 1 & Principle 3).
Governing Flow: Inventory -> Conflict Detected -> RETIRE -> Historical Archival -> Replaced by dynamic 1C.5b/c reasoning.

SCENARIO 4: Monolithic Diagnostic/Intervention Prompt
A legacy prompt takes user input and directly outputs an entire AT5 preset without intermediate steps.
Governing Flow: Inventory -> Disassembled into discrete Stage 01–11 prompts/records -> Original prompt RETIRED -> Modular replacements tested in shadow mode.

SCENARIO 5: Downstream Platform Mapping Isolation
A legacy table maps perceptual "warmth" directly to AT5 parameter ID "Amp.MasterVol".
Governing Flow: Inventory -> Separated into Stage 08 Engineering Requirement (acoustic harmonic density) and Stage 12 Platform Translation (AT5 MasterVol mapping) -> REMAP.

SCENARIO 6: Provenance Void
A legacy heuristic boosts 3 kHz by 4 dB on all British lead amps, but author, date, and acoustic rationale are completely unknown.
Governing Flow: Inventory -> Provenance Check Fails -> QUARANTINE -> Prohibited from authoritative knowledge base.

SCENARIO 7: Mutually Contradictory Legacy Heuristics
Legacy Module A states: "Never place ribbon mics on center cone"; Legacy Module B states: "Place ribbon mics on center cone for bright speakers".
Governing Flow: Conflict Detected -> Both isolated into QUARANTINE -> Sent to Knowledge Governance for resolution.

SCENARIO 8: Commercially Successful but Causally Unjustified Logic
A legacy preset generator produces highly rated tones using an arbitrary mathematical formula that lacks causal acoustic meaning.
Governing Flow: Retained in Legacy Production Path (PATH-PROD) -> Barred from AI Sound Engineer target reasoning -> Assigned to DEPRECATE pending 1C.5c/d causal modeling.

SCENARIO 9: Shadow Divergence Under Identical Input
Under identical reference audio, Legacy TT chooses a Marshall JCM800 with dynamic mic, while New Architecture selects a Friedman BE-100 with ribbon mic and off-axis condenser.
Governing Flow: Shadow Differential Logger captures differences, traces, and justifications -> Packages divergence report for Phase 1C.5h acoustic evaluation.

SCENARIO 10: New Architecture Fails While Production Succeeds
A shadow execution run throws an unhandled reasoning exception on an unusual pickup configuration, while Legacy TT succeeds using a simple fallback.
Governing Flow: Shadow failure caught and isolated; production output delivered without latency; incident logged for engineering triage.

SCENARIO 11: Production Fails While New Architecture Succeeds
Legacy TT crashes due to an unhandled gear ID, while New Architecture reasons across missing gear constraints (Principle 18) and produces a valid fallback chain.
Governing Flow: Comparative monitor logs legacy fault and new success; provides empirical justification for accelerating adoption.

SCENARIO 12: Semantic Drift in Transformation
A transformation intended to convert "punchy bass" into a Stage 08 low-frequency transient requirement accidentally alters the frequency boundary from 80 Hz to 200 Hz.
Governing Flow: Pre-adoption verification detects bandwidth shift -> Transformation rejected -> Remanded to TRANSFORM.

SCENARIO 13: Unimplemented Downstream Stage Dependency
A legacy speaker cabinet resonance heuristic is remapped to Stage 10, but the Stage 10 trade-off evaluation engine is not yet deployed in the runtime environment.
Governing Flow: Asset placed in DEFER -> Maintained in inventory until target stage runtime is online.

SCENARIO 14: Unapproved Candidate Lesson Ingestion Attempt
An automated script attempts to promote a candidate lesson from an empirical session into the active migration knowledge catalog without governance approval.
Governing Flow: Knowledge Migration Firewall blocks ingestion -> Security alert triggered -> Lesson returned to Stage 14 quarantine.

SCENARIO 15: Structural Pass but Empirical UAT Failure
A migrated microphone placement rule passes all 1C.5g structural gates but fails Phase 1C.5h phase-alignment acoustic testing.
Governing Flow: Phase 1C.5h rejects component -> 1C.5g receives rejection -> Component remanded to TRANSFORM or RETIRE.

SCENARIO 16: Immediate Production Rollback
A newly adopted Stage 09 intervention logic deployed to 5% of production traffic causes a spike in clipping distortion.
Governing Flow: Automated monitoring triggers PATH-ROLLBACK -> Traffic reverts 100% to PATH-PROD in <50ms -> Incident report and traces sealed for review.

SCENARIO 17: Tightly Coupled AT5 Platform Logic Decoupling
Legacy code directly queries AT5 preset XML structures within general gain-staging routines.
Governing Flow: Decoupled into generic acoustic gain calculation (Stage 08/11) and AT5 XML serialization adapter (Stage 12) -> Verified in shadow mode.

SCENARIO 18: Hardcoded Constant Replacement
A hardcoded legacy clamp `max_gain = 7.5` is replaced by dynamic headroom reasoning based on input transient measurements.
Governing Flow: Heuristic RETIRED -> New dynamic reasoning verified against reference tracks -> Sent to 1C.5h for dynamic range validation.

SCENARIO 19: Hidden Fallback Dependency Discovery
Discovery audits reveal that an entire guitar solo preset relies on a silent catch-block fallback that injects an unrequested chorus pedal.
Governing Flow: Hidden behavior documented -> Fallback classified as QUARANTINE -> Explicit causal reasoning required to justify modulation.

SCENARIO 20: Context-Restricted Legacy Validity
A legacy heuristic for extreme metal guitar tone is found to produce disastrous results when applied to jazz or blues.
Governing Flow: Asset remapped with strict genre/context preconditions (REMAP with preconditions) -> Verified across multi-genre fixtures.


25. TECHNOLOGY-NEUTRALITY REQUIREMENTS

25.1 Architecture Over Implementation
Phase 1C.5g is an architectural mandate and must remain strictly technology-neutral.
It defines governance requirements, data relationships, operational states, and behavioural fences, not low-level software libraries or vendor platforms.

25.2 Prohibited Premature Mandates

  - Specific programming languages or TypeScript type hierarchies;
  - Specific database technologies (e.g., Firestore, PostgreSQL, DynamoDB, SQLite);

25.3 Status of Technical References
Any code snippets, collection names, SQL schemas, or configuration examples appearing in later Phase 1C.5g documents must be explicitly marked as NON-NORMATIVE ILLUSTRATIONS.

  TIER 2: PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED

27. EXPLICIT PROHIBITIONS

Phase 1C.5g explicitly and strictly PROHIBITS:

  1. BIG-BANG REPLACEMENT: Mandating or attempting an all-at-once replacement of the legacy production system.
  2. SILENT MIGRATION: Moving, modifying, or adopting legacy assets without logging immutable migration records.
  3. SILENT REINTERPRETATION: Modifying the meaning, scope, or parameters of legacy rules without a formal TRANSFORM record.
  4. AUTOMATIC RETENTION: Granting any legacy asset retention rights simply because it exists in the current codebase.
  5. AUTOMATIC REJECTION: Dismissing legacy knowledge simply because it is legacy, without evaluating its underlying acoustic validity.
  6. HISTORY REWRITING: Altering, deleting, or overwriting historical traces, migration records, or rollback logs.
  7. MANUFACTURED PROVENANCE: Fabricating or guessing authors, origins, or acoustic rationales for undocumented rules.
  8. COERCED CERTAINTY: Forcing uncertain or ambiguous assets into definitive RETAIN or REMAP dispositions.
  9. ARCHITECTURAL DILUTION: Compromising or bypassing the frozen Phase 1C.5a–f architecture to maintain legacy compatibility.
  10. PRODUCTION CONTAMINATION: Permitting unvalidated shadow-mode executions or experimental reasoning runs to affect live production outputs.
  11. MIGRATION AS VALIDATION: Treating technical mapping completion as a substitute for Phase 1C.5h empirical acoustic testing.
  12. UPSTREAM PLATFORM LEAKAGE: Embedding AT5, DAW, or hardware-specific implementation details into upstream engineering reasoning stages (Stages 01–11).
  13. UNBOUNDED REFACTORING: Using Phase 1C.5g as a pretext to perform arbitrary cosmetic code changes outside the migration boundary.
  14. ASSET HOMOGENIZATION: Treating disparate assets (prompts, heuristics, configs, platform mappings) as identical migration objects.
  15. UNAPPROVED LESSON PROMOTION: Ingesting unapproved Stage 14 candidate lessons into authoritative knowledge without independent governance sign-off.
