1.1 ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE & SCOPE
Phase 1C.4d establishes the authoritative, platform-independent Sound Engineering
Knowledge Conflict, Uncertainty, and Governance Architecture for the Tone Translator
(TT) Professional Sound Engineering System.

The fundamental purpose of this phase is to define how Tone Translator's Professional
Sound Engineering Knowledge System remains rigorous, trustworthy, and epistemically
disciplined when domain knowledge is incomplete, uncertain, weakly evidenced, context-
dependent, disputed, contradicted by competing evidence, superseded by superior
measurement or physics, applicable only within tightly bounded operational domains,
derived from practitioner experience, challenged by new findings, or affected by
empirical feedback from TT's own engineering practice.

1.2 FOUR GOVERNING AXIOMS
The architecture is anchored by four governing axioms established across Phases
1C.1 through 1C.4c:

1. KNOWLEDGE GOVERNANCE DETERMINES WHAT TT IS JUSTIFIED IN TREATING AS CANONICAL
   PROFESSIONAL KNOWLEDGE. IT DOES NOT DETERMINE THE ENGINEERING DECISION FOR A
   PARTICULAR CASE.
   Governance adjudicates the epistemic validity, operational boundaries, and
   consensus standing of declarative entities. Case-specific diagnosis and
   intervention design belong strictly to the downstream AI Sound Engineer
   Reasoning Engine (Phase 1C.5).

2. UNCERTAINTY MUST BE REPRESENTED, NOT HIDDEN.
   Uncertainty is an inherent, inescapable physical and perceptual reality of sound
   engineering. Systems that collapse epistemic doubt into pseudo-precise numerical
   scalar confidences (e.g. "confidence: 0.84") deceive downstream reasoning. TT
   represents uncertainty qualitatively through explicit operational envelopes,
   epistemic basis classifications, consensus descriptors, and known confounding
   variables.

3. CONFLICT MUST BE PRESERVED UNTIL THERE IS SUFFICIENT JUSTIFICATION TO RESOLVE,
   BOUND, SUPERSEDE, OR OTHERWISE GOVERN IT.
   True scientific and engineering divergence is not an error state to be scrubbed,
   averaged, or voted away. When authoritative sources disagree, retrieval and
   governance must preserve competing positions within explicit `ConflictRecord`
   structures, providing full visibility to downstream cognitive reasoning.

4. EXPERIENCE IS EVIDENCE. EXPERIENCE IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY TRUTH.
   Runtime observations, unexpected acoustic outcomes, and user preferences are
   empirical evidence. However, correlation is not causation, and local studio success
   does not constitute an invariant physical law. Experiential learning enters
   quarantine as `CandidateLesson` records and must pass rigorous evidence-sufficiency
   dossiers and standard governance before any generalisable insight can achieve
   canonical `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` status.

4. GOVERNING PRINCIPLES OF KNOWLEDGE GOVERNANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Knowledge Conflict, Uncertainty, and Governance Architecture is governed by
ten binding principles:

4.1 PRINCIPLE 1: KNOWLEDGE GOVERNANCE VS. CASE-SPECIFIC REASONING
Governance evaluates claims, models, boundaries, and evidence. It determines what
the system is justified in believing as sound engineering science. It never determines
what is happening in a user's specific DAW project or audio track. Governance decides
whether "Proximity effect increases low-frequency energy in directional capsules"
is validated; the Sound Engineer decides whether a specific vocal track is boomy
due to proximity effect or room modes.

4.2 PRINCIPLE 2: QUALITATIVE EPISTEMICS OVER SCALAR PSEUDO-PRECISION
True engineering knowledge cannot be ranked by a universal scalar float (e.g.
"Claim Authority: 0.87"). Such scores homogenize orthogonal dimensions. In TT,
epistemic standing is represented qualitatively by preserving evidence type,
methodological rigor, replication status, operational boundaries, and consensus.

4.3 PRINCIPLE 3: PERMANENT ORTHOGONALITY OF PROVENANCE AND EPISTEMIC BASIS
Source rigor describes the evidential and methodological caliber of the source;
epistemic basis describes the scientific nature of the assertion. A high-rigor
source (e.g. an R1 physics textbook) does not confer physical law status onto an
offhand contextual opinion. Conversely, a master practitioner interview (R5) can
provide an empirical observation that subsequent laboratory measurement validates
as an established principle.

4.4 PRINCIPLE 4: BOUNDARY-AWARE DIVERGENCE (CONFLICT CONSERVATION)
When two credible sources make seemingly contradictory assertions, governance must
first interrogate their physical and operational boundaries. Most engineering disputes
are boundary divergence in disguise. If boundary reconciliation is impossible, the
conflict must be preserved as an active `ConflictRecord`.

4.5 PRINCIPLE 5: ASYMMETRIC BURDEN OF PROOF FOR FOUNDATIONAL REVISION
Extending, narrowing, or superseding an established physical law or foundational
engineering principle requires a vastly higher evidentiary threshold than modifying
a contextual studio practice. The burden of proof scales with foundational generality
and systemic risk.

4.6 PRINCIPLE 6: EXPERIENTIAL LEARNING QUARANTINE INTEGRITY
No empirical observation from runtime sessions may enter the production retrieval
index directly. Experiential learning is quarantined evidence until it is formulated
into canonical candidate entities, corroborated across diverse conditions, and
approved through standard governance.

4.7 PRINCIPLE 7: AUDITABLE IMMUTABILITY OF SCIENTIFIC DISSENT
Governance systems that force consensus produce epistemic blindness. When qualified
reviewers disagree, their dissent, technical rationale, and counter-evidence must be
permanently recorded in `ReviewRecord` entities. Consensus is recorded only when
genuinely achieved.

4.8 PRINCIPLE 8: EVIDENCE STANDARDS PROPORTIONAL TO NATURE AND RISK
Per Phase 1C.4a-DR: "Recover the best available underlying provenance and validate
the claim to an evidence standard appropriate to its nature and risk." No single
source class holds a monopoly on truth. A manufacturer schematic is the highest
authority for circuit topology; peer-reviewed acoustic literature is the authority
for wave diffraction; controlled listening tests are the authority for perceptual
masking thresholds.

4.9 PRINCIPLE 9: RETENTION ACCORDING TO GOVERNANCE POLICY
The lifecycle of evidence, candidate lessons, and superseded claims is governed by
explicit historical lineage and compliance policies, not ungrounded, dogmatic
claims of "infinite permanent storage."

4.10 PRINCIPLE 10: DETERMINISTIC ENFORCEMENT, HUMAN SCIENTIFIC JUDGEMENT
Deterministic software enforces schemas, reference integrity, cryptographic hashes,
quarantine isolation, and lifecycle guards. Human engineering experts (or verified
adversarial review panels) evaluate physical plausibility, methodological validity,
and causal soundness. Software guards the rules; engineers adjudicate the science.

5. CONFLICT TAXONOMY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A central failure of naive knowledge systems is treating all disagreement as binary
contradiction. In professional audio engineering, disagreement spans ten distinct
taxonomical categories, each requiring different governance treatment:

5.1 CATEGORY 1: DIRECT FACTUAL CONTRADICTION
- Definition: Two claims assert mutually exclusive, irreconcilable factual states
  for identical physical parameters under identical physical conditions.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  Claim A asserts that the speed of sound in dry air at 20°C is 343 m/s; Claim B
  asserts it is 310 m/s under identical temperature and humidity.
- Governance Treatment: High-priority factual adjudication. The claim contradicted
  by empirical physics standards is marked `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED`. Both claims
  cannot remain active.

5.2 CATEGORY 2: MEASUREMENT & INSTRUMENTATION DISAGREEMENT
- Definition: Disagreement arising from differences in measurement apparatus,
  calibration standards, test signals, weighting filters, or measurement geometry.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  Loudspeaker sensitivity measured using pink noise 1W/1m in an anechoic chamber
  versus swept sine wave in a half-space baffle.
- Governance Treatment: Preserved as measurement divergence. Reconciled by attaching
  explicit measurement methodology and test condition specifications to the
  respective `OperationalBoundary` records.

5.3 CATEGORY 3: METHODOLOGICAL & MODELING DISAGREEMENT
- Definition: Disagreement arising from competing mathematical simplifications,
  lumped-element vs. finite-element simulations, or linear vs. nonlinear models.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  Modeling vacuum tube grid current using simple diode approximations versus
  extended phenomenological space-charge differential equations.
- Governance Treatment: Both models remain valid within their respective domains
  of approximation. Governed via `CausalModel` links and boundary limits.

5.4 CATEGORY 4: COMPETING CAUSAL MECHANISMS
- Definition: Agreement on the observed acoustic symptom, but disagreement regarding
  the underlying physical or circuit mechanism generating it.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  Agreement that an overdriven tube amplifier exhibits low-frequency compression,
  but Model A attributes it to power supply rail sag, while Model B attributes it
  to control-grid bias drift (grid blocking).
- Governance Treatment: `ConflictRecord` linked to multiple `CausalModel` entities.
  Downstream retrieval must present both competing mechanisms so reasoning can
  evaluate which mechanism is active in a given circuit.

5.5 CATEGORY 5: OPERATIONAL BOUNDARY DIVERGENCE (APPARENT CONFLICT)
- Definition: Two claims appear to contradict, but closer physical examination
  reveals they describe phenomena operating under different boundary conditions.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  Source A states: "High-pass filtering at 80 Hz tightens low-end punch without
  loss of bass energy." Source B states: "High-pass filtering at 80 Hz thins out
  low-end foundation and introduces significant group delay smear."
  Resolution: Source A assumes a standard electric bass with significant sub-bass
  rumble and a ported cabinet; Source B assumes an 8-string drop-tuned guitar where
  the fundamental lies at 55 Hz.
- Governance Treatment: Reconciled by refining both `OperationalBoundary` entities.
  The conflict is marked `CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED`.

5.6 CATEGORY 6: TERMINOLOGICAL & SEMANTIC AMBIGUITY
- Definition: Apparent disagreement caused by loose, conflicting, or colloquial
  sound engineering jargon (e.g. "warmth", "air", "headroom", "transparency", "bite").
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  Author A defines "headroom" as peak voltage swing before clipping; Author B
  defines "headroom" as RMS margin below a nominal operating level.
- Governance Treatment: Reconciled by decomposing colloquial terms into rigorous
  DSP/physical metrics (e.g. dBFS peak margin vs. dBu continuous RMS margin).

5.7 CATEGORY 7: CONTEXT-DEPENDENT PRACTICE DIFFERENCES
- Definition: Disagreement between master practitioners regarding workflow, order of
  processing, or mixing strategy, where both methods achieve professional outcomes.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  Compressing before equalization versus equalizing before compression.
- Governance Treatment: Preserved as legitimate competing professional practices
  (`knowledge_role: CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_PRACTICE`). Explicitly forbidden to force a
  single dogmatic choice.

5.8 CATEGORY 8: HISTORICAL CONVENTION VS. CONTEMPORARY EVIDENCE
- Definition: Longstanding audio lore or studio rules-of-thumb that are challenged
  or refuted by contemporary high-resolution measurement or digital DSP science.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  Historical rule: "Never boost high frequencies with digital EQ because digital
  EQ introduces harshness." Modern measurement reveals that minimum-phase digital EQ
  is mathematically identical to analog minimum-phase EQ curves, and perceived
  harshness was caused by poor early anti-aliasing filters or clipping downstream.
- Governance Treatment: Historical rule is marked `SUPERSEDED` or narrowed to
  antiquated converters, linked to modern peer-reviewed research.

5.9 CATEGORY 9: DEVICE-SPECIFIC VS. GENERALISED CLAIMS
- Definition: A claim true for a specific piece of commercial hardware falsely
  asserted as a general acoustic or electronic principle.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  "Turning the tone knob to 0 produces a sharp mid-boost resonance." True for a
  passive guitar circuit with a specific inductor-capacitor load, but false for
  an active Baxandall tone stack.
- Governance Treatment: Bound to device-specific scope via `DeviceSpecificityContext`
  within `OperationalBoundary`.

5.10 CATEGORY 10: UNRESOLVED SCIENTIFIC & PSYCHOACOUSTIC UNCERTAINTY
- Definition: Legitimate, ongoing scientific disputes within acoustics and
  psychoacoustics where current empirical data is inconclusive.
- Example [UNVERIFIED ARCHITECTURAL ILLUSTRATION]:
  The perceptual audibility of ultrasonic frequencies (>20 kHz) via intermodulation
  distortion at the cochlea or bone conduction.
- Governance Treatment: Maintained permanently as an active, unresolved conflict
  with balanced attribution to primary literature.

7. CONFLICT LIFECYCLE & RECONCILIATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 CONFLICT LIFECYCLE WORKFLOW STATES
To preserve the distinction between an entity's deployment lifecycle (e.g. `VALIDATED_ACTIVE`,
`DEPRECATED`) and the ongoing state of a scientific dispute, `ConflictRecord` operates
under a distinct `workflow_state` machine:

1. `DETECTED`:
   - Trigger: Ingestion linting, candidate claim submission, or reviewer identification
     detects competing assertions between two or more claims.
   - Initial State: Claims remain in their pre-existing states (`NORMALIZED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`).
     If an active claim is newly challenged, it remains `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` but is tagged
     with `epistemic_validation.conflict_status = ACTIVE_COMPETING_CLAIM`.

2. `UNDER_EVALUATION`:
   - An assigned technical reviewer or review panel conducts divergence analysis.
   - Objectives: Determine whether divergence is real contradiction or boundary difference.

3. `ACTIVE_UNRESOLVED`:
   - Analysis confirms that competing claims represent a genuine, unresolved disagreement
     (competing causal mechanisms, conflicting empirical data, or master practitioner disputes).
   - Governance Rule: BOTH competing claims may achieve or maintain `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` status
     if each independently meets the evidence-sufficiency standards for its stated epistemic
     basis. They MUST NOT be forced into synthetic consensus. Retrieval MUST deliver both
     claims with their parent `ConflictRecord`.

4. `CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED` (BOUNDARY RECONCILED):
   - Analysis determines that both claims are physically true within distinct, non-overlapping
     operational envelopes.
   - Action: Both claims are amended to point to tightened `OperationalBoundary` records.
     The `ConflictRecord.reconciliation_status` transitions to `CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED`.
     The conflict record is retained to prevent future confusion.

5. `RESOLVED_SUPERSEDED`:
   - Competing evidence conclusively demonstrates that one position was based on faulty
     measurement, disproven assumptions, or obsolete apparatus.
   - Action: The invalid claim transitions to `SUPERSEDED` (pointing to the superior claim);
     the validated claim achieves `VALIDATED_ACTIVE`. The `ConflictRecord` transitions to
     `RECONCILED_SUPERSEDED`.

6. `REOPENED`:
   - New empirical data, improved measurement technology, or field observations challenge
     a previously resolved or superseded conflict.
   - Action: The `ConflictRecord` transitions from `CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED` or
     `RESOLVED_SUPERSEDED` back to `UNDER_EVALUATION` or `ACTIVE_UNRESOLVED`.

7.2 RECONCILIATION VS. SUPERSESSION MATRIX
The following decision matrix determines the appropriate governance action:

Conflict Nature             Primary Evidence Available           Resolution Pathway
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direct Factual              Conclusive Physics / Standards       `RESOLVED_SUPERSEDED`
Measurement Disagreement    Differing test apparatus/methods     `CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED` (OB updated)
Competing Causal Models     Plausible alternative mechanisms     `ACTIVE_UNRESOLVED` (preserve both)
Boundary Divergence         Distinct operational domains        `CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED` (OB updated)
Terminological Ambiguity    Jargon vs DSP definitions            `CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED` (re-worded)
Context-Dependent Practice  Competing valid studio workflows     `ACTIVE_UNRESOLVED` (preserve both)
Historical vs Contemporary  Modern high-res DSP refutation       `RESOLVED_SUPERSEDED`
Device vs Generalised       Hardware-specific behavior           `CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED` (OB updated)
Unresolved Scientific       Inconclusive empirical data          `ACTIVE_UNRESOLVED` (permanent hold)

8. QUALITATIVE UNCERTAINTY ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8.1 THE FALLACY OF SCALAR TRUTH SCORES
A fatal pathology in automated knowledge management is reducing epistemic uncertainty
to a single scalar floating-point value (e.g. `confidence: 0.88`, `truth_score: 0.73`).
In physical sound engineering, such numbers are mathematically meaningless:
  - Does 0.88 mean an 88% chance the speaker cone resonates, or that it resonates 88%
    of the time, or that 88% of engineers agree, or that the measurement has 12% error?
  - A scalar float obscures the nature of the uncertainty, making it impossible for
    a downstream reasoning engine to apply appropriate safeguards.

8.2 THE FIVE-DIMENSIONAL QUALITATIVE EPISTEMIC VALIDATION MODEL
Phase 1C.4d reinforces the qualitative `epistemic_validation` descriptor frozen in
Phase 1C.4a-R Section 6.2:

1. Evidence Strength (`evidence_strength`):
   - `THEORETICAL_PROOF`: Derived rigorously from established laws of electromagnetism,
     mechanics, or wave equations (e.g. Maxwell's equations, wave reflection).
   - `SYSTEMATIC_EMPIRICAL`: Corroborated by controlled, calibrated laboratory measurements
     with isolated independent variables (e.g. anechoic polar measurements).
   - `CORROBORATED_PRACTICE`: Documented across multiple independent master engineering
     accounts and published professional treatises.
   - `ANECDOTAL_OBSERVATION`: Unreplicated practitioner account or exploratory studio note.

2. Consensus State (`consensus_state`):
   - `ESTABLISHED_CONSENSUS`: Uniform agreement across academic acoustics, IEEE/AES
     standards, and major engineering textbooks.
   - `PREDOMINANT_VIEW`: Widely accepted working paradigm with minor academic or
     audiophile fringe dissent.
   - `DEBATED_VIEW`: Substantial, active professional disagreement among qualified peers.
   - `OUTLIER_HYPOTHESIS`: Novel, uncorroborated, or contrarian model.

3. Replication State (`replication_state`):
   - `INDEPENDENTLY_REPLICATED`: Verified across distinct laboratories, measurement teams,
     or unrelated production environments.
   - `LABORATORY_VERIFIED`: Verified by a single controlled experimental run or manufacturer test.
   - `OBSERVATIONAL_UNREPLICATED`: Derived from naturalistic observations without controlled controls.

4. Conflict Status (`conflict_status`):
   - `UNCONTESTED`: No competing contradictory or qualifying claim exists in the library.
   - `ACTIVE_COMPETING_CLAIM`: Linked to one or more active `ConflictRecord` entities.

5. Boundary Certainty (`boundary_certainty`):
   - `TIGHTLY_BOUNDED`: Quantitative mathematical limits fully specified (e.g. frequencies,
     voltages, distances).
   - `PARTIALLY_BOUNDED`: Qualitative limits described, but quantitative edges require caution.
   - `EXPLORATORY_LIMITS`: Operational limits unknown or unmapped outside a narrow test point.

8.3 UNCERTAINTY ABOUT KNOWLEDGE VS. UNCERTAINTY IN CASE DIAGNOSIS
To preserve the Phase 1C.4d / Phase 1C.5 boundary:
  - UNCERTAINTY ABOUT KNOWLEDGE (Governance Concern):
    "Do we know whether odd-order harmonic distortion is perceptually harsher than even-order
    distortion at equal THD percentages?"
    Answer: Qualitatively bounded as `ESTABLISHED_ENGINEERING_PRINCIPLE`, `PSYCHOACOUSTICS`,
    `PREDOMINANT_VIEW`, `PARTIALLY_BOUNDED` (depends on spectral distribution and masking).
  - UNCERTAINTY IN CASE DIAGNOSIS (Phase 1C.5 Concern):
    "Is the harshness in this specific user's guitar track caused by preamp clipping,
    speaker cone breakup, or excessive 3 kHz EQ?"
    Answer: Evaluated by the reasoning engine using runtime DSP metrics, competing working
    hypotheses, and intervention trials.

9. EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1 THE PROPORTIONAL EVIDENCE-SUFFICIENCY MANDATE
Per Phase 1C.4a-DR: "Recover the best available underlying provenance and validate the claim
to an evidence standard appropriate to its nature and risk."

Evidence sufficiency is NOT a simplistic count of sources (e.g. "3 sources = true").
Sufficiency is an asymmetric, multi-factor evaluation of five core dimensions:
  1. Epistemic Nature: A physical law claim requires mathematical derivation and physical
     proof; a contextual studio practice requires documented efficacy and trade-off awareness.
  2. Systemic Risk: The danger of downstream reasoning failures if the claim is false.
  3. Evidential Lineage: Independence of supporting citations (eliminating circularity).
  4. Methodological Rigor: Controlled experimental design vs. uncalibrated impression.
  5. Boundary Completeness: Explicit identification of prerequisites and failure modes.

9.2 EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY MATRICES BY KNOWLEDGE TYPE

A. PHYSICAL LAW (`epistemic_basis: PHYSICAL_LAW`):
   - Mandatory Standards:
     1. Derivation from first principles (classical mechanics, thermodynamics, electrodynamics).
     2. Primary literature citation (R1 or high-impact R2).
     3. Strict quantitative boundary formulation.
     4. Zero unmodeled empirical exceptions.
   - Rejection Triggers: Any reliance on anecdotal accounts, marketing literature, or
     unverifiable proprietary assertions.

B. ESTABLISHED ENGINEERING PRINCIPLE (`epistemic_basis: ESTABLISHED_ENGINEERING_PRINCIPLE`):
   - Mandatory Standards:
     1. Published in recognized engineering treatises (R1, R2, R4).
     2. Systematic empirical verification or formal circuit/acoustic simulation.
     3. Documented operational boundaries and failure modes.
     4. Established consensus across independent authorities.

C. EMPIRICAL RELATIONSHIP (`epistemic_basis: EMPIRICAL_RELATIONSHIP`):
   - Mandatory Standards:
     1. Calibrated laboratory or controlled measurement dataset.
     2. Independent replication across at least two independent measurement setups.
     3. Clear description of measurement apparatus, calibration, and test signals.
     4. Documented confounding variables.

D. ENGINEERING TENDENCY (`epistemic_basis: ENGINEERING_TENDENCY`):
   - Mandatory Standards:
     1. Corroborated observations across multiple experienced practitioners or verified studies.
     2. Plausible causal mechanism articulated in an associated `CausalModel`.
     3. Explicit trade-off vectors (acoustic benefits vs. collateral penalties).
     4. Identified known exceptions where the tendency does not hold.

E. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT PRACTICE (`knowledge_role: CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_PRACTICE`):
   - Mandatory Standards:
     1. Documented in authoritative studio manuals, engineer interviews (R4, R5), or curricula.
     2. Explicit artistic/production context prerequisites (genre, instrument, arrangement density).
     3. Competing valid alternative practices explicitly cross-referenced.

9.3 CITATION CIRCULARITY FIREWALL
A critical threat to evidence sufficiency is circular citation: multiple secondary articles
or forum threads quoting a single unverified claim.
  - Deterministic Check: Tracing `ClaimAttribution` links back to the originating
    `SourceDocument`. If five articles all derive from one 1978 manufacturer application note,
    the claim has ONE evidence point, not five.
  - Governance Rule: Corroboration requires independent experimental or observation origins.

12. CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE CHANGE CONTROL & VERSIONING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

12.1 IMMUTABLE LINEAGE & ENTITY VERSIONING
Canonical knowledge evolves under strict Git-tracked, append-only version control.
Every canonical entity possesses an integer `version` field. When an entity is modified:
  1. The existing version is frozen in the historical archive.
  2. A new version increment (e.g. `version: 2`) is instantiated in the editing workspace.
  3. All historical citations, prior `RetrievalTrace` records, and past session lineage
     continue to point to the exact version active at the time of execution.
  4. The runtime index hot-swap mechanism (Phase 1C.4c Decision P) points new sessions
     to the updated version upon formal release.

12.2 TAXONOMY OF KNOWLEDGE MUTATIONS

A. MINOR AMENDMENT (Non-Breaking / Additive):
   - Scope: Editorial clarification, addition of supplementary illustrative citations,
     or expansion of non-critical descriptive examples.
   - Version Impact: Version increment (e.g. v1 -> v2). Prior claims remain valid.
   - Governance Pathway: Tier C or Tier B depending on claim scope.

B. BOUNDARY REFINEMENT (Tightening or Narrowing):
   - Scope: New empirical findings reveal that a principle fails under specific extreme
     temperatures, impedances, or frequencies.
   - Version Impact: New version of `KnowledgeClaim` and associated `OperationalBoundary`.
   - Governance Pathway: Tier B review; mandatory verification that existing causal
     models do not break.

C. BOUNDARY EXPANSION (Broader Applicability):
   - Scope: Experimental testing proves that an empirical relationship holds across a wider
     range of transducer categories than previously documented.
   - Version Impact: Requires Tier B or Tier A evidence-sufficiency review.

D. SOURCE REPLACEMENT / ENHANCEMENT:
   - Scope: Upgrading an existing claim's provenance from an R4 treatise to an R1/R2
     peer-reviewed paper or primary manufacturer schematic.
   - Version Impact: `ClaimAttribution` update without altering core physical assertion.

E. CONFLICT RECORDING:
   - Scope: Attachment of a newly discovered competing claim or counter-evidence.
   - Version Impact: Claim status updated to `ACTIVE_COMPETING_CLAIM`; `ConflictRecord` created.

F. SUPERSESSION:
   - Scope: An existing claim is proven obsolete, physically incorrect, or superseded by a
     superior theoretical model.
   - Version Impact: Originating claim transitions to `SUPERSEDED`; points to `superseded_by`.

G. DEPRECATION:
   - Scope: A claim is retired due to withdrawal of underlying standards or irrecoverable
     provenance errors without a direct one-to-one replacement.
   - Version Impact: State transitions to `DEPRECATED`. Immediate exclusion from retrieval.

12.3 HISTORICAL AUDIT RECONSTRUCTION GUARANTEE
Under no circumstances may a knowledge update rewrite historical files or alter past
session replayability. If a past tone engineering session ran against `KC-D04-EQ-001@v1`,
an audit query MUST be able to retrieve the exact text, boundaries, and evidence of v1,
even if v3 is the current production standard.

13. KNOWLEDGE CHALLENGE & REOPENING ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

13.1 THE EPISTEMIC INTEGRITY PRINCIPLE: NO UNQUESTIONABLE CANON
In Tone Translator, `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` status denotes high current justification under
rigorous governance. It NEVER denotes dogmatic, infallible truth. All canonical knowledge
remains permanently open to legitimate, evidence-based challenge.

13.2 FORMAL CHALLENGE MECHANISMS & TRIGGERS
A formal challenge against an active canonical entity can be initiated via five triggers:

1. Primary Literature Retraction / Correction:
   - An underlying source document is formally retracted or amended by its publisher/institution.
   - Action: Immediate deterministic flagging. Review pipeline automatically triggered.

2. Empirical Laboratory Replication Failure:
   - A controlled measurement experiment conducted by TT research or published in the
     audio engineering community directly contradicts a canonical claim.
   - Action: Creation of a high-priority `ConflictRecord`; entity flagged for re-review.

3. Discovered Provenance / Extraction Error:
   - An audit reveals that the original human extractor misquoted, misinterpreted, or
     cherry-picked a passage out of context.
   - Action: Expedited Tier B review; entity returned to `UNDER_REVIEW`.

4. Recurrent Field Contradiction via CandidateLessons:
   - Multiple independent engineering sessions report consistent, corroborated acoustic
     outcomes that contradict a canonical `OperationalBoundary` or `CausalModel`.
   - Action: Experiential evidence dossier triggers a canonical review dossier.

5. Contemporary Scientific Breakthrough:
   - New acoustic, electronic, or psychoacoustic research supersedes classical approximations.
   - Action: Standard Tier A/B revision proposal.

13.3 INTERIM GOVERNANCE STATES DURING CHALLENGE
When an active entity is formally challenged, the system must not abruptly break active
engineering sessions or ignore the challenge:
  - If the challenge involves a potential circuit safety or transducer damage hazard, the
    entity is IMMEDIATELY suspended (`quarantine_state: SUSPENDED_PENDING_INVESTIGATION`).
  - For standard physical or acoustic disputes, the entity remains active but is tagged with
    `UNDER_ACTIVE_CHALLENGE` metadata. Downstream retrieval packages must include an
    Epistemic Warning banner informing the reasoning engine of the contested status.

14. SUPERSESSION & DEPRECATION WORKFLOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

14.1 THE DISTINCTION BETWEEN SUPERSESSION AND DEPRECATION
  - `SUPERSEDED`: Used when KnowledgeClaim A is replaced by KnowledgeClaim B. Claim B provides
    a more accurate physical model, tighter boundaries, or superior empirical validity.
    Claim A retains its identity and lineage, but explicitly points to Claim B via `superseded_by`.
  - `DEPRECATED`: Used when a claim is withdrawn without a direct replacement (e.g. invalid
    methodology, non-reproducible artifact, or vendor retraction). Deprecated claims are
    quarantined from runtime retrieval.

14.2 SUPERSESSION WORKFLOW SPECIFICATION
1. Proposal: Reviewer submits a supersession package linking the existing claim (`KC-OLD`)
   to the candidate replacement (`KC-NEW`).
2. Impact Analysis: The governance system executes an automated dependency audit to identify
   all `CausalModel`, `OperationalBoundary`, and `ConflictRecord` entities referencing `KC-OLD`.
3. Evidential Demonstration: The review panel verifies that `KC-NEW` accounts for all valid
   phenomena explained by `KC-OLD` while eliminating its errors or boundary failures.
4. Atomic Transition: Upon Governance Lead sign-off:
   - `KC-OLD.lifecycle_state` -> `SUPERSEDED`
   - `KC-OLD.superseded_by` -> `KC-NEW`
   - `KC-NEW.lifecycle_state` -> `VALIDATED_ACTIVE`
   - An immutable `ReviewRecord` documents the supersession rationale.

14.3 DEPRECATION WORKFLOW SPECIFICATION
1. Proposal: Submitter demonstrates that a claim possesses fatal methodological flaws or
   unresolvable circularity.
2. Immediate Quarantine: Claim transitions to `UNDER_DEPRECATION_REVIEW`.
3. Archival Sealing: Upon approval, state transitions to `DEPRECATED`. The claim is permanently
   suppressed from production retrieval indices while retained in the historical audit store.

16. ENGINEERING FEEDBACK & RUNTIME LEARNING BOUNDARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

16.1 THE FUTURE RUNTIME FEEDBACK LOOP
Phase 1C.5 will execute the cognitive engineering reasoning loop:
    Evidence -> Hypotheses -> Decision -> Intervention -> Predicted Outcome -> Actual Outcome

Phase 1C.4d governs how outcomes from this loop interact with canonical knowledge:
  - Good Acoustic Outcome != Valid Causal Reasoning: An engineer may turn a knob for
    the wrong reason and stumble onto a pleasing tone. Success does not validate a
    false physical law.
  - Bad Acoustic Outcome != False Knowledge: An accurate physical principle applied
    outside its operational boundary or with a broken cable produces a bad outcome.
    Failure does not invalidate a true physical law.
  - User Aesthetic Preference != Physical Ground Truth: A user preferring an ultra-scooped,
    phase-cancelled guitar tone is an artistic choice; it does not disprove acoustic
    comb-filtering physics.

16.2 FEEDBACK TRIAGE MATRIX
When runtime feedback occurs, it is categorized into one of five governance buckets:

Feedback Type               Physical Interpretation              Governance Action
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Predicted Outcome Verified  Intervention behaved as modeled      Increases empirical weight of model.
Unexpected Side-Effect      Collateral penalty unmodeled         Triggers update to `CausalModel`.
Boundary Failure            Principle failed under valid state   Triggers `OperationalBoundary` review.
Conflicting Field Data      Opposing outcomes in similar cases   Triggers new `ConflictRecord`.
Novel Causal Relationship   Previously undocumented behavior     Instantiates new `CandidateLesson`.

17.1 THE PRINCIPLE OF PROPORTIONAL HUMAN INTERVENTION

   - Adjudication of active scientific conflicts with high systemic risk.
   - Any modification to the core 22-domain competency mappings.

2. SINGLE HUMAN SPECIALIST REVIEW (LIGHTWEIGHT):
   - Tier B empirical relationships and device-specific hardware measurements.
   - Tightening of existing operational boundaries based on verified measurement papers.
   - Ingestion of manufacturer engineering schematics and published datasheets.

   - Bibliographic metadata validation and DOI/ISBN resolution.
   - Exact source locator hash verification.
   - Schema validation, required field checks, and type linters.
   - Platform implementation leakage detection (firewall against software GUIDs/XML paths).
   - Citation circularity graph checking.

20. GOVERNANCE FAILURE MODES & ARCHITECTURAL MITIGATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The architecture establishes active structural protections against fifteen critical
governance failure modes:

1. Popularity Masquerading as Consensus:
   - Failure: A widespread studio myth or forum meme is mistaken for established consensus.
   - Mitigation: Consensus is evaluated strictly across peer-reviewed literature (R1, R2)
     and authoritative engineering treatises (R4), NOT internet citation frequency or social popularity.

2. Source Prestige Masquerading as Invariant Truth:
   - Failure: An offhand remark by an eminent physicist or legendary producer is treated
     as an inviolable physical law.
   - Mitigation: Permanent orthogonality of Provenance and Epistemic Basis (Principle 3).
     A remark in an R1 source receives `ENGINEERING_TENDENCY` or `ILLUSTRATIVE_EXAMPLE` status
     if it lacks empirical proof or formal derivation.

3. Reviewer Authority Bias:
   - Failure: A senior engineer's personal workflow preference is codified as universal doctrine.
   - Mitigation: Mandatory adversarial counterfactual testing; requirement that all practices
     document competing alternatives and explicit trade-offs.

4. Circular Citation Masking:
   - Failure: Five secondary books cite each other, creating an illusion of multi-source replication.
   - Mitigation: Deterministic Lineage Guard traces claims back to primary empirical data or
     original manufacturer test documentation.

5. Correlated Evidence Presented as Independent Replication:
   - Failure: Multiple tests using the identical microphone capsule model or identical room
     geometry are counted as diverse replication.
   - Mitigation: Evidence-sufficiency dossier requires explicit `source_diversity_factors`
     (varied transducers, environments, impedances).

6. Survivorship Bias in Practitioner Reports:
   - Failure: Successful mixes created with a particular technique are celebrated while
     hundreds of failed mixes using the same technique are forgotten.
   - Mitigation: Mandatory documentation of `OperationalBoundary.known_exceptions` and
     collateral penalties before a practice can be validated.

7. Confirmation Bias in Experiential Learning:
   - Failure: An engineer assumes that turning a mid-range EQ fixed a mix, ignoring the
     fact that the bassist changed their playing technique at the same moment.
   - Mitigation: Mandatory controlled variable isolation within the `CandidateLesson`
     Evidence-Sufficiency Dossier.

8. Feedback Contamination:
   - Failure: Subjective user praise ("Sounds great!") is treated as physical validation
     of an unproven acoustic hypothesis.
   - Mitigation: Separation of aesthetic preference from physical truth; user feedback
     cannot validate physical claims without objective DSP metric confirmation.

9. Premature Conflict Resolution:
   - Failure: Reviewers artificially average two competing models to present a clean,
     unified answer.
   - Mitigation: Strict Conflict Conservation Mandate; competing models must remain
     active in a `ConflictRecord` until genuine boundary or physical resolution is proven.

10. Permanent Unresolved-Conflict Paralysis:
    - Failure: A minor terminological difference causes a system-wide block on useful knowledge.
    - Mitigation: Formal categorization of conflict types; boundary divergence and
      terminological ambiguity are rapidly reconciled via operational envelopes.

11. Stale Canonical Knowledge:
    - Failure: Outdated 1960s vacuum-tube approximations persist unchallenged despite
      superior modern finite-element simulation.
    - Mitigation: Formal Knowledge Challenge Architecture with automated triggers on
      primary literature updates.

12. Silent Historical Rewriting:
    - Failure: Correcting a bug silently modifies the knowledge database, making it
      impossible to diagnose why a past session behaved as it did.
    - Mitigation: Immutable, Git-tracked entity versioning with cryptographic SHA-256 seals.

13. Uncontrolled CandidateLesson Promotion:
    - Failure: An automated routine promotes frequently observed runtime correlations
      directly into active knowledge claims.
    - Mitigation: The Experiential Firewall: CandidateLesson is quarantined evidence;
      promotion requires complete canonical entity decomposition and human Tier A/B review.

14. Over-Governance Paralysis:
    - Failure: High governance overhead prevents the ingestion of basic descriptive facts.
    - Mitigation: Three-tier risk-based governance; Tier C allows rapid single-specialist
      approval for low-risk contextual notes.

15. Platform-Specific Contamination:
    - Failure: Commercial DAW plugin IDs or vendor-specific parameter curves leak into
      canonical claims.
    - Mitigation: Deterministic Platform Leakage Firewall automatically rejects proprietary
      identifiers and non-physical parameter scales.

DECISION A: CONFLICT DETECTION ARCHITECTURE
Classification: NORMATIVE ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT
Resolution: Hybrid Ingestion Linting and Reviewer Registration.
Rationale: Potential conflicts are identified mechanically during ingestion via concept-tag
and parameter overlap checks, but must be formally registered and analyzed by human specialists.
Alternatives Considered: Fully automated LLM conflict detection (rejected due to hallucinations
and inability to distinguish boundary differences from true contradiction).

DECISION B: CONFLICT CLASSIFICATION TAXONOMY
Classification: NORMATIVE ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT
Resolution: Ten-Category Conflict Taxonomy (Section 5).
Rationale: Accurately reflects physical and professional reality. Prevents treating boundary
divergence or terminology confusion as binary scientific contradiction.
Alternatives Considered: Binary Contradiction vs Non-Contradiction (rejected as inadequate).

DECISION C: CONFLICT LIFECYCLE WORKFLOW
Classification: NORMATIVE ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT
Resolution: Six-State Workflow Machine (`DETECTED`, `UNDER_EVALUATION`, `ACTIVE_UNRESOLVED`,
`CONTEXTUALLY_RESOLVED`, `RESOLVED_SUPERSEDED`, `REOPENED`) decoupled from entity deployment lifecycle.
Rationale: Allows competing claims to remain `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` while their conflict is tracked.

DECISION D: CONFLICT RESOLUTION & CONTEXTUAL RECONCILIATION
Classification: NORMATIVE ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT
Resolution: Boundary Refinement Preferred Over Universal Elimination.
Rationale: In physics and engineering, competing claims are usually true within distinct envelopes.
Reconciliation tightens `OperationalBoundary` records rather than declaring an arbitrary "winner."

DECISION E: QUALITATIVE UNCERTAINTY REPRESENTATION
Classification: NORMATIVE ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT
Resolution: Multi-Factor Qualitative `EpistemicValidation` Model (Section 8.2); Zero Scalar Truth Floats.
Rationale: Prevents pseudo-precise distortion of epistemic doubt. Preserves orthogonal facets.

DECISION F: EVIDENCE-SUFFICIENCY ASSESSMENT
Classification: NORMATIVE ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT
Resolution: Asymmetric, Multi-Factor Standards Proportional to Claim Nature and Risk (Section 9).
Rationale: Enforces Phase 1C.4a-DR binding mandate. Eliminates arbitrary source counts.

DECISION G: RISK-BASED GOVERNANCE TIERS
Classification: NORMATIVE ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT
Resolution: Three Governance Tiers (Tier A Foundational, Tier B Empirical/Device, Tier C Contextual).
Rationale: Allocates expert review effort proportionally to systemic epistemic risk.

Classification: NORMATIVE ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT
