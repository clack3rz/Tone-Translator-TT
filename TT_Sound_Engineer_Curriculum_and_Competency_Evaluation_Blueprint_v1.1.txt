SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 PURPOSE & CORE ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION
Phase 1C.3c-R establishes the corrected, authoritative educational blueprint and
competency evaluation architecture for the Tone Translator (TT) AI Sound Engineer.
Its purpose is to design the educational progression and evaluation system that will
transition the AI Sound Engineer from the demonstrated Current TT capability baseline
(Phase 1C.2 / 1C.3b) toward the independently frozen Professional Sound Engineer
Standards (Phase 1C.3a).

This blueprint directly answers four governing questions:
  1. What must the future TT Sound Engineer learn?
  2. What capabilities must the runtime and evidence architecture provide rather
     than teach?
  3. How should disciplined, first-principles professional engineering reasoning
     be developed rather than replaced with static rules or prompt recipes?
  4. How will the system prove, through counterfactual and adversarial evaluation,
     that genuine engineering competence has been achieved?

1.2 THE CARDINAL DISTINCTION: EDUCATION VS ARCHITECTURAL CAPABILITY
A fatal trap in sound engineering AI design is attempting to "educate" an AI model
on concepts it cannot observe, or attempting to solve architectural blindness
through prompt engineering. This blueprint strictly enforces the boundary between:
  - Education & Cognition: Physical principles, electrical models, causal acoustics,
    psychoacoustic mechanics, competing hypothesis formulation, trade-off analysis,
    and uncertainty calibration.
  - Runtime & Evidence Tooling: Objective audio DSP feature extraction (spectral
    centroid, harmonic distribution, crest factor, envelope onset/decay slopes,
    phase correlation), closed-loop audio comparison, checkpoint state persistence,
    and schema validation.

Education cannot teach a model to hear what the software runtime fails to measure.
Conversely, runtime measurements cannot produce tone without a causal reasoning
framework that understands WHY electrical and acoustic transformations interact.

1.3 AUTHORITATIVE PROJECT ROADMAP CONTEXT
This document operates within the frozen Phase 1C lifecycle sequence:
  - Phase 1C.3a: Professional Sound Engineer Standards & Target Maturity (Frozen)
  - Phase 1C.3b: Current TT Professional Capability Gap Matrix (Frozen)

  - Phase 1C.4: Sound Engineering Knowledge Architecture (Design of representation,

  - Phase 1C.6: Competency Evaluation / Benchmark Suite (Formal evaluation system
    verifying achievement of Phase 1C.3a standards)

SECTION 2 — EDUCATIONAL MISSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 THE MISSION STATEMENT
"To cultivate in the TT Sound Engineer an autonomous, first-principles engineering
intellect capable of interpreting objective acoustic evidence, forming competing
evidence-supported working hypotheses across the electro-acoustic signal chain,
selecting minimal and defensible interventions, evaluating cross-domain trade-offs,
and verifying outcomes against reference standards without relying on static recipes,
deterministic keyword overrides, or platform-specific artifacts."

2.2 THE ANTI-RECIPE DOCTRINE
Professional audio engineering is not a dictionary of symptoms and fixes. A system
trained on heuristics such as:
    "Metal rhythm -> boost 808 Screamer -> cut 400 Hz -> clamp noise gate"
has not learned sound engineering; it has memorized a caricature.

When real-world conditions diverge from textbook tropes—such as an active-pickup
extended-range guitar with excessive pre-filtering, or a vintage fuzz into a
high-headroom clean amplifier—static heuristics fail catastrophically. The educational
mission mandates that every curriculum unit develops the complete causal chain:
    PRINCIPLE -> CAUSAL MODEL -> EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS ->
    EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED WORKING HYPOTHESES -> DISCRIMINATING OBSERVATIONS ->
    INTERVENTION CANDIDATES -> TRADE-OFF REASONING ->
    PREDICTED CONSEQUENCES -> EMPIRICAL VERIFICATION -> REVISION.

SECTION 4 — EDUCATION VS CAPABILITY GAP FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 GAP TAXONOMY
Every identified deficiency across the 22 domains is classified into one or more of
the following six functional categories:

  Category A: KNOWLEDGE / EDUCATION
    Deficiencies where the cognitive model lacks understanding of physics,
    circuits, acoustics, or signal interactions.
    Resolution: Curated technical instruction and validated knowledge units.

  Category B: REASONING / COGNITIVE METHOD
    Deficiencies where the engineer possesses facts but lacks the structured
    reasoning process to formulate competing hypotheses, isolate variables, or
    evaluate trade-offs.
    Resolution: Cognitive workflow frameworks and adversarial problem training.

  Category C: EVIDENCE / TOOLING CAPABILITY
    Deficiencies where necessary physical data cannot be perceived by the model
    alone and must be extracted from the signal by external tools.
    Resolution: Objective DSP feature extraction services (spectral, temporal, phase).

  Category D: ARCHITECTURAL RUNTIME CAPABILITY
    Deficiencies where the execution pipeline lacks structural facilities to support
    the engineering behavior (e.g., feedback loops, multi-pass refinement, tracing).
    Resolution: Runtime execution engine, checkpoint lineage, and dual-stream harness.

  Category E: PLATFORM TRANSLATION CAPABILITY
    Deficiencies or artifacts that belong strictly downstream in platform-specific
    adapters and must not be taught as general engineering knowledge.
    Resolution: Independent platform translation layers (e.g., AmpliTube 5 adapter).

  Category F: DETERMINISTIC VALIDATION
    Deficiencies concerning logical consistency, mathematical boundaries, schema
    invariants, and physical impossibility checks.
    Resolution: Deterministic contract validators and schema enforcement guards.

4.2 THE "CAN EDUCATION ALONE FIX IT?" TRICHOTOMY
To prevent architectural misallocation, every domain gap is assessed against the
following triad:
  - YES (10 Domains): Defect is purely cognitive or informational. Resolvable entirely
    via knowledge representation and reasoning frameworks.
  - PARTIALLY (11 Domains): Knowledge and reasoning can improve hypotheses and
    trade-off decisions, but verified execution requires runtime evidence or tools.
  - NO (1 Domain): Defect is structural or perceptual. Teaching cannot provide
    sensory data that the system cannot measure, nor can it execute an iterative
    loop if the runtime lacks closed-loop plumbing (Domain 21).

SECTION 5 — CURRICULUM DESIGN PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 THE FIFTEEN-POINT CURRICULUM UNIT TEMPLATE
All curriculum units must conform to a unified structural contract:
  1. Domain: The governing engineering discipline (1 to 22).
  2. Concept: The specific physical or acoustic phenomenon.
  3. Engineering Principle: The foundational physical law or established theory.
  4. Causal Model: The mathematical/physical mechanism linking inputs to outputs.
  5. Relevant Evidence: Objective measurements and perceptual features indicating
     the state of this phenomenon.
  6. Evidence Limitations: Confounders, measurement ambiguities, and acoustic illusions.
  7. Plausible Hypotheses: Competing root causes or interacting mechanisms.
  8. Discriminating Tests: Observations or diagnostic probes that isolate the cause.
  9. Primary Interventions: Candidate adjustments across the signal chain.
  10. Alternative Interventions: Non-obvious or upstream/downstream alternative fixes.
  11. Trade-Off Analysis: What sonic attributes are compromised when this fix is applied.
  12. Expected Consequences: First-order and second-order acoustic results.
  13. Uncertainty Rating: Inherent ambiguity and confidence limits of the diagnosis.
  14. Cross-Domain Dependencies: Coupled upstream and downstream domains.
  15. Anti-Patterns & Pitfalls: Flawed heuristics, over-processing, and recipe traps.

5.2 FIVE-DIMENSIONAL PEDAGOGY
Education must develop all five assessment dimensions symmetrically:
  - D1 (Knowledge): Physical grounding, circuit topography, wave mechanics.
  - D2 (Evidence Interpretation): Reading DSP metrics, recognizing maskers/confounders.
  - D3 (Diagnosis): Causal tracing, abductive reasoning, hypothesis ranking.
  - D4 (Decision-Making): Minimalist intervention, constraint balancing, gain preservation.
  - D5 (Outcome Reasoning): Verification metric definition, falsification, refinement.

SECTION 6 — CURRICULUM ARCHITECTURE & LEARNING STAGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The educational architecture is structured into seven sequential, cumulative
learning stages:

  STAGE 1: FOUNDATIONAL PHYSICS & TRANSDUCTION MECHANICS
    Focus: Electrical signals, transducer physics, electromagnetic induction, wave mechanics.
    Domains: 1 (Source), 2 (Gain Staging), 10 (Microphone Physics), 11 (Wave Phase).

  STAGE 2: NONLINEAR SYSTEMS & DYNAMIC BEHAVIOUR
    Focus: Harmonic generation, clipping topologies, power supply sag, dynamic envelopes.
    Domains: 3 (Nonlinear/Distortion), 5 (Dynamics/Transients), 8 (Amplifier Circuits).

  STAGE 3: ACOUSTIC RESONANCE & SPATIAL PROPAGATION
    Focus: Loudspeaker impedance, cone breakup, cabinet acoustics, room acoustics, 3D space.
    Domains: 9 (Loudspeaker/Cabinet), 10 (Capture Geometry), 12 (Spatial Processing).

  STAGE 4: PSYCHOACOUSTICS & SPECTRAL INTEGRATION
    Focus: Auditory perception, equal-loudness contours, masking, critical bands, noise.
    Domains: 4 (Spectral Engineering), 6 (Noise Engineering), 13 (Psychoacoustics).

  STAGE 5: SYSTEM TOPOLOGY & CROSS-DOMAIN CAUSALITY
    Focus: End-to-end signal flow, reactive loading, pre/post filtering, contextual fit.
    Domains: 7 (Signal Topology), 14 (Musical Context), 18 (Trade-Offs).

  STAGE 6: DIAGNOSTIC REASONING & EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE
    Focus: Competing hypotheses, root-cause isolation, uncertainty bounds, reference deltas.
    Domains: 15 (Reference Analysis), 16 (Diagnosis), 19 (Uncertainty Discipline),
             20 (Alternative Solutions).

  STAGE 7: AUTONOMOUS PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE & CLOSED-LOOP REFINEMENT
    Focus: Minimalist intervention, closed-loop iteration, self-falsification, ethics.
    Domains: 17 (Intervention Selection), 21 (Iterative Evaluation), 22 (Professional Behaviour).

DOMAIN 1: SOURCE & PERFORMANCE
================================================================================
Target Maturity: L5 (Core)
Current Five-Dimensional Baseline:
  Knowledge: L1 | Evidence: L0 | Diagnosis: L0 | Decision: L1 | Outcome: L0
Required Educational Outcome:
  Mastery of guitar electro-mechanical generation: string alloy/gauge tension,
  magnetic pickup types (single-coil, PAF humbucker, high-output ceramic, active),
  pickup inductance/resonant peak (Q), volume/tone potentiometer loading (250k vs
  500k vs 1M, cable capacitance loading), pick materials/angles, and player touch.
Knowledge Requirements:
  - Equivalent RLC circuit of guitar pickups and cable capacitance.
  - Dynamic spectral variation caused by pick hardness, attack angle, and palm-mute damping.
  - Asymmetric vs symmetric string excursion harmonics; magnetic pull drag on strings.
Evidence-Interpretation Requirements:
  - Inferring general pickup class and resonance profile from spectral centroid and decay.
  - Distinguishing player muting technique from amplifier sag or gate clamp.
  - Recognizing that pickup/source characteristics are often underdetermined from audio alone.
Diagnostic Requirements:
  - Formulating competing hypotheses for dull attack: pickup height vs pot loading vs
    dead strings vs pick technique.
Decision-Making Requirements:
  - Determining when an upstream source limitation cannot be repaired downstream by EQ
    without degrading signal-to-noise ratio.
Outcome-Reasoning Requirements:
  - Predicting how shifting pickup height alters magnetic pull (intonation flutter)
    versus output voltage and dynamic punch.
Cross-Domain Dependencies:
  - Directly drives Domain 2 (Gain Staging), Domain 3 (Distortion), Domain 8 (Amp Input).
Gap Categories: A (Knowledge), B (Reasoning), C (Tooling - input pickup measurement).
Can Education Alone Fix It? PARTIALLY.
  Education teaches pickup/string physics, but runtime must extract input signal
  dynamic range and frequency profile from audio.
Required Non-Educational Capability:
  DSP analysis of input instrument audio; UI capture of guitar/pickup metadata.
Current TT Assets to Preserve:
  Keyword prompts distinguishing neck vs bridge pickups in geminiService.ts.
Anti-Patterns to Eliminate:
  Ignoring guitar pickup type and forcing uniform amp models; assuming all input
  guitars deliver 1.0 V RMS; claiming exact pickup model identification from audio.
Proposed Competency Evidence:
  Diagnosing pickup resonant peak mis-match from an audio sample and recommending
  input impedance, gain, or acoustic compensation.

DOMAIN 2: SIGNAL LEVEL, GAIN STAGING & HEADROOM
================================================================================
Target Maturity: L5 (Core)
Current Five-Dimensional Baseline:
  Knowledge: L3 | Evidence: L0 | Diagnosis: L1 | Decision: L2 | Outcome: L0
Required Educational Outcome:
  Rigorous quantitative gain staging across analog-modeled stages: nominal operating
  levels (dBu, dBV, dBFS), stage-by-stage headroom management, input pad compensation,
  inter-stage clipping margins, and SNR optimization. Context-dependent digital calibration.
Knowledge Requirements:
  - Operating level calibration: 0 VU nominal reference alignment is context-dependent
    (e.g., -18 dBFS EBU, -20 dBFS SMPTE/AES17, -14 dBFS broadcast/mastering).
  - Tube stage grid-leak, bias limits, and blocking distortion under heavy overdrive.
  - Dynamic range preservation across multi-effects blocks.

  - Quantifying stage peak-to-average power ratio (crest factor) and true peak overshoot.
  - Identifying digital converter overs versus modeled analog saturation.
Diagnostic Requirements:
  - Tracing harsh inter-stage distortion to accidental block gain buildup (+12 dB boost
    in parametric EQ driving chorus input).
Decision-Making Requirements:
  - Balancing drive level against noise floor; applying attenuation pads before sensitive
    modulation blocks.
Outcome-Reasoning Requirements:
  - Verifying that boosting drive on a pedal did not push the amplifier input grid into
    unwanted blocking distortion.
Cross-Domain Dependencies:
  - Strongly coupled to Domain 3 (Distortion), Domain 6 (Noise), Domain 8 (Amplifier).
Gap Categories: A (Knowledge), B (Reasoning), C (Tooling - peak/RMS metering), F (Validation).
Can Education Alone Fix It? PARTIALLY.
  Knowledge provides dBu/dBFS calibration; DSP metering must measure true dB levels.
Required Non-Educational Capability:
  True-peak and RMS meter probes between signal chain nodes.
Current TT Assets to Preserve:
  Clean boost mechanics in geminiService.ts (high level, low drive for front-end tightening).
Anti-Patterns to Eliminate:
  Treating gain knobs as dimensionless 1-10 dials; ignoring inter-stage level creep;
  prescribing a single universal digital 0 VU alignment level across all facilities.
Proposed Competency Evidence:
  Calculating stage-by-stage gain budgets to preserve dynamic headroom while achieving
  targeted saturation depth.

DOMAIN 3: NONLINEAR PROCESSING & DISTORTION
================================================================================
Target Maturity: L5 (Core)
Current Five-Dimensional Baseline:
  Knowledge: L3 | Evidence: L1 | Diagnosis: L2 | Decision: L3 | Outcome: L0
Required Educational Outcome:
  Deep first-principles comprehension of distortion mechanisms: soft vs hard diode
  clipping, asymmetrical vs symmetrical wave shaping, triode grid-clipping, pentode
  screen compression, crossover distortion, intermodulation distortion (IMD), and
  harmonic spectra distribution across octaves.
Knowledge Requirements:
  - Fourier analysis of clipping functions: odd harmonics dominate symmetric clipping
    (including push-pull stages and square-wave saturation); even harmonics dominate
    asymmetric clipping (single-ended stages). Both harmonic classes are musically vital;
    intermodulation distortion (IMD) from low frequencies entering non-linear stages
    is the primary cause of dissonant, flubby mud.
  - Slew-rate limiting, op-amp rail clamping, and fuzz Germanium/Silicon bias dependencies.
Evidence-Interpretation Requirements:
  - Analyzing Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) and harmonic distribution across octaves.
  - Detecting excessive IMD in polyphonic chord playing.
Diagnostic Requirements:
  - Discriminating between preamp tube saturation, power tube saturation, speaker cone
    breakup, and digital clipping.
Decision-Making Requirements:
  - Staging progressive saturation across multiple gentle stages vs one harsh clipper.
Outcome-Reasoning Requirements:
  - Predicting how cutting pre-distortion LF (80-200 Hz) alters harmonic density at 1-3 kHz.
Cross-Domain Dependencies:
  - Dependent on Domain 2 (Gain Staging), Domain 4 (Spectral), Domain 8 (Amplifier).
Gap Categories: A (Knowledge), B (Reasoning), C (Tooling - harmonic spectrum analyzer).
Can Education Alone Fix It? PARTIALLY.
  Educational curriculum teaches nonlinear transfer curves; DSP spectrum analyzer is
  required to inspect harmonic series.
Required Non-Educational Capability:
  Real-time FFT harmonic decomposition display.
Current TT Assets to Preserve:
  OverScream tightening logic in geminiService.ts and at5SignalChainNormalizer.ts.
Anti-Patterns to Eliminate:
  "Gain 10 solves everything"; teaching simplistic "even = warm, odd = harsh" dogmas;
  stacking multiple high-gain pedals into a high-gain amplifier without filtering.
Proposed Competency Evidence:
  Demonstrating why a soft-clipping mid-boost circuit preserves polyphonic chord clarity
  whereas an unfiltered hard-clipper creates dissonant intermodulation hash.

Target Maturity: L5 (Core)
Current Five-Dimensional Baseline:
  Knowledge: L4 | Evidence: L0 | Diagnosis: L2 | Decision: L3 | Outcome: L0
Required Educational Outcome:

  low-bass, mud/boxiness, body, nasal honk, presence, sibilance/fizz, air), passive

Diagnostic Requirements:
  - Distinguishing electrical tone-stack mid-scoop from cabinet wood cavity resonance
    or microphone off-axis cancellation.
Decision-Making Requirements:
  - Functional EQ allocation: pre-gain corrective filtering vs tone-stack character
    vs post-capture surgical cleanup.
Outcome-Reasoning Requirements:
  - Predicting phase artifacts and dynamic level shifts caused by steep filter slopes.
Cross-Domain Dependencies:
  - Intersects with every domain; foundational to 8 (Amp), 9 (Cab), 10 (Mic), 14 (Mix).
Gap Categories: A (Knowledge), B (Reasoning), C (Tooling - spectral analyzer), F (Validation).
Can Education Alone Fix It? PARTIALLY.
  Curriculum teaches acoustic band interactions; DSP spectral extraction is essential.
Required Non-Educational Capability:
  Multiband spectral centroid and energy distribution extractor.
Current TT Assets to Preserve:
  Functional EQ taxonomy (Pre, Stack, Post) and deduplication rules in at5SignalChainNormalizer.ts.
Anti-Patterns to Eliminate:
  Boosting EQ to fix acoustic phase cancellation; excessive narrow notch filtering creating ringing.
Proposed Competency Evidence:
  Resolving a boxy mid-frequency accumulation in a mix using complementary EQ allocation
  between guitar and snare without thinning the guitar's fundamental body.

Scenario 1: "Palm mutes are flubby, boomy, and lack tightness."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 1 (Source touch/pickup), Domain 2 (Gain staging), Domain 3 (Nonlinear IMD),
  Domain 4 (Pre-gain EQ), Domain 8 (Amp sag/damping), Domain 9 (Cab resonance),
  Domain 10 (Mic proximity).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Perceived loose, boomy low-end decay on palm-muted transient chugs.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Crest factor, low-frequency decay time, 80-150 Hz energy,
     pre-clipping vs post-clipping spectrum [CONTROLLED EVALUATION FIXTURE].
  3. Evidence-Supported Working Hypotheses:
     - H1: Excessive sub-150 Hz energy entering high-gain nonlinear stage, generating
       low-frequency intermodulation distortion and blocking recovery.
     - H2: Underdamped power amplifier allowing loudspeaker cone resonance to ring.
     - H3: Directional microphone placed close to grill, exaggerating bass via proximity effect.
     - H4: Loose player palm muting failing to damp string excursion behind bridge.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Inspect pre-distortion filtering profile.
     - Check microphone distance and polar pattern.
     - Check power amp negative feedback / resonance damping parameters.
  5. Intervention Candidates (Multiple Defensible Options):
     - Option A: Introduce pre-distortion low-cut shelf (e.g., ~6 dB cut below ~200 Hz [ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE]).
     - Option B: Back directional microphone off grill (e.g., ~5 cm [ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE]) to reduce proximity.
     - Option C: Increase power amp negative feedback damping.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Option A tightens mutes but may make single notes on high strings feel thinner.
     - Option B reduces boom naturally but introduces more room bleed/reflections.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Rapid transient decay on palm mutes, reduced intermodulation
     hash, punchy fundamental without lingering mud.
  8. Outcome Verification: Measure low-frequency decay slope and transient recovery.

10.2 THE SEVEN-STEP EDUCATIONAL CYCLE

  Step 2: EVIDENCE SEPARATION & VERIFICATION
    - Ingest objective measurements where available.
    - Segregate verified measurements from user claims, assumptions, and unknowns.
    - Declare uncertainty and confidence limits transparently.

  Step 3: EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED WORKING HYPOTHESIS FORMATION
    - Formulate plausible working hypotheses across the electro-acoustic chain.
    - Multiple hypotheses are expected where ambiguity or interacting causes exist;
      underdetermined systems are acknowledged honestly.
    - Identify discriminating physical observations that would falsify each hypothesis.

  Step 4: CAUSAL DIAGNOSIS & MECHANISM ISOLATION
    - Evaluate evidence against competing working hypotheses.
    - Rank hypotheses by probability, evidence support, and physical parsimony.
    - Identify primary causal mechanisms rather than superficial symptoms.

  Step 5: INTERVENTION DESIGN & TRADE-OFF BALANCING
    - Design candidate interventions targeting the causal mechanism.
    - Apply parsimony as a reasoning principle (preferring source/capture adjustments).
    - Explicitly articulate trade-offs, collateral acoustic consequences, and constraints.
    - Formulate primary and alternative engineering strategies.

  Step 6: PREDICTION & VERIFICATION CRITERIA
    - Define quantitative and perceptual criteria for success before executing.
    - Define explicit falsification conditions: what observation would prove the fix failed?

  Step 7: OUTCOME EVALUATION & ITERATIVE REVISION
    - Measure outcome audio against prediction criteria where closed loop is available.
    - If criteria are unmet, record error delta, revise working hypotheses, and iterate.

10.3 PHASE 1B CONTRACT RESPONSIBILITY BOUNDARIES
The curriculum aligns with Phase 1B contracts while maintaining strict ownership:
  - InputSnapshot & EvidenceRecord: Capture input provenance and objective measurements.
  - EngineeringDecisionRecord: Captures AI Sound Engineer cognitive reasoning (hypotheses,
    trade-offs, alternatives, decisions).
  - SemanticToneDesign: The platform-independent engineering contract produced by the AI Sound Engineer.
  - ValidationReport: Validates and checks semantic engineering results against physical
    boundaries and schema rules (Check/Warn/Fail). It DOES NOT own acoustic outcome
    reasoning and DOES NOT redesign engineering decisions.
  - RunTrace / RunTraceRecorder: Records execution trace, lineage, sequencing, and
    diagnostics. It DOES NOT reason about acoustic outcomes or own iterative engineering judgement.
  - Outcome reasoning, review, and iteration belong strictly to the AI Sound Engineer
    reasoning layer (Phase 1C.5).

SECTION 12 — SOURCE QUALITY & EDUCATION MATERIAL STRATEGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

12.1 MULTI-FACTOR EVIDENCE QUALITY
No source type possesses "incontrovertible" authority. The curriculum teaches that
evidence quality depends on multi-dimensional evaluation:
  - Source Rigor: Methodological soundness of theoretical derivation or experimental design.
  - Relevance to Claim: Directness of connection between source evidence and the specific claim.
  - Subject Jurisdiction: Whether the source is publishing within its core discipline.
  - Measurement Methodology: Precision, calibration, and control of empirical testing.
  - Reproducibility & Corroboration: Independent verification across multiple credible sources.
  - Operational Boundaries: Explicit documentation of where the claim holds and where it breaks down.
  - Contextual Applicability: Appropriateness to the specific engineering scenario at hand.

12.2 SOURCE TIERS (CONTEXTUAL ROLES)
The four source tiers represent contextual engineering roles, not rigid dogmas:

  TIER 1: FOUNDATIONAL PHYSICS & WAVE MECHANICS
    - Subjects: Wave propagation, electromagnetic induction, RLC circuits, Fourier analysis.
    - Sources: University physics/acoustics texts, AES/IEEE standard treatises.
    - Role: Explains immutable physical boundary conditions.

  TIER 2: PEER-REVIEWED RESEARCH & TECHNICAL MANUFACTURER LITERATURE
    - Subjects: Valve transfer curves, loudspeaker Thiele-Small parameters, polar response data.
    - Sources: AES papers, ASA journals, manufacturer engineering whitepapers (e.g. Celestion, Neumann).
    - Role: High-rigor models of real devices; authoritative for published device specifications
      without automatically establishing universal acoustic laws.

  TIER 3: SYSTEMATIC PROFESSIONAL RECORDING PRACTICE
    - Sources: Established studio handbooks (Huber & Runstein, Senior, Owsinski), master engineer treatises.
    - Role: Systematic studio methodologies and workflow conventions; contextual engineering practice.

  TIER 4: CONTEXTUAL ARTISTIC TECHNIQUE & GENRE PREFERENCE
    - Sources: Practitioner interviews, historical record deconstructions, artist presets.
    - Role: Contextual artistic aesthetics; must NEVER be taught as universal engineering laws.

SECTION 15 — EVALUATION PROBLEM CLASSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The evaluation suite draws from fifteen distinct problem classes:
  1. Foundational Knowledge Questions: Testing physical laws and circuit concepts.
  2. Causal Explanation Questions: Explaining why an electro-acoustic phenomenon occurs.
  3. Single-Domain Diagnostic Problems: Isolating a fault confined to one domain.
  4. Multi-Domain Diagnostic Problems: Isolating an issue spanning coupled stages.
  5. Ambiguous-Evidence Problems: Testing refusal to form premature diagnoses under partial data.
  6. Conflicting-Evidence Problems: Testing evidence hierarchy when observations clash.

  8. Alternative-Solution Problems: Demanding distinct viable architectural solutions.
  9. Trade-Off Problems: Evaluating compromises and justifying accepted penalties.
  10. Reference-Matching Problems: Deconstructing an audio stem into platform-independent designs.
  11. Outcome-Evaluation Problems: Evaluating simulated intervention results against predictions.
  12. Hypothesis-Revision Problems: Revising causal graphs after a simulated failed intervention.

  14. Platform-Independence Problems: Demanding pure acoustic designs with zero brand names.
  15. Full Professional Case Studies: Comprehensive end-to-end multi-track scenarios.

SECTION 16 — COUNTERFACTUAL & ADVERSARIAL EVALUATION STRATEGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

16.1 THE TRIAD DISCRIMINATION TEST
To prove that the AI has learned causal principles rather than memorized recipes,
adversarial triads are deployed. For example, three cases presenting identical symptoms:
  Symptom: "Heavy rhythm palm mutes sound boomy, flubby, and muddy."
  - Case A (Circuit Over-saturation): High-gain rhythm guitar with excessive LF entering
    preamp. Root cause: LF intermodulation distortion.
    Correct Fix: Pre-distortion high-pass filtering.
  - Case B (Acoustic Cabinet Resonance): Pre-filtering is tight, but cabinet enclosure
    has unbraced cavity resonance ringing at low frequencies.
    Correct Fix: Adjust cabinet damping or enclosure; do NOT cut pre-distortion EQ.
  - Case C (Player Touch / Technique): Signal chain is pristine, but player's palm
    anchor is displaced, failing to damp fundamentals properly.
    Correct Fix: Flag performance technique defect; do NOT alter amplifier or cab.

Evaluation Metric:
  A recipe learner applies "cut bass with Tube Screamer" to all three cases.
  A competent Sound Engineer isolates the true causal mechanism in each case and applies
  distinct, defensible interventions.

SECTION 18 — AUTOMATIC FAILURE CONDITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The occurrence of any condition below represents a fundamental failure of professional
engineering reasoning and results in non-negotiable failure of evaluation:

  1. Acoustic Evidence Fabrication: Hallucinating frequency measurements, dynamic ranges,
     or phase metrics not present in input data.
  2. Observation from Unavailable Channel: Claiming to perceive auditory characteristics
     from an evidence channel that was not present or processed (e.g., claiming to have
     "listened to" an audio clip without runtime DSP feature extraction).
  3. Spurious Certainty: Presenting unsupported assumptions or guesses as established
     facts without disclosing material uncertainty.
  4. Confusing Correlation with Causation: Asserting a causal link based solely on
     co-occurrence without demonstrating the physical mechanism.
  5. Safety or Integrity Violation: Proposing configurations that violate physical or
     digital system integrity (e.g., extreme levels causing severe digital clipping).
  6. Covert Goalpost Shifting: Silently altering the stated engineering objective when
     unable to achieve the requested target.
  7. Recipe Dogmatism: Treating a contextual heuristic or genre stereotype as an
     inflexible universal law despite contradictory evidence.
  8. Material Uncertainty Concealment: Failing to disclose significant causal ambiguity
     or conflicting hypotheses when such uncertainty directly impacts the decision.

Clarifications:
  - Closed-loop verification is required where evidence channels exist; it is NOT
    demanded where closed-loop testing is unavailable or inappropriate.
  - Downstream compensation is NOT an automatic failure if it is technically justified,
    proportionate, and consistent with the engineering objective.

  Stage 2: Extraction & Normalization (Decomposition into normalized representation).
  Stage 3: Technical Peer Review (Verification by qualified domain experts).
  Stage 4: Adversarial Validation (Stress testing against counterfactual triads).
  Stage 5: Promotion to Active Knowledge Base (Formal sign-off and indexing).

Runtime learnings and unexpected closed-loop outcomes are flagged as Candidate Lessons
and held in an isolated quarantine store. No candidate lesson can be promoted to global
knowledge without human expert review and regression testing against the benchmark suite.
