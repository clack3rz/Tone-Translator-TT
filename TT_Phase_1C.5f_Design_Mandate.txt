================================================================================
TONE TRANSLATOR (TT) AI SOUND ENGINEER
PHASE 1C.5f — FORMAL DESIGN MANDATE
Working Scope: Reasoning Traceability, Explainability & Governance Architecture
================================================================================

1. PURPOSE OF THIS MANDATE

This mandate defines the authoritative design boundaries for Phase 1C.5f before drafting the Phase 1C.5f architectural specification.

It exists to prevent Phase 1C.5f from:
  - redefining frozen upstream reasoning architecture;
  - inventing new lifecycle semantics;
  - assuming ownership of responsibilities assigned to adjacent phases;
  - prematurely freezing implementation technologies;
  - confusing auditability with reasoning correctness;
  - confusing explainability with unrestricted internal model reasoning;
  - converting incomplete provenance or uncertainty into fabricated certainty.

Phase 1C.5f shall operate as a cross-cutting governance and traceability architecture around the already-frozen AI Sound Engineer reasoning lifecycle.
Its role is to preserve, connect, explain, verify and govern the history of reasoning.
It does not redefine how the reasoning itself works.

2. WORKING PHASE IDENTITY

Working title:
  Phase 1C.5f — Reasoning Traceability, Explainability & Governance Architecture

This title is provisional until architectural drafting confirms that it accurately represents the complete Phase 1C.5f scope.
The title shall not constrain the architecture if the frozen source material supports a more precise final name.

3. PRIMARY ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE

Phase 1C.5f shall define the architecture required for the complete TT AI Sound Engineer lifecycle to be:
  - traceable;
  - attributable;
  - provenance-aware;
  - lineage-preserving;
  - integrity-governed;
  - explainable through governed engineering records;
  - retrospectively auditable;
  - version-aware;
  - historically non-destructive;
  - and suitable for controlled downstream governance.

The phase shall enable an independent reviewer to determine:
  - what evidence existed at each material decision point;
  - where that evidence came from;
  - what observations and interpretations were formed;
  - what hypotheses remained viable;
  - what diagnosis was actually authorized;
  - what uncertainty remained;
  - what engineering requirement was active;
  - what alternatives were considered;
  - what trade-offs were known;
  - what decision was selected;
  - what was predicted before execution;
  - what actually occurred after execution;
  - whether execution differed from intent;
  - what review concluded;
  - whether subsequent runs inherited or re-qualified previous evidence;
  - and whether historical records were altered after being sealed.

4. CORE ARCHITECTURAL IDENTITY

Phase 1C.5f shall be governed by the following distinction:
  Phases 1C.5a–1C.5e define how the TT AI Sound Engineer reasons. Phase 1C.5f defines how that reasoning history can be trusted, understood, reconstructed and governed.

Phase 1C.5f is therefore a cross-cutting architecture, not a new reasoning stage.
It shall not create an alternative reasoning lifecycle parallel to the frozen architecture.

5. AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE HIERARCHY

Phase 1C.5f shall operate in strict subordination to the frozen upstream architecture.

5.1 Phase 1C.5a
Phase 1C.5a v0.3c remains the constitutional authority for:
  - the 21 Principles of Sound Engineering Reasoning;
  - the 14 lifecycle stages;
  - the 27 frozen lifecycle statuses;
  - lifecycle record-presence semantics;
  - lifecycle transition semantics;
  - the governing invariant: UNCERTAINTY MUST SURVIVE THE DECISION.
Phase 1C.5f shall not redefine, renumber, reinterpret or replace these authorities.

5.2 Phase 1C.5b
Phase 1C.5b v0.2f remains authoritative for:
  - evidence intake;
  - observation;
  - interpretation;
  - hypothesis formation;
  - evidence sufficiency;
  - uncertainty;
  - discriminating evidence;
  - and the separation between observation and interpretation.
Phase 1C.5f may preserve provenance and trace relationships for these artifacts but shall not redefine their reasoning semantics.

5.3 Phase 1C.5c
Phase 1C.5c v0.1d remains authoritative for:
  - causal reasoning;
  - causal diagnosis;
  - surviving hypotheses;
  - causal exclusions;
  - diagnostic specificity;
  - unresolved mechanisms;
  - and causal uncertainty.
Phase 1C.5f may trace and explain diagnostic lineage but shall not independently revise diagnoses.

5.4 Phase 1C.5d
Phase 1C.5d v0.1l remains authoritative for:
  - Engineering Requirements;
  - Candidate Interventions;
  - Constraint and Trade-Off Evaluation;
  - Engineering Decisions;
  - Predicted Outcomes;
  - and Stages 08–11 ownership.
Phase 1C.5f may preserve the lineage and auditability of these records but shall not select or alter interventions.

5.5 Phase 1C.5e
Phase 1C.5e v0.1m remains authoritative for:
  - pre-action validation;
  - prediction consumption;
  - outcome evidence;
  - outcome evaluation;
  - iteration;
  - reversion;
  - return-upstream logic;
  - parent/child run relationships;
  - Stage 13 outcome evidence;
  - Stage 14 engineering review;
  - candidate lesson quarantine;
  - and downstream handoff boundaries.
Phase 1C.5f shall consume the traceability, provenance, lineage, integrity, explainability and governance requirements inherited from these frozen structures.

6. PHASE 1C.5f OWNERSHIP

Phase 1C.5f may define architecture in the following areas:

6.1 Trace Identity
Define how runs, records and governed artifacts maintain stable identities across the reasoning lifecycle.
This includes architectural requirements for:
  - stable record identity;
  - stable run identity;
  - relationship identity;
  - parent-child lineage;
  - successor relationships;
  - historical reference;
  - and record-version association.
The architecture shall not prematurely require a particular UUID format or identifier technology unless independently justified.

6.2 Cross-Stage Reasoning Lineage
Define how a reviewer can reconstruct the dependency path linking:
  Evidence
  -> Observation
  -> Interpretation
  -> Hypothesis
  -> Diagnosis
  -> Engineering Requirement
  -> Candidate Intervention
  -> Trade-Off Evaluation
  -> Engineering Decision
  -> Predicted Outcome
  -> Semantic / Execution Handoff
  -> Actual Outcome Evidence
  -> Engineering Review
  -> Iteration, Closure or Child Run.
Phase 1C.5f shall preserve these relationships without recomputing the reasoning itself.

6.3 Provenance Architecture
Define the architectural requirements needed to answer: Where did this information come from?
Provenance distinctions may include, where applicable:
  - user-supplied information;
  - measured evidence;
  - system-derived observations;
  - system interpretation;
  - inherited historical evidence;
  - human engineering input;
  - reference knowledge;
  - execution telemetry;
  - scenario-illustrative data;
  - external platform information.
Any vocabulary proposed for provenance shall be explicitly classified as inherited, proposed or illustrative.
No proposed provenance vocabulary shall be represented as an existing frozen lifecycle status.

6.4 Record Relationship Architecture
Define how records reference other records while preserving authoritative ownership.
Phase 1C.5f may define relationships such as:
  - produced-from;
  - derived-from;
  - evaluated-against;
  - supersedes;
  - references;
  - parent-of;
  - child-of;
  - execution-of;
  - outcome-of;
  - review-of.
These concepts shall describe lineage and governance only.
They shall not silently alter upstream record definitions.

6.5 Explainability Architecture
Phase 1C.5f shall define explainability as a governed engineering explanation assembled from authoritative records, evidence references, decisions, uncertainty and outcomes.
Explainability shall support reconstruction of:
  - what was observed;
  - what was interpreted;
  - what hypotheses survived;
  - what diagnosis was authorized;
  - what requirement followed;
  - what alternatives were considered;
  - why an intervention was selected;
  - what trade-offs were accepted;
  - what was predicted;
  - what evidence was later observed;
  - what review concluded;
  - and what uncertainty remained.
Explainability shall not require disclosure of unrestricted internal model scratch reasoning or hidden chain-of-thought.
The architecture shall prefer auditable engineering justification over opaque model introspection.

6.6 Uncertainty Traceability
Phase 1C.5f shall ensure uncertainty remains traceable across:
  - evidence acquisition;
  - interpretation;
  - hypothesis comparison;
  - diagnosis;
  - engineering decision;
  - prediction;
  - execution;
  - outcome evaluation;
  - review;
  - and child-run inheritance.
The architecture shall prohibit explanation layers from presenting uncertain reasoning as certain merely because the system is capable of producing a fluent explanation.

6.7 Record Immutability & Historical Preservation
Phase 1C.5f shall define the governance rules required to preserve sealed historical records.
A sealed record shall not be silently rewritten to improve the appearance of previous reasoning.
Where later evidence shows that an earlier record was incomplete, incorrect or outdated:
  - the historical record shall remain preserved;
  - a successor, corrective, review or child record may be created;
  - the relationship shall be traceable;
  - the later correction shall not erase the earlier state.
Phase 1C.5f shall not invent new lifecycle statuses to represent record-governance states.
Record state and lifecycle state must remain distinct architectural concepts.

6.8 Parent / Child Run Governance
Phase 1C.5f shall formalize traceability requirements for linked parent and child runs.
The architecture shall preserve the frozen Phase 1C.5e rule that:
  - parent evidence remains historically preserved;
  - parent evidence is not automatically re-qualified as current child-run evidence;
  - child-run evidence must be reassessed for relevance and comparability;
  - parent records remain immutable;
  - lineage remains explicit.

6.9 Execution & Outcome Lineage
Phase 1C.5f shall define how intended engineering action can be traced to:
  - semantic design;
  - platform translation;
  - actual applied configuration;
  - execution receipts;
  - execution deviations;
  - observed outcome evidence;
  - and retrospective review.
This must preserve the distinction between:
  - what was intended;
  - what was translated;
  - what was actually applied;
  - and what was observed afterwards.

6.10 Integrity Requirements
Phase 1C.5f shall define what must be integrity-protected before selecting how it is protected.
Integrity requirements may apply to:
  - record identity;
  - record ordering;
  - record version;
  - provenance;
  - parent/child relationships;
  - sealed history;
  - decision/prediction immutability;
  - evidence separation;
  - execution lineage;
  - review history.
Possible implementation mechanisms may be considered later, including:
  - hashes;
  - content fingerprints;
  - immutable snapshots;
  - append-only stores;
  - chained references;
  - digital signatures;
  - signed manifests;
  - content-addressed records.
No specific technology shall be declared mandatory unless the architectural requirement cannot otherwise be satisfied.

6.11 Independent Audit Reconstruction
Phase 1C.5f shall support independent reconstruction of the engineering lifecycle.
An auditor shall be able to determine:
  - what evidence was available;
  - what evidence was unavailable;
  - what reasoning artifacts existed at the time;
  - what decision was made;
  - what uncertainty existed;
  - whether the decision was justified by the recorded evidence;
  - what predictions preceded execution;
  - what actually occurred;
  - whether execution deviated;
  - whether post-hoc rewriting occurred;
  - and how later runs relate to the reviewed run.
The audit architecture shall not require trusting the same system that originally generated the reasoning.

6.12 Human Review & Override Provenance
Where a human engineer, user or authorized reviewer:
  - accepts;
  - rejects;
  - modifies;
  - overrides;
  - escalates;
  - or supplements
a system-generated decision or explanation, Phase 1C.5f shall preserve that intervention as explicit provenance.
Human involvement shall not be silently merged into AI-originated reasoning.
AI-originated reasoning shall not be silently presented as human-authorized judgment.

6.13 Candidate Learning Governance
Phase 1C.5f may define the governance architecture used to evaluate whether a quarantined Stage 14 candidate lesson is eligible for promotion.
This may include requirements for:
  - evidence provenance;
  - supporting cases;
  - contradictory cases;
  - applicability scope;
  - uncertainty;
  - review history;
  - reviewer authority;
  - conflict detection;
  - rollback or revocation governance.
However:
Phase 1C.5f shall not itself perform current-TT reasoning migration.
Migration remains owned by Phase 1C.5g.

7. NON-OWNERSHIP / OUT-OF-SCOPE BOUNDARIES

Phase 1C.5f MUST NOT:
  - redefine the 21 Principles;
  - create lifecycle stages;
  - create lifecycle statuses;
  - redefine record-presence semantics;
  - redefine evidence interpretation;
  - create new diagnostic rules;
  - revise causal diagnoses;
  - formulate Engineering Requirements;
  - select Candidate Interventions;
  - perform Trade-Off Evaluation;
  - make Engineering Decisions;
  - author Predicted Outcomes;
  - redefine Stage 13 outcome evaluation;
  - redefine Stage 14 review semantics;
  - alter checkpoint/reversion logic;
  - perform AT5 gear mapping;
  - perform AT5 parameter translation;
  - implement target-platform execution;
  - perform current-TT reasoning migration;
  - execute formal UAT;
  - promote a candidate lesson solely because one outcome was successful;
  - expose unrestricted private model chain-of-thought as an audit requirement.

8. ARCHITECTURAL INVARIANTS

Phase 1C.5f shall preserve the following governing invariants:

8.1 Uncertainty Survival
  UNCERTAINTY MUST SURVIVE THE DECISION.
  Traceability shall preserve uncertainty rather than retrospectively smoothing it away.

8.2 Trace Integrity Does Not Prove Reasoning Correctness
  A complete and intact trace can faithfully preserve bad reasoning.
  Integrity demonstrates historical preservation, not technical correctness.

8.3 Provenance Integrity Does Not Prove Evidence Validity
  Correctly attributed evidence may still be inadequate, confounded or wrong.
  Provenance describes source and history, not truth.

8.4 Explainability Does Not Create Certainty
  A well-formed explanation must not make a conclusion appear stronger than its evidence supports.
  Explanation confidence must remain bounded by underlying evidential confidence.

8.5 Trace Completeness Does Not Authorize Missing Inference
  Missing history shall remain missing.
  Phase 1C.5f shall not invent intermediate reasoning steps merely to make a trace appear complete.

8.6 Historical Preservation
  Later correction shall not erase earlier engineering history.
  Correction occurs through linked successor or child records, not retroactive rewriting.

9. FAILURE AND DEGRADATION GOVERNANCE

Phase 1C.5f shall define explicit behavior when traceability cannot be fully established.
Architectural failure concepts may include:
  - incomplete trace;
  - unresolved provenance;
  - unverifiable lineage;
  - integrity not established;
  - conflicting lineage;
  - missing parent record;
  - missing dependency;
  - unexplained mutation;
  - partial explanation;
  - broken execution chain.
Any vocabulary used for such conditions shall be explicitly labelled as Phase 1C.5f governance vocabulary unless inherited elsewhere.
Such labels MUST NOT be represented as frozen lifecycle statuses.
When trace integrity cannot be established:
  - the system shall not fabricate missing records;
  - the system shall not infer provenance without evidence;
  - the system shall not claim complete reconstruction;
  - the system shall retain and expose the uncertainty.

10. RECORD / SCHEMA DESIGN STANCE

Phase 1C.5f may define conceptual record requirements.
Examples:
  - stable identity;
  - provenance reference;
  - parent reference;
  - predecessor/successor relationship;
  - version association;
  - sealing state;
  - evidence reference;
  - governing run reference;
  - explanation reference;
  - review reference.
However, Phase 1C.5f shall not prematurely freeze implementation details such as:
  - TypeScript interfaces;
  - Firestore collections;
  - SQL schemas;
  - database vendors;
  - JSON serialization formats;
  - UUID versions;
  - hash algorithms;
  - signature algorithms;
  - cloud platforms;
  - message buses;
  - storage engines.
Implementation technologies may be recommended or illustrated where useful, but must be explicitly classified as non-normative unless architecture genuinely requires otherwise.

11. PROPOSED VS INHERITED VOCABULARY POLICY

Every architectural term introduced in Phase 1C.5f shall be classified as one of:
  - INHERITED
  - PHASE_1C5F_PROPOSED
  - ILLUSTRATIVE
  - DEFERRED
  - IMPLEMENTATION-NORMATIVE, only where explicitly justified
Phase 1C.5f shall not describe proposed vocabulary as frozen upstream terminology.
Phase 1C.5f shall not invent exact lifecycle status tokens.

12. ARCHITECTURAL CAPABILITY AREAS

The first Phase 1C.5f draft should provide sufficient architecture to cover at least the following capability areas:
  - Trace Identity & Run Lineage
  - Evidence and Record Provenance
  - Cross-Stage Dependency Trace
  - Record Immutability & Version Governance
  - Decision Explainability
  - Uncertainty Traceability
  - Execution & Outcome Lineage
  - Parent / Child Run Governance
  - Independent Audit Reconstruction
  - Candidate-Lesson Governance
  - Integrity Requirements
  - Human Review / Override Provenance
  - Failure, Missing-Trace & Corruption Handling
  - Downstream Interfaces

These are required capability areas, not mandatory section numbers.
The document must be organized according to the clearest architecture rather than mechanically producing exactly fourteen sections.

13. GOVERNANCE STRESS SCENARIOS

Phase 1C.5f shall include worked architectural illustrations sufficient to test the architecture across materially different governance cases.
Scenario coverage should include cases such as:
  - child run created after failed intervention;
  - parent evidence no longer comparable;
  - human engineer overrides AI recommendation;
  - execution platform clamps or alters parameters;
  - engineering decision proves correct but reasoning quality was poor;
  - reasoning was sound but execution failed;
  - required trace record is missing;
  - record appears modified after sealing;
  - conflicting lineage claims occur;
  - candidate lesson lacks sufficient support;
  - candidate lesson conflicts with governed knowledge;
  - artistic intent changes between runs;
  - platform implementation changes while semantic engineering intent remains constant;
  - explanation is only partially reconstructable;
  - trace integrity cannot be established;
  - user or human engineer supplies late corrective evidence.
Scenario count shall be driven by architectural coverage.
No arbitrary minimum or maximum number shall be imposed unless justified during design.
Scenario data shall be explicitly identified as synthetic or illustrative unless based on actual verified evidence.

14. DOWNSTREAM BOUNDARY — PHASE 1C.5g

Phase 1C.5f may provide Phase 1C.5g with governance information such as:
  - origin of candidate knowledge;
  - supporting evidence;
  - lineage;
  - review state;
  - approval state;
  - applicability constraints;
  - known conflicts;
  - uncertainty;
  - deprecation state;
  - and promotion eligibility.
Phase 1C.5f shall NOT decide how existing TT reasoning logic is migrated, remapped or adopted.
That responsibility belongs to Phase 1C.5g.

15. DOWNSTREAM BOUNDARY — PHASE 1C.5h

Phase 1C.5f may define requirements that later UAT should validate, including:
  - trace completeness;
  - provenance preservation;
  - lineage reconstruction;
  - immutable-history behavior;
  - explanation fidelity;
  - missing-trace handling;
  - parent/child consistency;
  - integrity failure handling;
  - audit reproducibility.
However:
Phase 1C.5h owns UAT fixtures, acceptance criteria, scenario selection, empirical execution and final validation/sign-off.
Phase 1C.5f shall not claim UAT has been executed merely because scenarios are architecturally illustrated.

16. FIRST-DRAFT ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The Phase 1C.5f v0.1 architectural draft shall not be considered suitable for substantive review unless it satisfies all of the following:
  - It explicitly identifies 1C.5a–e as frozen upstream authorities.
  - It does not redefine the 21 Principles.
  - It does not invent lifecycle stages or statuses.
  - It clearly distinguishes reasoning semantics from trace/governance semantics.
  - It defines provenance architecture.
  - It defines lineage architecture.
  - It defines parent/child trace governance.
  - It defines immutable-history requirements.
  - It distinguishes integrity from correctness.
  - It distinguishes provenance from evidential validity.
  - It defines explainability without requiring hidden chain-of-thought.
  - It preserves uncertainty explicitly.
  - It defines behavior for missing or unverifiable trace history.
  - It defines independent audit reconstruction.
  - It addresses human override provenance.
  - It defines candidate-learning governance without taking over 1C.5g migration.
  - It leaves UAT execution to 1C.5h.
  - It avoids premature implementation lock-in.
  - It labels proposed vocabulary explicitly.
  - It contains sufficient worked governance scenarios to exercise the architecture.
  - It does not claim runtime implementation or UAT has occurred.
  - It remains platform-neutral.
  - It does not silently redefine existing upstream records.
  - It does not convert governance labels into lifecycle statuses.
  - It preserves the governing invariant: UNCERTAINTY MUST SURVIVE THE DECISION.

17. PROHIBITED ARCHITECTURAL SHORTCUTS

Phase 1C.5f shall explicitly reject the following shortcuts:
  - “If it has a hash, it is trustworthy.”
  - “If the trace is complete, the reasoning was correct.”
  - “If the explanation sounds coherent, the decision was justified.”
  - “If the source is known, the evidence is valid.”
  - “If a parent run used evidence, the child run may automatically reuse it.”
  - “If a record is wrong, rewrite it.”
  - “If lineage is missing, reconstruct what probably happened.”
  - “If a candidate lesson worked once, promote it.”
  - “If a human overrides the AI, merge the result into AI provenance.”
  - “If a system status is needed, invent a new lifecycle state.”
  - “If implementation details are convenient, freeze them architecturally.”

18. FIRST-PASS GOVERNANCE TEST

Before Phase 1C.5f v0.1 is accepted for architecture review, reviewers shall be able to answer YES to the following:
  - Can a reasoning run be reconstructed without changing it?
  - Can every major conclusion be traced to its authoritative inputs?
  - Can uncertainty remain visible throughout the trace?
  - Can historical mistakes remain visible after correction?
  - Can a human override be distinguished from an AI decision?
  - Can intended execution be distinguished from actual execution?
  - Can a child run reuse history without silently treating it as current evidence?
  - Can missing trace information remain explicitly unknown?
  - Can an independent reviewer audit the run without relying solely on the original model?
  - Can candidate lessons remain quarantined until governed review?
  - Can downstream migration and UAT remain clearly outside 1C.5f ownership?
If any answer is NO, the architecture is incomplete.

19. WORKING DESIGN AXIOMS

Phase 1C.5f shall be developed around the following working axioms:
  - TRACEABILITY PRESERVES HISTORY; IT DOES NOT REWRITE IT.
  - INTEGRITY PRESERVES RECORDS; IT DOES NOT PROVE THEY ARE CORRECT.
  - PROVENANCE IDENTIFIES ORIGIN; IT DOES NOT PROVE VALIDITY.
  - EXPLAINABILITY EXPOSES THE ENGINEERING BASIS; IT DOES NOT CREATE CERTAINTY.
  - MISSING HISTORY REMAINS MISSING.
  - UNCERTAINTY MUST SURVIVE THE DECISION.

20. MANDATE STATUS

This document is a Phase 1C.5f Design Mandate, not the Phase 1C.5f architectural specification itself.
It governs the preparation and review of the initial Phase 1C.5f architecture.
It shall not be treated as having:
  - implemented production traceability;
  - selected production storage mechanisms;
  - defined final runtime schemas;
  - executed validation;
  - modified upstream frozen architecture;
  - or completed Phase 1C.5f.
Its purpose is to establish the architectural fence within which Phase 1C.5f may now be designed.
