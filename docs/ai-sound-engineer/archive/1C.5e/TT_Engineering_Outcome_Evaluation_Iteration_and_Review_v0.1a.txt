EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
Phase 1C.5e defines the reasoning architecture governing post-decision lifecycle activities in the Tone Translator AI Sound Engineer:
  1. OUTCOME PREDICTION CONSUMPTION: Ingesting the sealed outputs of Stage 11 (EngineeringDecisionRecord and PredictedOutcomeRecord).
  2. EXECUTION HANDOFF: Establishing the clean operational boundary to external DSP/runtime execution engines (Stage 12).
  3. ACTUAL OUTCOME EVIDENCE INGESTION: Sourcing, calibrating, and auditing post-intervention evidence across multiple modalities (Stage 13).
  4. OUTCOME EVALUATION: Conducting multi-dimensional delta comparisons against engineering requirements, preservation criteria, and predicted trade-offs.
  5. CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION & CONFOUNDER AUDITING: Determining whether observed acoustic changes were caused by the intervention or by confounding external factors.
  6. ITERATION & REVERSION DISCIPLINE: Governing parameter refinement, alternative selection, clean baseline rollback, and explicit return-upstream directives.
  7. RETROSPECTIVE ENGINEERING REVIEW: Conducting independent, non-punitive audits of diagnostic validity, intervention suitability, and architectural learning (Stage 14).

The absolute governing architectural distinction throughout this specification is:
  "A PREDICTED OUTCOME IS A TESTABLE EXPECTATION. IT IS NOT EVIDENCE THAT THE INTERVENTION WORKED."

Under this invariant:
  - Prediction is never evidence.
  - Absence of outcome evidence is never evidence of success; missing evidence survives as unresolved uncertainty.
  - An outcome cannot be judged successful merely because an action was executed.
  - A failed intervention does not automatically falsify a diagnosis, but a diagnosis cannot be sustained when post-intervention observations directly contradict its underlying causal mechanism.
  - Reversion to baseline is a first-class, respectable engineering outcome that prevents compounding damage.

SECTION 5 — CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION: 21 PRINCIPLES VERBATIM
================================================================================
Phase 1C.5e operates in strict subordination to the 21 Principles of Sound Engineering Reasoning, established in Phase 1C.5a v0.3c Section 4.1. In compliance with CM-01, they are recited verbatim without abbreviation, paraphrase, or weakening:

Principle 1 — Problem-First Primacy:
  Engineering reasoning begins with the problem, defect, or musical goal, never with an available tool, preset, or habituated processing chain.

Principle 2 — Evidence-Grounded Observation:
  No engineering claim may be asserted without traceable grounding in observed audio, acoustic measurement, explicit user direction, or established physical constraints.

Principle 3 — Causal Precedence:
  Interventions must target the verified or plausible causal mechanism producing an acoustic defect, not merely its superficial symptom.

Principle 4 — Multi-Hypothesis Competition:
  When observed symptoms are consistent with more than one physical or acoustic cause, multiple hypotheses must be formulated and systematically evaluated.

Principle 5 — Presumptive Abstention Under Uncertainty:
  When available evidence is insufficient to distinguish competing hypotheses, the system must declare uncertainty and avoid ungrounded irreversible actions.

Principle 6 — Requirement-Driven Intervention:
  Every intervention must satisfy an explicit, solution-neutral engineering requirement derived from verified diagnostic hypotheses.

Principle 7 — Alternative Diversity:
  The system must explore genuinely diverse intervention loci (source, transducer, pre-gain, gain, post-gain, acoustic space) before selecting an action.

Principle 8 — Minimum Necessary Intervention:
  The engineer selects the least disruptive intervention capable of achieving the engineering requirement, preserving original signal integrity.

Principle 9 — Unavoidable Trade-Off Explicit Recognition:
  Every acoustic intervention introduces trade-offs. Trade-offs must be explicitly predicted, quantified where possible, and evaluated against project tolerances.

Principle 10 — Preservation Imperative:
  Critical musical qualities (transient definition, low-end weight, articulation, harmonic character) must be explicitly monitored and protected against collateral degradation.

Principle 11 — Pre-Action Reversibility & Risk Assessment:
  The risk of sonic damage, cumulative degradation, and irreversibility must be evaluated before executing any intervention.

Principle 12 — Platform Independence:
  Engineering reasoning (problem formulation, causal diagnosis, requirement definition, intervention selection) is platform-neutral and occurs prior to translation into specific tool parameters.

Principle 13 — Explicit Prediction Requirement:
  Every engineering decision must be accompanied by explicit, falsifiable predictions of intended improvements, expected trade-offs, and non-exceedance bounds.

Principle 14 — Execution Boundary Rigor:
  The transition from engineering decision to physical or digital execution must cross a formalized operational boundary with verified parameter mappings.

Principle 15 — Outcome Evidence Firewall:
  A predicted outcome is a testable expectation, not evidence of success. Actual outcome evaluation requires independent, post-intervention empirical observation.

Principle 16 — Multi-Dimensional Outcome Evaluation:
  Outcomes must be evaluated across intended improvements, preservation requirements, predicted trade-offs, and unexpected collateral effects.

Principle 17 — Causal Attribution Integrity:
  Observed changes must be causally attributed to the specific intervention, systematically ruling out external confounders, gain discrepancies, and measurement artifacts.

Principle 18 — Non-Additive Reversion Discipline:
  When an intervention fails or introduces unacceptable side effects, the system must execute clean reversion to baseline rather than applying compounding corrective patches.

Principle 19 — Retrospective Review Honesty:
  Retrospective engineering review must evaluate the validity of upstream hypotheses, diagnostic reasoning, and predictive accuracy without defensive bias.

Principle 20 — Multi-Track Evaluative Coexistence:
  Objective acoustic measurements, engineering critical listening, reference comparisons, and subjective user preferences must coexist without artificial subordination.

Principle 21 — Non-Fabrication Invariant:
  Missing evidence remains missing. Unknowns remain unknown. The system must never invent measurements, simulate certainty, or fabricate evidence to justify decisions.

SECTION 7 — THE OUTCOME EVIDENCE FIREWALL
================================================================================
7.1 THE FIREWALL INVARIANT
  "PREDICTED OUTCOME IS AN EXPECTATION. POST-INTERVENTION OBSERVATION IS EVIDENCE."
  "UNDER NO CIRCUMSTANCE MAY AN EXPECTATION BE PROMOTED TO AN OBSERVATION WITHOUT EMPIRICAL DATA."

7.2 FIREWALL RULES
To prevent confirmation bias, illusory improvement, and automated self-deception:
  - Rule FW-01 (Strict Epistemic Isolation): The prediction engine and the outcome observation engine are decoupled. The outcome observation engine ingests raw post-intervention data without accessing prediction records until raw feature extraction is complete.
  - Rule FW-02 (Anti-Assumption Rule): The system must never assume an action succeeded simply because an execution script returned a success code (e.g. "DSP plug-in updated"). Execution success only proves that code ran; it provides zero evidence of acoustic efficacy.
  - Rule FW-03 (Anti-Conversion Rule): The system must never convert a predicted parameter into an observed metric (e.g. converting a predicted "-3 dB cut at 250 Hz" into an assertion that "250 Hz mud is reduced by 3 dB").
  - Rule FW-04 (Missing Evidence Preserved): If post-intervention audio, telemetry, or user listening feedback is missing or incomplete, the missing dimension must be explicitly recorded as `UNCERTAIN_UNMEASURED`. It must never be populated with predicted values.
  - Rule FW-05 (Temporal Precedence): Outcome evidence must have a timestamp strictly posterior to the execution timestamp recorded at Stage 12. Pre-intervention measurements cannot serve as outcome evidence.

SECTION 8 — ACTUAL OUTCOME EVIDENCE ARCHITECTURE (STAGE 13)
================================================================================
8.1 MULTI-MODAL EVIDENCE INTAKE
Actual outcome evidence enters Stage 13 through four distinct, non-fungible modalities:
  1. Acoustic Waveform & Spectral Telemetry:
     - Post-intervention audio captures, high-resolution FFT spectra, spectrograms, loudness metrics (LUFS momentary, short-term, integrated), crest factor, and true-peak levels.
  2. Measured Equipment/DSP Telemetry:
     - Realized parameter states from external engines (actual filter frequencies, Q factors, gain offsets, compression gain reduction meters, THD analyzers).
  3. Engineering Critical Listening Observations:
     - Structured auditory evaluations conducted by trained human sound engineers or qualified evaluators focusing on timbre, depth, mask dynamics, and musicality.
  4. User/Client Perceptual Feedback:
     - Unfiltered subjective reports, qualitative comments, and preference declarations from the end user or artist.

8.2 MODALITY-DEPENDENT & QUESTION-RELATIVE EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY (CM-05)
Evidence sufficiency cannot be evaluated as an abstract universal binary (PASS/FAIL) or a generic aggregate score. In sound engineering, sufficiency is strictly:
  - MODALITY-DEPENDENT: Certain questions require specific modalities. For example, verifying that total harmonic distortion did not exceed 0.5% requires calibrated spectral measurement; assessing whether vocal intimacy is musically preserved requires critical listening. Waveform telemetry cannot substitute for listening; user enthusiasm cannot substitute for crest factor verification.
  - QUESTION-RELATIVE: Evidence is evaluated relative to the specific hypothesis, requirement, or non-exceedance bound under test:
    * To answer "Did the 300 Hz acoustic resonance decrease?", an averaged 1/24-octave FFT delta is sufficient.
    * To answer "Did the intervention introduce audible phase smearing on the drum transient?", transient response analysis combined with critical listening across the initial 20 ms attack window is necessary.
    * If evidence is sufficient to evaluate spectral improvement but missing for transient preservation, the system records:
      `EVALUATION_STATUS: PARTIALLY_EVIDENCED (Improvement: SUFFICIENT; Preservation: INSUFFICIENT_UNCERTAIN)`.
      Uncertainty survives precisely where evidence is absent.

8.3 GAIN-MATCHING & CALIBRATION DISCIPLINE
A fundamental trap in sound engineering evaluation is the Fletcher-Munson loudness illusion (equal loudness contours): human listeners and uncalibrated metrics consistently prefer louder signals, misinterpreting level increases as clarity, warmth, or punch.
Stage 13 mandates:
  - Rigid Loudness Normalization: Pre- and post-intervention signals must be matched to within +/- 0.1 LUFS integrated across identical musical sections before comparative evaluation.
  - Level-Matched A/B Switching: Comparative critical listening must be conducted with instantaneous level-matched bypass switching to isolate spectral and dynamic changes from gain changes.
  - Measurement Calibration: Spectral measurements must account for source level fluctuations between takes.

SECTION 9 — OUTCOME COMPARISON & MULTI-DIMENSIONAL DELTA EVALUATION
================================================================================
9.1 THE 5-WAY COMPARISON MATRIX
Outcome evaluation compares the actual post-intervention evidence against five distinct benchmarks:
  1. Baseline State (Pre-Intervention Evidence): What was the state prior to the action? (Calculates the raw delta).
  2. Engineering Requirement (Phase 1C.5d Stage 08): What was the explicit physical/acoustic requirement that needed satisfaction?
  3. Predicted Outcome (Phase 1C.5d Stage 11): What exact changes were expected?
  4. Preservation Bounds: Did protected musical qualities stay within non-exceedance limits?
  5. Reference Target (if applicable): Did the change move the signal closer to the reference without copying non-contextual artifacts?

9.2 MULTI-DIMENSIONAL DELTA METRICS
Deltas are evaluated across independent acoustic dimensions:
  - Spectral Delta (Delta_S): Frequency-dependent energy redistribution (dBs across critical bands).
  - Dynamic Delta (Delta_D): Crest factor change, attack envelope sharpness, macro-dynamic range shift.
  - Phase & Time Delta (Delta_T): Group delay shifts, comb filtering notches, transient smearing.
  - Harmonic Delta (Delta_H): Total harmonic distortion, intermodulation distortion, saturation warmth vs. harshness.
  - Spatial Delta (Delta_W): Stereo correlation, width coefficient, mid/side energy balance, depth perception.

9.3 EVALUATING PREDICTED VS OBSERVED DELTAS
For each dimension, the system computes:
  - Accuracy of Improvement: Did the observed delta match the predicted direction and magnitude?
  - Falsification Check: Did any observed delta breach the explicit falsification criteria established in Stage 11?
  - Non-Exceedance Check: Did any secondary parameter breach the preservation bounds?

SECTION 10 — PRESERVATION REQUIREMENTS EVALUATION
================================================================================
10.1 THE PRESERVATION IMPERATIVE (PRINCIPLE 10)
An intervention that cures a defect while destroying essential musical qualities is a failed intervention. Outcome evaluation must explicitly audit all preservation requirements defined in `EngineeringRequirementRecord`.

10.2 AUDITED PRESERVATION QUALITIES
Stage 13 performs dedicated preservation audits across:
  1. Articulation & Intelligibility: Vocal consonant definition, guitar pick attack, bass note separation.
  2. Transient Punch & Definition: Snare crack, kick drum impact; verification that compressors or soft clippers have not blunted transient leading edges.
  3. Low-End Weight & Authority: Sub-bass fundamental integrity (40–90 Hz); ensuring high-pass filtering or multi-band control did not castrate foundation.
  4. Body, Warmth & Sustain: Core midrange resonance (200–800 Hz); ensuring notch filters or de-box cuts did not leave the instrument thin and hollow.
  5. High-Frequency Air & Smoothness: Top-end extension (>10 kHz); ensuring de-essing or low-pass cuts did not create a dull, suffocated presentation.
  6. Dynamic Expression: Micro-dynamics and natural musical breathing; ensuring over-compression did not create a static, lifeless wall of sound.
  7. Spatial Imaging & Phase Integrity: Stereo field width and mono compatibility; ensuring widener or chorus processing did not introduce destructive phase cancellation.
  8. Noise Floor & Artifact Profile: S/N ratio, absence of digital quantization noise, pump-and-breathe pumping artifacts, or aliasing.

10.3 PRESERVATION DISPOSITION
If any primary preservation requirement is breached beyond agreed tolerances:
  - The outcome CANNOT be classified as a clean success.
  - It is classified as `DISPOSITION_UNACCEPTABLE_TRADEOFF` or `DISPOSITION_REGRESSIVE_SONIC_DAMAGE`, triggering immediate consideration of clean baseline reversion.

SECTION 11 — TRADE-OFF, COLLATERAL DAMAGE & UNEXPECTED EFFECT EVALUATION
================================================================================
11.1 TRADE-OFF VERIFICATION
In Phase 1C.5d Stage 10, candidate interventions underwent trade-off deliberation. Post-intervention evaluation must determine:
  1. Did the predicted trade-offs actually materialize?
  2. Was the magnitude of the trade-off consistent with prediction?
  3. Did the trade-off remain acceptable within the track and project context?

11.2 UNEXPECTED COLLATERAL EFFECTS
Interventions frequently generate secondary consequences that were not foreseen during prediction:
  - Phase distortion from aggressive minimum-phase equalization;
  - Dynamic pumping or breathing in multiband processing;
  - Intermodulation distortion arising when saturating complex polyphonic material;
  - Masking alterations: unmasking previously buried harsh resonances when fixing low-mid mud.
When unexpected effects occur, they are documented in an `UnexpectedOutcomeRecord` with acoustic characterization, severity classification, and musical impact.

11.3 DE-DOGMATIZATION OF UNIVERSAL NUMERIC THRESHOLDS (CM-07)
A critical flaw in simplistic evaluation systems is imposing arbitrary universal numeric cutoffs across all audio engineering decisions (e.g. "any THD > 1% is always unacceptable", or "all changes must fall within +/- 0.5 dB").
In authentic sound engineering:
  - An acoustic guitar fingerpicking ballad may have zero tolerance for added THD (>0.1% unacceptable), whereas an aggressive industrial bass synth intentionally benefits from 15% third-harmonic saturation.
  - A transient notch filter on an isolated hi-hat track may tolerate a 1.5 dB phase shift, whereas the same shift on an overhead pair causes destructive stereo comb filtering.
Tolerances must therefore be:
  - DERIVED from the specific `EngineeringRequirementRecord` and track genre/context;
  - BOUNDED by explicit non-exceedance criteria established in Stage 11;
  - AUDITED against musical preservation rather than dogmatic universal constants.

SECTION 12 — CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION ARCHITECTURE & CONFOUNDER GOVERNANCE
================================================================================
12.1 THE CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION QUESTION (PRINCIPLE 17)
Did the executed intervention actually produce the observed change, or did the change result from confounding variables?
Causal attribution is never assumed. It requires rigorous confounder isolation.

12.2 COMMON SOUND ENGINEERING CONFOUNDERS
Stage 13 explicitly audits six primary confounder categories:
  1. Gain/Loudness Differences: An apparent improvement in low-end punch caused purely by an inadvertent 1.2 dB output level boost from an EQ plug-in.
  2. Performance/Take Variability: Differences in player dynamics, pick attack strength, or vocalist distance from the microphone between takes.
  3. Monitoring Chain & Room Acoustics: Inconsistencies introduced by non-linear monitor response, room mode interference, or monitoring volume shifts.
  4. Multi-Track Masking & Mix Context Interactions: An apparent clearing of guitar mud that actually resulted from the bass guitar fader being lowered or a mute being applied elsewhere in the mix.
  5. Measurement Window & FFT Windowing Artifacts: Mismatched time windows, window leakage, or analyzing non-representative sections of the performance.
  6. Concurrent Processing Changes: External plug-ins, bus compressors, or master limiters reacting differently to altered peak structures.

12.3 FAILED-REMEDY VS DIAGNOSTIC FALSIFICATION DISAMBIGUATION (CM-06)
A central mandate of this specification is preventing causal overreach when an intervention does not produce the desired result.
A failed intervention DOES NOT automatically prove that the diagnosis was wrong:
  - Scenario A (Failed Remedy / Ineffective Implementation): The diagnosis was "350 Hz buildup due to room mode reflection." The engineer applied a narrow notch filter at 350 Hz with Q=10. The resonance was actually broad (Q=1.5). The intervention failed because the remedy was parameterized sub-optimally, not because the diagnosis was incorrect. The diagnosis remains valid; the intervention requires refinement or replacement.
  - Scenario B (Causal Mechanism Falsification): The diagnosis was "harshness is caused by clipping distortion in the pre-amplifier." An intervention reduced pre-amplifier gain by 12 dB, completely eliminating clipping, yet the harshness remained identical in spectral profile and perceived ear fatigue. Here, the post-intervention evidence DIRECTLY CONTRADICTS the causal mechanism: the harshness was not caused by pre-amp clipping (it was likely microphone capsule resonance). The diagnosis is FALSIFIED, requiring a formal return upstream to Stage 06/07 for re-diagnosis.

The system must evaluate whether the observed result:
  (1) Supports both diagnosis and intervention;
  (2) Supports diagnosis but indicates ineffective/sub-optimal intervention;
  (3) Contradicts and falsifies the diagnostic mechanism; or
  (4) Is confounded and unattributable due to external variables.

SECTION 13 — OUTCOME DISPOSITION TAXONOMY
================================================================================
Phase 1C.5e establishes a formal, non-binary, 9-member outcome disposition taxonomy:

1. `DISPOSITION_SUCCESS_INTENDED_ACHIEVED`:
   - Intended improvements fully realized within predicted bounds.
   - All preservation requirements intact.
   - Predicted trade-offs within acceptable limits; zero unexpected collateral damage.
   - Causal attribution confident.
   - Routing: Complete and proceed to Stage 14 Engineering Review.

2. `DISPOSITION_SUCCESS_ACCEPTABLE_TRADEOFF`:
   - Intended improvement substantially achieved.
   - Secondary trade-offs occurred as predicted and remain strictly acceptable within project context.
   - Preservation requirements intact.
   - Routing: Complete and proceed to Stage 14.

3. `DISPOSITION_PARTIAL_IMPROVEMENT_BOUNDED`:
   - Acoustic improvement moved in the intended direction but fell short of requirement target.
   - Diagnostic mechanism remains supported by observed delta.
   - Preservation requirements intact.
   - Routing: Authorize bounded refinement (tuning parameters within the same intervention locus).

4. `DISPOSITION_INEFFECTIVE_DIAGNOSIS_SUPPORTED`:
   - Intervention produced negligible or insufficient acoustic change.
   - Diagnostic mechanism remains plausible, but the chosen intervention tool/locus was incapable of affecting it.
   - Routing: Abandon current intervention, cleanly revert, and select alternative intervention candidate.

5. `DISPOSITION_UNACCEPTABLE_TRADEOFF`:
   - Intended improvement may have occurred, but collateral trade-offs (e.g. loss of punch, severe phase shift) exceeded non-exceedance bounds.
   - Routing: Mandatory clean reversion to baseline; evaluate alternative intervention locus.

6. `DISPOSITION_REGRESSIVE_SONIC_DAMAGE`:
   - The intervention degraded the audio signal, introducing new defects (distortion, comb filtering, pumping) or worsening the original problem.
   - Routing: Immediate mandatory clean reversion to baseline; prohibit further additive tweaking.

7. `DISPOSITION_CONTRADICTORY_DIAGNOSIS_FALSIFIED`:
   - Post-intervention evidence directly contradicts the underlying causal hypothesis (e.g. removing hypothesized cause did not alter symptom).
   - Routing: Clean reversion to baseline; issue `ReturnUpstreamDirectiveRecord` to Stage 06/07 for re-diagnosis.

8. `DISPOSITION_CONFOUNDED_UNATTRIBUTABLE`:
   - Acoustic changes cannot be causally attributed to the intervention due to uncontrolled external variables (gain shifts, take changes, mix interactions).
   - Routing: Clean reversion or hold; require controlled re-measurement and confounder isolation.

9. `DISPOSITION_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`:
   - Ingested evidence lacks required modalities or duration to evaluate the question.
   - Uncertainty survives; system refuses to declare success or failure.
   - Routing: Hold in Stage 13; request necessary evidence or human critical listening.

SECTION 14 — ITERATION ARCHITECTURE & PATHWAY GOVERNANCE
================================================================================
14.1 THE EIGHT ITERATION PATHWAYS
Phase 1C.5e establishes eight distinct, formally governed iteration pathways:

  - PATHWAY A (Accept & Complete):
    * Trigger: `DISPOSITION_SUCCESS_INTENDED_ACHIEVED` or `DISPOSITION_SUCCESS_ACCEPTABLE_TRADEOFF`.
    * Action: Finalize decision, seal `ActualOutcomeEvidenceRecord`, advance to Stage 14 Engineering Review.

  - PATHWAY B (Bounded Parameter Refinement):
    * Trigger: `DISPOSITION_PARTIAL_IMPROVEMENT_BOUNDED`.
    * Condition: Diagnostic mechanism intact, preservation requirements satisfied, improvement moving along expected vector.
    * Action: Adjust parameter values (e.g. adjust filter gain from -2 dB to -3.5 dB) within the same intervention locus.

  - PATHWAY C (Alternative Intervention Selection):
    * Trigger: `DISPOSITION_INEFFECTIVE_DIAGNOSIS_SUPPORTED` or `DISPOSITION_UNACCEPTABLE_TRADEOFF` where locus was flawed.
    * Condition: Diagnostic mechanism remains supported, but current intervention locus proved ineffective or collateral damage too high.
    * Action: Cleanly revert current intervention to baseline; select next ranked candidate from `CandidateInterventionRecord`.

  - PATHWAY D (Clean Baseline Reversion):
    * Trigger: `DISPOSITION_REGRESSIVE_SONIC_DAMAGE` or unrecoverable trade-off violation.
    * Action: Immediately restore baseline state. Halt automated processing; require engineering deliberation.

  - PATHWAY E (Return Upstream to Re-Diagnosis):
    * Trigger: `DISPOSITION_CONTRADICTORY_DIAGNOSIS_FALSIFIED`.
    * Action: Cleanly revert to baseline; issue `ReturnUpstreamDirectiveRecord` to Phase 1C.5c (Stage 06/07) to invalidate hypothesis and re-diagnose.

  - PATHWAY F (Return Upstream to Requirement Reformulation):
    * Trigger: Outcome evaluation proves that original requirement was musically unachievable, physically contradictory, or omitted vital preservation goals.
    * Action: Cleanly revert to baseline; issue directive to Phase 1C.5d (Stage 08) to reformulate requirements.

  - PATHWAY G (Return Upstream to Evidence Re-Acquisition):
    * Trigger: `DISPOSITION_CONFOUNDED_UNATTRIBUTABLE` or `DISPOSITION_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.
    * Action: Hold processing; issue directive to Phase 1C.5b (Stage 02/03) to obtain calibrated, isolated audio captures.

  - PATHWAY H (Human Engineering Escalation):
    * Trigger: Persistent divergence between objective metrics and critical listening, or conflicting artistic preferences.
    * Action: Present multi-track evaluation dossier to senior sound engineer / user for authoritative resolution.

14.2 ITERATION AUTHORIZATION & TRAJECTORY GOVERNANCE (CM-08)
Iteration is not an automatic license to endlessly tweak parameters.
An iteration step is AUTHORIZED ONLY IF ALL of the following conditions are met:
  1. Diagnostic Mechanism Validated: The previous delta confirmed that the acoustic symptom responds to the targeted causal parameter.
  2. Trajectory Convergent: The improvement is moving monotonically toward the requirement target without deteriorating preservation metrics.
  3. Preservation Bounds Unbreached: Zero non-exceedance bounds have been violated.
  4. Stopping Criteria Pre-Defined: Clear stopping thresholds are established prior to the iteration step.

14.3 ANTI-THRASHING & TERMINATION SAFEGUARDS (CM-07)
Rather than imposing rigid universal iteration count numbers (e.g. arbitrarily mandating "exactly 3 iterations"), anti-thrashing is governed by dynamic convergence:
  - Divergence Rule: If an iteration step produces a smaller delta than the preceding step while trade-offs increase (diminishing returns), iteration terminates immediately.
  - Oscillation Rule: If parameter values flip between opposing directions (e.g. boosting then cutting the same frequency band), thrashing is declared; iteration halts and clean reversion is triggered.
  - Contextual Budget: Iteration cycles are bound by track-specific complexity budgets; once exhausted, the system halts and escalates to Pathway D or H.

SECTION 15 — REFINEMENT VS ALTERNATIVE VS RE-DIAGNOSIS BOUNDARY MATRIX
================================================================================
A core source of architectural failure in sound engineering systems is confusing:
  - When to slightly adjust a parameter (Refinement);
  - When to choose a different tool/locus (Alternative Intervention); and
  - When to admit the underlying causal theory was wrong (Re-Diagnosis).

The boundary matrix establishes strict decision rules:

+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Acoustic & Causal State    | REFINEMENT (Pathway B)      | ALTERNATIVE (Pathway C)     | RE-DIAGNOSIS (Pathway E)    |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Diagnostic Mechanism       | Confirmed & Active          | Confirmed & Active          | Falsified or Contradicted   |
| Status                     | (acoustic response aligns)  | (cause is real, tool wrong) | (cause was not as claimed)  |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Observed Acoustic Delta    | In intended direction, but  | Negligible change OR        | No change OR symptom moved  |
|                            | insufficient magnitude      | unresolvable side effect    | opposite to physical theory |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Intervention Locus         | Correct locus               | Sub-optimal locus; need     | Irrelevant locus; need      |
|                            | (e.g. pre-gain dynamic cut) | alternative locus           | new diagnostic graph        |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Action Boundary            | Parameter tweak on current  | Clean rollback of current;  | Clean rollback of current;  |
|                            | tool (+/- dB, Hz, ms)       | activate candidate from 1C5d| return to Stage 06/07       |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Allowed Cumulative Actions | Max bounded cycle           | Fresh single intervention   | Full upstream cycle         |
+----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+-----------------------------+

CRITICAL MANDATE:
Under no circumstances may a system disguise a failed diagnosis as "parameter refinement." If reducing a hypothesized cause fails to alter the defect, the system is FORBIDDEN from continuing to tweak that parameter; it must classify the state as diagnostic falsification.

SECTION 16 — REVERSION ARCHITECTURE & NON-ADDITIVE DISCIPLINE
================================================================================
16.1 REVERSION AS A FIRST-CLASS ENGINEERING OUTCOME (PRINCIPLE 18)
In amateur sound engineering, an ineffective action is frequently "corrected" by adding another filter, compressor, or exciter on top of the failed chain. This creates catastrophic additive creeping, phase smearing, headroom loss, and irrecoverable signal degradation.
In professional sound engineering:
  "REVERSION TO BASELINE IS A PRIMARY, RESPECTABLE, AND SAFEGUARDING ENGINEERING ACTION."

16.2 CLEAN BASELINE RESTORATION (CM-09)
When an intervention fails, proves regressive, or violates preservation bounds:
  - Clean Rollback: The signal chain is restored completely to the pre-intervention baseline snapshot.
  - Zero Residual Footprint: All parameter modifications, temporary plug-ins, side-chain routings, or gain trims introduced by the failed intervention are eradicated.
  - Non-Compounding Guarantee: The baseline for any subsequent intervention is the original clean baseline, NEVER the degraded state of the failed attempt.

16.3 PREVENTING INTERVENTION STACKING
The system strictly enforces the Anti-Stacking Rule:
  - No corrective patch may be applied to compensate for collateral damage caused by an active intervention.
  - If Intervention X creates unwanted side effect Y, the engineer CANNOT apply Intervention Z to cure side effect Y while leaving X active.
  - The correct engineering response is: REVERT X, and select an alternative intervention that achieves the goal without producing side effect Y.

SECTION 17 — RETURN-UPSTREAM DIRECTIVES & PARENT-CHILD RUN TRACEABILITY
================================================================================
17.1 RETURN-UPSTREAM DIRECTIVES
When an outcome demonstrates that upstream reasoning was deficient, Phase 1C.5e issues a formal `ReturnUpstreamDirectiveRecord`.
Each directive contains:
  - `directive_id`: Unique cryptographic identifier.
  - `target_upstream_stage`: Exact upstream destination (Stage 02/03 Evidence, Stage 06/07 Diagnosis, or Stage 08 Requirements).
  - `triggering_disposition`: Formal disposition from Section 13.
  - `justification_evidence`: Empirical data from Stage 13 disproving the upstream assumption.
  - `quarantined_elements`: Specific hypotheses, models, or intervention loci barred from re-use without new evidence.

17.2 PARENT-CHILD RUN LINEAGE
To prevent circular reasoning and preserve historical audit trails:
  - The failed run is sealed with its complete history intact.
  - A new child run is instantiated, referencing the parent `run_id`.
  - The child run inherits all verified baseline evidence but carries forward the explicit quarantine and falsification directives.
  - This ensures complete, immutable traceability across iterations without rewriting history.

SECTION 18 — RETROSPECTIVE ENGINEERING REVIEW ARCHITECTURE (STAGE 14)
================================================================================
18.1 PURPOSE OF RETROSPECTIVE REVIEW (PRINCIPLE 19)
Stage 14 provides an independent, post-hoc engineering review of the complete decision-and-outcome lifecycle.
The purpose of Stage 14 is NOT to justify or rationalize earlier choices, but to evaluate the objective quality of reasoning, identify systemic predictive or diagnostic errors, and extract generalizable engineering knowledge.

18.2 THE SIX REVIEW DIMENSIONS
Stage 14 evaluates the lifecycle across six rigorous dimensions:
  1. Diagnostic Validity Audit: Did the post-intervention evidence validate or falsify the causal mechanism identified in Stage 06/07?
  2. Intervention Suitability Audit: Was the chosen intervention locus the minimum necessary and most parsimonious action capable of achieving the goal?
  3. Predictive Accuracy Audit: How accurately did the Stage 11 predictions match empirical deltas across improvements, trade-offs, and bounds?
  4. Preservation Integrity Audit: Were critical musical qualities safeguarded, or were unpredicted collateral damages incurred?
  5. Trade-Off Proportionality Audit: Was the musical cost of the trade-off proportionate to the benefit gained?
  6. Generalizable Engineering Lessons: What transferable knowledge (acoustic behavior, instrument interactions, gear quirks) was revealed by this outcome?

18.3 INDEPENDENCE & ANTI-DEFENSIVE AUDITING
Stage 14 functions as an independent reviewer:
  - Non-Punitive Posture: Identifying that an intervention failed or a diagnosis was wrong is celebrated as an essential error-correction event, not penalized.
  - Zero Defensive Rationalization: The system is forbidden from asserting that "the audio would have sounded worse without the intervention" or shifting the definition of success post-hoc.
  - Candidate Lesson Quarantine: Any architectural lesson proposed for system-wide knowledge base updates is placed in quarantine until reviewed and ratified by sound engineering governance.

SECTION 20 — UNCERTAINTY HANDLING ACROSS EVALUATION & REVIEW
================================================================================
20.1 SURVIVAL OF UNCERTAINTY (PRINCIPLES 5 & 21)
In sound engineering evaluation, uncertainty is an inescapable physical reality. Where evidence is incomplete, ambiguous, or confounded, uncertainty MUST survive.
The system is strictly forbidden from:
  - Forcing an uncertain result into a binary PASS or FAIL;
  - Substituting missing post-intervention measurements with predicted numbers;
  - Assuming an intervention succeeded because no user complaints were received; or
  - Fabricating causal confidence when multiple confounders remain unisolated.

20.2 EXPLICIT UNCERTAINTY TAXONOMY IN STAGE 13 & 14
Uncertainty is categorized and explicitly tracked:
  - `UNCERTAINTY_MEASUREMENT`: Due to FFT windowing, background noise, or microphone placement variations.
  - `UNCERTAINTY_CONFOUNDING`: Due to concurrent mix processing or performance take differences.
  - `UNCERTAINTY_PERCEPTUAL`: Due to subtle acoustic changes that fall within psychoacoustic jnd (just-noticeable difference) thresholds.
  - `UNCERTAINTY_CAUSAL_DIRECTION`: When an observed improvement cannot be definitively linked to the intervention vs. external signal dynamics.
Each uncertain dimension is preserved with explicit bounds and carried forward into `EngineeringReviewRecord`.


================================================================================
SECTION 21 — HUMAN / USER EVALUATION & MULTI-TRACK COEXISTENCE
================================================================================
21.1 THE FOUR EVALUATIVE TRACKS (PRINCIPLE 20)
Sound engineering exists at the intersection of acoustic physics and human artistic emotion.
Stage 13 establishes four distinct evaluative tracks that must coexist without artificial subordination:
  1. TRACK 1: Objective Acoustic & DSP Measurements (FFT curves, LUFS, crest factor, THD).
  2. TRACK 2: Professional Critical Listening (trained auditory evaluation of timbre, balance, depth, mask dynamics).
  3. TRACK 3: Reference Track Comparison (contextual similarity to target commercial production).
  4. TRACK 4: User/Artist Subjective Experience (emotional response, perceived vibe, artistic intent).

21.2 HANDLING DIVERGENCE BETWEEN TRACKS
Divergence between tracks is natural and informative:
  - Divergence Type A (Measurement Perfect, Listening Rejects): An EQ curve perfectly flattens a resonant peak on an acoustic guitar, but critical listening reveals the instrument now sounds lifeless, plastic, and unmusical. The listening track takes precedence; the intervention must be refined or reverted.
  - Divergence Type B (Measurement Shows Trade-Off, Artist Enthusiastic): Adding heavy tube saturation increases THD to 8% and squashes dynamic range by 3 dB, but the artist loves the "vintage grit and swagger." The system must NOT override the artist by declaring technical failure; it documents the trade-off and registers user-authorized trade-off acceptance.
  - Divergence Type C (User Complains, Technical Metrics Unchanged): The user reports that the vocal sounds "distant" after an intervention, even though spectral analysis shows identical mid frequencies. This indicates a spatial or masking artifact that raw spectral metrics failed to capture. The system investigates depth and reverb reflections.

21.3 PROHIBITION OF GASLIGHTING & FORCED CONFORMITY
The system is strictly forbidden from:
  - Dismissing user perceptual feedback by asserting that "the numbers prove the track is objectively better";
  - Forcing the user to agree with algorithmic evaluations; or
  - Smoothing over genuine disagreements between critical listening and objective telemetry.
Divergences are preserved as explicit tension records in `OutcomeComparisonRecord`.

SECTION 22 — FAILURE MODES & PROHIBITED ANTI-PATTERNS
================================================================================
Phase 1C.5e explicitly catalogs and outlaws seven fatal architectural anti-patterns:

1. ANTI-PATTERN AP-01 (Confirmation Bias & Self-Fulfilling Success):
   - Prohibited Behavior: Assuming that because an intervention was chosen and executed, it must have achieved its goal.
   - Mandated Defense: Strict enforcement of the Outcome Evidence Firewall. Actual evidence must independently verify success.

2. ANTI-PATTERN AP-02 (Additive Stacking & Corrective Creep):
   - Prohibited Behavior: Applying additional corrective plug-ins or EQ filters on top of a failed intervention to fix the side effects created by that intervention.
   - Mandated Defense: Enforced clean reversion to baseline. Prohibit all compounding patches.

3. ANTI-PATTERN AP-03 (Goalpost Shifting & Defensive Rationalization):
   - Prohibited Behavior: Redefining the criteria of success after the action was executed (e.g. "We didn't fix the mud, but we made the guitar brighter, so it's a win!").
   - Mandated Defense: Requirements and non-exceedance bounds are locked in `EngineeringRequirementRecord` and `PredictedOutcomeRecord` prior to execution. Success is judged strictly against pre-locked criteria.

4. ANTI-PATTERN AP-04 (Infinite Iteration & Blind Parameter Hunting):
   - Prohibited Behavior: Endlessly tweaking filter frequencies or compressor thresholds without a convergent trajectory.
   - Mandated Defense: Strict anti-thrashing rules, oscillation detection, and mandatory termination to clean reversion.

5. ANTI-PATTERN AP-05 (Sacrificing Musicality for Telemetry):
   - Prohibited Behavior: Chasing flat frequency lines or arbitrary metric targets at the expense of instrument tone, punch, and soul.
   - Mandated Defense: Mandatory preservation audits across musical qualities; critical listening coexists with telemetry.

6. ANTI-PATTERN AP-06 (Causal Overreach & Premature Falsification):
   - Prohibited Behavior: Either declaring a diagnosis fully proven when external confounders were present, or throwing out a correct diagnosis simply because a single sub-optimal filter value was tried.
   - Mandated Defense: Rigorous confounder isolation; clear distinction between failed remedy parameterization and genuine diagnostic mechanism contradiction.

7. ANTI-PATTERN AP-07 (Blaming the User for System Failure):
   - Prohibited Behavior: Rationalizing bad acoustic outcomes by blaming the user's listening environment, monitoring chain, or musical tastes.
   - Mandated Defense: Honest logging of outcome divergence; non-defensive logging in `EngineeringReviewRecord`.
