SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE & SCOPE
Phase 1C.4a establishes the authoritative, platform-independent Sound Engineering
Knowledge Architecture for the future Tone Translator (TT) AI Sound Engineer.

This phase does NOT populate the Sound Engineer's knowledge base.
It does NOT ingest or harvest external text, websites, papers, or textbooks.
It does NOT modify production prompts, TypeScript application code, Firestore schemas,
or execution contracts.
It does NOT implement runtime DSP or retrieval (RAG) services.

Phase 1C.4a defines the structural data contracts, entity relationships, claim
granularity, provenance lineage, epistemic authority models, conflict resolution
frameworks, numerical value typing, lifecycle state machines, governance gates, and
retrieval-readiness metadata required so that when the library is later populated
(Phase 1C.4b onward), knowledge operates strictly to inform first-principles
reasoning without substituting for it.

1.2 THE CARDINAL ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE
    KNOWLEDGE INFORMS REASONING.
    KNOWLEDGE DOES NOT REPLACE REASONING.

In audio engineering, whenever an architectural design allows knowledge to encode:
    "Symptom X -> Mandatory Intervention Y"
the system ceases to be an engineer and degenerates into a rigid recipe engine.
The architecture designed herein guarantees that the Sound Engineering Library
contains principles, physical models, causal mechanisms, evidence requirements,
boundary conditions, and known trade-offs, while leaving hypothesis generation,
diagnostic discrimination, intervention design, and outcome evaluation strictly to
the cognitive reasoning process of the Sound Engineer.

1.3 STRUCTURAL ADVANCEMENT OVER PHASE 1C.3c
Phase 1C.3c proposed an initial single-entity `NormalizedKnowledgeUnit` schema as a
pedagogical blueprint. Phase 1C.4a critically audits and refactors that concept into
a relational multi-entity architectural model. A single monolithic object cannot
faithfully represent:
  - Multi-source provenance with diverging evidence tiers supporting one claim.
  - Competing, conflicting, or unresolved claims across different authorities.
  - The distinction between invariant physical laws and context-dependent tendencies.
  - The separation between illustrative numerical examples and normative constraints.
  - Fine-grained retrieval where symptoms, causal models, and interventions must be
    traversed dynamically without loading fixed recipes.

The resulting Phase 1C.4a architecture introduces atomic Knowledge Claims, Multi-Source
Attributions, Explicit Boundary & Conditionality Contexts, Reconciled Conflict
Records, and a Strict Five-State Governance Lifecycle with an isolated Experiential
Quarantine Store.

SECTION 2 — ARCHITECTURAL MISSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 THE MISSION
To construct a formal, platform-independent, verifiable knowledge representation
and lifecycle system that equips the future TT AI Sound Engineer with trustworthy,
scientifically grounded electro-acoustic and psychoacoustic understanding, while
maintaining strict epistemic humility, provenance transparency, and immunity to
dogmatic recipe generation or platform contamination.

2.2 PRIMARY ARCHITECTURAL OBJECTIVES
  1. Epistemic Grounding: Root all authoritative assertions in established physical
     laws, peer-reviewed acoustics, manufacturer circuit documentation, and systematic
     recording engineering literature.
  2. Recipe Immunity: Structurally prevent the storage or retrieval of static
     rule-pairs ("if thrash metal, then boost Tube Screamer with drive=0, tone=7").
  3. Strict Provenance: Ensure every atomic claim traces directly to its source,
     evidence tier, extraction method, reviewer signature, and confidence bounds.
  4. Platform Decoupling: Ensure zero leakage of commercial brand names, proprietary
     GUIDs, XML structures, or DAW/hardware-specific artifacts into the general
     sound engineering library.
  5. Asymmetric Authority: Rank claims based on domain-appropriate authority rather
     than a naive flat authority tier (e.g., physics textbooks govern wave mechanics;
     manufacturer schematics govern component nonlinearities; engineering handbooks
     govern studio workflow conventions).
  6. Cognitive Enablement: Package knowledge so that retrieval provides causal
     mechanisms, discriminating observations, and trade-off vectors to the
     Phase 1B `EngineeringDecisionRecord` pipeline.

SECTION 3 — GOVERNING PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The architecture enforces twelve non-negotiable design principles:

Principle 1: Knowledge First — Rules Only Where Rules Genuinely Exist
Physics, mathematics, and schema invariants have rules (e.g., conservation of energy,
0 dBFS digital ceiling, Nyquist-Shannon sampling limit, non-invertible comb filter
nulls). Engineering interventions do not have universal rules; they have principles,
trade-offs, and contextual tendencies. The architecture explicitly rejects the
universalization of engineering practices.

Principle 2: Principles, Not Answers
Knowledge units represent the *why* and the *how*, never the pre-determined outcome.
A unit explains why reducing low frequencies before clipping reduces intermodulation
distortion; it never mandates an equalizer setting.

Principle 3: Knowledge Is Not Reasoning
The library is a cognitive repository of models and facts. The AI Sound Engineer
performs abductive, deductive, and inductive reasoning using those models. Storing
completed diagnostic conclusions inside the knowledge base is strictly prohibited.

Principle 4: Platform Independence
All knowledge entities are expressed in SI physical units (Hz, ms, dB, V, ohms, cm,
degrees) and platform-independent semantic descriptors. Target platforms (such as
IK Multimedia AmpliTube 5, Line 6 Helix, Neural DSP, or analog hardware) exist
solely downstream in the Platform Translation Layer.

Principle 5: Provenance Is Mandatory & Atomic
A knowledge assertion without traceable source attribution, extraction metadata,
and reviewer sign-off is treated as an unverified speculation and cannot enter the
active library. Provenance attaches to individual atomic claims, not to entire
composite documents.

Principle 6: Claim Granularity & Atomicity
Complex documents are decomposed into atomic, independently verifiable claims.
This enables fine-grained versioning, multi-source consensus tracking, and modular
retrieval without dragging in irrelevant assumptions.

Principle 7: Conditional Knowledge & Boundary Limits
Every engineering claim is valid only within specific boundary conditions (e.g.,
small-signal linear operation, near-field acoustic radiation, high-gain clipping).
Claims must explicitly state their operational envelopes, confounders, and failure modes.

Principle 8: Preservation of Credible Conflict
When two authoritative sources disagree (e.g., differing views on optimal speaker
break-in mechanics or psychoacoustic masking thresholds), the architecture preserves
both claims with their respective evidence tiers and contextual boundaries. The
system never silently averages or arbitrarily discards conflicting evidence.

Principle 9: Current TT Is a Source, Not an Authority
Knowledge extracted from Current TT's prompts or code (e.g., microphone positioning
reasoning or OverScream tightening) is treated as an unverified source claim. It
must undergo identical extraction, classification, physical verification, and human
expert review before entering the authoritative library.

Principle 10: Rigorous Typing of Numerical Values
Numbers in the library must be explicitly categorized. A physical constant (speed of
sound = 343 m/s) is treated fundamentally differently from an illustrative frequency
(e.g., "around 800 Hz") or a measured threshold. Illustrative numbers must never
become normative boundaries.

Principle 11: Human Expert Governance
Machine learning models and autonomous agents may propose extracted claims or
candidate lessons, but only human domain experts can promote claims to the
authoritative `ACTIVE` state.

Principle 12: Experiential Quarantine
Insights gained from future runtime closed-loop iterations or user feedback are
strictly quarantined as `CANDIDATE_LESSONS`. They cannot alter global knowledge
without formal review and regression testing.

SECTION 5 — KNOWLEDGE CLASSIFICATION TAXONOMY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every piece of information entering the Knowledge Library must be categorized into
one of eight mutually exclusive epistemological classes:

Class 1: PHYSICAL_LAW
  - Invariant mathematical and physical truths governing the universe.
  - Examples: Wave equation, conservation of energy, Ohm's law, Faraday's law of
    induction, Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem, inverse square law.
  - Mutability: Immutable. Overrides all other classes without exception.

Class 2: ESTABLISHED_ENGINEERING_PRINCIPLE
  - Well-grounded, peer-reviewed engineering theories supported by robust empirical
    validation and mathematical modeling.
  - Examples: Velocity-gradient acoustic proximity effect, tube grid-clipping transfer
    characteristics, loudspeaker voice-coil reactive impedance rise, comb filtering
    null spacing f = (2n+1)/(2*tau).
  - Mutability: Highly stable; revised only upon rigorous scientific breakthrough.

Class 3: EMPIRICAL_RELATIONSHIP
  - Systematically measured correlations between physical variables observed across
    controlled laboratory experiments.
  - Examples: Fletcher-Munson / ISO 226 equal-loudness contours, paper cone breakup
    frequency ranges for 12-inch guitar drivers, Bark critical masking bandwidths.
  - Mutability: Stable; subject to measurement refinement and boundary qualification.

Class 4: ENGINEERING_TENDENCY
  - Observed behavioral tendency in complex electro-acoustic systems that holds true
    across standard operating envelopes but has known exceptions.
  - Examples: Pre-distortion high-pass filtering tends to increase perceived transient
    attack tightness; open-back cabinets tend to produce wider spatial dispersion
    with diminished sub-120 Hz fundamental weight.
  - Mutability: Moderate; requires explicit boundary conditions and confounder tags.

Class 5: CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_PRACTICE
  - Systematic engineering methodologies developed by professional practitioners for
    specific acoustic, musical, or production contexts.
  - Examples: High-passing electric rhythm guitars at 100 Hz in a dense modern metal
    mix to clear room for bass guitar fundamental; placing a ribbon mic 15 cm off-grill
    at 45 degrees to tame aggressive modern ceramic speaker fizz.
  - Mutability: Contextual; must never be applied outside its qualified context.

Class 6: PROFESSIONAL_CONVENTION
  - Industry agreements, calibration standards, and workflow heuristics that ensure
    interoperability and professional consistency.
  - Examples: 0 VU calibration to +4 dBu (-18 dBFS digital nominal operating level);
    standard guitar cabinet impedance matching (4, 8, 16 ohms); EBU R128 loudness.
  - Mutability: Governed by standards organizations (AES, EBU, IEC, IEEE).

Class 7: HYPOTHETICAL_OR_COMPETING_MODEL
  - Plausible engineering explanations where complete consensus has not been achieved
    or where multiple valid circuit/acoustic models coexist.
  - Examples: Competing models of vacuum tube cathode bypass capacitor phase smearing;
    differing theories on the subjective perception of inter-sample peaks.
  - Mutability: Dynamic; retains competing status until resolved.

Class 8: ILLUSTRATIVE_EXAMPLE
  - Concrete historical cases, reference settings, or laboratory demonstrations used
    solely to illuminate an underlying principle.
  - Examples: "In the 1970s, Malcolm Young's rhythm tone achieved extreme transient
    clarity using high-headroom Marshall Super Bass heads with minimal preamp clipping."
  - Mutability: Informational only. MUST NEVER BE TREATED AS A NORMATIVE TARGET.

SECTION 6 — PROPOSED KNOWLEDGE ENTITY MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 RELATIONAL ARCHITECTURE (AUDITING PHASE 1C.3c)
Phase 1C.3c proposed a monolithic `NormalizedKnowledgeUnit` where principle, causal
model, evidence indicators, hypotheses, interventions, trade-offs, and provenance
were stored in a single JSON document.

Architectural Audit Finding:
The monolithic approach suffers from four fatal structural defects:
  1. Provenance Conflation: A single document cannot capture when the *principle* is
     derived from a Tier 1 textbook, the *causal model* is verified by a Tier 2 paper,
     and the *trade-off* is documented in a Tier 3 studio manual.
  2. Recipe Vulnerability: Packaging evidence indicators, competing hypotheses, and
     specific interventions into one tight object creates a gravity well that pulls
     the system back into "symptom-to-fix" lookup tables.
  3. Conflict Paralysis: When two authorities disagree on a specific trade-off or
     frequency range, the entire monolithic unit must either split or choose a side.
  4. Redundant Proliferation: Fundamental principles (e.g., Comb Filtering) would need
     to be duplicated across dozens of units dealing with room mics, dual cab mics,
     flanging, and stereo widening.

Refactored Architecture:
Phase 1C.4a decomposes the knowledge library into five normalized, relational entities:
  1. `KnowledgeClaim` (Atomic assertions of fact, principle, or causal mechanism)
  2. `CausalModel` (Dynamic input -> mediating mechanism -> acoustic consequence networks)
  3. `OperationalBoundary` (Explicit validity envelopes, confounders, and failure limits)
  4. `SourceAttribution` (Atomic citation, quotation, evidence tier, and extraction lineage)
  5. `ConflictRecord` (Explicit tracking of competing, divergent, or reconciled claims)

SECTION 7 — KNOWLEDGEUNIT / CLAIM GRANULARITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 THE ATOMICITY MANDATE
A common failure in knowledge engineering is "chunky granularity," where an entire
50-page chapter on "Guitar Recording" is ingested as a single knowledge unit.
This leads to:
  - Inability to cite specific provenance for specific numbers.
  - Inability to invalidate one outdated claim without discarding fifty correct ones.
  - Contextual bleeding during retrieval.

The TT Sound Engineering Library mandates Atomic Claim Granularity:
An atomic claim represents a single, irreducible electro-acoustic assertion that can
be independently verified, assigned a specific evidence tier, bounded by operational
conditions, and linked to exact source citations.

7.2 GRANULARITY DECOMPOSITION EXAMPLE
Source Text:
"A cardioid dynamic microphone like the SM57 placed one inch from a guitar speaker grill
exaggerates frequencies below 200 Hz due to acoustic proximity effect, while its
heavy moving-coil diaphragm rolls off above 15 kHz and smooths high-frequency pick transients."

Decomposition into Atomic Knowledge Entities:
  - Claim 1: "Pressure-gradient transducers (cardioid/figure-8) exhibit acoustic
    proximity effect, boosting low frequencies as capsule-to-source distance decreases
    below the acoustic near-field threshold (r < lambda / 2*pi)."
    [Class: PHYSICAL_LAW / ESTABLISHED_PRINCIPLE]
  - Claim 2: "Moving-coil dynamic microphones exhibit higher diaphragm/coil mass than
    thin ribbon or condenser transducers, resulting in slower transient acceleration
    and inherent mechanical damping of high-frequency excursion above 12-15 kHz."
    [Class: ESTABLISHED_ENGINEERING_PRINCIPLE]
  - Claim 3: "Positioning a cardioid dynamic microphone 2.5 cm from a loudspeaker grill
    typically produces a 4 dB to 8 dB rise across the 80 Hz - 200 Hz region relative
    to a 30 cm distance."
    [Class: EMPIRICAL_RELATIONSHIP / ILLUSTRATIVE_EXAMPLE]

By splitting this single paragraph into three distinct claims, the architecture
preserves invariant wave physics (Claim 1) independently of transducer mechanics
(Claim 2) and illustrative numerical measurements (Claim 3).

SECTION 8 — PROVENANCE ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8.1 UNBROKEN CHAIN OF CUSTODY
Every claim in the library must maintain full provenance traceability from original
printed work to runtime retrieval.

Data Pipeline Lineage:
  1. Primary Document Acquisition -> Document Registry (Title, ISBN, Edition, Hash).
  2. Extraction -> Extraction Log (Tool/Expert ID, Quoted Text, Page/Section).
  3. Normalization -> Candidate Entity (Semantic formulation, parameter typing).
  4. Dual Review -> Sign-Off Record (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Comments, Date).
  5. Cryptographic Commitment -> SHA-256 Claim Signature binding claim text,
     source citation, and reviewer IDs.

8.2 DERIVED AND SYNTHESIZED CLAIMS
When a knowledge claim is synthesized from multiple sources (e.g., combining a
vacuum tube cathode-follower model from a circuit textbook with measured distortion
harmonics from a technical AES paper):
  - The `attribution_ids` array must reference all contributing sources.
  - The claim must be tagged with `synthesis_type: MULTI_SOURCE_CONSENSUS`.
  - The synthesis rationale must be documented in the extraction log.
  - The claim cannot claim an authority tier higher than the lowest tier among its
    constituent dependencies.

8.3 VERSIONING AND SUPERSEDING
  - Knowledge claims are immutable once promoted to `VALIDATED_ACTIVE`.
  - If an error is corrected or boundary conditions are refined, a new `KnowledgeClaim`
    entity is created with an incremented version number (`version: 2`).
  - The original claim's state transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, and its `superseded_by`
    pointer is populated with the ID of the new claim.
  - The runtime audit log maintains full historical lineage for retrospective analysis.

SECTION 9 — SOURCE AUTHORITY / EVIDENCE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1 CRITIQUE OF THE NAIVE LINEAR TIER MODEL
Phase 1C.3c proposed a simple linear hierarchy (Tier 1 Physics down to Tier 4 Genre).
While conceptually clear, a flat linear ranking fails in practice because
authority is Domain-Dependent.

Example of Linear Failure:
  - An elite university theoretical physics textbook is Tier 1 for Maxwell's equations,
    but provides zero useful guidance on the sonic breakup characteristics of a 1968
    Celestion Greenback speaker.
  - A technical whitepaper from Celestion's chief acoustic engineer is technically
    "manufacturer literature" (Tier 2/3), yet for speaker voice-coil thermal compression
    it is substantially more authoritative than a general university acoustics textbook.
  - An experienced Grammy-winning recording engineer's masterclass is Tier 4 in pure
    physics, but Tier 1 for practical studio workflow conventions regarding multi-mic
    handover boundaries in modern rock productions.

9.2 THE TWO-DIMENSIONAL AUTHORITY MATRIX
Phase 1C.4a replaces the naive linear model with a Two-Dimensional Epistemic
Authority Matrix combining Source Rigor with Domain Jurisdiction:

Dimension 1: Source Rigor Tier (R-Tier)
  - R1: Formally Proven Physical / Mathematical Law (Peer-reviewed, reproducible, textbook law).
  - R2: Controlled Empirical & Scientific Engineering Research (AES, ASA, IEEE, calibrated lab data).
  - R3: Authoritative Technical & Manufacturer Documentation (Circuit schematics, patents, whitepapers).
  - R4: Systematic Professional Engineering Literature (Published treatises by master engineers).
  - R5: Contextual Practitioner Practice & Interviews (Documented practitioner technique).

Dimension 2: Domain Jurisdiction (J-Tier)
  - J_CORE: The source is publishing within its primary technical discipline.
  - J_APPLIED: The source is applying concepts from another field to its discipline.
  - J_PERIPHERAL: The source is commenting informally outside its core expertise.

Effective Epistemic Authority Score:
    Authority = f(R-Tier, J-Tier)
A claim has High Epistemic Authority if it has R1/R2 rigor with J_CORE jurisdiction,
or R3/R4 rigor with J_CORE jurisdiction on practical engineering devices.
A claim has Low Epistemic Authority if an R1 source makes a J_PERIPHERAL aesthetic
comment, or an R5 source makes an unverified claim about quantum electrodynamics.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 10 — CONDITIONAL KNOWLEDGE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

10.1 AVOIDING FALSE UNIVERSALITY
The primary vector by which engineering knowledge degenerates into dogma is the
omission of boundary conditions. Statements that are true under specific conditions
become dangerously false when universalized.

Universalized Dogma (Fatal):
    "Boosting 800 Hz makes electric guitars cut through the mix."
Conditional Reality (Professional):
    "In a dense arrangement containing prominent synthesizers, bass guitar, and lead
    vocals, elevating energy in the 800 Hz to 1.4 kHz band increases guitar articulation,
    PROVIDED THAT the guitar is not already dominant in that band, AND PROVIDED THAT
    the boost does not unbalance the vocal presence pocket."

10.2 STRUCTURAL ENFORCEMENT OF BOUNDARIES
Every `KnowledgeClaim` must be bound to an `OperationalBoundary` record specifying:
  1. Envelope of Applicability: Quantitative ranges where the claim holds
     (e.g., "Input signal level: -24 dBFS to -12 dBFS", "Acoustic distance: < 15 cm").
  2. Prerequisites: Mandatory system states (e.g., "Amplifier must be operating in
     power-tube saturation region", "Loudspeaker must be in a closed-back enclosure").
  3. Confounders & Mimics: Other physical phenomena that produce identical symptoms
     (e.g., "Acoustic proximity effect mimics electrical low-shelf boost; speaker
     cone breakup mimics preamp diode clipping").
  4. Failure Modes: What happens when the boundary is violated (e.g., "Applying this
     high-pass filter to a single-coil guitar playing clean jazz leads results in a
     thin, brittle tone with severe loss of warmth").


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 11 — CONFLICT & CONTRADICTION MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

11.1 PRESERVATION OVER SILENT RESOLUTION
In sound engineering, authoritative sources frequently present divergent viewpoints.
Common examples include:
  - Tube amplifier bias settings: Some authorities advocate hot biasing (70% plate
    dissipation) for harmonic richness; others advocate cooler biasing (55-60%) to
    preserve transient headroom and tube life.
  - Microphone placement for high gain: Some master engineers mandate placing the
    microphone dead-center on the cap for maximum bite and filtering fizz downstream;
    others mandate placing the mic off-axis at the cap/cone junction to acoustic-filter
    fizz at the diaphragm.

If an AI knowledge architecture silently picks one side, it becomes dogmatic and
fragile. If it averages them, it creates a mediocre compromise that satisfies neither
physical reality.

11.2 THE CONFLICT RECORD LIFECYCLE
When two valid sources submit competing claims:
  1. Both claims are ingested as distinct `KnowledgeClaim` entities.
  2. A `ConflictRecord` entity is instantiated linking both Claim IDs.
  3. The conflict is classified:
     - `BOUNDARY_DIVERGENCE`: Both claims are true, but in different operational envelopes
       (e.g., Mic A works best for vintage low-gain; Mic B works best for modern high-gain).
     - `METHODOLOGICAL_DIFFERENCE`: Different technical philosophies achieving similar goals
       (e.g., acoustic filtering via mic angle vs electrical filtering via post-EQ).
     - `DIRECT_CONTRADICTION`: Mutual exclusion requiring controlled empirical adjudication.
  4. The Reasoning Engine, upon encountering a conflict during retrieval, is supplied
     with BOTH perspectives along with their respective trade-offs and boundary envelopes.
     The engineer must reason through which perspective best fits the specific musical
     and physical constraints of the task.

SECTION 12 — NUMERICAL KNOWLEDGE HANDLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

12.1 THE DANGER OF UNTYPED NUMBERS
Numbers in audio engineering are frequently misunderstood:
  - If a source states: "We notched out 400 Hz by 3 dB to clean up the boxy sound,"
    a naive AI system stores: "Boxy sound = Cut 400 Hz by 3 dB."
  - In reality, boxiness is a distributed resonance band between 300 Hz and 600 Hz
    depending on cabinet volume, room modes, and tuning. Fixing 400 Hz as a rigid
    constant is a catastrophic error.

12.2 THE SEVEN-TIER NUMERICAL TYPING CONTRACT
Every numerical value stored in the Knowledge Library must be explicitly typed into
one of the following seven numerical classes:

  Type 1: PHYSICAL_CONSTANT
    - Invariant physical quantities.
    - Example: Speed of sound in dry air at 20°C = 343.2 m/s; Electron charge = 1.602e-19 C.
    - Enforced as exact mathematical invariant.

  Type 2: HARD_ENGINEERING_BOUNDARY
    - Strict physical or format limits beyond which failure or clipping occurs.
    - Example: 0.0 dBFS (unquantized digital ceiling); 100% tube plate dissipation limit.
    - Enforced as absolute upper/lower boundary.

  Type 3: CALIBRATION_STANDARD
    - Industry standardized reference values.
    - Example: +4 dBu = 1.228 V RMS; 0 VU = -18 dBFS nominal alignment; 44.1 kHz / 48 kHz.
    - Enforced as default operating calibration.

  Type 4: EMPIRICALLY_MEASURED_RANGE
    - Validated statistical distribution from controlled laboratory measurements.
    - Example: Celestion Vintage 30 resonant frequency Fs = 75 Hz +/- 5 Hz; Guitar pickup
      output impedance = 5.8 kOhm to 16.5 kOhm.
    - Represented as a probability distribution (mean, standard deviation, min, max).

  Type 5: CONTEXTUAL_RECOMMENDED_RANGE
    - Published engineering guideline bounded by specific musical/technical contexts.
    - Example: "High-pass guitar between 80 Hz and 120 Hz for modern heavy rock."
    - Represented as an interval with soft edges [min, typical_low, typical_high, max].

  Type 6: ILLUSTRATIVE_NOMINAL_VALUE
    - A specific number cited in a case study solely to demonstrate a calculation or concept.
    - Example: "In this example, an SM57 was placed 2.5 cm from the grill."
    - MUST BE FLAGGED AS NON-NORMATIVE. Never used as a default or constraint.

  Type 7: PLATFORM_PARAMETER_MAPPING
    - Proprietary control scales belonging exclusively to the Platform Translation Layer.
    - Example: AT5 Gain knob (0.0 to 10.0 scale mapped non-linearly to -20 dB to +40 dB).
    - STRICTLY PROHIBITED FROM THE GENERAL SOUND ENGINEERING LIBRARY.

SECTION 13 — CROSS-DOMAIN RELATIONSHIP MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

13.1 AVOIDING SILOED KNOWLEDGE
Engineering problems are fundamentally cross-domain. An issue observed in the
Frequency domain (Domain 4) frequently originates in the Source domain (Domain 1),
is mediated by the Nonlinear domain (Domain 3), and is compounded by the Capture
domain (Domain 10).

13.2 DIRECTED CROSS-DOMAIN COUPLING
Every `CausalModel` in the library explicitly registers its cross-domain dependencies
via directed graph relations:
  - `UPSTREAM_SENSITIVITY`: Domains that dictate the input conditions to this model
    (e.g., Preamp Distortion is sensitive to Pickup Output and Cable Capacitance).
  - `DOWNSTREAM_CONSEQUENCE`: Domains that receive the output of this model
    (e.g., Loudspeaker Breakup alters the harmonic spectrum entering the Microphone).
  - `COLLATERAL_IMPACT`: Domains that suffer side-effects when this model is manipulated
    (e.g., Increasing Preamp Gain increases Noise Floor and compresses Dynamics).

This cross-domain wiring ensures that when the Sound Engineer retrieves knowledge
about a symptom in Domain 4, the retrieval engine traverses the graph to return
coupled causal models from Domains 1, 3, 8, 9, and 10.

The runtime retrieval engine (to be implemented in a subsequent phase) must be able

14.2 MULTI-FACETED RETRIEVAL VECTOR

SECTION 15 — KNOWLEDGE LIFECYCLE & GOVERNANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

15.1 THE FIVE-STAGE KNOWLEDGE LIFECYCLE
To ensure absolute integrity, knowledge claims advance through a deterministic
finite state machine:

  [STAGE 1: CAPTURED]
    - Raw source document ingested into registry with cryptographic hash and biblio metadata.
    - State: `SOURCE_REGISTERED`.

  [STAGE 2: EXTRACTION & NORMALIZATION]
    - Specialized parser or expert extracts atomic claims and maps them to the entity schema.
    - Assigned Epistemic Class, Domain, and Numerical Types.
    - State: `CANDIDATE_NORMALIZED`.

  [STAGE 3: TECHNICAL PEER REVIEW]
    - Two independent senior audio engineering experts evaluate the candidate:
      * Reviewer A checks physical accuracy and mathematical derivation.
      * Reviewer B checks boundary conditions, confounders, and anti-pattern risks.
    - If rejected -> State: `REJECTED` (with immutable explanation).
    - If approved -> State: `PEER_REVIEWED`.

  [STAGE 4: ADVERSARIAL VALIDATION]
    - The candidate unit is executed against the Phase 1C.3c Counterfactual Triad suite.
    - Checks whether the knowledge unit induces recipe behavior or false certainty.
    - If it fails -> Returned to Stage 2 for boundary tightening.
    - If it passes -> State: `VALIDATED_ACTIVE`.

  [STAGE 5: RETIREMENT & LINEAGE]
    - When new research supersedes an active claim:
    - State transitions to `SUPERSEDED` or `DEPRECATED`.
    - Cryptographic pointer links to successor claim.

15.2 THE GOVERNANCE STATE TRANSITION MATRIX
Current State       Trigger                     Target State        Authorized Role
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
None                Register Source Document    CAPTURED            Ingestion Tool
CAPTURED            Extract Atomic Claims       NORMALIZED          Knowledge Specialist
NORMALIZED          Submit for Peer Review      UNDER_REVIEW        Knowledge Specialist
UNDER_REVIEW        Expert Review Approved      PEER_REVIEWED       Senior Audio Reviewer (2x)
UNDER_REVIEW        Expert Review Rejected      REJECTED            Senior Audio Reviewer
PEER_REVIEWED       Passes Adversarial Triad    VALIDATED_ACTIVE    Governance Lead
VALIDATED_ACTIVE    New Evidence Supersedes     SUPERSEDED          Governance Committee
VALIDATED_ACTIVE    Flaw or Risk Discovered     DEPRECATED          Governance Committee

SECTION 17 — EXPERIENTIAL & CANDIDATE LESSON QUARANTINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

17.1 THE RUNTIME LEARNING RISK
In future phases (Phase 1C.4 and beyond), the TT AI Sound Engineer will execute
closed-loop iterations, comparing rendered audio against target references.
If the system is allowed to autonomously convert a successful experiment into a
global knowledge rule, catastrophic model collapse occurs:
  - An intervention that succeeded purely due to an idiosyncratic combination of
    source guitar, room acoustic, and pickup impedance would become a universal rule,
    poisoning future sessions.

17.2 THE ISOLATED QUARANTINE STORE
The architecture establishes an entirely isolated storage container:
`/knowledge/quarantine/candidate_lessons/`

Quarantine Rules:
  1. No entity in the quarantine store is visible to the runtime retrieval index.
  2. Candidate lessons must log complete context: InputSnapshot, EvidenceRecord,
     interventions applied, outcome audio deltas, and user feedback.
  3. A candidate lesson must accumulate at least 50 independent corroborating sessions
     across diverse simulated hardware and performance profiles before it can even
     be submitted for human review.
  4. Human governance review must determine whether the candidate lesson represents
     a genuine physical engineering tendency or an accidental correlation.

SECTION 19 — INTEGRITY & FAILURE PROTECTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The architecture implements ten active structural protections against systemic failure:

1. Unsupported AI Knowledge Ingestion Guard
   Autonomous LLMs are strictly prohibited from writing directly to the Knowledge
   Library. All candidate claims must originate from registered sources and pass
   human review.

2. Provenance Cryptographic Checksum
   Every `KnowledgeClaim` includes a SHA-256 hash computed over its claim text,
   source attribution ID, and boundary conditions. Tampering with claim text
   invalidates the signature and drops the claim from retrieval.

3. Source Misquotation Verification
   During Stage 3 Peer Review, human reviewers must verify the `quoted_claim` against
   the registered source document's exact page/section before signing off.

4. Accidental Rule Creation Detection (Anti-Recipe Linter)
   An automated schema linter scans candidate claims for imperative or normative
   keywords (e.g., "always", "must", "never use", "set to X for genre Y").
   Any claim containing imperative intervention rules is automatically rejected.

5. Platform Contamination Firewall
   A lexicon validator scans all knowledge entities for commercial vendor strings
   (e.g., "AmpliTube", "IK Multimedia", "Fender 65 Deluxe Reverb", "TS808", "V30").
   Entities containing commercial brand names are blocked unless explicitly categorized
   as `ILLUSTRATIVE_EXAMPLE` with a platform-independent translation attached.

6. Contradiction Isolation & Non-Deletion
   Conflicting claims cannot be deleted by automated processes. They must generate
   a `ConflictRecord` and require formal human adjudication.

7. Silent Supersession Prevention
   A claim cannot be replaced without creating an explicit `SUPERSEDED` state and
   maintaining the historical audit log.

8. Quarantine Isolation Enforcement
   The runtime retrieval engine possesses zero network or file permissions to access
   the `/knowledge/quarantine/` directory.

9. Retrieval Status Gate
   The retrieval query filter enforces: `WHERE lifecycle_state == 'VALIDATED_ACTIVE'`.
   Drafts, unreviewed candidates, and deprecated claims are structurally unretrievable.

10. Prompt-Injection Neutralization
    All text fields extracted from third-party sources are sanitized and treated as
    passive semantic data. Source text is never interpolated into LLM system prompts
    as executable instructions.

SECTION 21 — DECISIONS REQUIRED BEFORE KNOWLEDGE POPULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before Phase 1C.4b (Knowledge Base Population) can begin, the engineering leadership
must formally resolve the following four architectural decisions:

Decision 1: Storage Serialization Format
  - Option A: Pure JSON Schema files residing in `/src/knowledge/authoritative/`.
    * Pros: Highly transparent, version-controlled in Git, diffable, zero infrastructure.
    * Cons: Higher memory overhead when querying thousands of claims; requires manual indexing.
  - Option B: Embedded SQLite / DuckDB database compiled at build time.
    * Pros: High-speed relational joins between Claims, Boundaries, and Sources; structured SQL queries.
    * Cons: Binary diffs in Git; requires local query tooling.
  - Option C: Hybrid (JSON source files in Git compiled to typed in-memory search trees at build time).
  - Status: RECOMMENDED HYBRID (Option C).

Decision 2: Peer Review Quorum Requirements
  - Define whether one or two human expert signatures are mandatory for Tier 3 / Tier 4
    contextual practice claims, or if single-expert approval suffices for non-physics domains.
  - Recommendation: Dual review mandatory for Tier 1 & Tier 2; single expert plus
    automated adversarial triad pass permitted for Tier 3 & Tier 4.

Decision 3: Current TT Semantic Extraction Boundary
  - Finalize whether the refactored microphone reasoning rules from Phase 1C.3b
    will be ingested as a single comprehensive capture domain model or decomposed
    into ~15 atomic knowledge units.
  - Recommendation: Decompose into 15 atomic units (conforming to Section 7).

Decision 4: Multi-Language & Mathematical Notation Support
  - Determine whether mathematical formulas must be strictly encoded in LaTeX strings,
    MathML, or executable TypeScript evaluation lambdas.
  - Recommendation: LaTeX strings for documentation; pure mathematical evaluation lambdas
    restricted to Deterministic Validation contracts.
