1.1 ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE & CONTEXT
Tone Translator (TT) is undergoing a fundamental architectural evolution: transitioning
from an AmpliTube 5 (AT5) preset-generation application into a platform-independent,
acoustically grounded, professional AI Sound Engineer architecture.

The target macro-architecture establishes a strict, unidirectional causal flow:

    INPUT / EVIDENCE
        ↓
    AI SOUND ENGINEER (Evidence-informed engineering reasoning using governed knowledge, case evidence, causal models, operational boundaries, uncertainty, constraints and trade-offs)
        ↓
    ENGINEERING DECISION (Declarative, platform-agnostic diagnostic and intervention plans)
        ↓
    SEMANTIC TONE DESIGN (High-level abstract audio topology and parameterization)
        ↓
    DETERMINISTIC SEMANTIC VALIDATION (Checking truth, schema integrity, and platform-independent mathematical/logical bounds)
        ↓
    PLATFORM TRANSLATOR (Target-specific semantic-to-hardware mapping engine & 4-tier fidelity reporting)
        ↓
    PLATFORM-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION / EXPORT (AmpliTube 5, Quad Cortex, Helix, Kemper, etc.)

In this future architecture, target platforms such as AmpliTube 5 remain supported
deployment targets, but they NEVER dictate, constrain, or define the Sound Engineer's
internal reasoning, diagnostic ontology, or epistemic knowledge base.

Current TT predates this architecture. As documented in the comprehensive archaeological
survey (/TT_Current_TT_Archaeological_Survey_v1.txt), the legacy codebase is an
archaeological accumulation of valuable sound-engineering knowledge, heuristic rules,
genre conventions, artist recipes, post-generation deterministic overrides, prompt
instructions, platform-specific quirks, and dormant scaffolding.

Before the new AI Sound Engineer architecture can be populated with knowledge, a formal,
rigorous specification must govern how Current TT legacy assets will be decomposed,
evaluated, classified, preserved, relocated, investigated, or retired. This document
establishes that definitive migration specification for Phase 1C.4e-R.

1.4 CORE EPISTEMIC AXIOMS & TEN BINDING CALIBRATION LESSONS
The entire migration methodology is anchored in four foundational epistemic axioms:

AXIOM 1: CURRENT TT IS CLASSIFIED AS UNASSESSED_LEGACY_SOURCE
Existing behaviour, code structures, prompts, and recipes possess ZERO automatic
epistemic authority merely because they are implemented in the application codebase.
Code presence proves that an implementation exists; it does not prove that the underlying
engineering proposition is true, optimal, or acoustically justified.

AXIOM 2: OBSOLETE IMPLEMENTATION DOES NOT IMPLY USELESS KNOWLEDGE
A dormant recipe, hardcoded override, unused helper, or obsolete test fixture may
encapsulate a profound physical or engineering proposition. Do not dismiss, delete,
or ignore an asset merely because its current code path is dead, bypassed, or poorly
implemented.

AXIOM 3: SUCCESSFUL OUTPUT DOES NOT PROVE CORRECT REASONING
A legacy prompt or deterministic override may have historically generated subjectively
acceptable presets for a handful of test cases. This coincidental or heuristic success
does NOT constitute proof that its embedded engineering assumptions, causal claims,
or acoustic theories are valid.

AXIOM 4: BINDING PROVENANCE & RISK-PROPORTIONAL EVIDENCE STANDARD
Candidate propositions extracted from Current TT must recover the best available
underlying provenance and validate the claim to an evidence standard appropriate to its
nature and risk. Risk determines the required evidentiary sufficiency, scrutiny,
corroboration, and review burden; it does NOT dictate a rigid or mandatory source type.

THE TEN BINDING CALIBRATION LESSONS:
Across the archaeological calibration of Current TT, the following ten lessons govern
all migration operations:
  1. GOOD OUTCOME ≠ GOOD REASONING: Coincidental subjective success does not validate the
     logic that produced it.
  2. REPRESENTATION CAN BE WORTH KEEPING EVEN WHEN THE REASONING THAT PRODUCED IT IS NOT:
     Well-structured domain models (e.g. 4D mic coordinates) are valuable even if legacy
     heuristics populated them poorly.
  3. REAL-WORLD DEVICE KNOWLEDGE ≠ PLATFORM MODEL KNOWLEDGE ≠ HISTORICAL PRODUCTION
     KNOWLEDGE ≠ PERCEPTUAL SHORTHAND: These four domains have distinct physical and
     epistemic scopes and must never be conflated.
  4. A RECIPE IS NOT A KNOWLEDGE UNIT: A recipe is an aggregate configuration that must
     be decomposed into individual atomic propositions.
  5. SUCCESSFUL FIXED SETTINGS MAY BE PRESERVED AS REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE WITHOUT BECOMING
     ENGINEERING RULES: Historical presets are empirical cases, not prescriptive laws.
  6. DETERMINISTIC OVERRIDES MAY CONTAIN USEFUL UNDERLYING ENGINEERING PROPOSITIONS EVEN
     WHEN THE OVERRIDE MECHANISM ITSELF IS NLR: The intent behind a hack may reflect a genuine
     acoustic requirement.
  7. AN ANTI-PATTERN MAY EXPOSE A GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLE OR A MISSING COMPETENCY: A flawed
     patch often reveals where a system boundary or cognitive capability was absent.
  8. INPUT EXISTENCE ≠ INPUT CONSUMPTION ≠ DECISION RELEVANCE: An input parameter can exist
     in a function signature without being consumed, or be consumed without influencing decisions.
  9. PLATFORM TRANSLATION MAY BE PRESERVED ALMOST INTACT WHERE IT IS GENUINELY DETERMINISTIC,
     VERIFIED, AND CORRECTLY OWNED: Validated parameter maps and XML serializers belong in
     the Platform Translator.
  10. LEGACY TEST EXPECTATION ≠ ENGINEERING TRUTH: A passing legacy test proves only that
      the software does what the test author coded, not that the acoustic behavior is correct.

1.5 THE CORE MANDATE: ANTI-PATTERNS TO SYSTEMATICALLY PREVENT
The migration methodology defined in this specification is designed to systematically
prevent the following critical failure modes:
  1. Recipes Becoming Engineering Laws: Contextual settings dogmatized as physical laws.
  2. Platform Quirks Becoming Sound Engineer Knowledge: AT5 limitations encoded as audio physics.
  3. Successful Historical Outputs Becoming Proof of Reasoning: Conflating listener satisfaction
     with valid causal logic.
  4. Perceptual Shorthand Becoming Physical Fact: Treating subjective buzzwords as physical units.
  5. Dead or Unused Capability Represented as Operational Capability: Claiming bypassed
     features as functioning system competence.
  6. Deterministic Implementation Logic Silently Inheriting AI Judgement: Overrides secretly
     replacing AI decisions.
  7. Reference Case Evidence Silently Becoming Canonical Precedent: Historical setups binding
     future reasoning.
  8. Useful Knowledge Lost When Obsolete Scaffolding Is Retired: Destructive cleanup deleting
     valuable heuristics.

SECTION 2 — THE MIGRATION PIPELINE & ATOMIC DECONSTRUCTION METHODOLOGY

2.1 THE END-TO-END MIGRATION CHAIN
The migration process transforms legacy assets into formal, governed engineering knowledge
through an unbroken nine-stage pipeline:

  [ Stage 1: Legacy Asset Identification ]
         Identify discrete code, prompt, recipe, override, or schema asset in repository.
         ↓
  [ Stage 2: Atomic Deconstruction ]
         Isolate every discrete assertion, assumption, heuristic, rule, and calibration point.
         ↓
  [ Stage 3: Determine What It Actually Is (Migration Classification) ]
         Classify each atomic proposition against the 19 Migration Asset Categories.
         ↓
  [ Stage 4: Architectural Ownership Assignment ]
         Assign each atomic item to exactly one of the 10 Architectural Owners.
         ↓
  [ Stage 5: Epistemic Intake Characterisation ]
         Record required validation, provenance status, uncertainty, known conflicts,
         operational boundaries, consequence/risk if wrong, and validation pathway.
         (Does NOT adjudicate validity or promote to canonical status).
         ↓
  [ Stage 6: Primary Disposition & Multi-Annotation ]
         Assign exactly one Primary Disposition and apply secondary diagnostic tags.
         ↓
  [ Stage 7: Destination Mapping ]
         Route the item to its designated architectural subsystem or retirement archive.
         ↓
  [ Stage 8: Provenance Recovery & Dossier Compilation ]
         Trace underlying physical, historical, or platform provenance (SourceDocument).
         ↓
  [ Stage 9: Formal Migration Record Creation & Downstream Handoff ]
         Generate an immutable Migration Record and package for 1C.4b/1C.4d governance.

2.2 THE 1-TO-MANY ATOMIC DECOMPOSITION PRINCIPLE
A single legacy asset in Current TT is rarely atomic. A single function, prompt section,
or recipe almost always conflates an acoustic principle, a causal model, a genre convention,
a specific gear choice, a platform parameter, and a deterministic override.

THEREFORE: A SINGLE LEGACY ASSET MAY DECOMPOSE INTO MULTIPLE ATOMIC OUTPUTS
WITH DIFFERENT ARCHITECTURAL OWNERS, MIGRATION CLASSIFICATIONS, AND DISPOSITIONS.

2.3 DECONSTRUCTION HEURISTICS & BOUNDARY RULES
Engineers performing migration deconstruction must apply four strict boundary rules:
  Rule 1: Separate Intent from Implementation.
    Ask: "What acoustic or musical problem was the original author attempting to solve?"
    Do not confuse the problem being solved with the hack used to solve it.
  Rule 2: Strip Target-Specific Hardware Bindings.
    Ask: "Does this proposition remain true if the guitarist plays through a real analog
    Mesa/Boogie Dual Rectifier, a Neural DSP Quad Cortex, or a Kemper Profiler?"
    If yes, it is platform-agnostic sound engineering knowledge. If no, it is platform-specific.
  Rule 3: Decouple Causal Physics from Stylistic Preference.
    Ask: "Is this governed by physical electroacoustic behavior, or is it an aesthetic choice
    characteristic of a specific musical subculture?"
  Rule 4: Identify Silent Redesigns.
    Ask: "Does this code silently replace a sound engineer's judgement, or does it merely
    verify that the output is physically valid?"

2.4 DECOUPLING SOUND ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE FROM PLATFORM IMPLEMENTATION
Current TT is deeply coupled to AmpliTube 5. To achieve platform independence:
  1. Acoustical Signal Chain Abstraction:
     The Sound Engineer reasons in terms of abstract functional blocks:
     Input Transducer -> Input Conditioning / Filter -> Non-linear Pre-amplification ->
     Passive Tone Stack -> Power Stage Saturation -> Complex Load / Acoustic Transducer ->
     Spatial / Environmental Acoustics -> Post-transducer Equalization and Dynamics.
  2. Platform Translator Boundary:
     The Platform Translator receives the abstract Semantic Tone Design and maps it onto
     the capabilities and quirks of the target environment.
  3. Platform Limitations Handled Gracefully (Deterministic Constraint Satisfaction):
     Translators map canonical engineering decisions into target platform capabilities
     (e.g. AT5 parameters, Quad Cortex blocks, Helix nodes). The Sound Engineer's acoustic
     model is NEVER altered to accommodate platform poverty. Where platform poverty prevents
     exact execution of canonical engineering intent, the Platform Translator MUST NOT
     silently truncate, approximate without audit, or alter canonical intent.
     Instead, the Platform Translator:
       (1) executes deterministic constraint satisfaction within platform capability boundaries;
       (2) logs an explicit, audited capability deficit record;
       (3) alerts the reasoning layer to unresolved intent gaps;
       (4) preserves the uncompromised canonical engineering design in the audit trail.
     Platform translation may adapt representation to platform capability, but must not silently
     alter engineering intent.

  4. Explicit Four-Tier Platform Translation Fidelity Contract:
     Every Platform Translator must be capable of reporting semantic-intent fidelity using
     the following four standardized classifications:

     * EXACT:
       The target platform can represent the relevant semantic intent without material
       translation loss within the defined representation/calibration contract.

     * APPROXIMATED:
       The target cannot represent the semantic intent exactly, but a bounded alternative
       representation is available. The approximation MUST:
         - preserve the original semantic intent record;
         - describe what was changed;
         - describe the known translation loss;
         - identify the target limitation that caused it;
         - never pretend the approximation is the original engineering decision.

     * DEFAULTED:
       Required target implementation data is absent or cannot be directly resolved, and an
       explicit safe/default target representation is used. The default MUST:
         - be visible in translation diagnostics;
         - identify why defaulting occurred;
         - preserve the unresolved original semantic intent;
         - never silently become canonical engineering intent.

     * UNSUPPORTED:
       The target platform cannot provide a defensible representation of the requested
       semantic intent. The translator MUST:
         - report the unsupported capability;
         - preserve the original semantic request;
         - not silently remove, redesign or reinterpret it.

     CORE RULE:
       PLATFORM TRANSLATION MAY REPRESENT, APPROXIMATE, DEFAULT, OR DECLARE UNSUPPORTED.
       IT MUST NOT SILENTLY REDESIGN ENGINEERING INTENT.

     The Platform Translator determines HOW a semantic design can be represented on a target.
     It does NOT decide WHAT the engineering design should have been.
     A target platform's inability to represent a semantic design does NOT invalidate the
     Semantic Tone Design; it generates an audited translation fidelity assessment.

SECTION 3 — DISCIPLINED MIGRATION ASSET CLASSIFICATION ("WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 EPISTEMIC & ONTOLOGICAL PRECISION: MIGRATION METADATA VS CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE
In Current TT, code comments, prompt instructions, deterministic overrides, and UI labels
freely interchange physical facts, genre rules of thumb, and software hacks.
To construct a professional engineering knowledge system, every extracted asset must be
categorized according to what it ACTUALLY is, not what it purports to be.

CRITICAL DISTINCTION:
The 19 categories below represent MIGRATION ASSET CLASSIFICATION CATEGORIES.
They are migration intake metadata used to characterize legacy assets during triage.
They MUST NOT be confused with the 8 canonical knowledge entities (SourceDocument,
ClaimAttribution, KnowledgeClaim, CausalModel, OperationalBoundary, ConflictRecord,
ReviewRecord, CandidateLesson) defined in Phase 1C.4a.
Migration asset categories are NOT canonical knowledge entities, and legacy assets do NOT
obtain canonical status merely by being categorized.

3.2 THE 19 MIGRATION ASSET CLASSIFICATION CATEGORIES
Every atomic asset extracted from Current TT must be classified into exactly one of the
following 19 migration asset categories:

1. ENGINEERING PRINCIPLE
   - Definition: A foundational, invariant physical, electrical, or acoustical law that
     holds universally across audio production environments regardless of style or gear.
   - Example: Inverse-square law; acoustic wave superposition; Ohm's law in speaker loading.

2. CAUSAL RELATIONSHIP / MODEL
   - Definition: A predictable, directional cause-and-effect relationship between an engineering
     intervention (parameter adjustment, component choice) and its physical signal outcome.
   - Example: Attenuating low frequencies prior to clipping reduces intermodulation distortion.

3. REASONING FRAMEWORK
   - Definition: A structured cognitive methodology, diagnostic tree, or logical sequence used
     by a sound engineer to diagnose problems and construct interventions.
   - Example: The 10-step systematic tone design process in the Master Prompt.

4. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT ENGINEERING PRACTICE
   - Definition: An established engineering technique that is effective and appropriate within
     a well-defined acoustic, musical, or production context, but not universally applicable.
   - Example: High-pass filtering electric guitar at 90Hz in dense rock mixes to leave room for bass.

5. PROFESSIONAL CONVENTION
   - Definition: A widely adopted, standardized studio practice or workflow consensus that
     practitioners rely upon for predictability and efficiency.
   - Example: Placing a cardioid dynamic microphone (e.g. Shure SM57) pointing at the speaker
     cone-cap boundary as the standard baseline guitar cabinet recording setup.

6. UNIVERSALISED PRESCRIPTION
   - Definition: A context-dependent practice or personal habit that has been dogmatically
     and incorrectly codified as an absolute, universal rule.
   - Example: "NEVER boost bass on high-gain amplifiers" or "ALWAYS place an EQ after the cabinet".

7. SEMANTIC REPRESENTATION
   - Definition: A platform-agnostic, conceptual data structure or ontology used to describe
     sound, signal topology, gear capabilities, or spatial relationships.
   - Example: The 4D Microphone Placement coordinate model (X-radial, Y-lateral, Z-distance, Alpha-angle).

8. PHYSICAL / DEVICE-SPECIFIC ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE
   - Definition: Deep technical understanding of the physical construction, electronic circuit
     topology, component behavior, and electroacoustic response of real-world equipment.
   - Example: Marshall JCM800 2203 cold-clipper cathode stage; Celestion V30 resonant peak.

9. PLATFORM-SPECIFIC PRODUCT / MODEL KNOWLEDGE
   - Definition: Technical facts, parameters, capabilities, limitations, and operational quirks
     of a specific digital simulation platform or software product.
   - Example: AmpliTube 5's internal model ID for "British Tube Lead 800" is GUID 52.

10. PLATFORM CALIBRATION / TRANSLATION DATA
    - Definition: Quantitative mapping functions, calibration curves, and coordinate translation
      tables required to translate abstract semantic values into platform-specific parameter scales.
    - Example: AT5 VIR coordinate calibration table mapping normalized mic distance [0.0, 1.0]
      to actual millimeter grid bounds for the 4x12 Brit 800 cabinet model.

11. DETERMINISTIC EXECUTION / SERIALIZATION LOGIC
    - Definition: Pure software logic responsible for data transformation, XML document generation,
      binary encoding, file input/output, and schema compliance.
    - Example: `at5PresetBuilder.ts` constructing XML DOM nodes `<Stomp>`, `<Amp>`, `<Cab>`,
      and base64 encoding preset payloads for .at5p file export.

12. HISTORICAL PRODUCTION / CONTEXT INFORMATION
    - Definition: Factual, documentary records regarding how specific historical recordings,
      albums, or artist tones were originally engineered in real recording sessions.
    - Example: Malcolm Young recording AC/DC's 'Jailbreak' using a Gretsch Jet Firebird
      with Filter'Tron pickup into a Marshall JTM45 / Super Lead amplifier.

13. PERCEPTUAL SHORTHAND
    - Definition: Psychoacoustic, metaphorical, or subjective vocabulary used by musicians and
      engineers to describe perceived tonal sensations.
    - Example: "Chug" (dynamic palm-mute energy in 80Hz - 160Hz with fast decay);
      "Ice-pick" (excessive uncompressed energy spike between 3.5kHz and 6kHz).

14. REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE
    - Definition: An empirical record of a specific audio engineering configuration, its input
      conditions, applied interventions, and documented listening outcomes.
    - Example: The Pantera 'Cowboys From Hell' benchmark preset configuration and listening test.

15. GOVERNANCE / INTEGRITY PRINCIPLE
    - Definition: Explicit safety limits, boundary constraints, and architectural invariants
      enforced to protect audio fidelity, prevent equipment damage, or maintain system integrity.
    - Example: Enforcing maximum digital gain ceiling (+12dB) to prevent inter-sample clipping;
      preventing simultaneous 180-degree phase inversion across summing parallel channels.

16. ANTI-PATTERN
    - Definition: A flawed engineering practice, inappropriate software hack, or incorrect
      assumption that produces degraded audio outcomes, fragile code, or epistemic distortion.
    - Example: Inserting cascaded post-EQ blocks to compensate for incorrect microphone placement
      rather than moving the microphone; silent deterministic mutation of AI engineering plans.

17. CAPABILITY / COMPETENCY GAP
    - Definition: An area of professional sound engineering knowledge, reasoning, or sensory
      evaluation that Current TT completely lacks or implements in a deficient manner.
    - Example: Inability to perform actual spectral listening analysis on incoming audio;
      lack of room acoustics and boundary reflection modeling.

18. UI / INFRASTRUCTURE / OBSERVABILITY MECHANISM
    - Definition: User interface components, state management stores, debug panels, telemetry
      logging, and test fixtures designed to support user interaction and system observability.
    - Example: `MicPlacementManagementView.tsx` 2D grid visualization; `ToneProfileView.tsx`.

19. DEAD / OBSOLETE IMPLEMENTATION
    - Definition: Unreachable code paths, abandoned UI toggles, or unused services that perform
      no runtime function in active production.
    - Example: The abandoned `Recipe` UI toggle in `App.tsx` and dormant functions in `recipes.ts`.

3.3 BOUNDARY DISAMBIGUATION CRITERIA
When classifying candidate assets, engineers must apply the following disambiguation tests:
  Test A: Engineering Principle vs Context-Dependent Practice:
    - If invariant across ALL acoustic signals and platforms -> Category 1 (Engineering Principle).
    - If valid and desirable only within specific contexts/genres -> Category 4 (Context-Dependent Practice).
  Test B: Causal Model vs Universalised Prescription:
    - If "Under conditions X, intervention Y causes physical outcome Z" -> Category 2 (Causal Model).
    - If dogmatic unconditional mandate ("Must always do Y") -> Category 6 (Universalised Prescription).
  Test C: Real-World Device Knowledge vs Platform Product Knowledge:
    - If describing physical analog circuitry/tubes/speakers -> Category 8 (Physical Device Knowledge).
    - If describing digital software simulation IDs/knobs/quirks -> Category 9 (Platform Product Knowledge).
  Test D: Reference Case Evidence vs Declarative Knowledge:
    - If recording "What was done in a specific historical or benchmark case" -> Category 14 (Reference Case).
    - If stating "How acoustic systems behave in general" -> Declarative Engineering Knowledge.

3.4 TAXONOMIC CLASSIFICATION DECISION TREE
Traverse this 5-stage decision tree for every extracted asset:
  Step 1: Is this an invariant physical/electrical law of nature?
          YES -> Category 1 (Engineering Principle)
          NO  -> Proceed to Step 2.
  Step 2: Does it describe a physical cause-and-effect relationship?
          YES -> Category 2 (Causal Relationship / Model)
          NO  -> Proceed to Step 3.
  Step 3: Is it specific to a software simulation platform (e.g. AmpliTube 5)?
          YES -> Is it execution/file serialization? -> Category 11 (Deterministic Execution)
                 Is it parameter/coordinate mapping? -> Category 10 (Platform Calibration)
                 Is it software model metadata? -> Category 9 (Platform Product Knowledge)
          NO  -> Proceed to Step 4.
  Step 4: Does it describe real-world analog hardware or historical studio sessions?
          YES -> Is it hardware circuitry? -> Category 8 (Physical Device Knowledge)
                 Is it a specific album/session record? -> Category 12 (Historical Context)
                 Is it a specific benchmark preset case? -> Category 14 (Reference Case Evidence)
          NO  -> Proceed to Step 5.
  Step 5: Is it a rule of thumb, studio method, subjective term, or infrastructure?
          - If a structured cognitive sequence -> Category 3 (Reasoning Framework)
          - If valid in bounded musical context -> Category 4 (Context-Dependent Practice)
          - If industry standard consensus -> Category 5 (Professional Convention)
          - If dogmatic unconditional rule -> Category 6 (Universalised Prescription)
          - If perceptual vocabulary -> Category 13 (Perceptual Shorthand)
          - If safety/boundary limit -> Category 15 (Governance Principle)
          - If flawed practice or silent hack -> Category 16 (Anti-Pattern)
          - If missing professional capability -> Category 17 (Capability Gap)
          - If UI or debug panel -> Category 18 (UI / Infrastructure Mechanism)
          - If unreachable or dead code -> Category 19 (Dead / Obsolete Implementation)

SECTION 4 — FORMAL DISPOSITIONS & MULTI-ANNOTATION TAXONOMY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 THE SIX PRIMARY DISPOSITIONS
Every atomic asset extracted from Current TT must be assigned EXACTLY ONE primary disposition.
The six permitted primary dispositions are defined as follows:

1. KEEP CANDIDATE
   - Semantics: High-value sound engineering proposition, physical device insight, causal model,
     or reasoning framework worthy of entering the Sound Engineer Knowledge acquisition funnel.
   - Permitted Use: Applied to domain concepts that are platform-independent, physically plausible,
     and relevant to tone engineering.
   - Requirement: Must undergo formal provenance recovery and epistemic characterisation under
     Phase 1C.4b / 1C.4d before reaching canonical `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` status.

2. KEEP — RELOCATE
   - Semantics: A valid, necessary operational asset that belongs in the new architecture,
     but NOT inside the Sound Engineer's declarative knowledge base.
   - Permitted Use: Applied to platform calibration tables (e.g. AT5 VIR coordinates), platform
     translation algorithms (e.g. GUID lookups), deterministic XML serializers, UI components
     (e.g. 2D mic placement grid), and deterministic validation checks.
   - Target Destination: Specific subsystems outside the Sound Engineer Knowledge Base
     (e.g. Platform Translator, Exporter, Observability/UI).

3. PRESERVE — REFERENCE CASE
   - Semantics: A concrete historical configuration, album recording recipe, artist benchmark,
     or empirical listening test outcome preserved as evidence behind the Reference Case Firewall.
   - Permitted Use: Applied to curated recipes (e.g. Malcolm Young 'Jailbreak', Dimebag 'CFH',
     Metallica 'KEA') and benchmark tone profiles.
   - Boundary: Must NEVER be converted into a universal rule or general engineering law.

4. HOLD / INVESTIGATE
   - Semantics: An asset that is ambiguous, disputed, poorly understood, contradictory, or
     partially unverified, requiring deeper technical investigation, acoustic laboratory
     measurement, or engineering review.
   - Permitted Use: Applied when the engineering validity of a heuristic cannot be established
     from available legacy comments or existing literature.
   - Lifecycle: Placed in quarantine; cannot be used in production or discarded until resolved.

5. NLR — NO LONGER REQUIRED
   - Semantics: An asset, code path, override function, or prompt fragment that is obsolete,
     redundant, unphysical, destructive, or superseded by superior architecture.
   - Permitted Use: Applied to dead UI toggles, bypassed services, silent deterministic mutations,
     and redundant prompt instructions.
   - Critical Invariant: Subject to the Non-Destructive NLR Quarantine Rule (Section 4.4).
     NLR does NOT permit destructive deletion until all underlying propositions, calibrations,
     case evidence, and governance lessons have been extracted.

6. GAP
   - Semantics: A deficiency, omission, or missing competency exposed during legacy deconstruction
     where Current TT completely lacks necessary engineering logic, physics models, or sensory input.
   - Permitted Use: Applied when a legacy asset attempts to solve a problem using a crude hack
     because the underlying professional capability is missing.
   - Target Destination: Logged directly into the Capability Gap Matrix for future engineering.

4.2 PRIMARY DISPOSITION EXCLUSIVITY RULE
Every atomic migration record MUST carry exactly one primary disposition:
  - Hybrid or compound dispositions (e.g. "KEEP / NLR" or "RELOCATE / INVESTIGATE") are
    STRICTLY FORBIDDEN.
  - If an asset appears to require multiple dispositions, it has NOT been sufficiently
    deconstructed into atomic units. The engineer MUST decompose the asset further until
    each constituent atomic proposition has exactly one unambiguous primary disposition.

4.3 SECONDARY ANNOTATION TAGGING SYSTEM
While primary dispositions are strictly exclusive, an atomic record may carry one or more
standardized secondary annotations to preserve critical diagnostic, governance, and
contextual metadata.

The permitted secondary annotation tags are:
  - `#ANTI_PATTERN`: The asset represents bad practice, epistemic error, or an architectural hack
    (e.g. silent parameter override, cascading EQs to fix mic placement).
  - `#PERCEPTUAL_SHORTHAND`: The asset relies on subjective, psychoacoustic terminology
    ("warmth", "punch", "chug", "tightness") requiring translation into physical acoustics.
  - `#OVERRIDE_BURDEN`: Deterministic code created specifically to patch around an LLM failure
    or hallucination in legacy prompt execution.
  - `#UNVERIFIED_PROVENANCE`: A candidate claim that currently lacks verified external
    citations, datasheets, or textbook corroboration.
  - `#PLATFORM_COUPLED`: An acoustic claim that is deeply entangled with AmpliTube 5 specific
    quirks or data structures.
  - `#SHADOW_ONLY`: Scaffolding that executes only in shadow mode or background analysis,
    completely decoupled from live preset generation.
  - `#TEST_FIXTURE`: Logic or data that exists exclusively inside unit test suites or mock fixtures.
  - `#HISTORICAL_CONTEXT`: Documentary information regarding historical recording sessions or gear.
  - `#COGNITIVE_BIAS`: An engineering heuristic driven by subjective habit or genre mythology
    rather than electroacoustic reality.
  - `#ACOUSTIC_HAZARD`: A heuristic or rule that risks introducing phase cancellation, extreme
    resonant ringing, or inter-sample digital clipping.

4.4 NON-DESTRUCTIVE NLR QUARANTINE RULE
To prevent catastrophic loss of domain knowledge during migration:
  1. No Destructive Code Deletion:
     Assigning `NLR — NO LONGER REQUIRED` to a legacy function, prompt, or recipe does NOT
     authorize its immediate deletion from the codebase.
  2. Prerequisite Accounting:
     Before any legacy code marked NLR can be removed in future refactoring phases, the engineer
     must verify that:
       * All underlying engineering propositions have been extracted as KEEP CANDIDATE records;
       * All platform translation or calibration data has been extracted as KEEP — RELOCATE;
       * All historical benchmarks have been extracted as PRESERVE — REFERENCE CASE;
       * All governance lessons and anti-patterns have been documented.
  3. Quarantine Archive:
     All NLR assets are catalogued in the Migration Ledger as an immutable record of retired
     mechanisms, ensuring full traceability and future auditability.

SECTION 5 — ARCHITECTURAL OWNERSHIP & THE STRICT BOUNDARY FIREWALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 THE TEN ARCHITECTURAL OWNERS
Every atomic asset must be mapped to exactly one architectural owner within the target
Sound Engineer platform:

1. SOUND ENGINEER KNOWLEDGE
   - Scope: Platform-agnostic, declarative knowledge base containing foundational physical
     principles, circuit behavior, transducer physics, psychoacoustic boundaries, and causal models.
   - Boundaries: Contains zero platform-specific implementation code, zero XML generators,
     and zero target GUIDs.

2. ENGINEERING REASONING
   - Scope: Cognitive reasoning engine responsible for diagnostic evaluation, problem decomposition,
     trade-off balancing, intervention sequencing, and high-level tone design. Operates through
     evidence-informed engineering reasoning synthesizing case evidence, governed knowledge claims,
     causal/mechanistic models, operational boundaries, uncertainty, and professional engineering
     judgement (NOT pure first-principles derivation).
   - Boundaries: Decides WHAT acoustic goals to achieve and WHY. Does not perform XML serialization.

3. EVIDENCE / INPUT
   - Scope: Ingestion and analysis of incoming audio signals, user intent, style preferences,
     and environmental context (transducer extraction, spectral analysis, dynamic profiling).
   - Boundaries: Extracts physical and musical features from evidence; does not design presets.

4. SEMANTIC TONE DESIGN
   - Scope: Declarative representation of abstract signal chain topology, module functions,
     and normalized parameter intent (e.g. "pre-gain high-pass at 720Hz, Q=0.7").
   - Boundaries: Platform-independent; specifies the ideal acoustical configuration.

5. DETERMINISTIC SEMANTIC VALIDATION
   - Scope: Hardcoded verification rules that mechanically check Semantic Tone Designs for
     platform-independent schema validity, type correctness, required-field completeness,
     reference integrity, and platform-independent mathematical/logical bounds.
   - Boundaries: Strictly bounded to platform-independent deterministic verification.
     DOES NOT check target-specific constraints, slot counts, gear availability, or platform routing.
     DOES NOT redesign or silently mutate engineering decisions. Target-specific limitations
     belong strictly to Platform Translators and NEVER invalidate a Semantic Tone Design.

6. PLATFORM TRANSLATOR — AT5 (and other targets)
   - Scope: Target-specific mapping engine that translates Semantic Tone Designs into concrete
     hardware models, GUIDs, parameter warping curves, and VIR mic coordinates for AmpliTube 5.
     Responsible for AT5 capability inspection, AT5 representability constraints, AT5 slot limitations,
     AT5 available gear/model selection, target parameter mappings, target routing constraints,
     VIR calibration, and explicit 4-tier translation fidelity reporting (EXACT, APPROXIMATED,
     DEFAULTED, UNSUPPORTED).
   - Boundaries: Decides HOW a design is realized on a specific target. A target platform's
     inability to represent a design does NOT invalidate the Semantic Tone Design. Translators
     may represent, approximate, default, or declare unsupported, but MUST NEVER silently
     redesign or alter canonical engineering intent.

7. EXPORTER / EXECUTION
   - Scope: Deterministic file builders, XML serializers, checksum generators, and I/O handlers
     that package translated configurations into target preset files (e.g. .at5p).
   - Boundaries: Pure software serialization; zero audio engineering reasoning.

8. REFERENCE / CASE EVIDENCE
   - Scope: Isolated historical vault containing benchmark presets, album recording setups,
     listening evaluations, and session logs, protected by the Reference Case Firewall.
   - Boundaries: Preserved for illustrative and comparative purposes; cannot dictate future decisions.

9. OBSERVABILITY / INFRASTRUCTURE / UI
   - Scope: Interactive user interfaces (2D mic placement grid, tone profile viewers), debug panels,
     telemetry logging, and runtime tracing infrastructure.
   - Boundaries: Provides user and developer visibility; does not perform tone synthesis.

10. NONE / RETIRE
    - Scope: Dead code, superseded duplicate helpers, abandoned UI toggles, and obsolete hacks.
    - Boundaries: Archived in the migration ledger; targeted for retirement.

5.2 THE CORE OWNERSHIP AXIOM
The entire target architecture is governed by the Core Ownership Axiom:

    "AI DECIDES WHAT + WHY.
     DETERMINISTIC SYSTEMS OWN TRUTH, CHECKING, AND EXECUTION.
     PLATFORM TRANSLATORS DETERMINE HOW FOR A TARGET PLATFORM."

5.3 BOUNDED DETERMINISTIC SEMANTIC VALIDATION: CHECKING VS REDESIGN
Deterministic Semantic Validation must be strictly restricted to checks that can genuinely
and mechanically be established from verifiable facts, without imposing aesthetic dogma:
  - PERMITTED DETERMINISTIC CHECKS:
    * Schema and type validity (e.g. all required fields present, valid data types);
    * Structural topology integrity (e.g. closed signal path, no unconnected nodes);
    * Numeric parameter bounds (e.g. Q factor > 0, gain within normalized [-1.0, +1.0]);
    * Platform-independent domain invariants and reference integrity (e.g. valid node references,
      acyclic signal graphs where required);
    * Exact mathematical relationships where all variables are known (e.g. sample delay = d / c);
    * Explicitly defined safety limits (e.g. digital headroom ceiling to prevent overflow);
    * Deterministic encoding and translation invariants.

  - FORBIDDEN DETERMINISTIC TRESPASSES:
    * Deterministic validation MUST NOT declare a creative, experimental, or unconventional
      engineering topology invalid merely because it deviates from common genre practice
      (e.g. placing reverb before distortion, or extreme equalization curves).
    * Where an engineering decision involves subjective trade-offs, contextual judgement,
      or psychoacoustic balancing, deterministic validation must report the observed condition
      as structured diagnostic evidence, rather than forbidding the choice or dictating an answer.
    * DETERMINISTIC SYSTEMS CHECK TRUTH. THEY DO NOT DICTATE ARTISTIC OR ENGINEERING JUDGEMENT.

  - STRICT PLATFORM INDEPENDENCE BOUNDARY:
    * Deterministic Semantic Validation checks ONLY platform-independent invariants.
    * It MUST NOT check target-specific constraints such as AT5 stomp slot limits, AT5 gear availability,
      AT5 parameter bounds, AT5 routing topologies, or AT5 VIR dimensions.
    * All target-platform capability inspection, slot limitation checking, gear lookup, parameter warping,
      and translation fidelity assessment belong strictly to PLATFORM_TRANSLATOR_AT5 (Owner 6).
    * A target platform's inability to represent a semantic design does NOT invalidate the design.

5.4 THE ANTI-HIJACK PRINCIPLE & DOWNSTREAM REPLANNING HAND-OFF
In Current TT, deterministic functions repeatedly hijack and redesign engineering decisions:
`adjustThrashPedalSelection()` overrides pedal choices, `filterDuplicateEqsWithRemoved()`
silently strips equalizers, and `ensureSignalChainSemanticPlacements()` forcibly re-orders blocks.

THE FUTURE ARCHITECTURE STRICTLY FORBIDS SILENT DETERMINISTIC MUTATIONS:
  1. No Silent Redesign:
     A deterministic validation subsystem MUST NEVER silently alter, replace, or mutate an
     engineering decision made by the AI Sound Engineer.
  2. Explicit Violation Reporting:
     If a Semantic Tone Design violates a deterministic physical, mathematical, or schema invariant, the
     validator rejects the design and generates a structured, machine-readable report containing:
       * The violated constraint or invariant;
       * The exact parameter or topology node in violation;
       * The physical or platform boundary condition;
       * Relevant diagnostic context.
  3. Downstream Reasoning Hand-off:
     Downstream reasoning architecture (Phase 1C.5) determines how reported violations,
     constraints, or unsupported conditions are reconsidered, replanned, or remediated.
     Phase 1C.4e mandates that reasoning provenance remain completely transparent and auditable,
     without prescribing the internal implementation mechanics of the future reasoning engine.

SECTION 6 — THE EVIDENCE-QUALIFIED REFERENCE CASE FIREWALL & PRECEDENT ISOLATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 PURPOSE & ROLE OF REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE
Historical guitar tones from landmark albums (e.g. AC/DC's 'Back in Black', Metallica's
'Kill 'Em All', Pantera's 'Cowboys From Hell') and empirical benchmark experiments represent
valuable cultural, aesthetic, and observational evidence.

However, legacy systems frequently corrupt historical presets into universal engineering laws
(e.g. "Because Dimebag used a Furman PQ-4 on CFH, all metal guitar tones must have an 800Hz scoop").

THE REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE FIREWALL ISOLATES EMPIRICAL PRECEDENT FROM DECLARATIVE REASONING.
Reference cases are preserved as empirical evidence, but are strictly prohibited from
becoming general sound engineering rules.

6.2 EVIDENCE-QUALIFIED SCHEMA FOR REFERENCE CASES (EXPLICIT NON-FABRICATION)
A critical defect in legacy documentation is the tendency to invent or assume missing
parameters. In Tone Translator:
  - REFERENCE CASE RECORDS MUST BE STRICTLY EVIDENCE-QUALIFIED.
  - Highly detailed metadata (e.g. pickup inductance, wood density, exact mic millimeter
    coordinates, console channel strips) MUST NOT BE MANDATORY.
  - Unknown or unverified information must remain explicitly marked as `UNKNOWN` or `NOT_ESTABLISHED`.
  - Engineers and automated tools MUST NEVER infer, estimate, or fabricate missing case metadata.

A Reference Case Record encapsulates the following fields (populated ONLY where known):
  1. Case Identification:
     * Unique Case Identifier (e.g. `REF-CASE-HIST-METALLICA-KEA-001`, `REF-CASE-TT-EXP-004`);
     * Case Type (Historically Documented Production, TT Benchmark, User Listening Trial, A/B Test);
     * Source Track / Album / Artist / Session (if applicable);
     * Comparison Target (description of target sound or reference audio).
  2. Known Input Context (strictly evidence-qualified; omitted or marked UNKNOWN if unverified):
     * Instrument & pickup configuration;
     * Strings, tuning, and documented playing technique;
     * Signal routing prior to amplification.
  3. Applied Intervention & History:
     * Initial intervention settings;
     * Iterative tuning history (record of parameter adjustments made during listening);
     * Equipment models and settings verified to have been used.
  4. Documented Listening Observations & Outcomes:
     * Predicted outcome (if explicitly documented prior to trial);
     * Actual listening observations and perceived spectral/dynamic balance;
     * User / engineer subjective assessment;
     * Known limitations, compromises, or shortcomings in the result.
  5. Provenance & Corroboration:
     * Specific source citation for each material fact (interview, studio log, test run ID);
     * Epistemic standing of each cited source.

6.3 SUPPORT FOR MULTIPLE REFERENCE EVIDENCE FORMS & THE KEA ITERATIVE TUNING CASE
Reference Cases must support diverse empirical formats:
  1. Historically Documented Production Cases (e.g. studio track sheets, engineer memoirs);
  2. TT-Generated Benchmark Cases (controlled model evaluation presets);
  3. User Listening & Comparison Cases;
  4. Iterative Tuning Histories;
  5. Controlled A/B Comparison Outcomes;
  6. Target-vs-Result Empirical Observations.

THE GOVERNING SPECIMEN: THE KILL 'EM ALL (KEA) TT CASE
The legacy repository contains a static recipe for Metallica's 'Kill 'Em All'. Forensic
investigation reveals how this asset was actually created:
  - Initial Gemini / Current TT Generation: The LLM initially proposed an amplifier and pedal
    chain based on general thrash prompt keywords.
  - Subjective Evaluation: The initial result was auditioned against the album reference and
    judged to be reasonably close in character, but lacking attack tightness and bite.
  - Iterative Listening & Manual Tuning: The human engineer performed repeated listening passes,
    iteratively adjusting the overdrive tone knob, amplifier mid control, and microphone placement.
  - Recipe Mode Locking: Once a satisfactory setting was achieved, the resulting parameter
    snapshot was hardcoded into `recipes.ts` and `geminiService.ts` as a static recipe to
    prevent the LLM from fluctuating away from the tuned baseline.
  - Verification Trials: Comparisons were run with Recipe mode enabled vs disabled to confirm
    consistency.

MIGRATION LESSON FOR THE KEA SPECIMEN:
  - This configuration is preserved as valuable LEGACY REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE (an empirical record
    of an iterative tuning outcome).
  - It is NOT proof that the underlying LLM prompt reasoning was correct.
  - It is NOT a universal thrash metal recipe.
  - It is preserved behind the Reference Case Firewall with its complete tuning history.

6.4 THE FOUR ANTI-PRECEDENT FIREWALLS
The architecture enforces four strict firewalls between Reference Cases and the Sound Engineer:

  FIREWALL 1: REFERENCE CASES ARE NEVER CANONICAL ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE
  A reference case is an empirical record of an event, NOT an invariant principle of acoustics.
  The Sound Engineer Knowledge Base contains declarative physical models; the reference case
  remains isolated in the Reference Vault.

  FIREWALL 2: REFERENCE CASES ARE NEVER UNIVERSAL PRESCRIPTIONS
  The presence of a reference case in the repository never authorizes the system to mandate
  that configuration for other user prompts. It cannot become an unconditional rule.

  FIREWALL 3: REFERENCE CASES NEVER SUBSTITUTE FOR CURRENT CASE EVIDENCE
  When a user requests a tone, the Sound Engineer MUST reason from the user's specific input
  evidence (guitar, pickups, playing style, monitoring environment). It is strictly forbidden
  to bypass reasoning by simply copying a reference preset.

  FIREWALL 4: GOVERNED ANALOGICAL RETRIEVAL ONLY
  If the Sound Engineer retrieves a reference case, it may use it ONLY as an illustrative
  analogy or benchmark reference point, documenting how differences in gear and acoustic
  context require adaptation.

6.5 GOVERNED RETRIEVAL & QUERYING OF REFERENCE CASES
Retrieval of reference cases is governed by Phase 1C.4c runtime context policies:
  - Reference cases are queried via semantic embedding of musical context and gear topology.
  - Injected reference cases carry explicit `#REFERENCE_CASE_ONLY` lineage tokens.
  - The Sound Engineer prompt generator wraps reference cases in strict epistemic delimiters:
    `<reference_case_evidence precedent="non_binding"> ... </reference_case_evidence>`
  - The reasoning engine is instructed to extract lessons regarding trade-offs, not raw parameter copies.

SECTION 7 — OPERATIONAL REALITY INSPECTION METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 SEVEN OPERATIONAL REALITY EVIDENCE DIMENSIONS
An archaeological survey of legacy code reveals that what is written in source code often
bears little resemblance to what actually executes or matters at runtime. Types may be
declared but never instantiated; parameters may be passed but ignored; and services may
run in background threads without influencing system outputs.

To establish forensic epistemic honesty, archaeological uncertainty must not be forced into
premature booleans. Every legacy construct is forensically audited across seven explicit
evidence dimensions, each taking one of three evidence-qualified states:
  * `TRUE` (Direct forensic evidence confirms presence / activity)
  * `FALSE` (Direct forensic evidence confirms absence / inactivity)
  * `NOT_ESTABLISHED` (Available archaeological evidence is insufficient to verify state)

The Seven Authoritative Dimensions:
  1. `is_declared` (`TRUE` | `FALSE` | `NOT_ESTABLISHED`):
     The symbol, interface, parameter, or type exists in the TypeScript abstract syntax tree.
  2. `is_implemented` (`TRUE` | `FALSE` | `NOT_ESTABLISHED`):
     Non-trivial executable logic, algorithms, or data payloads exist (not a no-op placeholder).
  3. `is_reachable` (`TRUE` | `FALSE` | `NOT_ESTABLISHED`):
     A static or dynamic call path connects an active production entry point (`main.tsx`,
     `App.tsx`, Express API endpoints) to the code block.
  4. `is_executed` (`TRUE` | `FALSE` | `NOT_ESTABLISHED`):
     The code block actually executes during normal preset generation or user workflows.
  5. `is_consumed` (`TRUE` | `FALSE` | `NOT_ESTABLISHED`):
     The output or return value of the execution is received and read by downstream functions
     (rather than dropped, ignored, or overwritten).
  6. `is_decision_relevant` (`TRUE` | `FALSE` | `NOT_ESTABLISHED`):
     The ingested data actively affects, alters, or constrains the resulting engineering choices,
     parameter values, or preset topology.
  7. `is_user_visible` (`TRUE` | `FALSE` | `NOT_ESTABLISHED`):
     The outcome produces an audible difference, preset file parameter change, or visible
     UI state presented to the end user in active production.

7.2 THE OPERATIONAL SUMMARY STATES & INDETERMINATE HANDLING
The seven evidence dimensions are authoritative forensic observations. The operational
summary state is ONLY a derived convenience classification. Under no circumstances may a
summary state override, distort, or contradict the underlying seven dimensions.

Where available evidence is insufficient to classify a legacy construct into a deterministic
category, or where forensic dimensions exhibit partial / contradictory reachability, the
explicit summary state `INDETERMINATE` MUST be assigned.

The Standardized Summary States:

1. DECLARED_ONLY
   - Profile: declared=TRUE, implemented=TRUE/FALSE, reachable=FALSE, executed=FALSE, consumed=FALSE, decision_relevant=FALSE.
   - Meaning: The asset exists in type signatures or schema definitions, but runtime logic
     completely ignores or omits it.
   - Note on Complex Plumbing: If an asset is declared, partly implemented, transported through
     plumbing, but NOT consumed or decision-relevant, it must NOT be simplistically collapsed into
     DECLARED_ONLY if implementation/execution exists. The seven dimensions remain the ground truth.
   - Specimen Example: `recordingData` parameter signature in `translateTone()`.

2. ACTIVE_PRODUCTION
   - Profile: declared=TRUE, implemented=TRUE, reachable=TRUE, executed=TRUE, consumed=TRUE, decision_relevant=TRUE.
   - Meaning: Resides in the live execution path, actively influencing tone synthesis or UI outputs.
   - Specimen Example: Master Prompt system instructions guiding LLM parameter selection.

3. BYPASSED_OVERRIDDEN
   - Profile: declared=TRUE, implemented=TRUE, reachable=TRUE, executed=TRUE, consumed=FALSE, decision_relevant=FALSE.
   - Meaning: Executes or generates data, but downstream logic unconditionally overwrites or
     discards its output with hardcoded overrides.
   - Specimen Example: Gemini-generated EQ parameters completely replaced by Recipe Overrides.

4. SHADOW_ISOLATED
   - Profile: declared=TRUE, implemented=TRUE, reachable=FALSE, executed=FALSE, consumed=FALSE, decision_relevant=FALSE.
   - Meaning: Fully implemented logic that is detached from production entry points (dormant code,
     historical experiments, or developer testbeds).
   - Specimen Example: Historical Web Audio FFT analysis functions in `audioContextService.ts`.

5. TEST_FIXTURE_ONLY
   - Profile: declared=TRUE, implemented=TRUE, reachable=TRUE (tests only), executed=TRUE (tests), consumed=TRUE (tests), decision_relevant=FALSE (prod).
   - Meaning: Assets maintained exclusively to support the automated test suite.
   - Specimen Example: Mock preset builders and synthetic audio fixtures in `src/tests/`.

6. DEAD_CODE
   - Profile: declared=TRUE/FALSE, implemented=TRUE, reachable=FALSE, executed=FALSE, consumed=FALSE, decision_relevant=FALSE, user_visible=FALSE.
   - Meaning: Completely unreferenced, orphaned files or code blocks with no production or test utility.
   - Specimen Example: Deprecated UI components from early prototypes.

7. INDETERMINATE
   - Profile: Forensic dimensions exhibit partial, conflicting, or unverified operational reality
     (e.g., code plumbing exists, but UI visibility is `NOT_ESTABLISHED`).
   - Meaning: Forensic evidence is insufficient or contradictory; assignment to a rigid summary
     category would obscure archaeological reality.

7.3 FORENSIC AUDIT PROTOCOL: FROM ENTRY POINT TO DECISION RELEVANCE
Engineers performing migration analysis must trace legacy assets across five forensic steps:
  Step 1: Entry Point Reachability Audit (Check call graph from active UI and API endpoints).
  Step 2: Data Flow & Mutation Tracing (Verify if returned data reaches downstream consumers).
  Step 3: Branch & Feature Gate Analysis (Inspect conditional flags gating execution).
  Step 4: Shadow Execution Isolation Audit (Verify whether side-effects affect core state).
  Step 5: Test Fixture Confinement Check (Grep for production callers outside `src/tests/`).

7.4 DISTINGUISHING PRODUCTION EXECUTION FROM DECISION RELEVANCE & AUDITING UNVERIFIED VISIBILITY
A critical architectural pitfall is equating production execution with decision relevance.
The disconnected audio path in Current TT is the governing specimen:
  - Historical audio upload plumbing exists in code, but current active production UI visibility

8.2 RISK-PROPORTIONAL EVIDENTIARY SUFFICIENCY (ELIMINATION OF RIGID SOURCE LADDERS)
In strict accordance with Phase 1C.4a and 1C.4b:
  "Recover the best available underlying provenance and validate the claim to an evidence
   standard appropriate to its nature and risk."

ELIMINATION OF RIGID SOURCE-TYPE HIERARCHIES:
Tone Translator rejects the artificial mapping of risk tiers to rigid source classes
(such as "High Risk = Academic Paper, Medium Risk = Textbook, Low Risk = Interview").
Source type does NOT automatically determine evidentiary authority:
  - A manufacturer schematic or oscilloscope measurement is the gold standard for circuit topology,
    while an academic sociology paper on music is irrelevant to it.
  - A first-hand studio tracking sheet or engineer memoir is authoritative evidence for how an
    album was recorded, whereas a peer-reviewed physics paper cannot prove what happened in a studio.
  - An academic paper containing an unverified simulation is inferior to a published industry standard.

THE RISK-PROPORTIONAL PRINCIPLE & FROZEN PHASE 1C.4d GOVERNANCE TIERS:
Risk determines the REQUIRED EVIDENTIARY SUFFICIENCY, LEVEL OF SCRUTINY, NEED FOR INDEPENDENT
CORROBORATION, AND REVIEW BURDEN — NOT a mandatory source category:
Tone Translator directly applies the frozen Phase 1C.4d governance risk taxonomy:
  - `TIER_A`: High Systemic Consequence / Physical Safety / Hardware Hazard
    Direct equipment damage, electric shock, hearing damage, thermal limits, voice coil burnout,
    destructive overload. Mandates highest evidentiary sufficiency, verifiable provenance, and
    rigorous dual independent expert review prior to canonical promotion.
  - `TIER_B`: Moderate Systemic Consequence / Empirical Electroacoustics / Psychoacoustics / Studio Practice
    Acoustic causal mechanisms, circuit transfer functions, device-specific behavior models,
    psychoacoustic thresholds, DSP filtering principles. Mandates structured scrutiny,
    methodological adequacy, and formal corroboration review.
  - `TIER_C`: Low Systemic Consequence / Contextual Notes / Genre Conventions / Aesthetic Traditions
    Genre rules of thumb, historical tracking practices, artist reference setups, benchmark presets.
    Mandates practitioner consensus, documented empirical listening logs, or reference case archives.
  - `RISK_TIER_TO_BE_ASSESSED`:
    Where the correct governance tier cannot be deterministically established from the evidence
    available in this migration specification, the item must be recorded as `RISK_TIER_TO_BE_ASSESSED`
    rather than inventing certainty.

CRITICAL EPISTEMIC INVARIANT:
The governance architecture DOES NOT use or recognize an artificial scalar mapping into governance tiers.
Governance tiers reflect holistic systemic consequence and risk under the Phase 1C.4d contract.
BUT NO governance tier mandates a particular source category:
  * Higher consequence/risk increases required evidence strength;
  * Higher consequence/risk increases scrutiny;
  * Higher consequence/risk increases corroboration requirements;
  * Higher consequence/risk increases methodological adequacy requirements;
  * Higher consequence/risk increases review burden;
  * BUT NO governance tier mandates a particular source type (e.g. academic paper vs schematic).

Appropriate evidence form remains strictly dependent on:
  (1) Nature of the claim;
  (2) Physical scope and domain;
  (3) Methodological adequacy;
  (4) Provenance integrity and traceability;
  (5) Applicability and jurisdictional boundaries;
  (6) Uncertainty and confounding factors;
  (7) Consequence if wrong;
  (8) Evidentiary independence where corroboration is required.

Illustrative (Non-Mandatory) Evidence Examples:
  - High-Risk Claims (Physical safety, equipment protection, thermal / power dissipation limits):
    * Requires robust evidentiary sufficiency, deep scrutiny, and rigorous verification. Suitable
      forms may include verified circuit schematics, calibrated bench test logs, manufacturer
      engineering datasheets, or peer-reviewed electroacoustic research.
  - Medium-Risk Claims (Acoustic causal mechanisms, psychoacoustic thresholds, DSP filters):
    * Requires structured scrutiny and methodological adequacy. Suitable forms may include
      authoritative engineering treatises, established textbooks, acoustic simulations, or verified
      software documentation.
  - Low-Risk Claims (Genre conventions, aesthetic descriptive associations, artist reference setups):
    * Requires practitioner consensus, documented empirical listening logs, or curated reference
      case archives.

8.3 ELIMINATION OF SCALAR CONFIDENCE: MULTIDIMENSIONAL EPISTEMIC VALIDATION INTEGRATION
Tone Translator STRICTLY FORBIDS collapsing epistemic doubt into arbitrary scalar confidence
scores or arbitrary levels (e.g. `confidence: 0.84` or arbitrary pseudo-confidence scores). Such scores deceive
downstream reasoning by obscuring why evidence is uncertain.

Instead, recovered provenance integrates directly with the frozen Phase 1C.4a multidimensional
`EpistemicValidation` structure:
  - `evidence_strength`: Qualitative standing of the underlying evidence basis;
  - `consensus_state`: Degree of professional or scientific agreement;
  - `replication_state`: Status of empirical verification or bench testing;
  - `conflict_status`: Presence of competing theories or contradictory evidence;
  - `boundary_certainty`: Precision of the known operational boundary envelope.

8.4 RECOVERED PROVENANCE SCHEMA & DOWNSTREAM EPISTEMIC GOVERNANCE (PHASE 1C.4d)

  - `relevant_excerpt`: Direct text or data citation corroborating the proposition;

9.7 METHODOLOGICAL TREATMENT OF LEGACY TESTS: ARCHAEOLOGICAL EVIDENCE VS VALIDATION TRUTH
A critical correction in Phase 1C.4e-R is the proper epistemic treatment of legacy unit tests:
  LEGACY TEST EXPECTATION ≠ ENGINEERING TRUTH.

A passing legacy unit test proves ONLY that the software behaves as expected by the author of that test.
It does NOT prove that the expected behavior is acoustically valid, optimal, or physically true.
Therefore, legacy test assertions must NOT be automatically promoted into Deterministic Semantic
Validation rules.

THE CHANGE-CONTROLLED REGRESSION PRINCIPLE:
Existing automated tests are archaeological evidence of implementation behavior. They serve as regression
guards while the behavior they protect remains authoritative:
  - Unaffected production behavior must remain protected by applicable regression tests during migration.
  - Existing tests MUST NOT be casually ignored, disabled, rewritten, or deleted merely because a new
    architecture exists. The existence of new architecture is NEVER, by itself, sufficient justification
    for changing a test.
  - If an intentional architecture or behavior change has been explicitly reviewed, approved, signed off,
    and placed under formal change control, then tests whose expected behavior represents superseded legacy
    behavior may be updated, replaced, or retired under explicit change control.
  - Such test updates or retirements must be formally traceable to the approved architectural change record.
  - A failing legacy test requires forensic investigation and must NOT automatically mean the new
    architecture is wrong. Failure must be classified as either an unintended regression (Category A,
    which must be fixed) or an expected consequence of an approved change (Category B, permitting test update).
  - Conversely, a passing legacy test demonstrates conformance to its encoded behavior but does NOT
    independently prove engineering correctness, epistemic validity, physical truth, or professional competency.
  - Build integrity (`npm run build`) and applicable regression protection remain mandatory throughout migration.
  - Core Principle:
    "Existing automated tests remain regression guards for authoritative production behaviour.
     During migration, tests protecting behaviour unaffected by an approved architectural change MUST
     continue to pass.
     Where an intentional behaviour change has been explicitly reviewed, approved, signed off, and placed
     under change control, tests whose expected behaviour represents the superseded legacy behaviour may be
     updated, replaced, or retired.
     Such changes MUST remain traceable to the approved architecture or behaviour change.
     A failing legacy test may not be dismissed merely because new architecture exists.
     Conversely, a passing legacy test demonstrates conformance to its encoded behaviour but does not
     independently establish engineering correctness, epistemic validity, physical truth, or professional
     competency."

THE TEST DECONSTRUCTION PIPELINE:
Every legacy test assertion must traverse the standard migration deconstruction:
  TEST ASSERTION
      ↓

      ↓
  CLASSIFY (against the 19 Migration Categories)

  ASSIGN ARCHITECTURAL OWNER

      ↓

Classification Outcomes for Legacy Test Assertions:

Demonstration of Platform Translation Fidelity Contract (AT5 VIR):
  The AT5 Platform Translator processes Semantic Tone Designs against target capabilities using
  the explicit four-tier translation fidelity contract:
  - EXACT Representation:
    When a Semantic Tone Design specifies continuous 4D microphone placement within AT5 Brit 800
    grid bounds (e.g. Radial X=0mm, Distance Z=25mm, Angle=0°), AT5 maps to exact parameter
    coordinates without translation loss (`fidelity: EXACT`).
  - APPROXIMATED Representation:
    When a Semantic Tone Design specifies an angle of 32.5° or continuous coordinate X=37.4mm,
    and AT5 VIR provides discrete measurement steps (e.g. discrete angle increments or grid snapping),
    the AT5 Platform Translator snaps to the nearest available grid point (e.g. X=35mm or Angle=30°).
    The translator records an audited approximation record with explicit translation loss metric,
    preserving the uncompromised 32.5° semantic intent in the audit trail (`fidelity: APPROXIMATED`).
  - DEFAULTED Representation:
    When a legacy preset or partial semantic design specifies cabinet model without specifying
    secondary ambient microphone parameters, the AT5 Platform Translator applies safe default
    parameters (e.g. Mic 2 muted or placed at standard room boundary), logging the reason for defaulting
    in translation diagnostics while preserving the original intent (`fidelity: DEFAULTED`).
  - UNSUPPORTED Representation:
    When a Semantic Tone Design specifies a placement exceeding physical cabinet dimensions
    (e.g. Distance Z=500mm when Brit 800 VIR limit is 300mm), the AT5 Platform Translator declares
