1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE & STATUS
Phase 1C.4c establishes the authoritative, platform-independent Sound Engineering
Knowledge Retrieval and Runtime Context Architecture for the Tone Translator (TT)
Professional Sound Engineering System.

Building directly upon the frozen foundations of upstream phases:
  - Phase 1C.4a defined WHAT KNOWLEDGE IS (Atomic entity model, 3-axis taxonomy,
    qualitative epistemic validation, and governance lifecycles).
  - Phase 1C.4b defined HOW INFORMATION EARNS THE RIGHT TO BECOME KNOWLEDGE
    (Source typology, provenance locators, rigorous extraction, normalization,
    and multi-gate ingestion firewalls).
  - Phase 1C.4c defines HOW THE RIGHT KNOWLEDGE IS FOUND AND PRESENTED TO THE
    SOUND ENGINEER AT RUNTIME.

1.2 THE GOVERNING ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE
The foundational axiom of this specification is:

    RETRIEVAL SUPPLIES RELEVANT ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE.
    RETRIEVAL DOES NOT PERFORM THE ENGINEERING DECISION.

The future AI Sound Engineer (to be specified in Phase 1C.5) will own holistic,
contextual WHAT + WHY reasoning, hypothesis evaluation, diagnosis, trade-off
arbitration, and intervention selection. The retrieval subsystem is strictly an
epistemic delivery mechanism: it locates, filters, expands, validates, bounds,
and packages canonical professional sound-engineering knowledge so that the Sound
Engineer reasons from sound principles rather than ungrounded parametric intuition,
simplistic genre recipes, or out-of-context facts.

1.3 THE RETRIEVAL SISO PRINCIPLE
Retrieval introduces its own distinct Garbage-In, Garbage-Out (SISO) vulnerability:

    EXCELLENT LIBRARY
          +
    POOR RETRIEVAL
          =
    POOR ENGINEERING CONTEXT

A retrieval engine that returns technically true propositions that are irrelevant to
the active acoustic physics, out-of-boundary for the current signal chain, stripped
of their critical trade-offs, biased toward single-explanation dogmatism, or disguised
as fixed equipment presets has failed.

Phase 1C.4c rejects:
  - Naive vector-similarity RAG ("Just embed the chunks and retrieve Top-K");
  - Pure keyword/lexical search (blind to physical causality and acoustic meaning);
  - Source-prestige bias (assuming an R1 physics textbook comment on guitar pickups
    outweighs an R3 manufacturer engineering schematic);
  - Symptom-to-solution lookups (e.g. "flubby low end -> Tube Screamer");
  - Silent consensus fabrication (averaging conflicting engineering theories).

Instead, Phase 1C.4c specifies a deterministic, hybrid, graph-expanded,
boundary-aware, conflict-preserving, and uncertainty-retaining retrieval architecture
engineered to deliver compact, highly structured Knowledge Context Packages.

4. RETRIEVAL DESIGN PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Sound Engineering Knowledge Retrieval Architecture is governed by eight immutable
design principles:

4.1 PRINCIPLE 1: KNOWLEDGE INFORMS REASONING; IT DOES NOT REPLACE REASONING
Retrieval must provide the foundational laws, operational models, boundaries, and
trade-offs necessary for an engineer to think through an audio problem. Retrieval
must never deliver a ready-made engineering verdict, preset configuration, or dogmatic
recipe.

4.2 PRINCIPLE 2: CAUSALITY OVER KEYWORD PROXIMITY
In audio engineering, identical symptoms frequently arise from diametrically opposed
physical causes (e.g. low-end mud caused by acoustic room boundary reflections vs.
low-end mud caused by intermodulation distortion in an overdriven preamp vs. bass
transducer proximity effect). Retrieval must expand across causal mechanisms, not
simply fetch documents containing the word "mud."

4.3 PRINCIPLE 3: APPLICABILITY BEFORE RELEVANCE
A knowledge claim may match a query semantically with 99% similarity, but if its
`OperationalBoundary` specifies a push-pull pentode power amplifier and the current
circuit is a single-ended triode, the claim is inapplicable. Operational boundary
validation is a primary gate, not an afterthought.

4.4 PRINCIPLE 4: CONFLICT AND UNCERTAINTY PRESERVATION
Where professional engineering literature, acoustic science, or master practitioners
maintain legitimate, competing explanations or practices, retrieval must surface the
competing positions and their underlying evidence. Retrieval must never homogenize,
average, or arbitrarily pick a winner based on source prestige.

4.5 PRINCIPLE 5: ORTHOGONALITY OF PROVENANCE AND EPISTEMICS
Source rigor (`source_rigor_tier`) describes where information was published; epistemic
basis (`epistemic_basis`) describes the scientific nature of the claim. A high-rigor
source does not grant physical law status to an incidental remark, nor does a practitioner
interview prevent a validated observation from achieving engineering principle status.
Retrieval evaluates both dimensions independently.

4.6 PRINCIPLE 6: SEPARATION OF CASE EVIDENCE FROM CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE
Current session observations (e.g. "Input signal peaks at -2 dBFS with a high crest factor")
belong exclusively to Case Evidence. They must never be indexed, cached, or merged into
the canonical Knowledge Library. Canonical knowledge remains immutable and platform-independent.

4.7 PRINCIPLE 7: PLATFORM INDEPENDENCE AT RUNTIME
The knowledge retrieved by the Sound Engineer must describe the physical, electrical,
and acoustic reality of sound systems. It must not contain AmpliTube 5 GUIDs, software
preset names, GUI widget coordinates, or vendor-specific parameter curves. Platform
mapping occurs strictly downstream in the translation pipeline.

4.8 PRINCIPLE 8: FULL TRACEABILITY & REPLAYABILITY
Every token delivered in a runtime Knowledge Context Package must be traceable back
to its atomic `KnowledgeClaim`, its `ClaimAttribution`, and its cryptographic
`SourceDocument` hash. Given the same knowledge snapshot and query intent, retrieval
must produce a deterministic or fully auditable result.

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. TT PROFESSIONAL KNOWLEDGE                                                      |
|    - Curated, atomic, peer-reviewed, provenance-backed engineering knowledge.      |
|    - Source: Canonical Knowledge Library (normalized KnowledgeClaims,             |
|      CausalModels, OperationalBoundaries, ConflictRecords).                       |
|    - Boundary Rule: Primary target of retrieval; immutable, versioned,            |

|    - Implicit statistical representations encoded in the LLM's weights.           |

5.1 PREVENTING EPISTEMIC CONTAMINATION
  - Case Evidence Contamination Firewall: Intermediate session data, user preferences,
    and specific audio measurements must never be written into the retrieval index.
    The index is a pure derivative of the Git-tracked canonical library.
  - Model Knowledge Masquerade Firewall: If the AI Sound Engineer formulates a
    hypothesis based on its parametric memory that is not corroborated by retrieved
    TT knowledge, runtime traceability must mark that proposition as `SOURCE: MODEL_PARAMETRIC`
    rather than `SOURCE: TT_CANONICAL_LIBRARY`.

6. RETRIEVAL RESPONSIBILITY BOUNDARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To prevent the retrieval system from overstepping into engineering diagnosis or
usurping the role of the Sound Engineer, the architectural boundary is defined with
mathematical precision:

6.1 WHAT RETRIEVAL OWNS (MANDATORY RESPONSIBILITIES)
Retrieval is strictly responsible for:
  1. Parsing and validating the structured `KnowledgeNeed` contract;
  2. Filtering out ineligible knowledge lifecycle states (quarantined, draft, rejected);
  3. Evaluating physical applicability envelopes against active `OperationalBoundary` entities;
  4. Expanding graph relationships from atomic `KnowledgeClaim` to its parent `CausalModel`,
     `OperationalBoundary`, `ClaimAttribution`, and active `ConflictRecord`;
  5. Surfacing alternative causal pathways and plausible competing mechanisms;
  6. Preserving active scientific disputes and conflicting evidence packages;
  7. Assembling a compact, bounded, structured `KnowledgeContextPackage`;
  8. Emitting a complete, immutable `RetrievalTrace` for Phase 1B auditability;
  9. Explicitly signaling `INSUFFICIENT_VALIDATED_KNOWLEDGE` when the library lacks coverage.

6.2 WHAT RETRIEVAL MUST NOT DO (STRICT PROHIBITIONS)
Retrieval is strictly forbidden from:
  1. Diagnosing the audio problem (e.g. asserting "The guitar tone is muddy because of 250 Hz buildup");
  2. Selecting the final engineering intervention (e.g. deciding to apply an EQ cut or insert a pedal);
  3. Deciding which competing hypothesis is true (e.g. choosing between transformer saturation vs. speaker cone breakup);
  4. Silently resolving or suppressing scientific disagreements;
  5. Translating physical sound concepts into AmpliTube 5 gear, GUIDs, or slider values;
  6. Rewriting or summarizing canonical knowledge claims in ways that distort boundaries;
  7. Converting descriptive symptom tags directly into deterministic prescriptive fixes;
  8. Masking retrieval insufficiency by padding context with loosely related trivia.

6.3 CONTRACTUAL HANDOFF TO PHASE 1C.5 (REASONING ENGINE)
  - Retrieval provides: "Here are the physical laws, causal models, operational boundaries,
    known trade-offs, and competing theories relevant to the acoustic conditions described."
  - Phase 1C.5 Sound Engineer executes: "Based on these validated principles, the observed
    signal measurements, and the user's aesthetic target, I hypothesize that X is occurring,
    weigh the trade-off between Y and Z, and decide on action W."

8. QUERY FORMATION ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Query formation is the translation bridge between raw Case Evidence and the structured
`KnowledgeNeed`.

8.1 THE TRANSLATION PIPELINE
Raw Case Evidence consists of objective measurements and user reports:
  - Evidence: "FFT shows heavy energy concentration below 120 Hz entering high-gain stage;
    transient onset shows 45 ms smearing; user notes palm-muted notes lack tightness."

The Query Formation Architecture converts this evidence into an epistemic query without
prejudging the solution:
  1. Metric Extraction: Maps DSP measurements to physical phenomena:
     - Excess sub-120 Hz energy -> `LF_HEADROOM_CONSUMPTION`, `INTERMODULATION_SUSCEPTIBILITY`.
     - 45 ms smear -> `TIME_DOMAIN_RECOVERY`, `BIAS_EXCURSION`.
  2. Stage Identification: Tags the physical location in the signal flow:
     - `SIGNAL_LEVEL_GAIN_STAGING` (Domain 2), `NONLINEAR_PROCESSING` (Domain 3),
       `AMPLIFIER_PREAMP_STAGES` (Domain 6).
  3. Causal Expansion Request: Directs retrieval to search for mechanisms causing
     transient smearing and low-frequency saturation.
  4. Boundary Envelopes: Extracts operating parameters:
     - Passive magnetic pickup (high inductance), high-gain triode stages (operating in Class A).

8.2 SAFEGUARD AGAINST DIAGNOSTIC CREEP
Query formation does NOT say:
  "Find why we should cut 100 Hz before the amp."
Query formation says:
  "Find the physical and circuit interactions between pre-distortion low-frequency bandwidth,
  tube grid conduction, intermodulation distortion, and transient attack clarity."

9. RETRIEVAL FACET MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To balance precision, recall, and safety, retrieval dimensions are categorized into
four distinct functional tiers:

9.1 TIER 1: HARD ELIGIBILITY FILTERS (BOOLEAN GATES)
Candidates failing these filters are immediately discarded:
  - `lifecycle_state`: Must be `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` (quarantined or review drafts rejected).
  - `operational_boundary_incompatibility`: Claims whose boundaries explicitly exclude
    the active case physical topology are gated out.
  - `licensing_compliance`: Unlicensed raw full-text is strictly non-retrievable.

9.2 TIER 2: QUERY EXPANSION FACETS (GRAPH TRAVERSAL)
Used to broaden search along structural and physical relationships:
  - `causal_model_id`: Pulls all interconnected claims within an active causal network.
  - `competing_claim_ids`: Pulls opposing viewpoints linked via `ConflictRecord`.
  - `upstream_prerequisites`: Pulls foundational physics underlying an empirical rule.

9.3 TIER 3: RELEVANCE & SCORING FEATURES (MULTI-DIMENSIONAL RANKING)
Used to rank candidate claims within the eligible set:
  - `domain_jurisdiction`: Proximity to the core problem (`J_CORE` > `J_APPLIED` > `J_PERIPHERAL`).
  - `epistemic_basis`: Intrinsic scientific grounding (`PHYSICAL_LAW` > `ESTABLISHED_PRINCIPLE` > `EMPIRICAL_RELATIONSHIP`).
  - `source_rigor_tier`: Methodological caliber of origin document (`R1` through `R5`).
  - `evidence_strength` & `consensus_state`: Qualitative robustness descriptors.
  - `semantic_similarity`: Cosine similarity between query intent and claim vector.
  - `lexical_overlap`: BM25 score against technical formula and parameter tokens.

9.4 TIER 4: PRESENTATION & REASONING METADATA
Carried alongside the claim into the context package for the Sound Engineer:
  - `OperationalBoundary` constraints and known failure modes.
  - Known trade-offs and acoustic side-effects.
  - Compact attribution stubs and bibliographic locators.
  - Conflict markers and divergent evidence summaries.

  - Pure knowledge graphs struggle with fuzzy, naturalistic user symptom descriptions.

11. STRUCTURED RETRIEVAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Structured retrieval executes deterministic SQL/NoSQL filtering against the strongly
typed metadata schemas defined in Phase 1C.4a.

11.1 DETERMINISTIC FILTER EXECUTION
Structured retrieval evaluates:
  1. `lifecycle_state == VALIDATED_ACTIVE`: Eliminates draft, under-review, or deprecated claims.
  2. `domain_id IN target_domains`: Restricts candidates to relevant engineering domains
     (e.g. Domain 2: Gain Staging, Domain 6: Amplifiers).
  3. `physical_scope == target_scope`: Ensures circuit claims match circuit needs and
     transducer claims match acoustic capture needs.
  4. `operational_boundary_compatibility`: Executes boolean boundary checks against
     active circuit/acoustic conditions (e.g. checking whether signal level falls within
     the claim's documented valid range).

11.2 PERFORMANCE CHARACTERISTICS
Structured filtering runs in sub-millisecond time and eliminates up to 90% of the candidate
library before costly vector or graph expansion operations are initiated.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. LEXICAL RETRIEVAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Lexical retrieval employs BM25 scoring over specialized technical indices containing
exact acoustic and circuit terminology.

12.1 TECHNICAL VOCABULARY MATCHING
Lexical search is essential for:
  - Specific electronic component types: `12AX7`, `grid-leak resistor`, `bright cap`, `Alnico V`;
  - Formal mathematical constants and units: `dBFS`, `dBu`, `0 VU`, `Hz`, `Q-factor`, `THD+N`;
  - Exact standard identifiers: `AES17`, `EBU R128`, `IEC 60268`;
  - Named acoustic phenomena: `Fletcher-Munson`, `comb filtering`, `Haas effect`, `proximity effect`.

12.2 FAILURE MODES OF PURE LEXICAL RETRIEVAL
Lexical search fails when practitioners use informal, colloquial, or poetic vocabulary
(e.g. "blooming mids," "fizzy top," "sponge," "chug"). For this reason, lexical retrieval
operates in tandem with semantic vector retrieval.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. SEMANTIC RETRIEVAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Semantic retrieval maps natural language queries, clinical listening observations,
and symptom descriptions into dense vector representations to bridge conceptual and
terminological vocabularies.

13.1 VOCABULARY BRIDGING & SYNONYM BRIDGING
Semantic retrieval translates practitioner idioms into professional engineering concepts:
  - "Flubby / loose bottom end" -> Low-frequency phase lag, intermodulation distortion,
    under-damped speaker resonance, excessive pre-distortion LF energy.
  - "Harsh / bees in a jar fizz" -> Odd-order harmonic clustering above 4 kHz,
    direct on-axis acoustic beaming, absence of speaker inductive roll-off.
  - "Boxy / hollow tone" -> Mid-frequency comb filtering (300-800 Hz), acoustic boundary
    reflection, out-of-phase summing of dual microphone capsules.

13.2 STRICT SEPARATION OF SIMILARITY FROM TRUTH
  - Mathematical Definition: Cosine similarity measures geometric alignment in embedding
    space; it measures *topical relatedness*, NOT *epistemic truth*.
  - Strict Prohibition: The retrieval engine is architecturally forbidden from using vector
    similarity as a surrogate for evidence strength, replication state, or scientific consensus.
    A high-similarity practitioner anecdote remains an anecdote; a lower-similarity physical
    law remains an immutable constraint.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Retrieving an atomic `KnowledgeClaim` in isolation is an epistemic hazard. In professional
sound engineering, a claim stripped of its causal mechanism, validity envelope, or
provenance is dangerous.

14.2 MANDATORY GRAPH EXPANSION RULES
  1. Claim -> CausalModel: If `causal_model_id` is populated, the parent `CausalModel`
     (including input variables, output effects, mediating mechanisms, and trade-off vectors)
     is retrieved.
  2. Claim -> OperationalBoundary: If `operational_boundary_id` is populated, the boundary
     envelope (valid ranges, prerequisites, failure modes) MUST accompany the claim.
     Dropping the boundary while presenting the claim is a fatal architectural violation.
  3. Claim -> ConflictRecord: If `epistemic_validation.conflict_status == ACTIVE_COMPETING_CLAIM`,
     the associated `ConflictRecord` and the competing counterpart claim MUST be retrieved.
  4. Claim -> Attribution Stub: The `source_id`, exact locator, and rigor tier are attached
     for end-to-end auditability.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
15. CAUSAL EXPANSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sound engineering diagnostics require reasoning from effect back to multiple plausible
causes, and from proposed intervention forward to unintended side-effects.

15.1 STRUCTURAL CAUSAL TRAVERSAL
The retrieval engine traverses the `CausalModel` entities along five directional axes:
  1. Upstream Cause Traversal: "Given effect Y (e.g. low-frequency transient smear),
     what physical mechanisms X1, X2, X3 can generate Y?"
  2. Downstream Consequence Traversal: "If variable A (e.g. pre-distortion bass) is altered,
     what downstream nonlinearities and spectral shifts occur at stages B, C, and D?"
  3. Precondition Verification: "Does mechanism M require high signal amplitude, specific
     transducer impedance, or asymmetric clipping to manifest?"
  4. Confounder Identification: "What extraneous variables (e.g. room modes, pick attack
     velocity, pickup height) could produce effect Y independently of mechanism M?"
  5. Trade-off Vector Extraction: "What acoustic or electrical property is degraded when
     optimizing for mechanism M (e.g. cutting bass restores tightness but thins single-note body)?"

15.2 SUPPORTING HYPOTHESIS FORMATION WITHOUT PERFORMING IT
Causal expansion arms the Sound Engineer with the complete causal landscape. It does not
declare: "X is the cause of Y in this track." It delivers: "Physical science identifies
X1, X2, and X3 as distinct mechanisms capable of producing Y under these conditions."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16. OPERATIONAL BOUNDARY HANDLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The most catastrophic error in automated engineering advice is applying a principle
outside its physical domain of validity.

16.1 THE FOUR-STATE APPLICABILITY ENVELOPE
Every candidate claim's `OperationalBoundary` is evaluated against active Case Evidence:

+---------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+
| APPLICABILITY STATE       | RETRIEVAL ACTION                                      |
+---------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. STRICTLY APPLICABLE    | Delivered into primary context; fully qualified for   |
|                           | active engineering reasoning.                         |
+---------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. CONDITIONALLY          | Delivered with explicit boundary warnings and missing |
|    APPLICABLE             | prerequisite parameters flagged for the Engineer.     |
+---------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. APPLICABILITY UNKNOWN  | Delivered only if query explicitly requests exploratory|
|                           | hypotheses; marked `UNVERIFIED_BOUNDARY_STATUS`.      |
+---------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+
| 4. STRICTLY OUT-OF-BOUNDS | GATED OUT of primary context. Forbidden from entering |
|                           | reasoning context as an applicable recommendation.    |
+---------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+

16.2 CONCRETE BOUNDARY EXCLUSION EXAMPLES
  - Transducer Boundary: Proximity effect physics apply strictly to pressure-gradient
    transducers (figure-8, cardioid). If Case Evidence specifies an omnidirectional
    pressure transducer, proximity-based LF claims are STRICTLY OUT-OF-BOUNDS.
  - Circuit Topology Boundary: Push-pull power amp claims (even-harmonic cancellation)
    are STRICTLY OUT-OF-BOUNDS if the active topology is a single-ended Class-A circuit.
  - Signal Level Boundary: Small-signal linear circuit equations are STRICTLY OUT-OF-BOUNDS
    when the active stage is driven 20 dB into deep saturation.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
17. CONFLICT-AWARE RETRIEVAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Legitimate scientific divergence, competing circuit designs, and conflicting studio
methodologies are permanent features of professional sound engineering.

17.1 FIRST-CLASS CONFLICT DELIVERY
When a retrieved claim is marked with `conflict_status: ACTIVE_COMPETING_CLAIM`:
  1. The retrieval engine locates the corresponding `ConflictRecord`.
  2. The opposing competing claim(s) are retrieved regardless of similarity ranking.
  3. A unified `ConflictPackage` is constructed containing:
     - The competing propositions;
     - The divergence nature (`BOUNDARY_DIVERGENCE`, `METHODOLOGICAL_DIFFERENCE`,
       `DIRECT_CONTRADICTION`, `TERMINOLOGICAL_AMBIGUITY`);
     - The respective source rigor tiers and evidence profiles;
     - The boundary conditions under which each position claims validity.

17.2 ANTI-CONSENSUS ENFORCEMENT
Retrieval is strictly prohibited from:
  - Averaging competing numerical parameters (e.g. if Source A recommends 80 Hz high-pass
    and Source B recommends 140 Hz high-pass, retrieval must NEVER deliver "110 Hz");
  - Suppressing a minority view supported by rigorous empirical testing in favor of a
    popular textbook convention;
  - Hiding conflicts behind an opaque composite score.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18. UNCERTAINTY PRESERVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Professional sound engineers work under constant physical uncertainty: component
tolerances, room variations, transducer drift, and psychoacoustic variability.
Retrieval must expose uncertainty, not conceal it behind false precision.

18.1 QUALITATIVE UNCERTAINTY DESCRIPTORS
Every retrieved claim retains its frozen Phase 1C.4a qualitative descriptors:
  - `evidence_strength`: THEORETICAL_PROOF, SYSTEMATIC_EMPIRICAL, CORROBORATED_PRACTICE, ANECDOTAL_OBSERVATION.
  - `consensus_state`: ESTABLISHED_CONSENSUS, PREDOMINANT_VIEW, DEBATED_VIEW, OUTLIER_HYPOTHESIS.
  - `replication_state`: INDEPENDENTLY_REPLICATED, LABORATORY_VERIFIED, OBSERVATIONAL_UNREPLICATED.
  - `boundary_certainty`: TIGHTLY_BOUNDED, PARTIALLY_BOUNDED, EXPLORATORY_LIMITS.

18.2 REJECTION OF SCALAR PERCENTAGES
Retrieval strictly rejects scalar confidence numbers (e.g. "Confidence: 87.4%").
Such numbers are mathematically meaningless in sound engineering epistemology.
A claim is either theoretically derived, empirically replicated, corroborated in practice,
or an unverified practitioner observation.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
19. ALTERNATIVE MECHANISM RETRIEVAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A hallmark of amateur engineering is fixing on the first apparent cause of a problem.
Professional competency (Phase 1C.3a Domain 19: Diagnostic Acumen) requires evaluating
competing hypotheses.

19.1 STRUCTURAL RETRIEVAL DIVERSITY
When a symptom or phenomenon is queried, the retrieval engine enforces a Diversity
Guarantee across distinct physical domains:
  - Example: Query on "High-Frequency Harshness / Piercing Fizz":
    - Hypothesis 1 (Transducer/Acoustic): On-axis microphone high-frequency beaming (Domain 10).
    - Hypothesis 2 (Cabinet/Loudspeaker): Extended breakup modes of speaker dust cap (Domain 8).
    - Hypothesis 3 (Circuit/Amplifier): Power amp negative feedback instability or crossover distortion (Domain 6).
    - Hypothesis 4 (Gain Staging/Pedal): Diode clipping slew-rate limiting and intermodulation (Domain 3).

19.2 RETRIEVAL DIVERSITY VS. PREMATURE DIAGNOSIS
Retrieval does not decide which of these four hypotheses is active in the track. It ensures
that the AI Sound Engineer's reasoning context is populated with all four physically
plausible mechanisms, enabling genuine diagnostic deliberation.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
20. ANTI-RECIPE SAFEGUARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Current TT suffered from hardcoded keyword heuristics that short-circuited engineering
thought (e.g. "if metal guitar -> insert OverScream pedal with Drive=0, Level=10").

20.1 ARCHITECTURAL FIREWALLS AGAINST RECIPES
  1. No Direct Gear Prescriptions: Canonical claims state physical relationships
     (e.g. "Attenuating low-frequency content prior to asymmetric triode clipping reduces
     intermodulation distortion and sharpens transient leading edges"), NOT specific gear commands.
  2. Bounded Contextual Practices: Where a historical studio practice is retrieved
     (e.g. "Using a mid-hump overdrive pedal before a British high-gain amplifier"), it
     is tagged with `knowledge_role: CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_PRACTICE` and accompanied by its
     underlying electrical causal model (pre-filtering EQ curve + low-gain boost).
  3. Platform Neutrality: Preset names, knob values, and plugin manufacturers are excluded
     from the knowledge layer. The Sound Engineer reasons in frequency (Hz), level (dB),
     time (ms), impedance (Ohms), and harmonic ratios.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
21. DEVICE-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE HANDLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sound engineering balances universal acoustics with idiosyncratic device behaviors
(e.g. the specific mid-frequency scoop of a Fender blackface tone stack vs. the active
mid-boost of a Marshall JCM800).

21.1 STRICT TYPING OF DEVICE-SPECIFIC BEHAVIOR
  - Explicit Enum Marker: Any claim addressing specific commercial equipment must be
    tagged with `physical_scope: DEVICE_SPECIFIC_BEHAVIOUR`.
  - Upstream Physical Rigor: Documented schematics, transformer saturation thresholds,
    and voice-coil impedance curves are valid engineering knowledge (Source Class E or F).
  - Prohibition of Downstream AT5 Details: Downstream software artifacts (e.g. AmpliTube 5
    gear IDs, XML preset nodes, GUI pixel coordinates, VPA engine flags) are STRICTLY
    FORBIDDEN in the Knowledge Library.

21.2 PRECEDENCE & COMPLEMENTARITY
General physical laws establish the primary baseline of validity. Device-specific
knowledge provides concrete parametric and topological instances of those laws.
Neither replaces the other:
  - An amplifier tone-stack schematic shows a specific RC filter implementation;
  - Kirchhoff's Laws and wave filter theory govern how that RC filter operates.

23. RANKING & RELEVANCE ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ranking in Phase 1C.4c is strictly defined as RELEVANCE TO THE KNOWLEDGE NEED,
never as a pseudo-scientific "Truth Score."

23.1 RELEVANCE SCORING MECHANICS
Candidate claims passing the Tier 1 boolean eligibility gates are scored across three
independent dimensions:
  1. Dimensional Relevance Score (R_dim):
     - Domain proximity (J_CORE = 1.0, J_APPLIED = 0.7, J_PERIPHERAL = 0.3);
     - Stage match (Exact signal stage match = 1.0, adjacent stage = 0.5);
     - Epistemic robustness (Theoretical proof / Systematic empirical = 1.0,
       Corroborated practice = 0.8, Anecdotal = 0.4).
  2. Semantic / Lexical Fusion Score (R_text):
     - Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) combining BM25 keyword rank and dense embedding cosine rank:
       RRF_Score = 1 / (60 + Rank_BM25) + 1 / (60 + Rank_Dense).
  3. Causal Centrality Score (R_causal):
     - Graph distance to the queried phenomenon in the active `CausalModel` (Direct cause = 1.0,
       mediating mechanism = 0.8, secondary side-effect = 0.5).

23.2 INTERNAL RETRIEVAL FUNCTION VS. EPISTEMIC AUTHORITY
If an implementation computes a composite ranking score for sorting candidates within
a token budget, this score represents RETRIEVAL UTILITY ONLY. It is stripped from the
presentation payload to prevent the Sound Engineer from mistaking a high retrieval score
for immutable physical truth.

25. CONTEXT BUDGETING & COMPRESSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LLM reasoning contexts are finite. When the retrieved knowledge exceeds the configured
token budget, the engine applies principled, non-destructive pruning.

25.1 THE QUALIFIER PRESERVATION MANDATE
Under no circumstances may an `OperationalBoundary` or critical qualifier be stripped
from a `KnowledgeClaim` merely to save tokens while keeping the claim text.
A claim separated from its boundary is an epistemic falsehood.

25.2 GRACEFUL PRUNING STRATEGY
When token pressure is detected:
  1. Prune Tier 4 Presentation Verbosity: Shorten full textual derivations to LaTeX
     formula summaries;
  2. Prune Incidental Contextual Practices: Retain foundational physical laws and causal
     models; prune secondary illustrative studio anecdotes;
  3. Prune Distant Causal Nodes: Retain direct mechanisms (depth=1); prune depth=2 side-effects;
  4. Preserve Diversity: Never eliminate an entire alternative domain hypothesis to keep
     multiple redundant claims from a single domain.

29. RETRIEVAL FAILURE & INSUFFICIENCY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A fundamental failure mode in AI systems is the compulsion to return an answer even
when no authoritative knowledge exists, leading to hallucination or trivial filler.

29.1 THE EXPLICIT INSUFFICIENCY PROTOCOL
When the canonical library lacks validated, applicable knowledge for an issued
`KnowledgeNeed`, the retrieval engine must explicitly declare:

    RETRIEVAL_STATUS: INSUFFICIENT_VALIDATED_KNOWLEDGE

29.2 TYPED INSUFFICIENCY STATES
The system emits one of five distinct insufficiency codes:
  1. `INSUFFICIENT_DOMAIN_COVERAGE`: The library contains zero `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` claims
     for the targeted physical domain.
  2. `ALL_CANDIDATES_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`: Relevant claims exist topically, but all active
     `OperationalBoundary` entities exclude the current signal-chain parameters.
  3. `UNRESOLVED_CRITICAL_CONFLICT`: A fundamental conflict exists with zero empirical
     boundary data, rendering automated retrieval unsafe without human expert guidance.
  4. `MISSING_PREREQUISITE_EVIDENCE`: The query requires specific DSP metrics (e.g. THD+N
     or acoustic impulse response) that Case Evidence has not provided.
  5. `PROVENANCE_FAIL`: Candidates were rejected due to unverified citation lineage.

29.3 NO SILENT PADDING
The engine is strictly forbidden from fetching unrelated studio trivia or generic music
theory to fill the context package when technical engineering coverage is missing.

30. MODEL PARAMETRIC KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The AI Sound Engineer will possess vast pre-trained parametric knowledge from its base LLM.
Retrieval architecture must enforce an unmistakable boundary between what the library
authorizes and what the model independently conjectures.

30.1 PROVENANCE TAGGING IN REASONING
  - Canonical Propositions: Any assertion grounded in the retrieved package carries
    a claim citation (e.g. `[TT-KC-D03-004]`).
  - Parametric Propositions: If the model uses ungrounded pre-trained intuition, downstream
    reasoning prompts will mandate that it tag such statements as `[PARAMETRIC_HYPOTHESIS]`.
  - Non-Preemption: Model parametric intuition can NEVER override, contradict, or dilute
    a retrieved `PHYSICAL_LAW` or `ESTABLISHED_ENGINEERING_PRINCIPLE`.

31. SOURCE CONTENT / PROMPT-INJECTION BOUNDARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Acquired audio literature contains natural human phrasing, historical slang, and
occasionally imperative commands (e.g. "Never set your bias below 35 mA under any

31.1 DATA AS DATA, NEVER AS INSTRUCTION
The retrieval subsystem enforces strict structural isolation:
  - Canonical knowledge statements are serialized as inert JSON strings within bounded

  - Strict Tokenizer Delimiters: Injected context is wrapped in immutable structural
    XML blocks:
    `<canonical_sound_engineering_knowledge type="inert_data" eval_mode="passive"> ... </canonical_sound_engineering_knowledge>`
  - Instruction Stripping: The normalization gate in Phase 1C.4b strips second-person
    imperatives ("You must...", "Ignore previous rules...") and restructures all claims
    into declarative physical assertions.

33. PERFORMANCE & LATENCY PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Real-time audio engineering workflows demand responsive reasoning loops without
compromising epistemic rigor.

33.1 HIGH-PERFORMANCE RUNTIME PIPELINE
  1. Immutable Read-Only Indexes: Runtime indexes (vector, inverted BM25, and relational)
     are compiled offline into memory-mapped, immutable structures.
  2. Sub-50ms Retrieval Latency Target:
     - Stage 1: Structured boolean filter (< 5ms);
     - Stage 2: Lexical BM25 + Vector HNSW lookup (< 20ms);
     - Stage 3: Graph expansion & boundary evaluation (< 15ms);
     - Stage 4: Package assembly & pruning (< 10ms).
  3. Zero Runtime Ingestion: No document parsing, OCR, or embedding generation occurs
     during the live engineering reasoning loop.

34. KNOWLEDGE UPDATE / INDEX REFRESH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When the canonical Knowledge Library changes in Git (e.g. human expert signs off on a
new claim or updates a boundary in Phase 1C.4d), the runtime index updates cleanly
without service interruption.

34.1 DISPOSABLE DERIVATIVE ARCHITECTURE
  - The runtime index is a 100% disposable derivative of the Git-tracked canonical library.
  - Hot-Swap Index Compilation: A background build agent detects a new signed Git commit,
    compiles fresh vector embeddings and relational graph tables, validates the index
    against regression test suites, and executes an atomic pointer hot-swap.
  - Zero Runtime Drift: A running session binds to an immutable `index_version` hash,
    ensuring that a session never experiences mid-run knowledge shifts.

35. RETRIEVAL TESTABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Retrieval quality must be evaluated and verified independently of the downstream
AI Sound Engineer's reasoning quality.

35.1 INDEPENDENT RETRIEVAL BENCHMARKS
The retrieval subsystem is tested via five specialized test harnesses:
  1. Golden Intent Recall (GIR): Evaluates whether a known physical challenge retrieves
     100% of the mandatory foundational physical laws (e.g. testing whether Ohm's Law and
     Kirchhoff's Laws are retrieved for impedance mismatches).
  2. Out-of-Bounds Rejection (OBR): Injects queries deliberately violating boundary
     conditions to verify that 100% of out-of-bounds claims are gated out.
  3. Conflict Surfacing Precision (CSP): Verifies that queries touching contested areas
     (e.g. tube rectifier sag vs. solid-state diode speed) surface both competing claims
     and the active `ConflictRecord`.
  4. Anti-Recipe Audit (ARA): Tests that symptom queries NEVER return hardcoded gear
     prescriptions or single-brand plugins.
  5. Deterministic Replay Verification (DRV): Verifies that identical query inputs over
     an identical index hash produce bit-for-bit identical context packages.

39. ARCHITECTURAL DECISIONS (DECISIONS A THROUGH P)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 1C.4c formally resolves the sixteen core architectural decisions:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION A: PRIMARY RETRIEVAL ARCHITECTURE
Resolution: Hybrid Coordinated Pipeline.
Rationale: Single-modality engines fail in audio engineering. The architecture unifies
structured boolean filtering (lifecycle and boundary checks), lexical BM25 matching
(exact component models and formulas), dense semantic search (concept bridging), and
relational graph traversal (causal and boundary expansion).

DECISION B: CANONICAL SOURCE VS. RUNTIME INDEX AUTHORITY
Resolution: Canonical Git Repository is 100% Authoritative; Runtime Indexes are Disposable Derivatives.
Rationale: Prevents index drift, proprietary lock-in, and synchronization anomalies.
Any index corruption is resolved by deleting and rebuilding from the signed Git commit.

DECISION C: ELIGIBILITY OF LIFECYCLE STATES FOR RUNTIME RETRIEVAL
Resolution: `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` Only for Production Runtime Context.
Rationale: Unvalidated drafts (`CAPTURED`, `EXTRACTED`, `NORMALIZED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`),
quarantined feedback (`CandidateLesson`), and dead assets (`DEPRECATED`, `REJECTED`)
must never contaminate live sound engineering reasoning.

DECISION D: RETRIEVAL RANKING NUMERIC SCORES
Resolution: Ranking Scores are Purely Mechanical Retrieval Tools, NOT Epistemic Truth.
Rationale: Internal sorting numbers reflect query relevance and token utility. They are
stripped from presentation context to prevent the Sound Engineer from confusing a high
similarity score with physical certainty.

DECISION E: REQUIRED RELATIONSHIP EXPANSION FOR KNOWLEDGECLAIM
Resolution: Mandatory Graph Traversal to CausalModel, OperationalBoundary, Attribution, and ConflictRecord.
Rationale: An atomic claim isolated from its causal mechanisms and valid operating
envelope is epistemically unsafe and leads directly to incorrect studio interventions.

DECISION F: OPERATIONALBOUNDARY PARTICIPATION
Resolution: Hard Ineligibility Filter for Strict Incompatibilities; Contextual Warning for Conditional Bounds.
Rationale: Prevents physical laws applicable to pressure-gradient microphones from
being applied to pressure omni microphones, and prevents push-pull math from being applied
to single-ended circuits.

DECISION G: CONFLICTRECORD SURFACING
Resolution: First-Class Delivery of Complete Conflict Packages Without Forced Consensus.
Rationale: Scientific disputes and divergent studio practices must be presented with
their competing claims, divergence nature, and respective evidence profiles intact.

DECISION H: COMPETING MECHANISMS RETRIEVAL
Resolution: Structural Multi-Domain Diversity Guarantee.
Rationale: Prevents premature diagnostic fixation by ensuring that queries regarding
acoustic symptoms retrieve physically plausible explanations across multiple domains
(transducers, electronics, acoustics, psychoacoustics).

DECISION I: DEVICE-SPECIFIC VS. GENERAL KNOWLEDGE PRECEDENCE
Resolution: General Physical Principles Form the Baseline; Device Knowledge Provides Concrete Instances.
Rationale: Universal physics governs what is possible; device-specific data specifies
how a particular commercial circuit or speaker responds within that physical envelope.

DECISION J: RUNTIME PROVENANCE PAYLOAD DEPTH
Resolution: Compact Attribution Stubs with Cryptographic Locator Hashes.
Rationale: Preserves finite LLM token budgets while guaranteeing instantaneous,
unambiguous auditability back to exact page/paragraph sources in the archive.

DECISION K: CONTEXT-BUDGET REDUCTION STRATEGY
Resolution: Non-Destructive Pruning Preserving Boundaries and Causal Diversity.
Rationale: Verbosity and incidental anecdotes are pruned first; operational boundaries
and alternative hypotheses are NEVER dropped while retaining their associated claims.

DECISION L: RETRIEVAL INSUFFICIENCY SEMANTICS
Resolution: Explicit `INSUFFICIENT_VALIDATED_KNOWLEDGE` Status with 5 Typed Codes.
Rationale: Halts hallucination and prevents the system from padding prompts with
trivial or irrelevant filler when authoritative engineering knowledge is absent.

DECISION M: RETRIEVAL TRACE & REPLAY CONTRACT
Resolution: Immutable, Complete Execution Trace Conforming to Phase 1B Observability.
Rationale: Records every query intent, candidate count, boundary exclusion, and index
hash to enable 100% deterministic replay and independent post-session review.

DECISION N: MODEL PARAMETRIC KNOWLEDGE DISTINCTION
Resolution: Mandatory Provenance Tagging Between Canonical Claims and Parametric Hypotheses.
Rationale: Prevents opaque LLM training weights from masquerading as validated TT
sound engineering authority.

DECISION O: SOURCE-CONTENT / PROMPT-INJECTION BOUNDARY
Resolution: Strict Inert Data Enclosure in JSON/XML Structural Delimiters.
Rationale: Ensures natural language text from historical audio literature cannot execute
adversarial prompt injections or alter reasoning system instructions.

DECISION P: INDEX UPDATE & VERSIONING STRATEGY
Resolution: Deterministic Offline Index Compilation with Atomic Hot-Swapping.
Rationale: Guarantees zero downtime, zero runtime index mutation, and session-locked
version immutability.

41. FAILURE MODES & MITIGATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The architecture analyzes and mitigates twenty critical retrieval failure modes:

1. Semantic Drift: Dense vector search returning poetic metaphors instead of circuit physics.
   - Mitigation: Multi-stage hybrid filtering; mandatory domain and scope pre-filtering.
2. Boundary Blindness: Presenting a valid claim in an invalid physical circuit context.
   - Mitigation: Tier 1 Boolean boundary gate evaluates transducer, level, and topology rules.
3. Consensus Fabrication: Averaging conflicting numbers into a meaningless middle ground.
   - Mitigation: First-class `ConflictPackage` delivery; explicit anti-consensus rules.
4. Recipe Regression: Re-introducing "symptom -> fixed gear" lookups.
   - Mitigation: `KnowledgeNeed` schema enforces physical stage and mechanism queries, not gear requests.
5. Prestige Bias: Treating an R1 physics textbook comment on guitar tone as an invariant law.
   - Mitigation: Independent `domain_jurisdiction` scoring separates general standing from domain authority.
6. Vector Similarity Conflated with Truth: Mistaking 0.95 cosine similarity for scientific certainty.
   - Mitigation: Similarity scores are restricted to candidate selection; qualitative epistemic
     validation is displayed separately.
7. Qualifier Stripping: Dropping boundary conditions during context compression.
   - Mitigation: Architectural mandate: an `OperationalBoundary` can never be pruned while its claim remains.
8. Unbounded Practitioner Anecdotes: Treating a studio trick as a universal law.
   - Mitigation: Multi-axis classification restricts anecdotes to `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_PRACTICE`.
9. Silent Masquerading: Model parametric memory presented as canonical library knowledge.
   - Mitigation: Strict provenance tagging; mandatory citation checks during Phase 1B audit.
10. Feedback Contamination: Unvetted runtime lessons leaking into active retrieval.
    - Mitigation: CandidateLessons quarantined in separate database; hard eligibility filter gates them out.
11. Downstream Platform Leaks: AT5 GUIDs or XML nodes appearing in retrieved context.
    - Mitigation: Platform-independence firewall; automated schema linter rejects vendor tokens.
12. Retrieval Insufficiency Masking: Padding context with generic music trivia when knowledge is missing.
    - Mitigation: Explicit `INSUFFICIENT_VALIDATED_KNOWLEDGE` signaling protocol.
13. Diagnostic Creep: Retrieval subsystem attempting to diagnose the audio problem.
    - Mitigation: Responsibility boundary restricts retrieval to supplying principles and models.
14. Prompt Injection: Imperative source text altering agent system instructions.
    - Mitigation: Inert JSON string serialization; strict structural XML bounding delimiters.
15. Circular Lineage Masking: Multiple secondary articles presenting one study as multiple validations.
    - Mitigation: Graph traversal identifies shared `SourceDocument` ancestry and collapses duplicates.
16. False Precision in Search: Filtering on "3000.00 Hz" when source says "around 3 kHz."
    - Mitigation: Numerical typing engine supports `EMPIRICALLY_MEASURED_RANGE` and nominal bands.
17. Index Desynchronization: Runtime index diverging from canonical Git repository.
    - Mitigation: Index treated as disposable artifact; rebuild pipeline binds to signed Git commits.
18. Multi-Domain Siloing: Inability to traverse from microphone acoustics to preamp electronics.
    - Mitigation: Unified relational graph schema across all 22 competency domains.
19. Token Exhaustion: Retrieval exceeding LLM prompt window.
    - Mitigation: Structured four-tier context budgeting and non-destructive pruning.
20. Nondeterministic Audit Failure: Inability to reconstruct past engineering context packages.
    - Mitigation: Immutable `RetrievalTrace` logging snapshot hashes and candidate metadata.
