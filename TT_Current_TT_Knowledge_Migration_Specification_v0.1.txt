1.1 ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE & CONTEXT
Tone Translator (TT) is undergoing a fundamental architectural evolution: transitioning
from an AmpliTube 5 (AT5) preset-generation application into a platform-independent,
acoustically grounded, professional AI Sound Engineer architecture.

The target macro-architecture establishes a strict, unidirectional causal flow:

    INPUT / EVIDENCE
        ↓
    AI SOUND ENGINEER (Reasoning from foundational acoustic first principles)
        ↓
    ENGINEERING DECISION (Declarative, platform-agnostic diagnostic and intervention plans)
        ↓
    SEMANTIC TONE DESIGN (High-level abstract audio topology and parameterization)
        ↓
    DETERMINISTIC VALIDATION (Acoustic rule verification, conflict detection, bounds check)
        ↓
    PLATFORM TRANSLATOR (Target-specific semantic-to-hardware mapping engine)
        ↓
    PLATFORM-SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION / EXPORT (AmpliTube 5, Quad Cortex, Helix, Kemper, etc.)

In this future architecture, target platforms such as AmpliTube 5 remain supported
deployment targets, but they NEVER dictate, constrain, or define the Sound Engineer's
internal reasoning, diagnostic ontology, or epistemic knowledge base.

Current TT predates this architecture. As documented in the comprehensive archaeological
survey (/TT_Current_TT_Archaeological_Survey_v1.txt), the legacy codebase is an
archaeological accumulation of valuable sound-engineering knowledge, heuristic rules,
genre conventions, artist recipes, post-generation deterministic overrides, prompt
instructions, platform-specific quirks, and dormant scaffolding.

Before the new AI Sound Engineer architecture can be populated with knowledge, a formal,
rigorous specification must govern how Current TT legacy assets will be decomposed,
evaluated, classified, preserved, relocated, investigated, or retired. This document
establishes that definitive migration specification.

1.4 CORE EPISTEMIC AXIOMS
The entire migration methodology is anchored in four foundational epistemic axioms:

AXIOM 1: CURRENT TT IS CLASSIFIED AS UNASSESSED_LEGACY_SOURCE
Existing behaviour, code structures, prompts, and recipes possess ZERO automatic
epistemic authority merely because they are implemented in the application codebase.
Code presence proves that an implementation exists; it does not prove that the underlying
engineering proposition is true, optimal, or acoustically justified.

AXIOM 2: OBSOLETE IMPLEMENTATION DOES NOT IMPLY USELESS KNOWLEDGE
A dormant recipe, hardcoded override, unused helper, or obsolete test fixture may
encapsulate a profound physical or engineering proposition. Do not dismiss, delete,
or ignore an asset merely because its current code path is dead, bypassed, or poorly
implemented.

AXIOM 3: SUCCESSFUL OUTPUT DOES NOT PROVE CORRECT REASONING
A legacy prompt or deterministic override may have historically generated subjectively
acceptable presets for a handful of test cases. This coincidental or heuristic success
does NOT constitute proof that its embedded engineering assumptions, causal claims,
or acoustic theories are valid.

AXIOM 4: BINDING PROVENANCE & RISK-PROPORTIONAL EVIDENCE STANDARD
Candidate propositions extracted from Current TT must recover the best available
underlying provenance and validate the claim to an evidence standard appropriate to its
nature and risk.
There is NO universal requirement for primary laboratory research or peer-reviewed
academic literature across all claims. Low-risk genre conventions or historical
production practices require documentation of established professional consensus,
whereas high-risk physical or safety claims demand rigorous technical or electroacoustic
verification.

1.5 THE CORE MANDATE: ANTI-PATTERNS TO PREVENT
The migration methodology defined in this specification is designed to systematically
prevent the following critical failure modes:
  1. Recipes Becoming Engineering Laws:
     A specific setting (e.g. "set Tube Screamer drive to 1, tone to 7, level to 9 for thrash")
     must never be recorded as a general physical law. It is a contextual reference recipe,
     rooted in a specific equipment combination and historical aesthetic.
  2. Platform Quirks Becoming Sound Engineer Knowledge:
     AmpliTube 5 specific behaviors (e.g. digital gain staging anomalies, 4-stomp slot
     limits, specific GUID bindings) must never be encoded as Sound Engineer principles.
     They belong exclusively in the target-specific Platform Translator.
  3. Successful Historical Outputs Becoming Proof of Reasoning:
     The fact that a user liked a preset generated by a deterministic surgery must not be
     used to justify the surgical mutation as sound acoustic reasoning.
  4. Perceptual Shorthand Becoming Physical Fact:
     Subjective buzzwords ("warmth", "punch", "chug", "tightness", "glassy", "ice-pick")
     must not be treated as objective physical entities. They must be deconstructed into
     measurable spectral, dynamic, and temporal acoustic dimensions.
  5. Dead or Unused Capability Represented as Operational Capability:
     Scaffolding that is bypassed, disconnected, or isolated in shadow mode (e.g. audio
     input analysis, SoundEngineerService shadow mode) must be forensically audited and
     never presented as functioning runtime competence.
  6. Deterministic Implementation Logic Silently Inheriting AI Judgement:
     Hardcoded TypeScript overrides must not silently mutate or redesign engineering
     decisions made by the Sound Engineer. Truth checking and validation must report
     violations, not secretly replace decisions.
  7. Reference Case Evidence Silently Becoming Canonical Precedent:
     Historical recording recipes and album benchmark setups must not automatically
     bind future decisions. They must be quarantined behind a Reference Case Firewall.
  8. Useful Knowledge Lost When Obsolete Scaffolding Is Retired:
     Retiring dead code without first performing atomic deconstruction risks destroying
     valuable acoustic heuristics and platform calibration data embedded within it.

SECTION 2 — THE CANONICAL MIGRATION PIPELINE & ATOMIC DECONSTRUCTION METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 THE END-TO-END MIGRATION CHAIN
The migration process transforms legacy assets into formal, governed engineering knowledge
through an unbroken nine-stage pipeline:

  [ Stage 1: Legacy Asset Identification ]
         Identify discrete code, prompt, recipe, override, or schema asset in repository.
         ↓
  [ Stage 2: Atomic Deconstruction ]
         Isolate every discrete assertion, assumption, heuristic, rule, and calibration point.
         ↓
  [ Stage 3: Determine What It Actually Is ]
         Classify each atomic proposition against the 18 Disciplined Asset Categories.
         ↓
  [ Stage 4: Architectural Ownership Assignment ]
         Assign each atomic item to exactly one of the 10 Architectural Owners.
         ↓
  [ Stage 5: Epistemic Assessment & Risk Scoring ]
         Evaluate physical validity, operational envelope, falsifiability, and risk tier.
         ↓
  [ Stage 6: Primary Disposition & Multi-Annotation ]
         Assign exactly one Primary Disposition and apply secondary diagnostic tags.
         ↓
  [ Stage 7: Destination Mapping ]
         Route the item to its designated architectural repository or retirement archive.
         ↓
  [ Stage 8: Provenance Recovery ]
         Trace underlying physical, historical, or platform provenance (SourceDocument).
         ↓
  [ Stage 9: Formal Migration Record Creation ]
         Generate an immutable, auditable, machine-readable Migration Record.

2.2 THE 1-TO-MANY ATOMIC DECOMPOSITION PRINCIPLE
A single legacy asset in Current TT is rarely atomic. A single function, prompt section,
or recipe almost always conflates:
  - An acoustic principle (e.g. low-frequency phase cancellation);
  - A causal relationship (e.g. pre-distortion high-pass filtering prevents intermodulation);
  - A genre-specific convention (e.g. thrash metal rhythm guitar tracking);
  - A specific hardware choice (e.g. Ibanez TS9 Tube Screamer);
  - A platform-specific implementation parameter (e.g. AT5 OverScream GUID 102, Drive 1.0);
  - A deterministic override mechanism (e.g. adjusting pedal selection post-generation);
  - A user interface binding or test assertion.

THEREFORE: A SINGLE LEGACY ASSET MAY DECOMPOSE INTO MULTIPLE ATOMIC OUTPUTS
WITH DIFFERENT ARCHITECTURAL OWNERS, EPISTEMIC TYPES, AND DISPOSITIONS.

Decomposition Example:
The legacy function `adjustThrashPedalSelection()` in `src/services/geminiService.ts`:
  - Output 1 (Causal Model): Pre-gain bass cut reduces intermodulation distortion in high-gain
    amplification stages.
    -> Owner: Sound Engineer Knowledge. Category: Causal Relationship. Disposition: KEEP CANDIDATE.
  - Output 2 (Context Practice): Thrash metal rhythm guitar traditionally uses an overdrive pedal
    with Drive near zero and Level boosted to tighten low-end response.
    -> Owner: Sound Engineer Knowledge. Category: Context-Dependent Practice. Disposition: KEEP CANDIDATE.
  - Output 3 (Universalised Prescription): Thrash tones MUST ALWAYS use an OverScream pedal.
    -> Owner: Sound Engineer Knowledge. Category: Anti-Pattern. Disposition: NLR — NO LONGER REQUIRED.
  - Output 4 (Platform Translation): AT5 "OverScream" corresponds to IK Multimedia Model GUID 102.
    -> Owner: Platform Translator — AT5. Category: Platform Product Knowledge. Disposition: KEEP — RELOCATE.
  - Output 5 (Deterministic Override): Post-generation silent mutation of LLM pedal selection.
    -> Owner: None / Retire. Category: Anti-Pattern / Dead Code. Disposition: NLR — NO LONGER REQUIRED.

2.3 DECONSTRUCTION HEURISTICS & BOUNDARY RULES
Engineers performing migration deconstruction must apply four strict boundary rules:
  Rule 1: Separate Intent from Implementation.
    Ask: "What acoustic or musical problem was the original author attempting to solve?"
    Do not confuse the problem being solved with the hack used to solve it.
  Rule 2: Strip Target-Specific Hardware Bindings.
    Ask: "Does this proposition remain true if the guitarist plays through a real analog
    Mesa/Boogie Dual Rectifier, a Neural DSP Quad Cortex, or a Kemper Profiler?"
    If yes, it is platform-agnostic sound engineering knowledge. If no, it is platform-specific.
  Rule 3: Decouple Causal Physics from Stylistic Preference.
    Ask: "Is this governed by physical electroacoustic behavior, or is it an aesthetic choice
    characteristic of a specific musical subculture?"
  Rule 4: Identify Silent Redesigns.
    Ask: "Does this code silently replace a sound engineer's judgement, or does it merely
    verify that the output is physically valid?"

2.4 DECOUPLING SOUND ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE FROM PLATFORM IMPLEMENTATION
Current TT is deeply coupled to AmpliTube 5. To achieve platform independence:
  1. Acoustical Signal Chain Abstraction:
     The Sound Engineer reasons in terms of abstract functional blocks:
     Input Transducer -> Input Conditioning / Filter -> Non-linear Pre-amplification ->
     Passive Tone Stack -> Power Stage Saturation -> Complex Load / Acoustic Transducer ->
     Spatial / Environmental Acoustics -> Post-transducer Equalization and Dynamics.
  2. Platform Translator Boundary:
     The Platform Translator receives the abstract Semantic Tone Design and maps it onto
     the capabilities and quirks of the target environment (e.g. mapping an abstract
     "mid-boost overdrive with 720Hz hump" to an AT5 OverScream or a Helix Screamer).
  3. Platform Limitations Handled Gracefully:
     If a target platform cannot represent a design (e.g. AT5 has a 4-stomp slot limit
     and the design requires 5), the Platform Translator solves the constraint satisfaction
     problem or reports the limitation. The Sound Engineer's acoustic model is NOT altered
     to accommodate platform poverty.

SECTION 3 — DISCIPLINED ASSET & PROPOSITION TAXONOMY ("WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 NECESSITY OF EPISTEMIC & ONTOLOGICAL PRECISION
In Current TT, code comments, prompt instructions, deterministic overrides, and UI labels
freely interchange physical facts, genre rules of thumb, and software hacks.
To construct a professional engineering knowledge system, every extracted asset must be
categorized according to what it ACTUALLY is, not what it purports to be.

An asset cannot be assumed to be sound engineering knowledge merely because it resides
in a prompt, a "knowledge" file, a recipe, a service, or a configuration structure.

3.2 THE 18 CANONICAL ASSET CATEGORIES
Every atomic asset extracted from Current TT must be classified into exactly one of the
following 18 canonical categories:

1. ENGINEERING PRINCIPLE
   - Definition: A foundational, invariant physical, electrical, or acoustical law that
     holds universally across all audio production environments regardless of style or gear.
   - Criteria: Grounded in wave mechanics, electromagnetism, circuit theory, or acoustic physics.
     Falsifiable via standard laboratory measurement. Invariant across platforms.
   - Example: Inverse-square law of acoustic radiation; comb filtering caused by coherent
     signals arriving with temporal offset (tau = d / c); Ohm's law in speaker loading.

2. CAUSAL RELATIONSHIP / MODEL
   - Definition: A predictable, directional cause-and-effect relationship between an engineering
     intervention (parameter adjustment, component choice) and its physical signal outcome.
   - Criteria: Describes how altering X causes a change in Y across linear or non-linear stages.
   - Example: Attenuating low frequencies prior to asymmetric diode clipping reduces intermodulation
     distortion, increasing clarity and perceived attack tightness.

3. REASONING FRAMEWORK
   - Definition: A structured cognitive methodology, decision tree, or logical sequence used
     by a sound engineer to diagnose problems and construct interventions.
   - Criteria: Governs the sequence and dependencies of engineering choices; platform-independent.
   - Example: The 10-step systematic tone design process in the Master Prompt (diagnosing acoustic
     context before selecting transducers; setting amplifier gain structure before post-EQ).

4. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT ENGINEERING PRACTICE
   - Definition: An established engineering technique that is effective and appropriate within
     a well-defined acoustic, musical, or production context, but not universally applicable.
   - Criteria: Valid within specific operational boundaries (genre, instrument role, mix density);
     counterproductive if applied outside those boundaries.
   - Example: Scooping 800Hz by 4-6dB on high-gain rhythm guitars in dense modern metal mixes
     to create spectral space for vocal intelligibility and bass guitar fundamental.

5. PROFESSIONAL CONVENTION
   - Definition: A widely adopted, standardized studio practice or workflow consensus that
     practitioners rely upon for predictability and efficiency.
   - Criteria: Based on professional consensus and historical standardization rather than
     immutable physical laws; represents established best practice.
   - Example: Placing a cardioid dynamic microphone (e.g. Shure SM57) pointing at the speaker
     cone-cap boundary as the standard baseline guitar cabinet recording setup.

6. UNIVERSALISED PRESCRIPTION
   - Definition: A context-dependent practice or personal habit that has been dogmatically
     and incorrectly codified as an absolute, universal rule.
   - Criteria: Uses unconditional language ("always", "never", "must") for choices that are
     inherently contextual. Represents an epistemic error in legacy code.
   - Example: Master Prompt instruction stating: "NEVER boost bass on high-gain amplifiers"
     or "ALWAYS place an EQ after the cabinet".

7. SEMANTIC REPRESENTATION
   - Definition: A platform-agnostic, conceptual data structure or ontology used to describe
     sound, signal topology, gear capabilities, or spatial relationships.
   - Criteria: Abstract schema that captures domain concepts without platform-specific syntax.
   - Example: The 4D Microphone Placement coordinate model (X-axis radial, Y-axis lateral,
     Z-axis distance, Alpha orientation angle).

8. PHYSICAL / DEVICE-SPECIFIC ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE
   - Definition: Deep technical understanding of the physical construction, electronic circuit
     topology, component behavior, and electroacoustic response of real-world equipment.
   - Criteria: Pertains to real analog hardware; documented in schematics, datasheets, or bench tests.
   - Example: Marshall JCM800 2203 preamp topology utilizing cold clipper cathode stages;
     Celestion Vintage 30 speaker cone resonant peak around 2.5kHz - 3.0kHz.

9. PLATFORM-SPECIFIC PRODUCT / MODEL KNOWLEDGE
   - Definition: Technical facts, parameters, capabilities, limitations, and operational quirks
     of a specific digital simulation platform or software product.
   - Criteria: Applies strictly to the software implementation (e.g. AmpliTube 5, Helix, Kemper);
     irrelevant to real analog hardware or competing platforms.
   - Example: AmpliTube 5's internal model ID for the "British Tube Lead 800" is GUID 52;
     AT5 stomp chain supports a maximum of 4 serial slots in Section A.

10. PLATFORM CALIBRATION / TRANSLATION DATA
    - Definition: Quantitative mapping functions, calibration curves, and coordinate translation
      tables required to translate abstract semantic values into platform-specific parameter scales.
    - Criteria: Mathematical or lookup data connecting semantic models to platform parameters.
    - Example: AT5 VIR coordinate calibration table mapping normalized mic distance [0.0, 1.0]
      to actual millimeter grid bounds for the 4x12 Brit 800 cabinet model.

11. DETERMINISTIC EXECUTION / SERIALIZATION LOGIC
    - Definition: Pure software logic responsible for data transformation, XML document generation,
      binary encoding, file input/output, and schema compliance.
    - Criteria: Platform-specific execution code containing zero audio engineering judgement.
    - Example: `at5PresetBuilder.ts` constructing XML DOM nodes `<Stomp>`, `<Amp>`, `<Cab>`,
      and base64 encoding preset payloads for .at5p file export.

12. HISTORICAL PRODUCTION / CONTEXT INFORMATION
    - Definition: Factual, documentary records regarding how specific historical recordings,
      albums, or artist tones were originally engineered in real recording sessions.
    - Criteria: Grounded in historical evidence (studio logs, producer interviews, track sheets).
    - Example: Malcolm Young recording AC/DC's 'Jailbreak' using a 1963 Gretsch Jet Firebird
      with Filter'Tron pickup into a Marshall JTM45 / Super Lead amplifier and 4x12 cabinet.

13. PERCEPTUAL SHORTHAND
    - Definition: Psychoacoustic, metaphorical, or subjective vocabulary used by musicians and
      engineers to describe perceived tonal sensations.
    - Criteria: Subjective descriptors that lack direct physical units but correlate with
      measurable acoustic phenomena.
    - Example: "Chug" (high dynamic palm-mute energy in 80Hz - 160Hz with fast decay);
      "Ice-pick" (excessive uncompressed energy spike between 3.5kHz and 6kHz).

14. REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE
    - Definition: An empirical record of a specific historical audio engineering configuration,
      its input conditions, applied interventions, and documented listening outcomes.
    - Criteria: A concrete, bounded case study preserved for illustrative or benchmark purposes;
      not a generalizable rule.
    - Example: The Pantera 'Cowboys From Hell' benchmark preset configuration and listening test.

15. GOVERNANCE / INTEGRITY PRINCIPLE
    - Definition: Explicit safety limits, boundary constraints, and architectural invariants
      enforced to protect audio fidelity, prevent equipment damage, or maintain system integrity.
    - Criteria: Guardrails that prevent invalid, unphysical, or destructive states.
    - Example: Enforcing maximum digital gain ceiling (+12dB) to prevent inter-sample clipping;
      preventing simultaneous 180-degree phase inversion across summing parallel channels.

16. ANTI-PATTERN
    - Definition: A flawed engineering practice, inappropriate software hack, or incorrect
      assumption that produces degraded audio outcomes, fragile code, or epistemic distortion.
    - Criteria: Practices proven to be sub-optimal, misleading, or destructive.
    - Example: Inserting cascaded post-EQ blocks to compensate for incorrect microphone placement
      rather than moving the microphone; silent deterministic mutation of AI engineering plans.

17. CAPABILITY / COMPETENCY GAP
    - Definition: An area of professional sound engineering knowledge, reasoning, or sensory
      evaluation that Current TT completely lacks or implements in a deficient manner.
    - Criteria: Documented discrepancy against professional standards (Phase 1C.3b gap matrix).
    - Example: Inability to perform actual spectral listening analysis on incoming audio;
      lack of room acoustics and boundary reflection modeling.

18. UI / INFRASTRUCTURE / OBSERVABILITY MECHANISM
    - Definition: User interface components, state management stores, debug panels, telemetry
      logging, and test fixtures designed to support user interaction and system observability.
    - Criteria: Software infrastructure that does not participate in sound engineering reasoning.
    - Example: `MicPlacementManagementView.tsx` 2D grid visualization; `ToneProfileView.tsx`.

(19. DEAD / OBSOLETE IMPLEMENTATION)
    - Sub-type of UI/Infrastructure: Unreachable code paths, abandoned UI toggles, or unused services.
    - Example: The abandoned `Recipe` UI toggle in `App.tsx` and dormant `recipes.ts`.

3.3 BOUNDARY DISAMBIGUATION CRITERIA
When classifying candidate assets, engineers must apply the following disambiguation tests:

  Test A: Engineering Principle vs. Context-Dependent Practice
    - If the proposition holds true for ALL signals and acoustic systems (e.g. Nyquist theorem,
      acoustic phase addition), it is an ENGINEERING PRINCIPLE.
    - If the proposition is desirable in some genres or mixes but undesirable in others (e.g.
      high-pass filtering electric guitar at 100Hz), it is a CONTEXT-DEPENDENT PRACTICE.

  Test B: Causal Model vs. Universalised Prescription
    - If the proposition states: "If you do X, physical effect Y occurs under conditions Z",
      it is a CAUSAL MODEL.
    - If the proposition states: "You must always do X because Y is good", it is a
      UNIVERSALISED PRESCRIPTION (and must be flagged as an Anti-Pattern).

  Test C: Physical Device Knowledge vs. Platform Product Knowledge
    - If the statement describes the actual analog circuitry of a Tube Screamer (e.g. symmetrical
      silicon diode clipping in the op-amp feedback loop with a 720Hz high-pass filter),
      it is PHYSICAL / DEVICE-SPECIFIC ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE.
    - If the statement describes AmpliTube 5's digital model (e.g. "OverScream model GUID is 102
      and its Drive knob scales from 0.0 to 10.0"), it is PLATFORM-SPECIFIC PRODUCT KNOWLEDGE.

  Test D: Reference Case Evidence vs. Canonical Knowledge
    - If the asset represents "What was done on a specific recording or preset benchmark",
      it is REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE.
    - If the asset represents "How acoustic systems behave in general", it is declarative
      ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE.

3.4 TAXONOMIC CLASSIFICATION DECISION TREE
Engineers must traverse this 5-stage decision tree for every extracted asset:

  Step 1: Is this an invariant physical/electrical law of nature?
          YES -> Category 1 (Engineering Principle)
          NO  -> Proceed to Step 2.

  Step 2: Does it describe a physical/acoustic cause-and-effect relationship?
          YES -> Category 2 (Causal Relationship / Model)
          NO  -> Proceed to Step 3.

  Step 3: Is it specific to a software simulation platform (e.g. AmpliTube 5)?
          YES -> Is it execution/file logic? -> Category 11 (Deterministic Execution)
                 Is it parameter/coordinate mapping? -> Category 10 (Platform Calibration)
                 Is it software model metadata? -> Category 9 (Platform Product Knowledge)
          NO  -> Proceed to Step 4.

  Step 4: Does it describe real-world analog hardware or historical studio sessions?
          YES -> Is it hardware circuitry? -> Category 8 (Physical Device Knowledge)
                 Is it a specific album/session record? -> Category 12 (Historical Context)
                 Is it a specific benchmark preset case? -> Category 14 (Reference Case Evidence)
          NO  -> Proceed to Step 5.

  Step 5: Is it a rule of thumb, studio method, or subjective descriptor?
          - If a structured decision sequence -> Category 3 (Reasoning Framework)
          - If valid in bounded musical context -> Category 4 (Context-Dependent Practice)

SECTION 4 — FORMAL DISPOSITIONS & MULTI-ANNOTATION TAXONOMY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 THE SIX PRIMARY DISPOSITIONS
Every atomic asset extracted from Current TT must be assigned EXACTLY ONE primary disposition.
The six permitted primary dispositions are defined as follows:

1. KEEP CANDIDATE
   - Semantics: High-value sound engineering proposition, physical device insight, causal model,
     or reasoning framework that is worthy of entering the Sound Engineer Knowledge Base.
   - Permitted Use: Applied to domain concepts that are platform-independent, physically plausible,
     and relevant to tone engineering.
   - Requirement: Must undergo formal provenance recovery and epistemic validation under
     Phase 1C.4b / 1C.4d before reaching canonical `VALIDATED_ACTIVE` status.

2. KEEP — RELOCATE
   - Semantics: A valid, necessary operational asset that belongs in the new architecture,
     but NOT inside the Sound Engineer's declarative knowledge base.
   - Permitted Use: Applied to platform calibration tables (e.g. AT5 VIR coordinates), platform
     translation algorithms (e.g. GUID lookups), deterministic XML serializers, UI components
     (e.g. 2D mic placement grid), and deterministic validation checks.
   - Target Destination: Specific subsystems outside the Sound Engineer Knowledge Base
     (e.g. Platform Translator, Exporter, Observability/UI).

3. PRESERVE — REFERENCE CASE
   - Semantics: A concrete historical configuration, album recording recipe, artist benchmark,
     or listening test outcome that must be preserved as empirical evidence behind the
     Reference Case Firewall.
   - Permitted Use: Applied to curated recipes (e.g. Malcolm Young 'Jailbreak', Dimebag 'CFH',
     Metallica 'KEA') and benchmark tone profiles.
   - Boundary: Must NEVER be converted into a universal rule or general engineering law.

4. HOLD / INVESTIGATE
   - Semantics: An asset that is ambiguous, disputed, poorly understood, contradictory, or
     partially unverified, requiring deeper technical investigation, acoustic laboratory
     measurement, or engineering review.
   - Permitted Use: Applied when the engineering validity of a heuristic cannot be established
     from available legacy comments or existing literature.
   - Lifecycle: Placed in quarantine; cannot be used in production or discarded until resolved.

5. NLR — NO LONGER REQUIRED
   - Semantics: An asset, code path, override function, or prompt fragment that is obsolete,
     redundant, unphysical, destructive, or superseded by superior architecture.
   - Permitted Use: Applied to dead UI toggles, bypassed services, silent deterministic mutations,
     and redundant prompt instructions.
   - Critical Invariant: Subject to the Non-Destructive Quarantine Rule (Section 4.4).
     NLR does NOT permit destructive deletion until all underlying propositions, calibrations,
     case evidence, and governance lessons have been extracted.

6. GAP
   - Semantics: A deficiency, omission, or missing competency exposed during legacy deconstruction
     where Current TT completely lacks necessary engineering logic, physics models, or sensory input.
   - Permitted Use: Applied when a legacy asset attempts to solve a problem using a crude hack
     because the underlying professional capability is missing.
   - Target Destination: Logged directly into the Capability Gap Matrix for future engineering.

4.2 PRIMARY DISPOSITION EXCLUSIVITY RULE
Every atomic migration record MUST carry exactly one primary disposition:
  - Hybrid or compound dispositions (e.g. "KEEP / NLR" or "RELOCATE / INVESTIGATE") are
    STRICTLY FORBIDDEN.
  - If an asset appears to require multiple dispositions, it has NOT been sufficiently
    deconstructed into atomic units. The engineer MUST decompose the asset further until
    each constituent atomic proposition has exactly one unambiguous primary disposition.

4.3 SECONDARY ANNOTATION TAGGING SYSTEM
While primary dispositions are strictly exclusive, an atomic record may carry one or more
standardized secondary annotations to preserve critical diagnostic, governance, and
contextual metadata.

The permitted secondary annotation tags are:
  - `#ANTI_PATTERN`: The asset represents bad practice, epistemic error, or an architectural hack
    (e.g. silent parameter override, cascading EQs to fix mic placement).
  - `#PERCEPTUAL_SHORTHAND`: The asset relies on subjective, psychoacoustic terminology
    ("warmth", "punch", "chug", "tightness") requiring translation into physical acoustics.
  - `#OVERRIDE_BURDEN`: Deterministic code created specifically to patch around an LLM failure
    or hallucination in legacy prompt execution.
  - `#UNVERIFIED_PROVENANCE`: A candidate claim that currently lacks verified external
    citations, datasheets, or textbook corroboration.
  - `#PLATFORM_COUPLED`: An acoustic claim that is deeply entangled with AmpliTube 5 specific
    quirks or data structures.
  - `#SHADOW_ONLY`: Scaffolding that executes only in shadow mode or background analysis,
    completely decoupled from live preset generation.
  - `#TEST_FIXTURE`: Logic or data that exists exclusively inside unit test suites or mock fixtures.
  - `#HISTORICAL_CONTEXT`: Documentary information regarding historical recording sessions or gear.
  - `#COGNITIVE_BIAS`: An engineering heuristic driven by subjective habit or genre mythology
    rather than electroacoustic reality.
  - `#ACOUSTIC_HAZARD`: A heuristic or rule that risks introducing phase cancellation, extreme
    resonant ringing, or inter-sample digital clipping.

4.4 NON-DESTRUCTIVE NLR QUARANTINE RULE
To prevent catastrophic loss of domain knowledge during migration:
  1. No Destructive Code Deletion:
     Assigning `NLR — NO LONGER REQUIRED` to a legacy function, prompt, or recipe does NOT
     authorize its immediate deletion from the codebase.
  2. Prerequisite Accounting:
     Before any legacy code marked NLR can be removed in future refactoring phases, the engineer
     must verify that:
       * All underlying engineering propositions have been extracted as KEEP CANDIDATE records;
       * All platform translation or calibration data has been extracted as KEEP — RELOCATE;
       * All historical benchmarks have been extracted as PRESERVE — REFERENCE CASE;
       * All governance lessons and anti-patterns have been documented.
  3. Quarantine Archive:
     All NLR assets are catalogued in the Migration Ledger as an immutable record of retired
     mechanisms, ensuring full traceability and future auditability.

SECTION 5 — ARCHITECTURAL OWNERSHIP & THE STRICT BOUNDARY FIREWALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 THE TEN ARCHITECTURAL OWNERS
Every atomic asset must be mapped to exactly one architectural owner within the target
Sound Engineer platform:

1. SOUND ENGINEER KNOWLEDGE
   - Scope: Platform-agnostic, declarative knowledge base containing foundational physical
     principles, circuit behavior, transducer physics, psychoacoustic boundaries, and causal models.
   - Boundaries: Contains zero platform-specific implementation code, zero XML generators,
     and zero target GUIDs.

2. ENGINEERING REASONING
   - Scope: Cognitive reasoning engine responsible for diagnostic evaluation, problem decomposition,
     trade-off balancing, intervention sequencing, and high-level tone design.
   - Boundaries: Decides WHAT acoustic goals to achieve and WHY. Does not perform XML serialization.

3. EVIDENCE / INPUT
   - Scope: Ingestion and analysis of incoming audio signals, user intent, style preferences,
     and environmental context (transducer extraction, spectral analysis, dynamic profiling).
   - Boundaries: Extracts physical and musical features from evidence; does not design presets.

4. SEMANTIC TONE DESIGN
   - Scope: Declarative representation of abstract signal chain topology, module functions,
     and normalized parameter intent (e.g. "pre-gain high-pass at 720Hz, Q=0.7").
   - Boundaries: Platform-independent; specifies the ideal acoustical configuration.

5. DETERMINISTIC SEMANTIC VALIDATION
   - Scope: Hardcoded verification rules that check Semantic Tone Designs for acoustic safety,
     phase cancellation risks, severe gain staging errors, and topological coherence.
   - Boundaries: Verifies integrity; DOES NOT redesign or silently mutate engineering decisions.

6. PLATFORM TRANSLATOR — AT5 (and other targets)
   - Scope: Target-specific mapping engine that translates Semantic Tone Designs into concrete
     hardware models, GUIDs, parameter warping curves, and VIR mic coordinates for AmpliTube 5.
   - Boundaries: Decides HOW a design is realized on a specific target; does not make acoustic decisions.

7. EXPORTER / EXECUTION
   - Scope: Deterministic file builders, XML serializers, checksum generators, and I/O handlers
     that package translated configurations into target preset files (e.g. .at5p).
   - Boundaries: Pure software serialization; zero audio engineering reasoning.

8. REFERENCE / CASE EVIDENCE
   - Scope: Isolated historical vault containing benchmark presets, album recording setups,
     listening evaluations, and session logs, protected by the Reference Case Firewall.
   - Boundaries: Preserved for illustrative and comparative purposes; cannot dictate future decisions.

9. OBSERVABILITY / INFRASTRUCTURE / UI
   - Scope: Interactive user interfaces (2D mic placement grid, tone profile viewers), debug panels,
     telemetry logging, and runtime tracing infrastructure.
   - Boundaries: Provides user and developer visibility; does not perform tone synthesis.

10. NONE / RETIRE
    - Scope: Dead code, superseded duplicate helpers, abandoned UI toggles, and obsolete hacks.
    - Boundaries: Archived in the migration ledger; targeted for retirement.

5.2 THE CORE OWNERSHIP AXIOM
The entire target architecture is governed by the Core Ownership Axiom:

    "AI DECIDES WHAT + WHY.
     DETERMINISTIC SYSTEMS OWN TRUTH, CHECKING, AND EXECUTION.
     PLATFORM TRANSLATORS DETERMINE HOW FOR A TARGET PLATFORM."

Detailed Division of Responsibility:
  - WHAT + WHY (AI Sound Engineer):
    * Diagnoses tonal balance and identifies acoustic deficiencies.
    * Decides necessary engineering interventions (e.g. "tighten bass response before distortion").
    * Formulates declarative Semantic Tone Designs explaining why each block is placed.
  - TRUTH & CHECKING (Deterministic Systems):
    * Verifies mathematical and acoustic safety invariants.
    * Checks for unphysical configurations (e.g. microphone placed inside the speaker magnet).
    * Validates structural integrity and bounds.
  - HOW (Platform Translators):
    * Resolves target platform constraints (e.g. mapping 5 pedals into a 4-stomp AT5 chain).
    * Warps abstract parameters to target knob scales.
    * Selects best-matching available platform gear.

5.3 THE ANTI-HIJACK PRINCIPLE & DETERMINISTIC SEPARATION OF CONCERNS
In Current TT, deterministic functions repeatedly hijack and redesign engineering decisions:
  - `adjustThrashPedalSelection()` overrides the LLM's pedal selection;
  - `filterDuplicateEqsWithRemoved()` silently strips equalizer modules;
  - `ensureSignalChainSemanticPlacements()` forcibly re-orders stomp chains.

THE FUTURE ARCHITECTURE STRICTLY FORBIDS SILENT DETERMINISTIC MUTATIONS:
  1. No Silent Redesign:
     A deterministic validation subsystem MUST NEVER silently alter, replace, or mutate an
     engineering decision made by the AI Sound Engineer.
  2. Explicit Violation Reporting:
     If a Semantic Tone Design violates a deterministic physical or platform constraint, the
     validator must reject the design and generate a structured `ConstraintViolationReport`
     containing:
       * The violated acoustic or structural invariant;
       * The physical reason for the violation;
       * Suggested bounding parameters.
  3. Cognitive Replanning:
     The report is returned to the AI Sound Engineer, which replans its intervention with full
     cognitive awareness of the constraint, maintaining complete auditability and reasoning integrity.

SECTION 6 — THE REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE FIREWALL & PRECEDENT ISOLATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 PURPOSE & ROLE OF REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE
Historical guitar tones from landmark albums (e.g. AC/DC's 'Back in Black', Metallica's
'Master of Puppets', Pantera's 'Cowboys From Hell') represent valuable cultural, aesthetic,
and empirical benchmarks.

However, in legacy systems, historical presets are frequently corrupted into general engineering
prescriptions (e.g. "Because Dimebag used a 6-band graphic EQ with a mid scoop on CFH,
all metal rhythm guitars must have an aggressive mid scoop").

THE REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE FIREWALL ISOLATES HISTORICAL PRECEDENT FROM DECLARATIVE REASONING.
Reference cases are preserved as historical evidence, but are strictly prohibited from
becoming general sound engineering rules.

6.2 CANONICAL SCHEMA OF REFERENCE CASE EVIDENCE
Every preserved reference case must be encapsulated within a formal schema preserving:
  1. Case Metadata:
     * Unique Case Identifier;
     * Source Track / Album / Artist;
     * Recording Date and Studio Location;
     * Historical Production Team (Producer, Tracking Engineer, Mix Engineer).
  2. Input Acoustic Context:
     * Instrument (Body wood, construction, scale length);
     * Pickups (Inductance, DC resistance, magnet type, active/passive);
     * Strings (Gauge, core type, tuning);
     * Playing Technique (Plectrum hardness, attack angle, palm mute intensity).
  3. Applied Signal Chain Intervention:
     * Conditioning pedals (exact settings, circuit mods);
     * Amplifiers (preamp gain, master volume, tone stack, presence/depth);
     * Speakers / Cabinets (cabinet construction, speaker models, impedance loading);
     * Microphone Setup (mic model, capsule angle, distance, speaker cone locus);
     * Console Preamp, Outboard Compression, and Console EQ.
  4. Documented Listening Evidence & Outcomes:
     * Resulting spectral distribution (measured frequency profile);
     * Dynamic response and transient envelope;
     * Mix placement context (isolated track vs. full band balance).
  5. Critical Engineering Assessment:
     * Why the setup worked in that specific production context;
     * Known acoustic compromises or flaws in the setup;
     * How modern production techniques would alter the approach.
  6. Provenance & Citations:
     * Documentary sources (studio tracking sheets, mix engineer interviews, technical articles).

6.3 THE FOUR ANTI-PRECEDENT FIREWALLS
The architecture enforces four strict firewalls between Reference Cases and the Sound Engineer:

  FIREWALL 1: REFERENCE CASES ARE NEVER CANONICAL ENGINEERING KNOWLEDGE
  A reference case is an empirical record of an event, NOT an invariant principle of acoustics.
  The Sound Engineer Knowledge Base must contain the physical principles governing why the
  setup sounded the way it did, but the reference case itself remains in the Case Vault.

  FIREWALL 2: REFERENCE CASES ARE NEVER UNIVERSAL PRESCRIPTIONS
  The presence of a reference case in the repository does not authorize the system to
  mandate that configuration for any user prompt. A reference case must never be turned into
  an unconditional rule.

  FIREWALL 3: REFERENCE CASES NEVER SUBSTITUTE FOR CURRENT CASE EVIDENCE
  When a user requests a tone, the Sound Engineer MUST reason from the user's specific input
  evidence (guitar, pickups, playing style, monitoring environment). It is strictly forbidden
  to bypass reasoning by simply copying a reference preset.

  FIREWALL 4: GOVERNED ANALOGICAL RETRIEVAL ONLY
  If the Sound Engineer retrieves a reference case, it may use it ONLY as an illustrative
  analogy or benchmark reference point. The system must explicitly document:
    "Referencing historical setup from [Case ID] as an aesthetic baseline; adapting gain staging
     and EQ balance to account for user's higher-output pickups and differing speaker load."

6.4 GOVERNED RETRIEVAL & QUERYING OF REFERENCE CASES
Retrieval of reference cases is governed by Phase 1C.4c runtime context policies:
  - Reference cases are queried via semantic embedding of musical context and gear topology.
  - Injected reference cases carry explicit `#REFERENCE_CASE_ONLY` lineage tokens.
  - The Sound Engineer prompt generator wraps reference cases in strict epistemic delimiters:
    `<reference_case_evidence precedent="non_binding"> ... </reference_case_evidence>`
  - The reasoning engine is instructed to extract lessons regarding trade-offs, not parameter values.

SECTION 7 — OPERATIONAL REALITY INSPECTION METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 THE SIX OPERATIONAL STATES
An archaeological survey of legacy code reveals that what is written in source code often
bears little resemblance to what actually executes at runtime. Types may be declared but
never instantiated; services may execute in shadow mode without affecting outputs; and
functions may be completely bypassed by hardcoded overrides.

To ensure epistemic truthfulness, every legacy asset must be audited and assigned one of
the following six Operational States:

1. DECLARED_ONLY
   - Definition: The asset exists in TypeScript types, interfaces, schemas, or function
     signatures, but is never instantiated, populated, or invoked by executable runtime logic.
   - Example: The `recordingData` parameter in `translateTone()`. The type accepts recorded
     audio buffers, but the function body completely ignores the parameter and never passes
     it to Gemini or audio analysis engines.

2. ACTIVE_PRODUCTION
   - Definition: The asset resides in the direct, live execution path of user tone generation
     or primary UI workflows, actively contributing to system outputs.
   - Example: The central `MASTER_PROMPT` in `src/services/geminiService.ts` and the core
     XML node generation in `at5PresetBuilder.ts`.

3. BYPASSED_OVERRIDDEN
   - Definition: The asset executes during runtime, but its output is subsequently mutated,
     overridden, or completely replaced by downstream deterministic code or static bypass flags.
   - Example: The LLM's pedal selection for thrash metal prompts, which is generated during
     API execution but immediately overwritten by `adjustThrashPedalSelection()`.

4. SHADOW_ISOLATED
   - Definition: The asset executes in an isolated or asynchronous pipeline that runs alongside
     production, but its results do not affect the generated preset or user experience.
   - Example: `SoundEngineerService` executing in shadow mode, generating synthetic reasoning
     traces stored in state but completely decoupled from the live AT5 preset output.

5. TEST_FIXTURE_ONLY
   - Definition: The asset exists exclusively inside automated test suites (`src/tests/`),
     mock objects, or test fixtures, with no production caller or operational deployment.
   - Example: The synthetic execution trace generators in `soundEngineerService.test.ts`
     and mock VIR coordinate test fixtures.

6. DEAD_CODE
   - Definition: The asset is completely uncalled, unexported, unreachable via any runtime
     path, or gated behind permanently false feature toggles.
   - Example: The abandoned `Recipe` UI toggle in `App.tsx` and dormant functions in `recipes.ts`.

7.2 FORENSIC AUDIT & REALITY INSPECTION PROTOCOL
Engineers conducting migration analysis must execute a strict 5-step forensic audit before
documenting any legacy asset's operational status:

  Step 1: Entry Point Reachability Audit
    - Trace the call graph backwards from application entry points (`main.tsx`, `App.tsx`,
      and Express API endpoints).
    - If no call path reaches the function, classify as `DEAD_CODE`.

  Step 2: Data Flow & Mutation Tracing
    - Trace the data output from the function forward to the final preset generation response.
    - If the output is discarded or overwritten before reaching the user, classify as
      `BYPASSED_OVERRIDDEN`.

  Step 3: Branch & Feature Gate Analysis
    - Inspect conditional statements gating the asset (e.g. `if (isValidationActive)`).
    - Check whether flags are hardcoded or togglable from active production UI.
    - If gated behind permanent false conditions, classify as `DEAD_CODE`.

  Step 4: Shadow Execution Isolation Audit
    - Determine if the asset's side effects are purely observational (logging, telemetry,
      shadow state) or if they influence core application state.
    - If strictly observational, classify as `SHADOW_ISOLATED`.

  Step 5: Test Fixture Confinement Check
    - Grep the codebase for all occurrences of the symbol.
    - If all callers reside in `src/tests/` or `*.test.ts`, classify as `TEST_FIXTURE_ONLY`.

7.3 OPERATIONAL STATUS TRANSITION LIFECYCLE
Tracking operational status informs migration priorities:
  - `ACTIVE_PRODUCTION` assets must be migrated with highest priority to prevent regressions.
  - `BYPASSED_OVERRIDDEN` assets indicate architectural friction; the underlying engineering
    need must be extracted while the override hack is retired.
  - `SHADOW_ISOLATED` and `TEST_FIXTURE_ONLY` assets provide valuable reference contracts and
    verification assertions for future implementations.
  - `DEAD_CODE` and `DECLARED_ONLY` assets must be audited for underlying insights, then safely
    retired to reduce codebase complexity.

SECTION 8 — PROVENANCE RECOVERY & RISK-PROPORTIONAL VALIDATION WORKFLOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 OPERATIONALIZING THE UNASSESSED_LEGACY_SOURCE INGESTION PIPELINE
Under Phase 1C.4b-R v1.1g, Current TT is classified as an `UNASSESSED_LEGACY_SOURCE`.

         ↓
  [ Forensic Provenance Recovery ]

         ↓
  [ Risk-Proportional Evidence Assessment ]

         ↓
  [ SourceDocument Generation ]

  [ Epistemic Validation Dossier (Phase 1C.4d) ]

8.2 RISK-PROPORTIONAL EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY TIERS
In accordance with the frozen Phase 1C.4b-R contract, Tone Translator enforces a
risk-proportional evidence standard. Claims do NOT face an arbitrary, blanket requirement
for peer-reviewed academic literature:

TIER 1: HIGH-RISK CLAIMS (Physical Safety, Electroacoustics, Circuit Damage, Phase Hazards)
  - Scope: Electrical impedance matching, tube amplifier load safety, extreme phase cancellation,
    digital inter-sample clipping, and fundamental wave physics.
  - Evidence Standard: RIGOROUS SCIENTIFIC & TECHNICAL PROVENANCE.
  - Permitted Sources: Electrical engineering textbooks, published AES papers, manufacturer
    circuit schematics, bench oscilloscope/analyzer measurements, IEEE standards.
  - Example: "Operating a tube amplifier without an inductive speaker load damages output
    transformers due to high-voltage inductive flyback spikes."

TIER 2: MEDIUM-RISK CLAIMS (Psychoacoustic Models, Studio Practices, Acoustic Boundaries)
  - Scope: Masking thresholds, off-axis high-frequency microphone roll-off, preamp distortion
    harmonic distribution, compressor attack/release dynamics.
  - Evidence Standard: ESTABLISHED PROFESSIONAL LITERATURE & BROAD CONSENSUS.
  - Permitted Sources: Authoritative audio engineering texts (Everest 'Master Handbook of
    Acoustics', Rumsey 'Sound and Recording', Katz 'Mastering Audio', Owsinski 'The Mixing
    Engineer's Handbook'), gear service manuals, published acoustic measurements.
  - Example: "Dynamic moving-coil microphones exhibit off-axis high-frequency attenuation
    above 4kHz due to acoustic phase cancellation across the diaphragm."

TIER 3: LOW-RISK CLAIMS (Genre Conventions, Aesthetic Heuristics, Historical Recipes)
  - Scope: Genre-specific EQ curves, overdrive pedal boost settings, album tone recreation.
  - Evidence Standard: DOCUMENTED HISTORICAL PRODUCTION EVIDENCE & INDUSTRY CONSENSUS.
  - Permitted Sources: Producer/engineer interviews (Sound on Sound, Mix Magazine, Tape Op),
    studio tracking sheets, verified artist rig rundowns, published multi-album track analyses.
  - Example: "In 1980s thrash metal production, boosting an overdrive pedal's level with drive
    near zero was standard practice to tighten low-end attack into Marshall JCM800 amplifiers."

8.3 RECOVERED SOURCEDOCUMENT SCHEMA & CITATION REQUIREMENTS
When provenance is recovered for a legacy asset, a formal `SourceDocument` record is created:
  - `source_id`: Unique identifier (e.g. `SRC-TEXTBOOK-EVEREST-001`, `SRC-SCHEM-MARSHALL-2203`).
  - `source_type`: Academic Literature, Technical Textbook, Manufacturer Schematic, Patent,
    Engineering Datasheet, Industry Standard, Historical Production Record.
  - `title` & `author`: Formal bibliographic citation.
  - `publication_year`: Date of publication or release.
  - `relevant_excerpt`: Verbatim or summarized technical citation corroborating the claim.
  - `provenance_confidence`: HIGH (Primary technical source), MEDIUM (Secondary textbook),
    LOW (Informal interview or oral history).

8.4 DOWNSTREAM EPISTEMIC LIFECYCLE INTEGRATION (PHASE 1C.4d)
Once provenance is attached, the candidate proposition is packaged into a Review-Ready Candidate
Dossier and submitted to the Phase 1C.4d governance lifecycle:
  - If evidence meets the sufficiency threshold and no contradictions exist -> `VALIDATED_ACTIVE`.
  - If competing evidence or professional disagreement exists -> `ConflictRecord` created.
  - If evidence is weak or uncorroborated -> Retained in `HOLD / INVESTIGATE` quarantine.
  - If proven unphysical, misleading, or destructive -> `REJECTED` and archived.

SECTION 10 — THE FORMAL ATOMIC MIGRATION RECORD SCHEMA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 SCHEMA FIELD DEFINITIONS & SPECIFICATIONS
Every atomic asset extracted from Current TT must be formally recorded using the following
canonical schema:

  1. `migration_record_id` (string, required):
     Unique identifier following pattern `MIG-[LAYER]-[ASSET_SLUG]-[INDEX]`
     (e.g. `MIG-PROMPT-GAINPIVOT-001`, `MIG-RECIPE-CFH-002`, `MIG-OVERRIDE-TS9-001`).
  2. `legacy_source_asset_id` (string, required):
     Identifier of the parent legacy construct (e.g. `LEG-SRC-GEMINI-MASTERPROMPT`).
  3. `legacy_source_location` (string, required):
     Precise file path and line numbers (e.g. `src/services/geminiService.ts:425-450`).
  4. `legacy_operational_reality` (enum, required):
     Must be exactly one of: `DECLARED_ONLY`, `ACTIVE_PRODUCTION`, `BYPASSED_OVERRIDDEN`,
     `SHADOW_ISOLATED`, `TEST_FIXTURE_ONLY`, `DEAD_CODE`.
  5. `atomic_proposition_text` (string, required):
     Unambiguous, declarative statement of the extracted claim, rule, or calibration data.
  6. `what_it_actually_is` (enum, required):
     Must be exactly one of the 18 canonical categories (e.g. `ENGINEERING_PRINCIPLE`,
     `CAUSAL_RELATIONSHIP`, `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_PRACTICE`, `PLATFORM_PRODUCT_KNOWLEDGE`, etc.).
  7. `architectural_owner` (enum, required):
     Must be exactly one of the 10 owners (e.g. `SOUND_ENGINEER_KNOWLEDGE`,
     `ENGINEERING_REASONING`, `PLATFORM_TRANSLATOR_AT5`, `NONE_RETIRE`, etc.).
  8. `primary_disposition` (enum, required):
     Must be exactly one of: `KEEP_CANDIDATE`, `KEEP_RELOCATE`, `PRESERVE_REFERENCE_CASE`,
     `HOLD_INVESTIGATE`, `NLR_NO_LONGER_REQUIRED`, `GAP`.
  9. `secondary_annotations` (array of strings, optional):
     Zero or more permitted tags (`#ANTI_PATTERN`, `#PERCEPTUAL_SHORTHAND`, `#OVERRIDE_BURDEN`,
     `#UNVERIFIED_PROVENANCE`, `#PLATFORM_COUPLED`, `#SHADOW_ONLY`, `#TEST_FIXTURE`, etc.).
  10. `risk_tier` (enum, required):
      `HIGH` (safety/physics), `MEDIUM` (psychoacoustics/studio), `LOW` (genre/aesthetic).
  11. `validation_pathway` (string, required):
      Method for evaluating validity (e.g. "Circuit schematic analysis", "Acoustics textbook
      cross-reference", "Historical session log documentation").
  12. `recovered_provenance` (object, optional):
      Link to formal SourceDocument (`source_id`, `citation`, `confidence`).
  13. `target_destination_system` (string, required):
      Specific architectural target (e.g. `src/knowledge/causal_models/`, `src/translators/at5/`).
  14. `governance_notes` (string, optional):
      Contextual justification, anti-pattern explanation, or prerequisite retirement checks.
