SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 PURPOSE & CORE ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION
Phase 1C.3c establishes the formal educational blueprint and competency evaluation
architecture designed to transition the Tone Translator (TT) AI Sound Engineer from
its demonstrated Current TT capability baseline (characterized in Phase 1C.2 and
scored in Phase 1C.3b) to the independently defined, platform-independent
Professional Sound Engineer Standard established in Phase 1C.3a.

This blueprint directly answers the four governing architectural questions of
Phase 1C.3c:
  1. What must the future TT Sound Engineer learn?
  2. What capabilities must the runtime and evidence architecture provide rather
     than teach?
  3. How should disciplined, first-principles professional engineering reasoning
     be developed rather than replaced with static rules or prompt recipes?
  4. How will the system prove, through counterfactual and adversarial evaluation,
     that genuine engineering competence has been achieved?

1.2 THE CARDINAL DISTINCTION: EDUCATION VS ARCHITECTURAL CAPABILITY
A fatal trap in AI sound engineering design is attempting to "educate" an AI model
on concepts it cannot observe, or attempting to solve architectural blindness
through prompt engineering. This blueprint strictly enforces the boundary between:
  - Education & Cognition: Physical principles, electrical models, causal acoustics,
    psychoacoustic mechanics, competing hypothesis formulation, trade-off analysis,
    and uncertainty calibration.
  - Runtime & Evidence Tooling: Objective audio DSP feature extraction (spectral
    centroid, harmonic distribution, crest factor, envelope onset/decay slopes,
    phase correlation), closed-loop audio comparison, checkpoint state persistence,
    and schema validation.

Education cannot teach a model to hear what the software runtime fails to measure.
Conversely, runtime measurements cannot produce tone without a causal reasoning
framework that understands WHY electrical and acoustic transformations interact.

1.3 SCOPE & BOUNDARY COMPLIANCE
This blueprint operates in strict read-only design mode:
  - No application code, prompts, tests, or Firestore rules are modified.
  - No external source collection or web harvesting is conducted during this phase.
  - No DSP implementation or RAG retrieval services are created.
  - No Current TT re-scoring is performed; all baseline metrics are imported
    unaltered from Phase 1C.3b.
  - Phase 1C.4 (Curriculum Implementation & Knowledge Base Population) is NOT started.

SECTION 2 — EDUCATIONAL MISSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 THE MISSION STATEMENT
"To cultivate in the TT Sound Engineer an autonomous, first-principles engineering
intellect capable of interpreting objective acoustic evidence, forming competing
causal hypotheses across the electro-acoustic signal chain, selecting minimal and
defensible interventions, evaluating cross-domain trade-offs, and verifying outcomes
against reference standards without relying on static recipes, deterministic
keyword overrides, or platform-specific artifacts."

2.2 THE ANTI-RECIPE DOCTRINE
Professional audio engineering is not a dictionary of symptoms and fixes. A system
trained on heuristics such as:
    "Metal rhythm -> boost 808 Screamer -> cut 400 Hz -> clamp noise gate"
has not learned sound engineering; it has memorized a caricature.

When real-world conditions diverge from textbook tropes—such as an active-pickup
extended-range guitar with excessive pre-filtering, or a vintage fuzz into a
high-headroom clean amplifier—static heuristics fail catastrophically. The educational
mission mandates that every curriculum unit develops the complete causal chain:
    PRINCIPLE -> CAUSAL MODEL -> EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS -> COMPETING HYPOTHESES ->
    DISCRIMINATING OBSERVATIONS -> INTERVENTION CANDIDATES -> TRADE-OFF REASONING ->
    PREDICTED CONSEQUENCES -> EMPIRICAL VERIFICATION -> REVISION.

SECTION 4 — EDUCATION VS CAPABILITY GAP FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 GAP TAXONOMY
Every identified deficiency across the 22 domains is classified into one or more of
the following six functional categories:

  Category A: KNOWLEDGE / EDUCATION
    Deficiencies where the cognitive model lacks understanding of physics,
    circuits, acoustics, or signal interactions.
    Resolution: Curated technical instruction and validated knowledge units.

  Category B: REASONING / COGNITIVE METHOD
    Deficiencies where the engineer possesses facts but lacks the structured
    reasoning process to formulate competing hypotheses, isolate variables, or
    evaluate trade-offs.
    Resolution: Cognitive workflow frameworks and adversarial problem training.

  Category C: EVIDENCE / TOOLING CAPABILITY
    Deficiencies where necessary physical data cannot be perceived by the model
    alone and must be extracted from the signal by external tools.
    Resolution: Objective DSP feature extraction services (spectral, temporal, phase).

  Category D: ARCHITECTURAL RUNTIME CAPABILITY
    Deficiencies where the execution pipeline lacks structural facilities to support
    the engineering behavior (e.g., feedback loops, multi-pass refinement, tracing).
    Resolution: Runtime execution engine, checkpoint lineage, and dual-stream harness.

  Category E: PLATFORM TRANSLATION CAPABILITY
    Deficiencies or artifacts that belong strictly downstream in platform-specific
    adapters and must not be taught as general engineering knowledge.
    Resolution: Independent platform translation layers (e.g., AmpliTube 5 adapter).

  Category F: DETERMINISTIC VALIDATION
    Deficiencies concerning logical consistency, mathematical boundaries, schema
    invariants, and physical impossibility checks.
    Resolution: Deterministic contract validators and schema enforcement guards.

4.2 THE "CAN EDUCATION ALONE FIX IT?" TRICHOTOMY
To prevent architectural misallocation, every domain gap is assessed against the
following triad:
  - YES: Defect is purely cognitive or informational. Resolvable entirely via
    knowledge representation and prompt/reasoning frameworks.
  - PARTIALLY: Knowledge and reasoning can improve hypotheses and trade-off decisions,
    but verified execution requires runtime evidence or architectural loops.
  - NO: Defect is structural or perceptual. Teaching cannot provide sensory data
    that the system cannot measure, nor can it execute an iterative loop if the
    runtime lacks closed-loop plumbing.

SECTION 5 — CURRICULUM DESIGN PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 THE FIFTEEN-POINT CURRICULUM UNIT TEMPLATE
To prevent superficial "bullet-point knowledge," all curriculum units must conform
to a unified structural contract:
  1. Domain: The governing engineering discipline (1 to 22).
  2. Concept: The specific physical or acoustic phenomenon.
  3. Engineering Principle: The foundational physical law or established theory.
  4. Causal Model: The mathematical/physical mechanism linking inputs to outputs.
  5. Relevant Evidence: Objective measurements and perceptual features indicating
     the state of this phenomenon.
  6. Evidence Limitations: Confounders, measurement ambiguities, and acoustic illusions.
  7. Plausible Hypotheses: Competing root causes that could produce identical symptoms.
  8. Discriminating Tests: Observations or diagnostic probes that isolate the true cause.
  9. Primary Interventions: Candidate adjustments across the signal chain.
  10. Alternative Interventions: Non-obvious or upstream/downstream alternative fixes.
  11. Trade-Off Analysis: What sonic attributes are degraded when this fix is applied.
  12. Expected Consequences: First-order and second-order acoustic results.
  13. Uncertainty Rating: Inherent ambiguity and confidence limits of the diagnosis.
  14. Cross-Domain Dependencies: Coupled upstream and downstream domains.
  15. Anti-Patterns & Pitfalls: Flawed heuristics, over-processing, and recipe traps.

5.2 FIVE-DIMENSIONAL PEDAGOGY
Education must develop all five assessment dimensions symmetrically:
  - D1 (Knowledge): Physical grounding, circuit topography, wave mechanics.
  - D2 (Evidence Interpretation): Reading DSP metrics, recognizing maskers/confounders.
  - D3 (Diagnosis): Causal tracing, abductive reasoning, hypothesis ranking.
  - D4 (Decision-Making): Minimalist intervention, constraint balancing, gain preservation.
  - D5 (Outcome Reasoning): Verification metric definition, falsification, refinement.

SECTION 6 — CURRICULUM ARCHITECTURE & LEARNING STAGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The educational architecture is structured into seven sequential, cumulative
learning stages. Progression through stages is governed by prerequisite gates;
higher-level stages cannot be mastered if foundational stages contain unaddressed gaps.

  STAGE 1: FOUNDATIONAL PHYSICS & TRANSDUCTION MECHANICS
    Focus: Electrical signals, transducer physics, electromagnetic induction, wave mechanics.
    Domains: 1 (Source), 2 (Gain Staging), 10 (Microphone Physics), 11 (Wave Phase).
    Prerequisite for: All subsequent stages.

  STAGE 2: NONLINEAR SYSTEMS & DYNAMIC BEHAVIOUR
    Focus: Harmonic generation, clipping topologies, power supply sag, dynamic envelopes.
    Domains: 3 (Nonlinear/Distortion), 5 (Dynamics/Transients), 8 (Amplifier Circuits).
    Prerequisite for: Stages 3, 4, 5.

  STAGE 3: ACOUSTIC RESONANCE & SPATIAL PROPAGATION
    Focus: Loudspeaker impedance, cone breakup, cabinet acoustics, room acoustics, 3D space.
    Domains: 9 (Loudspeaker/Cabinet), 10 (Capture Geometry), 12 (Spatial Processing).
    Prerequisite for: Stages 4, 5.

  STAGE 4: PSYCHOACOUSTICS & SPECTRAL INTEGRATION
    Focus: Auditory perception, equal-loudness contours, masking, critical bands, noise.
    Domains: 4 (Spectral Engineering), 6 (Noise Engineering), 13 (Psychoacoustics).
    Prerequisite for: Stages 5, 6, 7.

  STAGE 5: SYSTEM TOPOLOGY & CROSS-DOMAIN CAUSALITY
    Focus: End-to-end signal flow, reactive loading, pre/post filtering, contextual fit.
    Domains: 7 (Signal Topology), 14 (Musical Context), 18 (Trade-Offs).
    Prerequisite for: Stages 6, 7.

  STAGE 6: DIAGNOSTIC REASONING & EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE
    Focus: Competing hypotheses, root-cause isolation, uncertainty bounds, reference deltas.
    Domains: 15 (Reference Analysis), 16 (Diagnosis), 19 (Uncertainty Discipline),
             20 (Alternative Solutions).
    Prerequisite for: Stage 7.

  STAGE 7: AUTONOMOUS PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE & CLOSED-LOOP REFINEMENT
    Focus: Minimalist intervention, closed-loop iteration, self-falsification, ethics.
    Domains: 17 (Intervention Selection), 21 (Iterative Evaluation), 22 (Professional Behaviour).
    Cap-stone Stage: Complete engineering autonomy.

DOMAIN 1: SOURCE & PERFORMANCE
================================================================================
Target Maturity: L5 (Core)
Current Five-Dimensional Baseline:
  Knowledge: L1 | Evidence: L0 | Diagnosis: L0 | Decision: L1 | Outcome: L0
Required Educational Outcome:
  Mastery of guitar electro-mechanical generation: string alloy/gauge tension,
  magnetic pickup types (single-coil, PAF humbucker, high-output ceramic, active),
  pickup inductance/resonant peak (Q), volume/tone potentiometer loading (250k vs
  500k vs 1M, cable capacitance loading), pick materials/angles, and player touch.
Knowledge Requirements:
  - Equivalent RLC circuit of guitar pickups and cable capacitance.
  - Dynamic spectral variation caused by pick hardness, attack angle, and palm-mute damping.
  - Asymmetric vs symmetric string excursion harmonics.
Evidence-Interpretation Requirements:
  - Inferring pickup class from spectral centroid, low-frequency resonance, and hum.
  - Distinguishing player palm-mute muting technique from amplifier sag or gate clamp.
Diagnostic Requirements:
  - Formulating competing hypotheses for dull attack: pickup height vs pot loading vs
    dead strings vs pick technique.
Decision-Making Requirements:
  - Determining when an upstream source limitation cannot be repaired downstream by EQ
    without degrading signal-to-noise ratio.
Outcome-Reasoning Requirements:
  - Predicting how shifting pickup height alters magnetic string pull (intonation flutter)
    versus output voltage.
Cross-Domain Dependencies:
  - Directly drives Domain 2 (Gain Staging), Domain 3 (Distortion), Domain 8 (Amp Input).
Gap Categories: A (Knowledge), B (Reasoning), C (Tooling - input pickup measurement).
Can Education Alone Fix It? PARTIALLY.
  Education teaches pickup/string physics, but runtime must extract input signal
  dynamic range and frequency profile from audio.
Required Non-Educational Capability:
  DSP analysis of input instrument audio; UI capture of guitar/pickup metadata.
Current TT Assets to Preserve:
  Keyword prompts distinguishing neck vs bridge pickups in geminiService.ts.
Anti-Patterns to Eliminate:
  Ignoring guitar pickup type and forcing uniform amp models; assuming all input
  guitars deliver 1.0 V RMS.
Proposed Competency Evidence:
  Successfully diagnosing pickup resonant peak mis-match from an audio sample and
  recommending impedance or input gain compensation.

Outcome-Reasoning Requirements:
  - Predicting how cutting pre-distortion 100-250 Hz alters harmonic density at 1-3 kHz.
Cross-Domain Dependencies:

  "Gain 10 solves everything"; stacking multiple high-gain distortion pedals into

Outcome-Reasoning Requirements:

Cross-Domain Dependencies:
  - Directly affects Domain 1 (Touch), Domain 2 (Gain Staging), Domain 8 (Amp Sag).

  Curriculum provides dynamic ballistics knowledge; DSP envelope detector measures actual audio.

  Transient envelope detector measuring attack duration and crest factor.
Current TT Assets to Preserve:
  Compressor catalog parameters in at5ParameterManifest.ts and compressorResolver.test.ts.

  Applying fast-attack brickwall limiting to guitar signals, destroying pick dynamics;

Proposed Competency Evidence:

  predicted criteria, diagnosing residual errors or unintended side-effects, and
  executing refined follow-up adjustments until convergence is achieved.
Knowledge Requirements:
  - Control systems theory: feedback loops, error signals, overshoot, damping, and

  - Falsification logic: knowing what observation proves an intervention failed.
Evidence-Interpretation Requirements:
  - Comparing Before vs After audio measurements across spectral, temporal, and phase metrics.
Diagnostic Requirements:
  - Diagnosing whether an unexpected outcome resulted from model error, unmodeled
    hardware interactions, or secondary side-effects.
Decision-Making Requirements:
  - Deciding whether to refine the current approach, roll back to a previous checkpoint,

Outcome-Reasoning Requirements:
  - Rigorous self-evaluation; refusing to declare success until objective and perceptual
    criteria are satisfied.
Cross-Domain Dependencies:
  - The capstone execution engine governing all engineering decisions and outcomes.
Gap Categories: A (Knowledge), B (Reasoning), C (Tooling - delta analysis), D (Architecture - feedback loop).
Can Education Alone Fix It? NO.
  While the scientific mindset is taught, closed-loop iteration is physically impossible

Required Non-Educational Capability:

Proposed Competency Evidence:

  pass 2 diagnoses the overshoot, and pass 3 converges within +/- 0.5 dB of target.

SECTION 9 — CROSS-DOMAIN PROFESSIONAL REASONING CURRICULUM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Professional engineering competence is demonstrated when an engineer solves complex,
coupled electro-acoustic defects across multiple domains. Below are the curriculum
specifications for the nine Phase 1C.3a engineering scenarios:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 1: "Palm mutes are flubby, boomy, and lack tightness."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 1 (Source touch/pickup), Domain 2 (Gain staging), Domain 3 (Nonlinear IMD),
  Domain 4 (Pre-gain EQ), Domain 8 (Amp sag/damping), Domain 9 (Cab resonance),
  Domain 10 (Mic proximity).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Perceived loose, boomy low-end decay on palm-muted transient chugs.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Crest factor, low-frequency decay time, 80-150 Hz energy,
     pre-clipping vs post-clipping spectrum.
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: Excessive sub-150 Hz energy entering high-gain nonlinear stage, generating
       low-frequency intermodulation distortion and blocking recovery.
     - H2: Underdamped power amplifier with high output impedance allowing loudspeaker
       cone resonance (80-100 Hz) to ring uncontrolled.
     - H3: Directional microphone placed <2 cm from grill, exaggerating bass via
       acoustic proximity effect.
     - H4: Loose player palm muting failing to damp string excursion behind bridge.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Check pre-distortion high-pass filtering (is low-end cut before clipping?).
     - Check mic distance and polar pattern (is it an on-grill cardioid?).
     - Check power amp negative feedback / resonance damping controls.
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Introduce a pre-distortion high-pass shelf (cut 6 dB below 200 Hz).
     - Option B: Back microphone off grill by 5 cm to eliminate proximity effect.
     - Option C: Increase power amp negative feedback damping.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Option A tightens mutes but may make unmuted single notes on high strings feel thin.
     - Option B reduces boom naturally but introduces more room ambiance and bleeds.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Rapid transient decay on palm mutes, eliminated intermodulation
     hash, punchy 100 Hz fundamental without lingering mud.
  8. Outcome Verification: Measure 80-150 Hz decay slope; verify mute decay <80 ms.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 2: "Top-end distortion is fizzy, harsh, and buzzing."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 3 (Clipping harmonics), Domain 4 (Post-EQ), Domain 8 (Presence/bright cap),
  Domain 9 (Speaker cone breakup/beaming), Domain 10 (Mic placement angle/offset).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Grating, buzzy high-frequency energy between 4 kHz and 8 kHz.
  2. Evidence Requirements: High-frequency harmonic distribution, mic angle/offset,
     presence control position.
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: Microphone positioned directly on-axis aiming at dust cap center (beaming zone).
     - H2: Harsh solid-state/diode clipping generating dense odd-order high harmonics.
     - H3: Amp presence control lifted, disabling negative feedback at 4-6 kHz.
     - H4: Stacking bright caps across volume controls without post-distortion attenuation.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Inspect capture geometry (is mic at (0,0) center cap?).
     - Check loudspeaker frequency response curve (is it a modern ceramic with 4.5 kHz peak?).
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Angle microphone 45° off-axis or move laterally 5 cm toward cone edge.
     - Option B: Reduce amplifier presence or treble control.
     - Option C: Insert a post-distortion low-pass filter at 6.5 kHz.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Moving mic off-axis smooths highs via acoustic wave integration without phase
       smearing of digital filters.
     - Post-EQ LPF removes fizz but can dull pick attack presence.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Warm, singing top end with smooth transition into saturation;
     elimination of aggressive buzz.
  8. Outcome Verification: FFT analysis showing 12 dB/octave smooth acoustic rolloff above 5 kHz.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 3: "Tone lacks pick attack definition and transient punch."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 1 (Pick technique/impedance), Domain 2 (Gain staging), Domain 3 (Clipping depth),
  Domain 5 (Dynamics/envelope), Domain 8 (Power supply sag).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Note attacks are mushy, compressed, and indistinct; fast runs blend together.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Peak-to-RMS crest factor, attack onset slope (ms), preamp gain level.
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: Excessive preamp saturation obliterating dynamic pick transient into square wave.
     - H2: Fast-attack compressor placed before amplifier squashing transient spike.
     - H3: Power supply rectifier sag compressing immediately on transient onset.
     - H4: Pickup set too close to strings (magnetic damping) or soft pick gauge.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Measure crest factor: if <6 dB, signal is severely squashed.
     - Inspect signal chain for pre-amp compression or excessive drive levels.
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Back off preamp gain from 8 to 5.5 and add clean front-end level boost.
     - Option B: Slow down compressor attack time to 30 ms to let transient through.
     - Option C: Switch amplifier power supply to stiffer solid-state rectification.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Backing off gain restores pick attack but reduces overall sustain and compression feel.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Crisp initial pick click and transient punch (10-15 ms) followed
     by controlled sustain.
  8. Outcome Verification: Crest factor increases from 5 dB to 9 dB; attack onset <12 ms.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 4: "Guitar sounds huge in solo, but completely disappears in a dense mix."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 4 (Spectral allocation), Domain 12 (Spatial wash), Domain 13 (Psychoacoustics/masking),
  Domain 14 (Production arrangement), Domain 17 (Minimalism).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Massive, thick tone when played alone vanishes behind bass, drums, and vocals.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Multitrack spectral overlap, 500 Hz - 2 kHz energy ratio,
     reverb wet/dry percentage.
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: Scooped midrange (500 Hz - 1.5 kHz) where guitar carries articulation; masked by vocal/snare.
     - H2: Excessive low-end energy (100-200 Hz) masked by bass guitar and kick drum.
     - H3: Heavy stereo reverb washing out direct acoustic energy into diffuse background blur.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Mute bass and drums: does guitar clarity suddenly return? (Confirms masking).
     - Check stereo correlation and reverb decay times.
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Boost 800 Hz - 1.4 kHz midrange band by 3-4 dB; high-pass at 120 Hz.
     - Option B: Reduce reverb wet mix from 35% to 15% and increase pre-delay to 30 ms.
     - Option C: Narrow stereo width to anchor guitar in a dedicated pan pocket.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Mid-forward guitar tone sounds somewhat nasal or honky in isolation ("solo trap"),
     - but sounds powerful, punchy, and clear in the mix.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Guitar cuts effortlessly through dense rhythm section without
     requiring fader volume increase.
  8. Outcome Verification: Verify guitar intelligibility ratio against mix track;
     midrange energy dominant.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 5: "Dual-microphone setup sounds hollow, thin, and nasal."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 9 (Speaker dispersion), Domain 10 (Microphone geometry), Domain 11 (Wave phase mechanics),
  Domain 17 (Intervention parsimony).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Soloing either mic sounds full; combining both sounds hollow, weak, and nasal.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Phase correlation meter, individual mic distances (cm),
     summed spectrum nulls.
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: Acoustic comb filtering caused by capsule distance offset (time delay mismatch).
     - H2: Accidental polarity inversion (180° out of phase) between channels.
     - H3: Spatial acoustic cancellation between two speakers in a 4x12 cab.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Solo mic 1, solo mic 2, sum both: if summed level is LOWER than solo level in
       bass, polarity is inverted.
     - Check distance delta: is one mic 3 cm further from the grill than the other?
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Align physical mic diaphragms to identical distance plane (+/- 1 mm).
     - Option B: Flip polarity switch on one microphone channel.
     - Option C: Mute one microphone entirely (minimalist solution).
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Perfect physical alignment provides full frequency reinforcement without EQ phase smearing.
     - Using a single mic eliminates all phase issues at the cost of dual-transducer color blending.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Rich, full-bodied low-mid response (+3 to +6 dB summation)
     and elimination of nasal notches.
  8. Outcome Verification: Phase correlation meter reads > +0.85 across 100 Hz - 5 kHz.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 6: "Reference sounds wider, deeper, and more three-dimensional."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 7 (Parallel routing), Domain 9 (Cabinet dispersion), Domain 10 (Room capture),
  Domain 11 (Stereo phase), Domain 12 (Spatial early reflections), Domain 13 (Haas effect).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Current tone is flat, 2D, and centered; reference has immersive width and depth.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Stereo vectorscope, L/R cross-correlation, early reflection
     energy (10-40 ms).
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: Reference utilizes true double-tracking (two distinct performances panned hard L/R).
     - H2: Reference utilizes distant ambient room microphones blended behind close mics.
     - H3: Reference employs micro-pitch shifting or Haas delay (<20 ms) in stereo.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Check mono fold-down: does reference collapse or retain two guitars? (Distinguishes
       double-tracking from stereo widening tricks).
     - Analyze early reflection impulse response in reference stem.
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Introduce room microphones placed 2 meters back in a reflective space.
     - Option B: Double-track the guitar part with slight tonal variations between L and R.
     - Option C: Apply subtle stereo micro-delay (12 ms) with mono-compatibility safeguard.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Adding room mics adds depth but reduces dry immediacy and clarity in fast tempos.
     - Synthetic stereo widening risks phase cancellation in mono playback environments.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Expansive stereo panorama with palpable physical depth and room ambiance.
  8. Outcome Verification: Vectorscope displays healthy elliptical stereo spread; mono
     summation loses <1 dB.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 7: "High-gain idle hiss and noise staging."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 1 (Pickup shielding), Domain 2 (Gain staging), Domain 3 (Saturation gain),
  Domain 6 (Noise engineering), Domain 8 (Preamp tube noise), Domain 17 (Minimalism).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Severe hiss and buzz during pauses between notes; noise louder than -40 dBFS.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Idle noise floor spectrum (dBFS), 50/60 Hz hum vs wideband
     white hiss, stage gains.
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: Excessive compounding of gain blocks (+20 dB boost pedal into high-gain amp on 10).
     - H2: Noisy front-end pedal or poorly shielded single-coil pickups inducing RF/hum.
     - H3: Uncalibrated post-amp digital gain boosting thermal noise floor.
     - H4: Absence of an intelligent downward expander/gate.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Spectral analysis of noise: sharp 60/120 Hz lines (ground/EMF) vs flat hiss (thermal gain).
     - Mute pedalboard input: does hiss vanish? (Isolates upstream pedals vs amplifier).
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Rebalance gain staging: reduce pedal boost level, optimize amp preamp gain.
     - Option B: Implement an input-sidechain downward expander (gate key on clean input).
     - Option C: Remove redundant gain or EQ boost stages.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Overly aggressive gating cuts off note tails and sounds artificial.
     - Optimizing gain staging reduces noise at the source without gating artifacts.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Idle noise drops below -65 dBFS; smooth, natural note decay into silence.
  8. Outcome Verification: Measure idle noise floor; verify note decay sustains naturally
     down to -50 dBFS.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 8: "Stiff, sterile touch response and lack of sustain."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 1 (Player dynamics), Domain 2 (Input sensitivity), Domain 3 (Nonlinear compression),
  Domain 5 (Dynamics/sag), Domain 8 (Cathode bias/power supply), Domain 9 (Speaker compression).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Tone feels unyielding, harsh, and unresponsive to picking strength; notes die abruptly.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Dynamic transfer curve (input dB vs output dB), recovery envelope,
     rectifier sag.
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: High-headroom solid-state power amp running completely linear with zero sag.
     - H2: Amplifier biased cold (crossover distortion and lack of bloom).
     - H3: Over-clamping noise gate cutting low-level sustained vibrations.
     - H4: High input impedance mismatch or padded converter input dropping guitar level.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Play soft vs hard: does harmonic content change organically?
     - Inspect noise gate threshold and decay ballistics.
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Introduce power supply sag (emulating tube rectifier voltage drop under load).
     - Option B: Warm the amplifier bias into Class AB sweet spot.
     - Option C: Add an optical compressor with gentle 2:1 ratio for singing sustain.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Adding sag increases touch sensitivity and sustain but softens fast palm-mute tightness.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Elastic, blooming feel under the fingers; soft picking yields clean/warm
     tone, hard picking bites.
  8. Outcome Verification: Dynamic range compression ratio measures 2.5:1; sustain time
     extends by 4+ seconds.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario 9: "Spectrum matches RTA but perceptually sounds completely wrong."
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cooperating Domains:
  Domain 3 (Harmonic orders), Domain 5 (Temporal envelope), Domain 11 (Phase alignment),
  Domain 13 (Psychoacoustics), Domain 15 (Tone matching), Domain 16 (Diagnosis).
Educational Reasoning Pathway:
  1. Observation: Static FFT curve matches the reference within 1 dB, but the tone
     sounds artificial, harsh, or dead.
  2. Evidence Requirements: Crest factor, harmonic order decomposition (2nd vs 3rd vs 5th),
     waterfall/decay plot.
  3. Competing Hypotheses:
     - H1: Matching achieved via violent static EQ rather than proper nonlinear harmonic generation.
     - H2: Dynamic envelope mismatch: reference has slow blooming compression; current has fast clipping.
     - H3: Phase smearing introduced by steep minimum-phase matching filters.
     - H4: Spectral balance matches on average, but time-domain decay (reverb/cabinet) is radically different.
  4. Discriminating Tests:
     - Inspect harmonic distortion profile (THD and harmonic orders).
     - Compare spectral waterfall plots over 500 ms note decay.
  5. Intervention Candidates:
     - Option A: Remove artificial static match EQ; recreate spectral balance using
       proper amp saturation and mic angle.
     - Option B: Correct temporal envelope using compressor attack/release and power amp sag.
     - Option C: Address cabinet impulse response length and acoustic decay.
  6. Trade-Off Analysis:
     - Acoustic/circuit matching requires deeper causal modeling but avoids artificial
     - static filter phase smearing.
  7. Predicted Outcome: Tone feels alive, organic, and dynamic while matching the
     reference both spectrally and temporally.
  8. Outcome Verification: Perceptual spectral distance, crest factor, and THD profile
     converge simultaneously.

SECTION 10 — PROFESSIONAL REASONING METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

10.1 THE SEVEN-STEP REASONING METHOD
The future AI Sound Engineer executes a formal seven-step cognitive cycle for every
engineering task, moving strictly from empirical observation to verified outcome:

  Step 1: OBSERVATION & PERCEPTUAL CHARACTERIZATION
    - Transcribe raw acoustic observations (spectral balance, dynamics, saturation,
      space, defects).
    - Map subjective artistic language to physical domains (e.g., "muddy" -> 200-350 Hz accumulation).

  Step 2: EVIDENCE SEPARATION & VERIFICATION
    - Ingest objective DSP metrics (FFT, LUFS, crest factor, correlation).
    - Segregate verified measurements from user claims, assumptions, and unknowns.
    - Check for missing evidence; declare confidence limits.

  Step 3: COMPETING HYPOTHESIS FORMATION
    - Formulate at least two (ideally three or four) plausible, distinct causal hypotheses.
    - Trace each hypothesis across the electro-acoustic chain (Source -> Electrical -> Acoustic).
    - Identify discriminating physical observations that would falsify each hypothesis.

  Step 4: CAUSAL DIAGNOSIS & ROOT-CAUSE ISOLATION
    - Evaluate evidence against competing hypotheses.
    - Discard falsified causes; rank surviving hypotheses by probability and parsimony.
    - Select the true root cause rather than a surface symptom.

  Step 5: INTERVENTION DESIGN & TRADE-OFF BALANCING
    - Design candidate interventions targeting the root cause.
    - Apply the Law of Parsimony (minimal processing stages, source-first intervention).
    - Explicitly articulate trade-offs, collateral acoustic consequences, and constraints.
    - Formulate the primary plan and at least one viable alternative plan.

  Step 6: PREDICTION & VERIFICATION METRICS
    - Define quantitative and perceptual criteria for success before executing.
    - Define explicit falsification conditions: "What measurement proves this fix failed?"

  Step 7: OUTCOME EVALUATION & ITERATIVE REVISION
    - Measure outcome audio against prediction criteria.
    - If criteria are unmet, record error delta, revise causal graph, and iterate.

10.2 CONTRACT COMPATIBILITY
This cognitive method maps seamlessly into the Phase 1B contracts:
  - Step 1 & 2 -> InputSnapshot & EvidenceRecord
  - Step 3 & 4 & 5 -> EngineeringDecisionRecord
  - Step 5 Output -> SemanticToneDesign
  - Step 6 & 7 -> ValidationReport & RunTraceRecorder

SECTION 12 — SOURCE QUALITY & EDUCATION MATERIAL STRATEGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

12.1 FOUR-TIER HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY
Not all technical claims carry equal engineering weight. The educational architecture
enforces a strict four-tier hierarchy of authority:

  TIER 1: ESTABLISHED PHYSICS & WAVE MECHANICS (Highest Authority)
    - Subjects: Maxwell's equations, RLC circuit theory, acoustic wave propagation,
      Fourier transform, velocity-gradient pressure acoustics, Faraday induction.
    - Source Classes: University physics textbooks, AES (Audio Engineering Society)
      journal papers, IEEE transactions, authoritative electro-acoustic treatises.
    - Authority: Incontrovertible; cannot be overridden by user preference or engineer habit.

  TIER 2: PEER-REVIEWED & MANUFACTURER ENGINEERING RESEARCH
    - Subjects: Vacuum tube transfer curves, loudspeaker Thiele-Small modeling,
      polar pattern response measurements, transformer hysteresis, psychoacoustic scales.
    - Source Classes: Neumann/Schoeps technical papers, Celestion engineering whitepapers,
      ASA (Acoustical Society of America) journals, standard textbooks (e.g., Davis & Patronis,
      Eargle, Everest & Pohlmann, Blencowe, Kuehnel).
    - Authority: Authoritative engineering models; exceptions exist only under extreme boundary conditions.

  TIER 3: SYSTEMATIC PROFESSIONAL RECORDING PRACTICE
    - Subjects: Console workflow, gain staging calibration, multi-mic alignment procedures,
      mix masking mitigation, classic microphone positioning conventions.
    - Source Classes: Established professional studio handbooks (e.g., Huber & Runstein,
      Senior, Owsinski), published master engineer technical documentation.
    - Authority: Standard industry engineering practice; contextual and subject to
      artistic adaptation.

  TIER 4: CONTEXTUAL ARTISTIC TECHNIQUE & GENRE PREFERENCE (Lowest Authority)
    - Subjects: "Scooped thrash metal midrange", "80s chorus polish", "vintage lo-fi fuzz".
    - Source Classes: Producer interviews, historical track deconstructions, artist presets.
    - Authority: Context-dependent heuristic. Never taught as universal engineering truth;
      must be framed as contextual artistic options.

SECTION 13 — KNOWLEDGE PROVENANCE STANDARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

13.1 PROVENANCE DATA CONTRACT
Every piece of knowledge ingested into the curriculum must maintain an unbroken chain
of custody:
  - Source Title & DOI/ISBN: Formal citation of origin.
  - Source Class: Tier 1 to Tier 4 classification.
  - Extracted Claim: The literal physical claim made by the source.
  - Normalized Engineering Formulation: The mathematical or physical translation of the claim.
  - Boundary Conditions: Where the claim is valid (e.g., "valid only when distance < wavelength / 2pi").
  - Conflicting Literature: Documented disagreements or historical debates.
  - Technical Reviewer ID: Human expert who verified the unit.
  - Review Timestamp: Date of validation sign-off.

SECTION 14 — COMPETENCY EVALUATION ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

14.1 EVALUATION CAPABILITIES (A THROUGH H)
Competence cannot be measured by multiple-choice tests or factual regurgitation.
The evaluation engine tests eight distinct cognitive capabilities:
  - Capability A: Knowledge Recall & Explanation (Explaining the underlying physics).
  - Capability B: Evidence Interpretation (Reading measurements without hallucinating).
  - Capability C: Causal Diagnosis (Generating and ranking competing causes).
  - Capability D: Decision-Making & Parsimony (Selecting minimal, defensible fixes).
  - Capability E: Outcome Reasoning & Falsification (Predicting results, defining test criteria).
  - Capability F: Cross-Domain Integration (Solving multi-domain coupled defects).
  - Capability G: Uncertainty Discipline (Stating "insufficient evidence" when ambiguous).
  - Capability H: Anti-Recipe Robustness (Rejecting genre tropes when evidence contradicts).

SECTION 15 — EVALUATION PROBLEM CLASSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The future evaluation suite must contain test cases drawn from fifteen distinct problem classes:

  Class 1: Foundational Knowledge Questions
    Testing physical laws, circuit math, and transducer acoustics in the abstract.

  Class 2: Causal Explanation Questions
    Explaining why a specific electro-acoustic phenomenon occurs.

  Class 3: Single-Domain Diagnostic Problems
    Isolating a fault confined to one domain (e.g., open-back dipole bass cancellation).

  Class 4: Multi-Domain Diagnostic Problems
    Isolating an issue spanning coupled stages (e.g., pickup impedance + preamp sag).

  Class 5: Ambiguous-Evidence Problems
    Providing incomplete data where multiple hypotheses are equally valid; tests capability to withhold premature diagnosis.

  Class 6: Conflicting-Evidence Problems
    Injecting contradictory observations (e.g., text prompt claims "too bright" while FFT shows 6 dB high-end deficit); tests evidence hierarchy.

  Class 7: Insufficient-Evidence Problems
    Withholding critical variables; tests refusal to guess and formal request for missing data.

  Class 8: Alternative-Solution Problems
    Demanding three distinct architectural solutions across different domains with trade-off analysis.

  Class 9: Trade-Off Problems
    Evaluating impossible compromises; demanding explicit justification of accepted side-effects.

  Class 10: Reference-Matching Problems
    Deconstructing an audio stem and designing a platform-independent plan to replicate it.

  Class 11: Outcome-Evaluation Problems
    Analyzing a simulated intervention result and determining whether the fix succeeded or caused secondary faults.

  Class 12: Hypothesis-Revision Problems
    Simulating a failed intervention and requiring the system to revise its causal graph.

  Class 13: Anti-Stereotype / Anti-Recipe Problems
    Presenting an atypical genre problem (e.g., modern metal requiring dark warm mids) that breaks common preset tropes.

  Class 14: Platform-Independence Problems
    Demanding pure acoustic/electrical designs with zero commercial gear names or platform parameters.

  Class 15: Full Professional Case Studies
    Complete end-to-end multi-track engineering scenarios requiring full seven-step execution.

SECTION 16 — COUNTERFACTUAL & ADVERSARIAL EVALUATION STRATEGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

16.1 THE TRIAD DISCRIMINATION TEST
To prove that the AI has learned causal principles rather than memorized recipes,
adversarial test triads are deployed. Consider three cases presenting identical symptoms:

  Symptom: "Heavy rhythm palm mutes sound boomy, flubby, and muddy."
  - Case A (Circuit Over-saturation): High-gain rhythm guitar with excessive bass entering
    the preamp. Root cause: LF intermodulation distortion.
    Correct Fix: Pre-distortion high-pass shelf.
  - Case B (Acoustic Cabinet Resonance): Pre-distortion filtering is already tight, but
    cabinet enclosure has an unbraced 100 Hz cavity resonance ringing for 200 ms.
    Correct Fix: Adjust cabinet damping or switch to a stiffer enclosure; do NOT cut pre-distortion EQ.
  - Case C (Player Touch / Technique): Signal chain is pristine, but audio waveform
    reveals the player's palm is resting 2 cm forward of the bridge saddle, muting
    fundamentals prematurely while exciting boomy string harmonics.
    Correct Fix: Flag performance technique defect; advise player to reposition palm
    anchor; do NOT modify amplifier or cabinet settings.

Evaluation Metric:
  A recipe learner applies "cut bass with Tube Screamer" to all three cases.
  A competent Sound Engineer isolates the true root cause in each case and applies
  three completely different solutions.

SECTION 18 — AUTOMATIC FAILURE CONDITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The occurrence of any single condition below results in immediate, non-negotiable
failure of the competency evaluation, regardless of overall performance:

  1. Acoustic Evidence Fabrication: Hallucinating frequency measurements, dynamic ranges,
     or phase metrics not present in input data.
  2. Spurious Certainty: Declaring >80% confidence when critical evidence is missing
     or contradictory.
  3. Platform Contamination Leak: Emitting commercial model names, GUIDs, or proprietary
     parameters inside the platform-independent Semantic Tone Design.
  4. Recipe Dogmatism: Applying a genre stereotype intervention when objective evidence
     directly contradicts the genre assumption.
  5. Single-Hypothesis Fixation: Failing to formulate at least two competing hypotheses
     in complex multi-domain diagnostic scenarios.
  6. Closed-Loop Falsification Refusal: Declaring an intervention successful without
     measuring or verifying outcome audio.
  7. Destructive Stacking: Applying cumulative downstream EQ/compression stages to mask
     a defect caused by an earlier stage without addressing the root cause.
  8. Audio Blindness Denial: Claiming to have "listened to" or "audited" an audio file
     that was not processed through runtime DSP feature extraction.

SECTION 19 — HUMAN REVIEW & KNOWLEDGE GOVERNANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

19.1 THE KNOWLEDGE PROMOTION LIFECYCLE
To ensure the integrity of the educational knowledge base, no machine-generated or
extracted rule may be promoted autonomously. All assets follow a strict five-stage
governance pipeline:

  Stage 1: SOURCE ACQUISITION
    Documented retrieval from Tier 1 to Tier 4 authoritative sources.

  Stage 2: EXTRACTION & NORMALIZATION
    Decomposition into the Normalized KnowledgeUnit schema.

  Stage 3: DUAL TECHNICAL REVIEW
    Independent verification by two senior audio engineering specialists checking
    physical accuracy and boundary limits.

  Stage 4: ADVERSARIAL VALIDATION
    Subjecting the knowledge unit to counterfactual test fixtures to ensure it does
    not induce recipe behavior.

  Stage 5: PROMOTION TO ACTIVE KNOWLEDGE BASE
    Cryptographically signed sign-off and inclusion in runtime retrieval index.

19.2 EXPERIENTIAL LEARNING GOVERNANCE
When the AI Sound Engineer learns from closed-loop outcomes during runtime:
  - Unexpected outcomes and hypothesis revisions are flagged as Candidate Lessons.
  - Candidate Lessons are isolated in an experimental quarantine store.
  - No Candidate Lesson is promoted to the global knowledge base without human expert
    review and regression testing against the entire benchmark suite.

SECTION 21 — WHAT EDUCATION CANNOT SOLVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

21.1 THE STRUCTURAL BOUNDARY OF EDUCATION
Education trains the mind; it cannot build the body or supply missing senses.
The following engineering capabilities CANNOT be solved by education alone and
demand dedicated architectural and tooling implementations:

  1. Audio Signal Perception:
     - Education cannot give an AI the ability to hear. The runtime must implement
       an objective Audio DSP Extraction Service computing spectral centroid, crest
       factor, harmonic distortion orders, LUFS loudness, and phase correlation.

  2. Closed-Loop Iteration:
     - Education cannot evaluate results if the software pipeline cannot render
       audio, compare output to reference, and feed error deltas back into a second pass.
       The runtime must provide closed-loop execution.

  3. Stateful Checkpoint Lineage:
     - The cognitive model cannot backtrack from failed interventions without runtime
       state persistence supporting tree-based branching and rollbacks.

  4. Schema Invariant Enforcement:
     - Education cannot guarantee mathematical bounds or prevent impossible signal
       connections. Deterministic contract validation must remain an external gate.

  5. Platform-Specific Translation:
     - The AI Sound Engineer must remain platform-independent. Translating semantic
       parameters into AmpliTube 5 XML, Helix SysEx, or analog hardware belongs
       strictly in downstream translation modules.
