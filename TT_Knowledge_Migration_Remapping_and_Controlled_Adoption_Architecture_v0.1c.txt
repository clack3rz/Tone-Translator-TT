2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PURPOSE

2.1 The Architectural Transition Challenge
The existing Tone Translator application possesses an extensive collection of acoustic heuristics, prompt templates, hardcoded parameter clamps, gear catalogues, alias dictionaries, IK Multimedia AmpliTube 5 (AT5) export mappings, and deterministic helper services.
While this legacy system currently produces viable commercial guitar presets, much of its operational logic was developed organically prior to the formalization of the 14-stage sound engineering reasoning lifecycle and the 21 Principles of Sound Engineering Reasoning.

Specifically, the legacy estate contains:
  - Monolithic prompts that collapse evidence intake, diagnosis, requirements, and intervention selection into single, untraceable LLM generation steps;
  - Hardcoded heuristic rules and "magic numbers" that bypass causal reasoning;
  - Upstream platform leakage, where target-platform AT5 parameter IDs and GUI constraints dictate acoustic requirements;
  - Unverified heuristics with lost or undocumented historical provenance;
  - Implicit error-handling fallbacks that silently fabricate acoustic decisions;
  - Hidden dependencies between UI components and deep audio processing services.

2.2 Purpose of Phase 1C.5g
Phase 1C.5g exists to build the governed, reversible, and auditable bridge from this legacy estate into the frozen AI Sound Engineer architecture defined in Phases 1C.5a through 1C.5f.
Its mission is to ensure that:
  1. No valid domain knowledge is discarded simply because it was written in legacy form;
  2. No invalid, unprovenanced, or monolithic legacy heuristic is promoted into authoritative reasoning simply because it exists in the codebase;
  3. Current production tone generation and AT5 export remain 100% stable, protected, and operational while migration proceeds in parallel;
  4. Migration progresses through structured shadow execution and differential comparison without big-bang risk;
  5. Empirical validation and final production sign-off remain strictly under the authority of Phase 1C.5h.

6. GOVERNING MIGRATION INVARIANTS

Every architectural mechanism, workflow, and data structure defined in Phase 1C.5g must strictly enforce the ten governing invariants:

6.1 Invariant 1 — Frozen Architecture Wins
Where legacy Tone Translator behaviour conflicts with the frozen Phase 1C.5a–f architecture, the frozen architecture is authoritative without exception.
Migration must never distort or dilute the frozen architecture to maintain backwards compatibility with flawed legacy behaviour.

6.2 Invariant 2 — Existing Production Capability Must Be Protected
Current production Tone Translator tone generation, signal-chain construction, and AT5 configuration/export capabilities must remain fully functional and operationally available throughout the migration process.
Migration work must never require destructive in-place replacement of the operational production path before thorough validation.

6.3 Invariant 3 — No Big-Bang Migration
Migration must proceed via incremental, staged, and reversible transitions.
No architectural plan, release design, or deployment strategy may mandate an all-or-nothing cutover of the complete reasoning system.

6.4 Invariant 4 — Legacy Provenance Must Survive Migration
Every legacy asset—whether retained, remapped, transformed, quarantined, or retired—must retain full provenance documenting:
  - its exact legacy source location and identifier;
  - its historical behaviour and operational context;
  - the migration disposition assigned to it;
  - the technical rationale behind the disposition;
  - its target destination in the frozen architecture (if applicable);
  - whether its semantic meaning was altered during migration;
  - the governance approval metadata where approval is required.

6.5 Invariant 5 — Migration Must Not Manufacture Validity
Historical existence or operational longevity does not constitute architectural correctness.
The fact that a legacy prompt or heuristic historically produced acceptable tones is empirical evidence of prior utility, not proof of architectural validity or causal truth.

6.6 Invariant 6 — Uncertainty Must Survive Migration
If the correctness, scope, evidential basis, or architectural fit of a legacy asset is uncertain, that uncertainty must remain explicitly recorded and visible.
Uncertain legacy assets must NEVER be forced into RETAIN or REMAP dispositions solely to satisfy migration milestones or give an illusion of completeness.

6.7 Invariant 7 — Migration Must Be Reversible Until Validated
Every cutover, remapping, and adoption step executed prior to formal Phase 1C.5h empirical sign-off must possess an operational, deterministic rollback path.

6.8 Invariant 8 — Historical Behaviour Must Remain Reconstructable
When legacy rules are transformed, deprecated, or retired, their original code, prompts, configurations, and behavioural traces must remain archived and accessible for regression testing, historical audit, and post-mortem analysis.

6.9 Invariant 9 — Shadow Results Are Not Production Authority
Outputs, parameter maps, and signal chains generated by the new AI Sound Engineer architecture during shadow-mode execution must never silently override, replace, or pollute live production outputs prior to governed validation and cutover approval.

6.10 Invariant 10 — Migration Completion Is Not UAT Success
Technical completion of asset remapping or code migration does not constitute empirical success.
Only Phase 1C.5h holds the authority to certify acoustic fidelity, behavioural equivalence, regression resistance, and operational readiness.

7. MIGRATION ESTATE DISCOVERY & ASSET IDENTITY MODEL

7.1 Discovery Architecture & Imperative
Migration governance cannot control what has not first been discovered.
The architecture establishes a comprehensive Legacy Estate Discovery framework designed to audit and index all existing Tone Translator assets across the codebase, configuration files, prompt repositories, and documentation.

7.2 Conceptual Migration Asset Identity (migration_asset_id)
Every legacy asset identified during discovery is assigned a stable, unique, technology-neutral migration asset identity:
  `migration_asset_id` (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED conceptual vocabulary)

This identity is immutable once assigned. It persists across all migration stages, tracking the asset through remapping, transformation, shadowing, validation, and final disposition.
The architecture does NOT prescribe a specific technical implementation for this identity (such as UUIDv4, UUIDv7, ULID, or database auto-increment keys). Any implementation that guarantees uniqueness, stability, and referential integrity is architecturally compliant.

7.3 Conceptual LegacyEstateInventory
The discovery process populates the conceptual `LegacyEstateInventory`.
Each inventory entry conceptually maintains the following governance metadata:
  1. migration_asset_id: Unique conceptual migration asset identifier;
  2. legacy_source_identifier: Exact codebase file path, function name, prompt template name, or data key;
  3. asset_name: Human-readable descriptive name of the legacy asset;
  4. asset_type: Syntactic category (e.g., system prompt, heuristic function, lookup table, clamp constant, error handler);
  5. migration_object_class: Formal assignment to one of the six migration object classes (Section 8);
  6. discovered_version: Historical version, Git commit hash, or modification date where known;
  7. source_provenance: Authorship, documentation reference, historical commit message, or origin notes;
  8. operational_owner_domain: Current subsystem domain (e.g., amplifier modeling, mic placement, catalog normalization);
  9. current_production_dependency: Boolean flag and dependency details indicating whether PATH-PROD actively relies on this asset;
  10. runtime_criticality: Qualitative assessment of operational failure impact (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW);
  11. known_consumers: List of services, components, or UI views that invoke or reference this asset;
  12. known_dependencies: Upstream libraries, files, globals, or assumptions required by this asset;
  13. current_behaviour_summary: Exact description of what the asset does at runtime in current TT;
  14. known_assumptions: Implicit acoustic or runtime assumptions embedded within the asset;
  15. known_fallbacks: Hidden defaults, catch-block returns, or fail-soft behaviours in the asset;
  16. known_platform_coupling: Direct couplings to IK Multimedia AmpliTube 5 IDs, XML structures, or DAW quirks;
  17. known_reasoning_stage_coupling: Legacy mixing of distinct reasoning stage concerns;
  18. evidential_basis: Acoustic or engineering evidence supporting the asset's existence;
  19. known_uncertainty: Explicit documentation of gaps in knowledge, missing provenance, or unverified claims;
  20. historical_usage_info: Empirical frequency of execution, user feedback, or preset usage if known;
  21. candidate_disposition: Proposed initial migration disposition (Section 9);
  22. inventory_completeness_state: Degree of discovery audit completion (DISCOVERED, AUDITED, VERIFIED);
  23. discovery_notes: Contextual notes and annotations recorded by the discovery auditor.

These metadata fields represent conceptual architectural requirements, NOT a frozen JSON schema or TypeScript interface.


8. SIX MIGRATION OBJECT CLASSES

To prevent the catastrophic mistake of treating all code, data, and prompts as interchangeable entities, Phase 1C.5g categorizes all legacy assets into six distinct Migration Object Classes:

8.1 MIG-OBJ-KNOW: Migrated Knowledge
  - Scope: Domain understanding of acoustics, psychoacoustics, electrical engineering, guitar amplifier circuits, speaker cone break-up, room acoustics, and microphone polar patterns.
  - Nature: Declarative, empirical, or theoretical audio engineering principles.
  - Target Architectural Home: Stages 01–07 (Evidence, Observation, Interpretation, Hypothesis, Diagnosis) and Stage 10 (Constraint and Trade-Off Models).
  - Migration Treatment: Must be verified against sound engineering physics. Monolithic knowledge must be decomposed into explicit observation-interpretation-hypothesis chains.

8.2 MIG-OBJ-CONF: Migrated Configuration
  - Scope: Static datasets, gear metadata catalogs, hardware physical specifications, parameter ranges, visual UI knob ranges, and component lists.
  - Nature: Factual, descriptive reference data.
  - Target Architectural Home: Reference catalogs, gear manifests, and platform definition databases.
  - Migration Treatment: Normalized and validated for completeness and accuracy. Never allowed to masquerade as reasoning rules.

8.3 MIG-OBJ-DET: Migrated Deterministic Logic
  - Scope: Deterministic mathematical algorithms, coordinate conversions (e.g., VIR 3D coordinates to polar angles), unit conversions (dB to linear, Hz to Bark), and physical boundary clamps.
  - Nature: Pure, reproducible computational logic.
  - Target Architectural Home: Deterministic utility services, platform translation helpers, and pre-action validation checks.
  - Migration Treatment: Unit-tested for mathematical precision. Barred from replacing subjective or contextual engineering judgement.

8.4 MIG-OBJ-GEN: Migrated Generative / Prompted Reasoning
  - Scope: LLM prompts, system instructions, few-shot examples, chain-of-thought instructions, and prompt formatting templates.
  - Nature: Generative linguistic instructions previously used to drive single-pass or multi-pass reasoning.
  - Target Architectural Home: Governed prompts modularized strictly within individual reasoning stages (e.g., discrete Stage 04 hypothesis generation prompts or Stage 09 intervention candidate prompts).
  - Migration Treatment: Monolithic prompts must be dismantled. Platform-specific instructions must be stripped out. Implicit reasoning must be converted into explicit governed contracts.

8.5 MIG-OBJ-RUN: Migrated Runtime Behaviour
  - Scope: Session persistence, run lifecycle coordination, error handling, cancellation tokens, user interaction flows, and retry policies.
  - Nature: Operational and execution infrastructure.
  - Target Architectural Home: Execution controllers, shadow orchestrators, and session managers.
  - Migration Treatment: Rebuilt to support Phase 1C.5f run identity and immutable trace preservation.

8.6 MIG-OBJ-PLAT: Migrated Platform-Specific Logic
  - Scope: AT5 XML serialization, IKMPAK archive generation, AT5 parameter index mappings, GUI knob step scaling, and platform-specific workarounds.
  - Nature: Target delivery technology logic.
  - Target Architectural Home: Downstream Platform Translation (Stage 12 Platform Translation Plan) and export engines.
  - Migration Treatment: Strictly quarantined downstream of Stage 11. Forbidden from influencing upstream acoustic reasoning, diagnosis, or decision-making.


9. SEVEN MIGRATION DISPOSITIONS

Every discovered asset must be formally assigned exactly one of seven mutually exclusive migration dispositions:

9.1 RETAIN (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
  - Definition: The legacy asset conforms cleanly to the frozen Phase 1C.5a–f architecture, has verified provenance, exhibits no architectural conflicts, and can be adopted directly into the target system without substantive semantic or structural change.
  - Governance Threshold: Very high. Requires positive proof of conformity, verified origin, and full compatibility.

9.2 REMAP (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
  - Definition: The underlying engineering substance of the asset is sound, but its architectural location, taxonomy, record type, or stage boundary is incorrect. The asset is moved to its proper architectural location without changing its core meaning.
  - Example: Moving an acoustic cabinet resonance rule from an AT5 export helper into Stage 10 Constraint Evaluation.

9.3 TRANSFORM (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
  - Definition: The engineering intent or domain insight is valuable, but its representation, structure, or scope is incompatible with frozen contracts (e.g., a prompt that combines diagnosis and intervention). The asset is fundamentally restructured while preserving historical lineage.
  - Governance Requirement: Mandatory transformation record documenting removed assumptions and added constraints.

9.4 QUARANTINE (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
  - Definition: The asset's correctness, evidential foundation, provenance, or compatibility cannot currently be determined or verified. The asset is safely isolated, preserved in history, but strictly barred from execution in the authoritative path.
  - Governance Rule: Quarantine is not permanent rejection; it is an active protective isolation holding the asset until resolved or retired.

9.5 DEPRECATE (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
  - Definition: The asset is maintained temporarily in the legacy production path for backwards compatibility during phased migration, but is marked as obsolete and barred from target architecture development.
  - Governance Requirement: Must have an explicit retirement trigger and replacement plan.

9.6 RETIRE (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
  - Definition: The asset is permanently deactivated from active use because it conflicts irreconcilably with the 21 Principles, duplicates modern functionality, or represents invalid engineering practices.
  - Governance Rule: Retirement does NOT mean deletion. The historical asset remains permanently archived for audit and regression comparison.

9.7 DEFER (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
  - Definition: A migration determination cannot yet be responsibly reached due to missing upstream information, unbuilt downstream runtime dependencies, or pending empirical evidence.
  - Governance Rule: DEFER is not failure; it is the governed postponement of a decision until required dependencies or evidence become available.

9.8 The Zero-Entitlement Mandate
Governing Rule:
"NO LEGACY ASSET IS AUTOMATICALLY ENTITLED TO RETENTION."
The default disposition for any legacy asset upon initial discovery is DEFER or QUARANTINE.
An asset moves to RETAIN, REMAP, or TRANSFORM only upon positive, auditable verification against the disposition decision process.


10. DISPOSITION DECISION PROCESS & DECISION TREES

10.1 Structured Multi-Factor Assessment
The assignment of a migration disposition is governed by a rigorous, multi-factor decision tree.
The architecture strictly rejects arbitrary confidence percentages, weighted heuristic scores, or automated probabilistic disposition assignments. Every disposition decision must be explicitly reasoned and documented.

10.2 Decision Tree Walkthrough
For each discovered asset in `LegacyEstateInventory`:

Step 1: Provenance and Purpose Verification
  - Is the original source, author, commit, or acoustic derivation known?
  - Is the operational purpose and runtime behavior clearly understood?
  -> If NO: Assign QUARANTINE (if potential value exists) or DEFER (if investigation pending). Stop.
  -> If YES: Proceed to Step 2.

Step 2: Upstream Architectural Conflict Analysis
  - Does the asset contradict any of the 21 Principles of Sound Engineering Reasoning?
  - Does it erase uncertainty, fabricate missing evidence, or assert unearned certainty?
  - Does it use an arbitrary hardcoded clamp to override engineering reasoning?
  -> If YES (Irreconcilable Conflict): Assign RETIRE. Archive historical record. Stop.
  -> If YES (Valuable intent trapped inside flawed rule): Proceed to Step 4 (Transformation).
  -> If NO: Proceed to Step 3.

Step 3: Structural & Boundary Analysis
  - Does the asset cleanly map 1:1 to an existing frozen Stage 01–14 record, catalog, or utility?
  - Is it completely free from platform leakage (AT5-specific logic in upstream reasoning)?
  - Is it completely free from stage-monolithic mixing (e.g., diagnosis mixed with intervention)?
  -> If YES: Assign RETAIN. Register destination mapping. Stop.
  -> If NO: Proceed to Step 4.

Step 4: Remapping vs. Transformation Determination
  - Can the asset's structural mismatch be resolved solely by re-attaching it to the correct stage or record type without altering its internal logic?
  -> If YES: Assign REMAP. Document source and destination. Stop.
  -> If NO: Proceed to Step 5.

Step 5: Feasibility of Governed Transformation
  - Can the core engineering intent be cleanly extracted and restructured into compliant 1C.5a–f records (e.g., splitting a prompt into Stage 04 and Stage 09)?
  - Does a target architecture home exist for the transformed components?
  -> If YES: Assign TRANSFORM. Initiate transformation pipeline. Stop.
  -> If target component not yet built: Assign DEFER. Stop.

Step 6: Production Compatibility & Deprecation
  - Is the legacy asset actively required by PATH-PROD to prevent operational downtime?
  - Has an alternative target capability been validated in Phase 1C.5h?
  -> If required by production but obsolete for target architecture: Assign DEPRECATE. Stop.


11. KNOWLEDGE MIGRATION FIREWALL ARCHITECTURE

11.1 The Firewall Mandate
"LEGACY KNOWLEDGE MUST NOT BE PROMOTED INTO FROZEN REASONING STRUCTURES SOLELY BECAUSE IT EXISTS IN CURRENT TT."

11.2 Architectural Position of the Firewall
The Knowledge Migration Firewall is a formal governance checkpoint separating:
  THE UNVERIFIED LEGACY ESTATE (Code, Prompts, Configs, Overrides)
  from
  THE AUTHORITATIVE FROZEN AI SOUND ENGINEER SYSTEM (Phases 1C.5a–f Records)

No asset, rule, prompt fragment, or parameter table may cross the firewall into authoritative reasoning without passing all eleven firewall checkpoints:
  1. Provenance Verification: Source origin, commit, or extraction lineage is fully established;
  2. Object Class Assignment: Formal classification into MIG-OBJ-KNOW/CONF/DET/GEN/RUN/PLAT;
  3. Governed Disposition Approval: Formal assignment of RETAIN, REMAP, or TRANSFORM;
  4. Target Destination Binding: Explicit binding to a validated frozen schema or service;
  5. Compatibility Certification: Documented verification of semantic and structural fit;
  6. Zero Principles Conflict: Proven absence of conflict with the 21 Principles;
  7. Transformation Audit (if applicable): Complete transformation record with no unrecorded loss of meaning;
  8. Preserved Uncertainty: Full documentation of residual doubts, limitations, and caveats;
  9. Authorized Governance Decision: Formally recorded migration governance decision for the asset class;
  10. Candidate-Lesson Ingestion Proof (if applicable): Independent Knowledge Governance promotion approval;
  11. Validation Package Readiness: Complete specification of validation criteria for Phase 1C.5h.

11.3 Firewall Failure Handling
Any asset that fails to clear the firewall is immediately stopped.
The asset receives a governed failure disposition:
  - QUARANTINE: When provenance is missing, evidence is ambiguous, or potential value requires isolation;
  - DEFER: When target dependencies are unimplemented or validation fixtures are unavailable;
  - TRANSFORM: When engineering substance is sound but structure requires redesign;
  - RETIRE: When the asset irreconcilably violates frozen architecture.
Binary forcing (accept or discard) is strictly prohibited.

12. TARGET DESTINATION MAPPING MODEL & CARDINALITY HANDLING

12.1 Destination Mapping Architecture
Every migrated asset must bind to an explicit target destination within the frozen AI Sound Engineer architecture.
Phase 1C.5g establishes the `MigrationDestinationMapping` concept, which pairs an inventoried legacy asset with its authorized destination class and specific target schema.

12.2 Target Destination Classes
A target destination must belong to one of twelve authorized destination classes:
  1. DEST-1C5B-EVID: Inherited Phase 1C.5b Evidence Intake, Observation, Interpretation, or Hypothesis structures;
  2. DEST-1C5C-DIAG: Inherited Phase 1C.5c Causal Reasoning, Diagnostic Hypothesis, or Specificity structures;
  3. DEST-1C5D-DECIS: Inherited Phase 1C.5d Engineering Requirement, Candidate Intervention, Constraint/Trade-Off, or Decision structures;
  4. DEST-1C5E-EVAL: Inherited Phase 1C.5e Pre-Action Validation, Prediction Consumption, Outcome Evaluation, or Review structures;
  5. DEST-1C5F-TRACE: Phase 1C.5f RunTrace, Lineage, Audit, or Integrity structures;
  6. DEST-PLAT-TRANS: Downstream Platform Translation Plan (Stage 12) or serialization services;
  7. DEST-DET-CONF: Deterministic gear manifests, parameter catalogs, or reference configuration databases;
  8. DEST-RUN-ORCH: Runtime execution controllers, shadow dispatchers, or session managers;
  9. DEST-COMPAT-ADAPT: Temporary compatibility adapters bridging legacy interfaces to target APIs;
  10. DEST-QUARANTINE: Isolated historical quarantine repository;
  11. DEST-LEGACY-PATH: Legacy-only production path (PATH-PROD) maintaining backwards compatibility;
  12. DEST-RETIRE-ARCH: Sealed historical retirement archive.

12.3 Governing Non-Conflation Rules
  - Gear metadata (knob ranges, GUIDs, models) does NOT map to reasoning stages (Stages 01–11). It maps strictly to DEST-DET-CONF or DEST-PLAT-TRANS.
  - UI state and user toggles do NOT map to acoustic evidence unless verified by physical or perceptual intake contracts.
  - Fallback error handlers do NOT map to engineering knowledge; they map to runtime orchestration (DEST-RUN-ORCH).

12.4 Mapping Cardinality Models
The architecture supports five distinct mapping cardinalities:
  1. 1:1 Clean Mapping:
     A single legacy asset maps directly to a single target structure (e.g., a verified transformer impedance table mapping to Stage 10 Constraint Reference).
  2. 1:N Decomposition:
     A monolithic legacy asset is split into multiple distinct target structures across different stages (e.g., a monolithic prompt split into a Stage 04 Hypothesis template, a Stage 09 Intervention rule, and a Stage 12 AT5 parameter mapping).
  3. N:1 Consolidation:
     Multiple redundant, overlapping, or conflicting legacy rules are harmonized and consolidated into a single unified target structure (e.g., three separate British amplifier EQ rules consolidated into one Stage 10 trade-off model).
  4. N:M Complex Mapping:
     A network of interacting legacy heuristics is systematically refactored into a coordinated set of target reasoning records.
  5. 1:0 Zero Mapping:
     The legacy asset has no valid target architectural home and is routed to DEST-QUARANTINE or DEST-RETIRE-ARCH.


13. TRANSFORMATION GOVERNANCE & SEMANTIC PRESERVATION ARCHITECTURE

13.1 Governed Transformation Model
When an asset receives the `TRANSFORM` disposition, it enters the governed transformation pipeline.
Transformation is a formal architectural engineering procedure, never an undocumented code rewrite.

13.2 Mandatory Transformation Invariants
  1. Source Non-Destruction: The original legacy asset, code, prompt, or configuration is NEVER overwritten or modified in place. It remains permanently archived in its raw historical form.
  2. Complete Transformation Provenance: Every transformation produces a sealed `MigrationTransformationRecord` capturing:
     - Original legacy asset identity and independently verifiable integrity evidence;
     - Original semantic behaviour and operational intent;
     - Identified architectural defects or boundary violations;
     - Target architectural destination and structure;
     - Explicit statement of technical rationale for transformation;
     - Exhaustive list of removed assumptions, unearned certitudes, or hardcoded constants;
     - Exhaustive list of added constraints, evidential requirements, or uncertainty declarations;
     - Verification requirements for Phase 1C.5h testing.

13.3 Three Semantic Transformation Classifications
Every transformation must be formally categorized into one of three semantic outcomes:
  1. SEMANTICALLY_PRESERVED (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
     The representation, syntax, or stage attachment was restructured, but the underlying engineering intent, acoustic effect, and domain logic remain materially identical.
  2. SEMANTICALLY_NARROWED (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
     The legacy asset claimed overly broad, unearned applicability (e.g., an EQ rule claiming universal validity across all genres). The transformation explicitly constrains its scope to defensible acoustic domains (e.g., heavy rock guitar only).
  3. SEMANTICALLY_REPLACED (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED)
     The legacy behaviour was flawed, causally ungrounded, or violated core principles. The transformation intentionally replaces it with sound, principled reasoning logic, recording the intentional departure from legacy behaviour.

Semantic equivalence must NEVER be assumed or claimed without empirical comparative evidence.


14. CONFLICT DETECTION, CLASSIFICATION & GOVERNANCE

14.1 Systematic Conflict Detection
Phase 1C.5g enforces mandatory conflict detection across all legacy assets prior to migration approval.
The architecture defines the `MigrationConflictRecord` to capture and govern discovered incompatibilities.

14.2 Supported Conflict Classes
The conflict architecture explicitly recognizes and manages fifteen conflict types:
  1. CONF-PRINCIPLE: Direct contradiction of one of the 21 Principles (e.g., asserting certainty from ambiguous evidence);
  2. CONF-LIFECYCLE-OWN: Violation of frozen stage ownership boundaries (e.g., an intervention rule executing during Stage 02 observation);
  3. CONF-EVID-BYPASS: Jumping directly from raw input to intervention, completely bypassing evidence interpretation and causal diagnosis;
  4. CONF-DIAG-BYPASS: Formulating engineering interventions without establishing a causal diagnosis explaining why the tone requires adjustment;
  5. CONF-PLAT-LEAK: Upstream reasoning polluted by target platform constraints (e.g., selecting an EQ requirement because AmpliTube 5 has a specific 7-band graphic EQ module);
  6. CONF-UNCERT-ERASE: Silently discarding, rounding off, or suppressing evidential, diagnostic, or acoustic uncertainty;
  7. CONF-CAUSAL-FAB: Fabricating causal relationships or physical explanations that have no scientific or acoustic basis;
  8. CONF-STAGE-MONO: Monolithic prompt or function collapsing multiple distinct reasoning stages into a black-box execution;
  9. CONF-HARD-OVERRIDE: Hardcoded numerical constant or heuristic overriding reasoned engineering decisions;
  10. CONF-PROV-VOID: Asset with no historical documentation, origin commit, or engineering justification;
  11. CONF-INTER-RULE: Two legacy rules giving contradictory commands for the identical acoustic scenario;
  12. CONF-HIDDEN-DEP: Implicit reliance on global state, side-effects, or hidden UI interaction sequences;
  13. CONF-TARGET-ABSENT: The destination stage or record schema required in the target architecture has not yet been built;
  14. CONF-PROD-TENSION: A legacy asset is architecturally invalid but operationally critical to current PATH-PROD;
  15. CONF-SEM-DRIFT: Transformation logic unintentionally alters domain meaning or distorts acoustic behaviour.

14.3 Conflict Resolution Governance
When a conflict is recorded:
  - The legacy artifact is immediately preserved in its existing state;
  - The conflict is logged with qualitative impact assessment and governing authority reference;
  - The asset is routed to TRANSFORM, QUARANTINE, DEFER, or RETIRE based on the nature of the conflict:
    * TRANSFORM: where valuable engineering intent or domain insight survives, but representation, structure, or stage boundary requires correction;
    * QUARANTINE: where correctness, evidential foundation, or provenance cannot be established;
    * DEFER: where required evidence or target dependencies are unavailable;
    * RETIRE: where the asset irreconcilably conflicts with frozen architecture or represents invalid, obsolete engineering behaviour;
  - History is NEVER rewritten to conceal the conflict.


15. MIGRATION PROVENANCE, LINEAGE & AUDIT ARCHITECTURE

15.1 Integration with Phase 1C.5f Traceability
Phase 1C.5g directly consumes the traceability, provenance, and tamper-evident sealing standards established in Phase 1C.5f.
It does NOT create a competing traceability framework.
Migration records are first-class governance artifacts managed under Phase 1C.5f immutable history guidelines.

15.2 Mandatory Separation: Migration Lineage vs. Reasoning-Run Lineage
To prevent structural conflation, the architecture draws a strict constitutional boundary:
  - Reasoning-Run Lineage (Phase 1C.5e / 1C.5f):
    Governs parent/child relationships between successive *reasoning lifecycle runs* (Stages 01–14) exploring acoustic hypotheses, evaluating outcomes, or iterating on tone presets.
  - Migration Lineage (Phase 1C.5g):
    Governs the historical evolution, remapping, transformation, and version succession of *knowledge assets, code artifacts, and configuration records* as they transition from legacy to target architecture.

Migration iterations, remappings, and asset version updates MUST NOT be classified as Phase 1C.5e reasoning parent/child runs unless an actual runtime reasoning execution is involved.

15.3 The Twelve Audit Questions of Migration Lineage
The migration lineage architecture must allow an independent auditor to answer twelve questions for any asset:
  1. What was the exact legacy asset in its original codebase location?
  2. What was its historical source provenance, author, and commit?
  3. What version was audited during legacy estate discovery?
  4. What migration object class and disposition were formally assigned?
  5. Who or what governing authority approved the disposition decision?
  6. What technical and architectural rationale justified the decision?
  7. What target destination structure in 1C.5a–f was selected?
  8. If transformed, what specific semantic and structural modifications were made?
  9. What successor asset or record replaced the legacy asset?
  10. Was the asset subsequently deprecated, retired, or quarantined?
  11. What shadow comparison fixtures and Phase 1C.5h UAT packages tested the asset?
  12. Did the asset complete governed production cutover, or was rollback invoked?

17. PRODUCTION PROTECTION ARCHITECTURE (PATH-PROD)

17.1 Operational Authority of Current TT
Current Tone Translator production capabilities must remain online, fully functional, and completely stable throughout the entire migration lifecycle.
The legacy production engine is formally designated as `PATH-PROD` (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED).

17.2 The Role and Boundaries of PATH-PROD
PATH-PROD is responsible for:
  - Serving active user requests for tone generation and preset creation;
  - Assembling signal chain topologies using current operational heuristics;
  - Generating and exporting valid IK Multimedia AmpliTube 5 (.at5p) presets and IKMPAK archives;
  - Providing stable operational fallback in the event of new architecture anomalies.

17.3 Operational Authority vs. Epistemic Authority
The architecture establishes a crucial governing distinction:
  "PATH-PROD IS OPERATIONALLY AUTHORITATIVE UNTIL CUTOVER, BUT NOT EPISTEMICALLY AUTHORITATIVE OVER THE FROZEN REASONING ARCHITECTURE."
The fact that PATH-PROD is currently live and generating acceptable guitar tones does not mean its internal heuristics represent sound engineering truth.
PATH-PROD holds operational custody of user tone generation, but possesses ZERO authority to redefine or constrain the frozen AI Sound Engineer reasoning architecture (Phases 1C.5a–f).


18. COEXISTENCE & SHADOW ARCHITECTURE (PATH-SHADOW)

18.1 Parallel Shadow Execution Framework
To validate the new AI Sound Engineer architecture without endangering live production users, Phase 1C.5g establishes `PATH-SHADOW` (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED).
PATH-SHADOW executes the frozen 14-stage reasoning lifecycle in parallel with incoming production workloads or curated benchmark fixtures.

18.2 Invariants of PATH-SHADOW Operation
  1. Complete Execution Isolation:
     PATH-SHADOW executes asynchronously and out-of-band. Its execution time, resource consumption, or memory overhead must have zero impact on PATH-PROD response latency.
  2. Non-Authoritative Output:
     Outputs generated by PATH-SHADOW (signal chains, parameter maps, explanations) are strictly non-authoritative. They are never returned to live end-users or written to production preset storage.
  3. Zero State Contamination:
     PATH-SHADOW has read-only access to input requests and reference catalogs. It is physically and logically prohibited from writing to production databases, mutating active user sessions, or modifying production cache tiers.
  4. Fault Containment:
     Any unhandled exception, reasoning timeout, or constraint violation occurring in PATH-SHADOW is trapped, sealed in an error trace, and suppressed. It must NEVER propagate to PATH-PROD or cause user-facing request failure.
  5. Shadow Success is Not UAT Success:
     A successful, error-free run in PATH-SHADOW demonstrates that the new architecture can execute without crashing. It does NOT prove that the generated tone is acoustically superior or equivalent. Acoustic validation belongs strictly to Phase 1C.5h.


19. DIFFERENTIAL COMPARISON & COMPARABILITY ARCHITECTURE (PATH-COMPARE)

19.1 Objective Differential Evaluation Framework
To understand how the new AI Sound Engineer behaves relative to legacy Tone Translator, Phase 1C.5g defines `PATH-COMPARE` (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED).
PATH-COMPARE captures outputs from both PATH-PROD and PATH-SHADOW for the same input stimulus and performs an automated, multi-dimensional differential analysis.

19.2 The Prohibition of Legacy Truth
Governing Rule:
"PATH-COMPARE STRICTLY PROHIBITS THE ASSUMPTION: LEGACY OUTPUT = EXPECTED TRUTH."
Legacy output is merely historical baseline data, not the target acoustic ground truth.
A difference between PATH-PROD and PATH-SHADOW does not automatically indicate an error in the new architecture. Often, the difference represents the correction of a legacy acoustic defect or the removal of an arbitrary heuristic.

19.3 The Comparability Gate
Before PATH-COMPARE may evaluate a run pair, it must pass the Comparability Gate to ensure comparison is scientifically valid:
  - Input Identity: Both paths consumed the exact same reference audio or semantic prompt;
  - User Intent: The target acoustic intent and genre constraints are identical;
  - Platform Target: Both paths are configured for the identical target delivery platform (e.g., AmpliTube 5);
  - Gear Catalog Parity: Both paths operated against compatible gear model definitions;
  - Calibration Parity: Input audio gain staging and calibration assumptions match.
If comparability cannot be verified, PATH-COMPARE records `COMPARABILITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED`, logs the limitation, preserves uncertainty, and aborts differential scoring.

19.4 Suggested Conceptual Divergence Classes
When comparability is established, PATH-COMPARE classifies differences into eight conceptual categories (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED):
  1. STRUCTURAL_DIVERGENCE: Variations in block order, split routing, or bus topology;
  2. GEAR_SELECTION_DIVERGENCE: Differences in selected amplifier, cabinet, microphone, or pedal models;
  3. PARAMETER_DIVERGENCE: Differences in specific knob values (e.g., gain, bass, mid, treble, bias);
  4. REASONING_DIVERGENCE: Variations in identified hypotheses, acoustic requirements, or trade-off evaluations;
  5. CONSTRAINT_DIVERGENCE: Differences in physical or electrical constraint evaluations;
  6. UNCERTAINTY_DIVERGENCE: Differences in declared uncertainty bounds (e.g., new path explicitly captures doubt that legacy ignored);
  7. PLATFORM_TRANSLATION_DIVERGENCE: Variations in final parameter ID mapping or XML formatting;
  8. EXECUTION_OUTPUT_DIVERGENCE: Audio waveform or spectral differences between exported presets.

Divergence is empirical evidence for analysis. It must NEVER be automatically categorized as a "regression" without Phase 1C.5h acoustic evaluation.


20. ADOPTION READINESS ARCHITECTURE & GATES 1–7

20.1 Gated Progression Framework
To prevent premature release of unverified components, Phase 1C.5g establishes an objective, seven-stage readiness framework.
An asset, component, or capability must pass Gates 1 through 7 before it is eligible for handoff to Phase 1C.5h.

20.2 The Seven Phase 1C.5g Adoption Readiness Gates
  - Gate 1: Structural Mapping Complete
    The asset is formally catalogued in `LegacyEstateInventory` and bound to an authorized target destination in 1C.5a–f.
  - Gate 2: Architectural Compatibility Verified
    Automated and architectural review confirms zero conflicts with the 21 Principles, stage boundaries, or frozen contracts.
  - Gate 3: Provenance & Lineage Sealed
    The asset's complete migration provenance, transformation rationale, and lineage chain are recorded and sealed under Phase 1C.5f standards.
  - Gate 4: Shadow Execution Exercised
    The asset has executed successfully within PATH-SHADOW across a representative battery of benchmark workloads without runtime errors.
  - Gate 5: Differential Analysis Documented
    PATH-COMPARE has captured and catalogued all divergences between legacy and new behaviour, with engineering rationales provided for all intentional departures.
  - Gate 6: Rollback Path Operational
    Deterministic, automated reversion to the legacy PATH-PROD implementation has been tested and verified under failure simulation.
  - Gate 7: Handoff Package Assembled
    The complete migration delivery package—including inventory data, transformation records, shadow diffs, and rollback plans—is sealed and transmitted to Phase 1C.5h.


21. CONTROLLED ADOPTION ARCHITECTURE (PATH-ADOPT) & PHASE 1C.5h GATE 8 AUTHORITY

21.1 Activation of PATH-ADOPT
Once a capability satisfies Gates 1 through 7, it stands ready for production consideration.
However, it CANNOT be deployed to live users until it passes Gate 8:
  "GATE 8: CUTOVER AUTHORIZATION — EXCLUSIVELY OWNED BY PHASE 1C.5h."

21.2 The Role of PATH-ADOPT
Upon receipt of formal Gate 8 authorization from Phase 1C.5h, Phase 1C.5g activates `PATH-ADOPT` (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED).
PATH-ADOPT is the governed production routing path that channels live user traffic to the validated AI Sound Engineer architecture.

21.3 Controlled Adoption Granularities
The architecture rejects monolithic, all-or-nothing deployments.
PATH-ADOPT supports controlled, incremental cutover across multiple orthogonal dimensions:
  - By Capability: Migrating microphone placement reasoning while keeping amplifier selection on PATH-PROD;
  - By Subsystem: Migrating post-amp rack EQ while keeping pre-amp stompboxes on PATH-PROD;
  - By Domain / Genre: Migrating high-gain metal tone crafting while keeping vintage jazz tones on PATH-PROD;
  - By Gear Family: Migrating British lead amplifiers while keeping American clean amplifiers on PATH-PROD;
  - By User Cohort: Channeling beta-opted users to the new path while general users remain on PATH-PROD;
  - By Workload Class: Routing single-track guitar tasks to the new path while complex multi-track stems remain on PATH-PROD.

21.4 Handling Phase 1C.5h Rejection
If Phase 1C.5h fails an asset during empirical UAT, Gate 8 is DENIED.
A Phase 1C.5h rejection may cause Phase 1C.5g to change the migration disposition or route the asset for corrective work, but any substantive engineering correction must occur under the applicable frozen reasoning authority.
Phase 1C.5g updates the asset's migration records and routes the asset accordingly:
  - Remanded to TRANSFORM: If the failure was due to acoustic tuning or constraint formulation defects; substantive reformulation of requirements, interventions, or constraints must occur under the applicable frozen reasoning authorities (Phases 1C.5b–1C.5d);
  - Remanded to REMAP: If the failure revealed stage boundary or classification errors;
  - Remanded to QUARANTINE: If the failure exposed unverified acoustic assumptions or ungrounded heuristics;
  - Remanded to DEFER: If target execution engines were unable to fulfill the reasoning plan;
  - Remanded to RETIRE: If empirical testing proved the underlying engineering premise is acoustically invalid.
Phase 1C.5g does NOT itself reformulate acoustic requirements, re-diagnose causes, redesign interventions, alter trade-off evaluations, or select new engineering decisions.


22. ROLLBACK & REVERSION GOVERNANCE (PATH-ROLLBACK)

22.1 Immediate Fail-Safe Reversion
If an adopted capability operating under PATH-ADOPT exhibits operational anomalies, unexpected latency spikes, unhandled exceptions, or user-reported acoustic degradations, the architecture immediately invokes `PATH-ROLLBACK` (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED).

22.2 Invariants of PATH-ROLLBACK Execution
  1. Deterministic Reversion:
     Traffic is immediately and deterministically rerouted from PATH-ADOPT back to the proven legacy PATH-PROD implementation.
  2. Multi-Level Granularity:
     Rollback can be executed at the system level (100% reversion), subsystem level, genre level, or on an isolated transaction level (instant fallback for a single failing request).
  3. History and Trace Preservation:
     Rollback restores operational stability without destroying history. The full execution trace, error context, input stimulus, and diagnostic state of the failing run must be preserved in accordance with Phase 1C.5f.
  4. Technology-Neutral Performance:
     Rollback must occur within an operationally safe time interval. The architecture does not prescribe arbitrary numeric millisecond targets; exact latency SLAs are deferred to deployment infrastructure contracts and Phase 1C.5h validation.
  5. Rollback is Not Architectural Falsification:
     A rollback event means that operational execution failed or acoustic performance degraded. It does NOT mean the entire target architecture is invalid. Rollback triggers structured engineering post-mortem analysis.


23. DEPRECATION, RETIREMENT, QUARANTINE & DEFER LIFE CYCLES

23.1 Life Cycle of DEPRECATE
  - Purpose: Accommodates legacy components that are operationally necessary for current PATH-PROD stability but are designated for permanent obsolescence in the target architecture.
  - Governance Contract: Every deprecated asset must possess a `DeprecationRecord` detailing:
    - Deprecation trigger and technical rationale;
    - Reference to the replacement target capability under development (where replacement reasoning must conform to the applicable Phase 1C.5d architecture and be produced through the appropriate governed reasoning process);
    - Permitted operational boundary (e.g., "PATH-PROD only, strictly barred from PATH-SHADOW");
    - Explicit retirement conditions (e.g., "Full retirement upon Phase 1C.5h Gate 8 sign-off of replacement logic").

23.2 Life Cycle of RETIRE
  - Purpose: Formal deactivation of assets that violate the 21 Principles, duplicate target capabilities, or represent obsolete code.
  - Invariant: Retirement is NOT deletion. The asset, its source code, its historical performance data, and its retirement rationale are sealed in `DEST-RETIRE-ARCH`. Historical runs that used the asset remain 100% reconstructable.

23.3 Life Cycle of QUARANTINE
  - Purpose: Safe containment of assets whose acoustic validity, provenance, or compatibility cannot be verified.
  - Governance Rule: Quarantined assets are locked in `DEST-QUARANTINE`. They cannot be referenced by PATH-SHADOW or PATH-ADOPT.
  - Release Pathway: A quarantined asset may only be released if new provenance is discovered or formal acoustic investigation resolves the ambiguity, allowing re-classification to TRANSFORM or REMAP.

23.4 Life Cycle of DEFER
  - Purpose: Governed postponement of migration decisions when external dependencies (such as an unbuilt target stage or missing UAT test fixtures) prevent immediate assessment.
  - Invariant: DEFER is a normal, healthy governance state, not a failure. When the blocking dependency is delivered, the asset is automatically re-evaluated under the disposition decision tree.

24. TONE TRANSLATOR SPECIFIC MIGRATION DOMAINS

To ensure exhaustive coverage of the existing codebase, Phase 1C.5g identifies fourteen concrete migration domains across Tone Translator.
These domains must be comprehensively inventoried during legacy discovery:

Domain A: Tone-Generation Prompts & Generative Reasoning
  - Includes current single-pass generation prompts, multi-pass recipe builders, and perceptual tone-shaping templates.
Domain B: Audio Feature Extraction & Interpretation Logic
  - Includes frequency centroid analysis, transient detection, RMS energy calculation, and raw spectral heuristics.
Domain C: Signal-Chain Topology Generation
  - Includes routing heuristics, serial vs. parallel split determinations, block ordering rules, and stereo bus logic.
Domain D: Gear Selection Knowledge
  - Includes heuristic selection logic for stompboxes, amplifiers, cabinets, speakers, microphones, and rack units.
Domain E: Gear Aliases & Identity Mapping
  - Includes fuzzy matching tables, historical naming aliases, brand mappings, and hardware cross-references.
Domain F: Gear Parameter Manifests & Verified Parameter Overrides
  - Includes parameter index manifests, min/max range limits, verified parameter overrides, and default settings.
Domain G: Parameter Normalization, Visual Range & Conversion Logic
  - Includes mathematical functions converting visual UI percentages (0–10) to internal DAW float values (0.0–1.0) and decibel curves.
Domain H: AT5 .at5p Export & Serialization Logic
  - Includes XML preset generation, XML node nesting, GUID injection, IKMPAK packaging, and checksum calculations.
Domain I: VIR Microphone Placement Reasoning & Calibration
  - Includes 3D volumetric speaker coordinates (X, Y, Z, distance, angle), speaker cone phase alignment, and off-axis high-frequency roll-off heuristics.
Domain J: Existing Comparison & Tuning Logic
  - Includes legacy A/B preset comparison logic, error tolerance checking, and iterative parameter tweaking scripts.
Domain K: Session & Runtime State Persistence
  - Includes browser localStorage caching, Firebase Firestore session synchronization, and run history logging.
Domain L: Error Handling, Fallbacks & Missing-Gear Handling
  - Includes catch-block default presets, missing amplifier fallback substitutions, and silent parameter clamping.
Domain M: Historical Artist/Album/Genre-Specific Tuning Heuristics
  - Includes specialized hardcoded rules developed for specific artists (e.g., AC/DC Malcolm Young studio tone heuristics), vintage genres, or album styles.
Domain N: Reference Presets & Reference Knowledge Assets
  - Includes curated reference JSON presets, benchmark audio stems, and static curriculum documents.

Governing Rule:
These fourteen domains define the discovery perimeter. The architecture does NOT assume that all assets in these domains will migrate into reasoning stages, nor does it assign final dispositions in this specification. Dispositions are assigned strictly through the governed discovery and assessment process.


25. IK MULTIMEDIA AMPLITUBE 5 PLATFORM-KNOWLEDGE SEPARATION

25.1 The Architectural Separation Mandate
One of the most critical responsibilities of Phase 1C.5g is untangling valid target-platform knowledge from invalid upstream platform leakage.
Tone Translator was historically built around IK Multimedia AmpliTube 5 (AT5). Over time, target platform constraints became deeply entangled with acoustic engineering reasoning.

25.2 Valid Downstream Platform Knowledge
The architecture explicitly recognizes that AmpliTube 5 specific knowledge is highly valuable and must be preserved:
  - Hardware model identifiers and GUIDs;
  - Parameter IDs, control indexes, and enumeration maps;
  - Legitimate parameter ranges and non-linear taper curves;
  - XML preset schema formatting and IKMPAK bundle serialization;
  - Volumetric Impulse Response (VIR) speaker cone coordinate systems and microphone capsule orientation limits;
  - Verified parameter overrides that correct known discrepancies between the AT5 UI and DAW automation.
Target Architectural Home: All valid AT5 platform knowledge is strictly isolated downstream of Stage 11, residing in DEST-DET-CONF and Stage 12 Platform Translation Plan (DEST-PLAT-TRANS).

25.3 Invalid Upstream Platform Leakage
The architecture strictly prohibits and eliminates upstream platform leakage:
  - Formulating an engineering requirement because an AT5 knob exists (violating Principle 15: Platform Translation Operates Downstream of Engineering Reasoning, and Principle 8: Engineering Intent Constrains the Solution);
  - Restricting causal diagnosis of a muddy tone to only gear models currently present in AT5;
  - Distorting perceptual acoustic requirements to match AT5 GUI parameter limitations;
  - Treating AT5 parameter numbers (e.g., "Gain = 6.2") as upstream acoustic requirements.
Target Architectural Action: All upstream platform leakage must be stopped at the Knowledge Migration Firewall and assigned to TRANSFORM or RETIRE.


26. PROMPT DECOMPOSITION & GENERATIVE REASONING MIG-OBJ-GEN

26.1 Dismantling the Monolithic Prompt
Legacy Tone Translator relies heavily on large generative prompts that ask a language model to take a user prompt (e.g., "Give me a punchy 80s hard rock guitar tone") and output complete amplifier, cabinet, and pedal parameters in a single pass.
This monolithic structure is completely incompatible with the frozen 14-stage reasoning architecture. It bypasses evidence parsing, obliterates hypothesis discrimination, conceals causal diagnosis, and erases uncertainty.

26.2 Systematic Decomposition Procedure
When a generative prompt enters the migration pipeline (MIG-OBJ-GEN):
  1. Semantic Decomposition Audit:
     The prompt is analyzed to extract embedded cognitive functions:
     - Evidence Assumptions: What input acoustic cues does the prompt assume?
     - Perceptual Interpretations: What acoustic adjectives does it translate?
     - Diagnostic Hypotheses: What causal assumptions does it make about frequency problems?
     - Engineering Requirements: What targets does it define (e.g., harmonic saturation, bandwidth)?
     - Candidate Interventions: What gear types and settings does it recommend?
     - Platform Formatting: What XML, JSON, or parameter templates does it generate?
  2. Structural Extraction & Isolation:
     - Embedded physical knowledge is extracted to Stage 08–10 constraint models;
     - Platform-specific XML templates are moved to Stage 12 translation helpers;
     - The monolithic prompt is decommissioned and assigned to RETIRE;
     - Clean, modular prompts are constructed, each constrained strictly to a single lifecycle stage (e.g., a prompt dedicated exclusively to Stage 04 Hypothesis Formation or Stage 09 Intervention Generation).
  3. Preservation of Explicit Governance:
     Private, unguided LLM "chain-of-thought" is never accepted as an architectural substitute for explicit, governed reasoning records. Only verifiable Stage 01–14 records cross the boundary.


27. DETERMINISTIC LOGIC MIGRATION & PHYSICAL CONSTRAINT VALIDATION

27.1 Role of Deterministic Code in the Frozen Architecture
Deterministic code (MIG-OBJ-DET) provides mathematical precision, reproducible conversions, and rigid physical boundary checks.
However, in the legacy codebase, deterministic code was frequently used to bypass sound engineering judgement.

27.2 Permitted Roles for Migrated Deterministic Logic
Deterministic logic is welcomed and retained when performing:
  - Physical and electrical constraint validation (e.g., verifying amplifier impedance load against cabinet impedance);
  - Unit conversions (e.g., converting milliseconds of pre-delay to beat divisions based on BPM);
  - Coordinate conversions (e.g., transforming VIR cartesian coordinates into polar microphone angles);
  - Reference lookup (e.g., mapping a gear name to its physical component topology);
  - Platform serialization (e.g., building valid XML trees).

27.3 Prohibited Roles for Deterministic Logic
In accordance with Principle 17 (Deterministic Code Validates Constraints; It Does Not Replace Sound Engineering Judgement), deterministic code is strictly PROHIBITED from:
  - Silently selecting an engineering intervention without a causal diagnosis;
  - Applying hardcoded "magic clamps" that override reasoned engineering trade-offs;
  - Overriding explicit acoustic uncertainty with arbitrary default values;
  - Silently mutating user-specified requirements.
Deterministic code that violates these rules must be assigned to TRANSFORM or RETIRE.


28. MIGRATION DEPENDENCY GRAPH, UNITS, PACKAGES & SEQUENCING

28.1 The Conceptual Migration Unit
The architecture defines the `MigrationUnit` as the smallest atom of migration governance that can independently:
  - Be discovered and inventoried;
  - Receive an approved disposition;
  - Be mapped to a target structure;
  - Be exercised in PATH-SHADOW;
  - Be validated in Phase 1C.5h;
  - Be adopted in PATH-ADOPT;
  - Be reverted in PATH-ROLLBACK.

28.2 Migration Packages for Co-Dependent Assets
Where two or more legacy assets exhibit tight circular dependencies (e.g., an amplifier model definition, its verified parameter overrides, and its bias calculation heuristic), migrating them individually would cause runtime inconsistency.
In such cases, assets are bundled into a `MigrationPackage` (PHASE_1C5G_PROPOSED). The entire package progresses through the adoption gates as a cohesive, atomic unit.

28.3 The Migration Dependency Graph
Phase 1C.5g constructs a formal Migration Dependency Graph.
This graph tracks:
  - Prerequisite configuration dependencies;
  - Downstream platform translation requirements;
  - Cross-asset heuristic couplings;
  - Target runtime engine availability dependencies.
The Migration Dependency Graph is completely distinct from the runtime reasoning DAG (Phase 1C.5a) and sealed trace graphs (Phase 1C.5f).

28.4 Recommended Migration Sequencing Principles
To minimize operational risk, migration sequencing follows an incremental, dependency-first hierarchy:
  Phase 1: Static catalogs, gear manifests, and parameter definitions (low risk, high stability);
  Phase 2: Deterministic mathematical utilities and coordinate converters;
  Phase 3: Downstream platform serialization and export adapters;
  Phase 4: Bounded, domain-specific acoustic knowledge assets;
  Phase 5: Decomposed generative prompts and modular reasoning templates;
  Phase 6: Multi-stage reasoning orchestration and shadow execution;
  Phase 7: Gated capability-by-capability production cutover.


29. CANDIDATE-LESSON INGESTION BOUNDARY & GOVERNANCE INTEGRATION

29.1 Strict Authority Boundaries
The architecture preserves the constitutional boundary established in Phases 1C.5e and 1C.5f:
  "PHASE 1C.5g DOES NOT APPROVE THE PROMOTION OF CANDIDATE LESSONS INTO AUTHORITATIVE KNOWLEDGE."

29.2 The Governed Promotion Lifecycle
  1. Generation: Candidate lessons are generated during Stage 14 Engineering Review (Phase 1C.5e) following empirical runs;
  2. Containment: Candidate lessons are immediately placed into quarantine under Phase 1C.5f governance;
  3. Authoritative Review: Independent Knowledge Governance evaluates the lesson against acoustic theory, empirical evidence, and regression risk;
  4. Governance Approval: Knowledge Governance records explicit, independently verifiable approval evidence authorizing promotion;
  5. Ingestion by 1C.5g: Phase 1C.5g migration tooling verifies the approval record and executes the technical remapping and ingestion into the target knowledge base;
  6. Empirical Handoff: The promoted lesson is delivered to Phase 1C.5h for empirical regression verification.

29.3 Firewall Rejection of Unapproved Lessons
Any automated script, runtime process, or developer attempt to ingest a candidate lesson that lacks independently verifiable Knowledge Governance approval evidence is instantly blocked by the Knowledge Migration Firewall and remanded to QUARANTINE. No particular cryptographic algorithm is mandated, but evidence must be tamper-evident and auditable.

32. MIGRATION FAILURE & DEGRADATION GOVERNANCE

The architecture provides deterministic, fail-safe handling for the fifteen migration failure conditions defined in the Design Mandate:

32.1 MIG-FAIL-01: Unidentified Legacy Dependency
An asset relies on an undocumented global variable or legacy helper.
Governance Action: Migration halted immediately; asset assigned to QUARANTINE; discovery audit re-opened.

32.2 MIG-FAIL-02: Unresolved Provenance
An asset has no verifiable origin, documentation, or author.
Governance Action: Asset barred from RETAIN; assigned to QUARANTINE or DEFER; origin investigated.

32.3 MIG-FAIL-03: Ambiguous Migration Destination
An asset addresses multiple overlapping concepts without a clear target.
Governance Action: Asset assigned to TRANSFORM for mandatory decomposition; direct mapping blocked.

32.4 MIG-FAIL-04: Frozen Architecture Conflict
An asset violates the 21 Principles or frozen stage boundaries.
Governance Action: Immediate rejection; assigned to RETIRE; historical copy preserved.

32.5 MIG-FAIL-05: Conflicting Legacy Rules
Two historical rules give contradictory instructions for the same condition.
Governance Action: Both assets assigned to QUARANTINE; referred to Knowledge Governance.

32.6 MIG-FAIL-06: Semantic Information Loss
A proposed transformation strips away critical acoustic nuances or physical constraints.
Governance Action: Transformation rejected; asset remanded to TRANSFORM for redesign.

32.7 MIG-FAIL-07: Scope Expansion / Semantic Drift
A remapped asset broadens its applicability beyond its valid domain.
Governance Action: Mapping rejected; scope strictly constrained; remanded to TRANSFORM.

32.8 MIG-FAIL-08: Shadow Comparison Infrastructure Failure
PATH-COMPARE fails to capture or compare differential outputs.
Governance Action: Shadow runs marked UNVERIFIED; advancement through Gates 4 and 5 blocked.

32.9 MIG-FAIL-09: Rollback Mechanism Failure
Automated rollback testing fails or exceeds safe operational windows.
Governance Action: Gate 6 failed; all cutover preparations locked until rollback reliability is restored.

32.10 MIG-FAIL-10: Target Dependency Unimplemented
The target stage or service in 1C.5a–f is not yet available in runtime.
Governance Action: Asset assigned to DEFER; held safely until target is deployed.

32.11 MIG-FAIL-11: Downstream Validation Unavailable
Phase 1C.5h testing harness or golden audio stems are unavailable.
Governance Action: Gate 7 handoff held in staging; production cutover prohibited.

32.12 MIG-FAIL-12: Production-Critical but Architecturally Incompatible
A legacy heuristic is vital to live production but fundamentally flawed architecturally.
Governance Action: Retained strictly in PATH-PROD; assigned to DEPRECATE; replacement reasoning must conform to the applicable Phase 1C.5d architecture and be produced through the appropriate governed reasoning process.

32.13 MIG-FAIL-13: Material Unexplained Divergence
PATH-SHADOW produces radically divergent parameters without causal justification in the trace.
Governance Action: Flagged for diagnostic triage; Gate 5 blocked; asset investigated.

32.14 MIG-FAIL-14: Missing Evidential Foundation
A transformed rule requires structured evidence that frontend intake cannot supply.
Governance Action: Asset assigned to DEFER or constrained to conditional execution.

32.15 MIG-FAIL-15: Unapproved Candidate Lesson Ingestion Attempt
An attempt is made to ingest an unapproved Stage 14 candidate lesson.
Governance Action: Knowledge Migration Firewall blocks ingestion; lesson quarantined; governance alert generated.

Governing Rule: Missing information must remain explicitly missing. The architecture strictly prohibits fabricating missing provenance, rationale, or test data.
